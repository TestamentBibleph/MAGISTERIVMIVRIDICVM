II. DE MUNERE B. VIRGINIS IN OECONOMIA SALUTIS
II. ON THE OFFICE OF THE BLESSED VIRGIN IN THE ECONOMY OF SALVATION
Sacrae Litterae Veteris Novique Testamenti et veneranda Traditio munus Matris Salvatoris in salutis oeconomia modo magis magisque dilucido ostendunt et veluti conspiciendum proponunt. Libri quidem Veteris Testamenti historiam salutis, qua Christi in mundum adventus lento gradu praeparatur, describunt. Quae primaeva documenta, qualiter in Ecclesia leguntur et sub luce ulterioris et plenae revelationis intelliguntur, clarius pedetentim in lucem proferunt figuram mulieris, Matris Redemptoris. Ipsa, sub hac luce, iam prophetice adumbratur in promissione, lapsis in peccatum primis parentibus data, de victoria super serpentem (cf. Gn 3,15). Similiter haec est Virgo quae concipiet et pariet Filium, cuius nomen vocabitur Emmanuel (cf. Is 7,14 cf. Mic. 5, 2-3; Mt 1,22-23). Ipsa praecellit inter humiles ac pauperes Domini, qui salutem cum fiducia ab Eo sperant et accipiunt. Cum Ipsa tandem praecelsa Filia Sion, post diuturnam exspectationem promissionis, complentur tempora et nova instauratur Oeconomia, quando Filius Dei humanam naturam ex ea assumpsit, ut mysteriis carnis suae hominem a peccato liberaret.
The Father of mercies willed that the acceptance of the pre-destined mother should precede the incarnation, so that, just as a woman contributed to death, so also a woman should contribute to life. Which is most eminently true of the Mother of Jesus, who poured out upon the world that very Life, renewing all things, and was endowed by God with gifts worthy of so great an office. Hence it is no wonder that among the holy Fathers the custom grew up whereby they called the Mother of God all holy and immune from every stain of sin, as if formed by the Holy Spirit and molded into a new creature. Enriched with the splendors of altogether singular holiness from the first instant of her conception, the Virgin of Nazareth is greeted by the angel announcing, by God's command, as "full of grace" (cf. Lk 1:28), and she herself responds to the heavenly message: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done to me according to thy word" (Lk 1:38). Thus Mary, daughter of Adam, consenting to the divine word, became the Mother of Jesus, and embracing the saving will of God with full heart, no sin impeding, totally devoted herself as a handmaid of the Lord to the person and work of her Son, under Him and with Him, by the grace of omnipotent God, serving the mystery of redemption. Therefore the holy Fathers rightly think that Mary was not merely passively used by God, but cooperated in human salvation by free faith and obedience. For she, as St. Irenaeus says, "being obedient, became the cause of salvation for herself and for the whole human race." Hence not a few of the ancient Fathers in their preaching gladly assert with him: "The knot of Eve's disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary; what the virgin Eve bound by unbelief, the Virgin Mary loosed by faith"; and comparing her with Eve, they call Mary the mother of the living, and often affirm: "Death through Eve, life through Mary."
¶193
Voluit autem misericordiarum Pater, ut acceptatio praedestinatae matris incarnationem praecederet, ut sic, quemadmodum femina contulit ad mortem, ita etiam femina conferret ad vitam. Quod praecedentissime valet de Matre Iesu, quae ipsam Vitam, omnia renovantem, mundo effudit, et a Deo donis tanto munere dignis praedita est. Unde nil mirum apud Sanctos Patres usum invaluisse quo Deiparam appellarunt totam sanctam et ab omni peccati labe immunem, quasi a Spiritu Sancto plasmatam novamque creaturam formatam. Singularis prorsus sanctitatis splendoribus a primo instante suae conceptionis ditata, Nazarethana Virgo ab Angelo nuntiante, Dei mandato, ut gratia plena salutatur (cf. Lc 1,28), et caelesti nuntio ipsa respondet: Ecce Ancilla Domini, fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum (Lc 1,38). Ita Maria filia Adam, verbo divino consentiens, facta est Mater Iesu, ac salvificam voluntatem Dei, pleno corde et nullo retardata peccato, complectens, semetipsam ut Domini ancillam personae et operi Filii sui totaliter devovit, sub Ipso et cum Ipso, omnipotentis Dei gratia, mysterio redemptionis inserviens. Merito igitur SS.Patres Mariam non mere passive a Deo adhibitam, sed libera fide et oboedientia humanae saluti cooperantem censent. Ipsa enim, ut ait S.Irenaeus, oboediens et sibi et universo generi humano causa facta est salutis, Unde non pauci Patres antiqui in praedicatione sua cum eo libenter asserunt: Hevae inoboedientiae nodum solutionem accepisse per oboedientiam Mariae; quod alligavit virgo Heva per incredulitatem, hoc virginem Mariam solvisse per fidem; et comparatione cum Heva instituta, Mariam matrem viventium appellant, saepiusque affirmant: a mors per Hevam, vita per Mariam.
This conjunction of the Mother with the Son in the saving work is manifested from the time of the virginal conception of Christ to His death: first, when Mary, rising up with haste to visit Elizabeth, is hailed by her as blessed on account of her faith in the promised salvation and the forerunner exults in his mother's womb (cf. Lk 1:41-45); in the nativity, when the Mother of God joyfully shows to shepherds and Magi her firstborn Son, who did not diminish but consecrated her virginal integrity; when she presented Him to the Lord in the temple, offering the gift of the poor, and heard Simeon foretelling at the same time that the Son would be a sign of contradiction and that the mother's soul would be pierced by a sword, that the thoughts of many hearts might be revealed (cf. Lk 2:34-35). When the child Jesus was lost and sought with sorrow, his parents found him in the temple occupied with the things of His Father, and did not understand the word of the Son. But His mother kept all these things in her heart, pondering them (cf. Lk 2:41-51).
¶194
Haec autem Matris cum Filio in opere salutari coniunctio a tempore virginalis conceptionis Christi ad Eius usque mortem manifestatur; in primis quidem cum Maria, exsurgens cum festinatione ad visitandam Elisabeth, ab ea ob fidem suam in salutem promissam beata salutatur et praecursor in sinu matris exsultavit (cf. Lc 1,41-45); in nativitate vero, cum Deipara Filium suum primogenitum, qui virginalem eius integritatem non minuit sed sacravit, pastoribus et Magis laetabunda ostendit. Cum autem Eum in templo, dono pauperum oblato, Domino praesentavit, audivit Simeonem simul praenuntiantem Filium futurum contradictionis signum et matris animam gladium pertransiturum, ut revelarentur ex multis cordibus cogitationes (cf. Lc 2,34-35). Puerum Iesum deperditum ac cum dolore quaesitum, parentes eius in templo invenerunt in his quae Patris Eius erant occupatum; verbumque Filii non intellexerunt. Mater vero Eius omnia haec in corde suo meditabunda conservabat (cf. Lc 2,41-51).
In the public life of Jesus, the Mother appears signally, first at the wedding in Cana of Galilee, when moved with compassion, she by her intercession brought about the beginning of the signs of Jesus the Messiah (cf. Jn 2:1-11). In the course of His preaching, she received the words by which the Son, extolling the Kingdom beyond the reasons and bonds of flesh and blood, proclaimed blessed those who hear and keep the word of God, as she faithfully did (cf. Lk 2:19, 51; cf. Mk 3:35 par.; Lk 11:27-28). Thus the Blessed Virgin also progressed in the pilgrimage of faith, and faithfully sustained her union with her Son even to the cross, where she stood (cf. Jn 19:25), not without divine counsel, vehemently suffering with her Only-begotten and associated herself with maternal heart to His sacrifice, lovingly consenting to the immolation of the victim born of her; and finally by the same Christ Jesus dying on the cross she was given as a mother to the disciple with these words: "Woman, behold your son" (cf. Jn 19:26-27).
¶195
In vita publica Iesu, Mater Eius signanter apparet, in initio quidem, cum ad nuptias in Cana Galilaeae, misericordia permota, initium signorum Iesu Messiae intercessione sua induxit (cf. Jn 2,1-11). In decursu praedicationis Eius suscepit verba, quibus Filius, Regnum ultra rationes et vincula carnis et sanguinis extollens, audientes et custodientes verbum Dei, sicut ipsa fideliter faciebat (cf. Lc 2,19 Lc 2,51), beatos proclamavit (cf. Mc 3,35 par. Lc 11,27-28. Ita etiam B. Virgo in peregrinatione fidei processit, suamque unionem cum Filio fideliter sustinuit usque ad crucem, ubi non sine divino consilio stetit (cf. Jn 19,25), vehementer cum Unigenito suo condoluit et sacrificio Eius se materno animo sociavit, victimae de se genitae immolationi amanter consentiens; ac demum ab eodem Christo Iesu in cruce moriente uti mater discipulo, hisce verbis data est: Mulier, ecce filius tuus (cf. Jn 19,26-27).
Since it pleased God not to manifest the sacrament of human salvation solemnly before He would pour out the Spirit promised by Christ, we see the Apostles before the day of Pentecost persevering unanimously in prayer with the women, and Mary the Mother of Jesus, and His brethren (Acts 1:14), Mary also imploring the gift of the Spirit by her prayers, who at the Annunciation had already overshadowed her. Finally, the Immaculate Virgin, preserved immune from all stain of original sin, the course of her earthly life having been completed, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory, and exalted by the Lord as Queen of the universe, that she might be more fully conformed to her Son, the Lord of lords (cf. Rev 19:16) and conqueror of sin and death.
¶196
Cum vero Deo placuerit humanae salutis sacramentum non ante solemniter manifestare quam promissum a Christo Spiritum effunderet, Apostolos videmus ante diem Pentecostes perseverantes unanimiter in oratione cum mulieribus, et Maria Matre Iesu et fratribus Eius (Ac 1,14), Mariam quoque precibus suis implorantem donum Spiritus, qui in Annuntiatione ipsam iam obumbraverat. Denique Immaculata Virgo, ab omni originalis culpae labe praeservata immunis, expleto terrestris vitae cursu, corpore et anima ad caelestem gloriam assumpta est, ac tamquam universorum Regina a Domino exaltata, ut plenius conformaretur Filio suo, Domino dominantium (cf. Ap 19,16) ac peccati mortisque victori.
There is one Mediator, according to the words of the Apostle: "For there is one God, and one Mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself a redemption for all" (1 Tim 2:5-6). But the maternal office of Mary toward men in no way obscures or diminishes this unique mediation of Christ, but rather shows its power. For all the salvific influence of the Blessed Virgin on men does not come from any necessity of the matter, but from the divine good pleasure, and flows from the superabundance of the merits of Christ, rests on His mediation, depends entirely on it, and draws all its power from it; and it in no way impedes the immediate union of believers with Christ but rather fosters it.

III. DE B. VIRGINE ET ECCLESIA
III. ON THE BLESSED VIRGIN AND THE CHURCH
Unicus est Mediator noster secundum verba Apostoli: Unus enim Deus, unus et Mediator Dei et hominum, homo Christus Iesus, qui dedit redemptionem semetipsum pro omnibus (1Tm 2,5-6). Mariae autem maternum munus erga homines hanc Christi unicam mediationem nullo modo obscurat nec minuit, sed virtutem eius ostendit. Omnis enim salutaris Beatae Virginis influxus in homines non ex aliqua rei necessitate, sed ex beneplacito divino exoritur et ex superabundantia meritorum Christi profluit, Eius mediationi innititur, ab illa omnino dependet, ex eademque totam virtutem haurit; unionem autem immediatam credentium cum Christo nullo modo impedit sed fovet.
The Blessed Virgin, predestined from eternity together with the incarnation of the divine Word as the Mother of God, by the design of divine Providence existed on earth as the loving Mother of the divine Redeemer, singularly generous associate, and humble handmaid of the Lord. Conceiving, bearing, nourishing Christ, presenting Him to the Father in the temple, suffering with her Son dying on the cross, she cooperated in a wholly singular way in the work of the Savior, by obedience, faith, hope, and burning charity, to restore supernatural life to souls. On which account she exists as a mother to us in the order of grace.
¶198
Beata Virgo, ab aeterno una cum divini Verbi incarnatione tamquam Mater Dei praedestinata, divinae Providentiae consilio, his in terris exstitit alma divini Redemptoris Mater, singulariter prae aliis generosa socia, et humilis ancilla Domini. Christum concipiens, generans, alens, in templo Patri sistens, Filioque suo in cruce morienti compatiens, operi Salvatoris singulari prorsus modo cooperata est, oboedientia, fide, spe et flagrante caritate, ad vitam animarum supernaturalem restaurandam. Quam ob causam mater nobis in ordine gratiae exstitit.
This motherhood of Mary in the economy of grace continues unceasingly, from the consent which she faithfully gave at the Annunciation and which she unhesitatingly sustained under the cross, until the perpetual consummation of all the elect. For assumed into heaven, she did not lay aside this saving office, but by her manifold intercession continues to obtain for us the gifts of eternal salvation. By her motherly charity she cares for the brothers of her Son still pilgrimaging and placed in perils and difficulties, until they be led into the blessed homeland. Therefore the Blessed Virgin is invoked in the Church under the titles of Advocate, Helper, Assister, Mediatrix. Which, however, is so understood that it detracts nothing from the dignity and efficacy of the one Mediator Christ, nor adds anything to it.
¶199
Haec autem in gratiae oeconomia maternitas Mariae indesinenter perdurat, inde a consensu quem in Annuntiatione fideliter praebuit, quamque sub cruce incunctanter sustinuit, usque ad perpetuam omnium electorum consummationem. In caelis enim assumpta salutiferum hoc munus non deposuit, sed multiplici intercessione sua pergit in aeternae salutis donis nobis conciliandis. Materna sua caritate de fratribus Filii sui adhuc peregrinantibus necnon in periculis et angustiis versantibus curat, donec ad felicem patriam perducantur. Propterea B. Virgo in Ecclesia, titulis Advocatae, Auxiliatricis, Adiutricis, Mediatricis invocatur. Quod tamen ita intelligitur, ut dignitati et efficacitati Christi unius Mediatoris nihil deroget, nihil superaddat.
For no creature can ever be counted with the incarnate Word and Redeemer; but just as the priesthood of Christ is participated in various ways both by ministers and by the faithful people, and as the one goodness of God is really diffused in diverse ways in creatures, so also the unique mediation of the Redeemer does not exclude, but arouses a varied participated cooperation from creatures from the one source. Such a subordinate office of Mary the Church does not doubt to profess, continually experiences it, and commends it to the heart of the faithful, that, supported by this motherly aid, they may adhere more intimately to the Mediator and Savior.
¶200
Nulla enim creatura cum Verbo incarnato ac Redemptore connumerari umquam potest; sed sicut sacerdotium Christi variis modis tum a ministris tum a fideli populo participatur, et sicut una bonitas Dei in creaturis modis diversis realiter diffunditur, ita etiam unica mediatio Redemptoris non excludit, sed suscitat variam apud creaturas participatam ex unico fonte cooperationem. Tale autem munus subordinatum Mariae Ecclesia profiteri non dubitat, iugiter experitur et fidelium cordi commendat, ut hoc materno fulti praesidio Mediatori ac Salvatori intimius adhaereant.
The Blessed Virgin, however, by the gift and office of divine motherhood, by which she is united with the Son the Redeemer, and by her singular graces and gifts, is also intimately joined with the Church: the Mother of God is the type of the Church, as St. Ambrose already taught, in the order of faith, charity, and perfect union with Christ. For in the mystery of the Church, which is also justly called mother and virgin, the Blessed Virgin Mary preceded, eminently and singularly presenting the exemplar of both virgin and mother. For, believing and obeying, she brought forth the very Son of the Father on earth, not knowing man, overshadowed by the Holy Spirit, as the new Eve, giving faith not to the ancient serpent but to the messenger of God, no doubt adulterated. She brought forth the Son, whom God placed as the firstborn among many brothers (Rom 8:29), namely the faithful, to whose generation and education she cooperates with motherly love.
¶201
Beata autem Virgo divinae maternitatis dono et munere, quo cum Filio Redemptore unitur, suisque singularibus gratiis et muneribus, etiam cum Ecclesia intime coniungitur: Deipara est Ecclesiae typus, ut iam docebat S.Ambrosius, in ordine scilicet fidei, caritatis et perfectae cum Christo unionis. In mysterio enim Ecclesiae, quae et ipsa iure mater vocatur et virgo, Beata Virgo Maria praecessit, eminenter et singulariter tum virginis tum matris exemplar praebens. Credens enim et oboediens, ipsum Filium Patris in terris genuit, et quidem viri nescia, Spiritu Sancto obumbrata, tamquam nova Heva, non serpenti antiquo, sed Dei nuntio praestans fidem, nullo dubio adulteratam. Filium autem peperit, quem Deus posuit primogenitum in multis fratribus (Rm 8,29), fidelibus nempe, ad quos gignendos et educandos materno amore cooperatur.
Now the Church, contemplating her hidden holiness and imitating her charity, and faithfully fulfilling the will of the Father, by faithfully receiving the word of God also becomes a mother: for by preaching and baptism she brings forth sons, conceived of the Holy Spirit and born of God, to a new and immortal life. And she herself is a virgin, who keeps whole and pure the faith given to the Spouse, and imitating the Mother of her Lord, by the power of the Holy Spirit, keeps intact the whole faith, firm hope, sincere charity.
¶202
Iamvero Ecclesia, eius arcanam sanctitatem contemplans et caritatem imitans, voluntatemque Patris fideliter adimplens, per verbum Dei fideliter susceptum et ipsa fit mater: praedicatione enim ac baptismate filios, de Spiritu Sancto conceptos et ex Deo natos, ad vitam novam et immortalem generat. Et ipsa est virgo, quae fidem Sponso datam integre et pure custodit, et imitans Domini sui Matrem, virtute Spiritus Sancti, virginaliter servat integram fidem, solidam spem, sinceram caritatem.
While the Church already attains to perfection in the Blessed Virgin without blemish and wrinkle (cf. Eph 5:27), the faithful still strive, overcoming sin, to grow in holiness; therefore they lift up their eyes to Mary, who shines as an exemplar of virtues for the whole community of the elect. The Church, piously reflecting on her and contemplating her in the light of the Word made man, enters more deeply into the sublime mystery of the incarnation, and more and more conforms herself to her Spouse. For Mary, who intimately entered into the history of salvation, in herself in a certain way unites and echoes the greatest articles of faith, while she is preached and honored, calls believers to her Son and His sacrifice and to the love of the Father. The Church, pursuing the glory of Christ, becomes more like her sublime Type, continually progressing in faith, hope, and charity, and seeking and obeying the divine will in all things. Hence also in her apostolic work the Church rightly looks to her, who gave birth to Christ, thus conceived of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin, that through the Church He may be born and grow in the hearts of the faithful also. The Virgin in her life was an example of that motherly affection, by which all cooperating in the apostolic mission of the Church for the regeneration of men ought to be animated.
¶203
Dum autem Ecclesia in Beatissima Virgine ad perfectionem iam pertingit, qua sine macula et ruga existit (cf. Ep 5,27), christifideles adhuc nituntur, ut devincentes peccatum in sanctitate crescant; ideoque oculos suos ad Mariam attollunt, quae toti electorum communitati tamquam exemplar virtutum praefulget. Ecclesia de Ea pie recogitans Eamque in lumine Verbi hominis facti contemplans, in summum incarnationis mysterium venerabunda penitius intrat, Sponsoque suo magis magisque conformatur. Maria enim, quae, in historiam salutis intime ingressa, maxima fidei placita in se quodammodo unit et reverberat, dum praedicatur et colitur, ad Filium suum Eiusque sacrificium atque ad amorem Patris credentes advocat. Ecclesia vero, gloriam Christi prosequens, praecelso suo Typo similior efficitur, continuo progrediens in fide, spe et caritate, ac divinam voluntatem in omnibus quaerens et obsequens. Unde etiam in opere suo apostolico Ecclesia ad Eam merito respicit, quae genuit Christum, ideo de Spiritu Sancto conceptum et de Virgine natum, ut per Ecclesiam in cordibus quoque fidelium nascatur et crescat. Quae Virgo in sua vita exemplum exstitit materni illius affectus, quo cuncti in missione apostolica Ecclesiae cooperantes ad regenerandos homines animentur oportet.
Mary, exalted by the grace of God after the Son above all angels and men, as the most holy Mother of God, who was present at the mysteries of Christ, is justly honored by the Church with a special cult. And indeed from the most ancient times the Blessed Virgin is venerated under the title of Mother of God, under whose protection the faithful fleeing in all perils and necessities pray. From that time, especially from the Council of Ephesus, the cult of the people of God toward Mary has wonderfully grown in veneration and love, in invocation and imitation, according to her own prophetic words: "Behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed, for He that is mighty has done great things for me" (Lk 1:48). This cult, as it has always existed in the Church, is altogether singular, yet it differs essentially from the cult of adoration, which is rendered to the incarnate Word equally to the Father and the Holy Spirit, and most greatly favors it. For the various forms of piety toward the Mother of God, which the Church has approved within the limits of sound and orthodox doctrine, according to the conditions of times and places and the character and genius of the faithful, bring it about that while the Mother is honored, the Son, through whom all things (cf. Col 1:15-16) and in whom it pleased the eternal Father that all fullness should dwell (Col 1:19), is duly known, loved, and glorified, and His commands are kept.

IV. DE CULTU B. VIRGINIS IN ECCLESIA
IV. ON THE CULT OF THE BLESSED VIRGIN IN THE CHURCH
Maria, per gratiam Dei post Filium prae omnibus angelis et hominibus exaltata, utpote sanctissima Dei Mater, quae mysteriis Christi interfuit, speciali cultu ab Ecclesia merito honoratur. Et sane ab antiquissimis temporibus Beata Virgo sub titulo Deiparae colitur, sub cuius praesidium fideles in cunctis periculis et necessitatibus suis deprecantes confugiunt. Inde praesertim ab Ephesina Synodo cultus populi Dei erga Mariam mirabiliter crevit in veneratione et dilectione, in invocatione et imitatione, secundum ipsius verba prophetica: Beatam me dicent omnes generationes, quia fecit mihi magna qui potens est (Lc 1,48). Qui cultus, prout in Ecclesia semper exstitit, singularis omnino quamquam est, essentialiter differt a cultu adorationis, qui Verbo incarnato aeque ac Patri et Spiritui Sancto exhibetur, eidemque potissimum favet. Variae enim formae pietatis erga Dei Genitricem, quas Ecclesia intra limites sanae et orthodoxae doctrinae, pro temporum et locorum conditionibus et pro indole ingenioque fidelium approbavit, id efficiunt ut, dum Mater honoratur, Filius, propter quem omnia (cf. Col 1,15-16) et in quo aeterno Patri complacuit omnem plenitudinem inhabitare (Col 1,19), rite noscatur, ametur, glorificetur, Eiusque mandata serventur.
This sacred Synod knowingly teaches this catholic doctrine, and at the same time admonishes all the sons of the Church that they generously foster the cult, especially the liturgical, toward the Blessed Virgin, hold in great esteem the practices and exercises of piety toward Her commended by the Magisterium in the course of the centuries, and religiously keep those things which have been decreed in past times concerning the cult of images of Christ, the Blessed Virgin, and the saints. Moreover, it earnestly exhorts theologians and preachers of the divine word that they sedulously abstain equally from all false exaggeration as from too narrow a mind in considering the singular dignity of the Mother of God. Cultivating the study of sacred Scripture, the holy Fathers and Doctors and the liturgies of the Church under the guidance of the Magisterium, let them rightly illustrate the offices and privileges of the Blessed Virgin, which always look to Christ, the source of all truth, holiness, and piety. Let them sedulously avoid whatever could lead separated brothers or any others into error concerning the true doctrine of the Church. Let the faithful, moreover, be mindful that true devotion does not consist in sterile and transitory affection, nor in a certain vain credulity, but proceeds from true faith, by which we are led to acknowledge the excellence of the Mother of God, and are excited to filial love toward our mother and to the imitation of her virtues.
¶205
Hanc catholicam doctrinam Sacrosancta Synodus consulto docet, simulque omnes Ecclesiae filios admonet, ut cultum, praesertim liturgicum, erga Beatam Virginem generose foveant, praxes autem et exercitia pietatis erga Eam saeculorum cursu a Magisterio commendata magni faciant et ea quae anteactis temporibus de cultu imaginum Christi, Beatae Virginis et Sanctorum decreta fuere, religiose servent. Theologos autem verbique divini praecones enixe exhortatur, ut aeque ab omni falsa superlatione, quemadmodum et a nimia mentis angustia, in singulari Deiparae dignitate consideranda sedulo abstineant. Studium Sacrae Scripturae, Sanctorum Patrum et Doctorum Ecclesiaeque liturgiarum sub ductu Magisterii excolentes, recte illustrent munera et privilegia Beatae Virginis, quae semper Christum spectant, totius veritatis, sanctitatis et pietatis originem. Sedulo arceant quaecumque sive in dictis sive in factis fratres seiunctos vel alios quoscumque in errorem circa veram Ecclesiae doctrinam inducere possent. Meminerint porro fideles veram devotionem neque in sterili et transitorio affectu, neque in vana quadam credulitate consistere, sed a vera fide procedere, qua ad Dei Genetricis excellentiam agnoscendam adducimur, et ad filialem erga Matrem nostram amorem eiusque virtutum imitationem excitamur.
In the meantime, the Mother of Jesus, just as she is already glorified in heaven in body and soul, the image and beginning of the Church to be consummated in the future age, so on earth, until the day of the Lord shall come (cf. 2 Pet 3:10), she shines as a sign of sure hope and solace for the pilgrim People of God.
¶206
V. MARIA, SIGNUM CERTAE SPEI ET SOLATII PEREGRINANTI POPULO DEI
V. MARY, A SIGN OF SURE HOPE AND SOLACE FOR THE PILGRIM PEOPLE OF GOD
Interim autem Mater Iesu, quemadmodum in caelis corpore et anima iam glorificata, imago et initium est Ecclesiae in futuro saeculo consummandae, ita his in terris quoadusque advenerit dies Domini (cf. 2P 3,10), tamquam signum certae spei et solatii peregrinanti Populo Dei praelucet.
It brings great joy and solace to this sacred Synod that also among separated brothers there are not wanting those who bring due honor to the Mother of the Lord and Savior, especially among the Easterns, who with fervent impulse and devoted mind run to the cult of the Mother of God ever Virgin. Let all Christ's faithful pour forth urgent supplications to the Mother of God and Mother of men, that She, who assisted by her prayers the first-fruits of the Church, now also exalted in heaven above all the blessed and angels, in the Communion of all Saints may intercede with her Son, until all families of peoples, whether they are adorned with the Christian name or yet know not their Savior, may be happily gathered with peace and concord into one People of God, to the glory of the most holy and undivided Trinity.
¶207
Sacrosanctae huic Synodo magnum affert gaudium et solatium, etiam inter fratres seiunctos non deesse, qui Matri Domini ac Salvatoris debitum afferunt honorem, speciatim apud Orientales, qui ad cultum Deiparae semper Virginis fervido impulsu ac devoto animo concurrunt. Universi christifideles supplicationes instantes ad Matrem Dei et Matrem hominum effundant, ut Ipsa, quae primitiis Ecclesiae precibus suis adstitit, nunc quoque in caelo super omnes beatos et angelos exaltata, in omnium Sanctorum Communione apud Filium suum intercedat, donec cunctae familiae populorum, sive quae christiano nomine decorantur, sive quae Salvatorem suum adhuc ignorant, cum pace et concordia in unum Populum Dei feliciter congregentur, ad gloriam Sanctissimae et individuae Trinitatis.
SACROSANCTUM CONCILIUM, since it proposes to increase the Christian life among the faithful day by day, to better accommodate those institutions which are subject to change to the needs of our age, to foster whatever can contribute to the union of all who believe in Christ, and to strengthen whatever can help to call all into the bosom of the Church, judges it to pertain to itself to take care with special solicitude to also restore and foster the Liturgy.
¶208
SACROSANCTUM CONCILIUM
CONSTITUTIO DE SACRA LITURGIA
CONSTITUTION ON THE SACRED LITURGY
SACROSANCTUM CONCILIUM, cum sibi proponat vitam christianam inter fideles in dies augere; eas institutiones quae mutationibus obnoxiae sunt, ad nostrae aetatis necessitates melius accommodare; quidquid ad unionem omnium in Christum credentium conferre potest, fovere; et quidquid ad omnes in sinum Ecclesiae vocandos conducit, roborare; suum esse arbitratur peculiari ratione etiam instaurandam atque fovendam Liturgiam curare.
For the Liturgy, through which, especially in the divine Sacrifice of the Eucharist, the work of our Redemption is exercised, most greatly contributes to the faithful living expressing and manifesting to others the mystery of Christ and the genuine nature of the true Church, whose proper character is to be human and at the same time divine, visible and endowed with invisible, fervent in action and given to contemplation, present in the world and yet pilgrim, and so in her that what is human is ordered to the divine and subordinated to it, what is visible to the invisible, action to contemplation, and what is present to the future city which we seek. Hence, since the Liturgy daily builds those who are within into a holy temple in the Lord, into a dwelling of God in the Spirit, unto the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ, it at the same time wonderfully strengthens their powers to preach Christ, and thus shows the Church to those who are outside as a sign lifted up among the nations, under which the scattered sons of God may be gathered into one, until there be one fold and one shepherd.
¶209
Liturgia enim, per quam, maxime in divino Eucharistiae Sacrificio, opus nostrae Redemptionis exercetur, summe eo confert ut fideles vivendo exprimant et aliis manifestent mysterium Christi et genuinam verae Ecclesiae naturam, cuius proprium est esse humanam simul ac divinam, visibilem invisibilibus praeditam, actione ferventem et contemplationi vacantem, in mundo praesentem et tamen peregrinam; et ita quidem ut in ea quod humanum est ordinetur ad divinum eique subordinetur, quod visibile ad invisibile, quod actionis ad contemplationem, et quod praesens ad futuram civitatem quam inquirimus. Unde cum Liturgia eos qui intus sunt cotidie aedificet in templum sanctum in Domino, in habitaculum Dei in Spiritu, usque ad mensuram aetatis plenitudinis Christi, miro modo simul vires eorum ad praedicandum Christum roborat, et sic Ecclesiam iis qui foris sunt ostendit ut signum levatum in nationes, sub quo filii Dei dispersi congregentur in unum quousque unum ovile fiat et unus pastor.
Therefore the sacred Synod, concerning the fostering and restoring of the Liturgy, judges that the following principles are to be called to mind and practical norms established. Among these principles and norms, some are held which can and ought to be applied both to the Roman Rite and to all other Rites, although the practical norms which follow are to be understood as pertaining only to the Roman Rite, unless it concerns those things which from the nature of the matter also affect other Rites.
¶210
Quare Sacrosanctum Concilium, de fovenda atque instauranda Liturgia quae sequuntur principia censet in mentem revocanda et practicas normas statuendas esse. Inter haec principia et normas nonnulla habentur quae tum ad Ritum romanum tum ad omnes alios Ritus applicari possunt ac debent, licet normae practicae quae sequuntur solum Ritum romanum spectare intellegendae sint, nisi agatur de iis quae ex ipsa rei natura alios quoque Ritus afficiant.
Finally, faithfully obeying tradition, the sacred Synod declares that Holy Mother Church holds all legitimately recognized Rites in equal right and honor, and wishes them to be preserved in the future and fostered in every way, and desires that, where necessary, they be carefully and wholly revised according to the mind of sound tradition and be endowed with new vigor according to present circumstances and needs.
¶211
Traditioni denique fideliter obsequens, Sacrosanctum Concilium declarat Sanctam Matrem Ecclesiam omnes Ritus legitime agnitos aequo iure atque honore habere, eosque in posterum servari et omnimode foveri velle, atque optat ut, ubi opus sit, caute ex integro ad mentem sanae traditionis recognoscantur et novo vigore, pro hodiernis adiunctis et necessitatibus, donentur.
God, who wills all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth (1 Tim 2:4), spoke of old in many and various ways to the fathers by the prophets (Heb 1:1), when the fullness of time came, sent His Son, the Word made flesh, anointed with the Holy Spirit, to preach the Gospel to the poor, to heal the contrite of heart, a physician bodily and spiritual, the Mediator of God and men. For His humanity, in the unity of the Person of the Word, was the instrument of our salvation. Therefore in Christ the perfect placation of our reconciliation proceeded, and the fullness of divine worship was given to us.
¶212
CAPUT 1. DE PRINCIPIIS GENERALIBUS AD SACRAM LITURGIAM INSTAURANDAM ATQUE FOVENDAM
CHAPTER I. ON GENERAL PRINCIPLES FOR RESTORING AND FOSTERING THE SACRED LITURGY
I - DE SACRAE LITURGIAE NATURA EIUSQUE MOMENTO IN VITA ECCLESIAE
I - ON THE NATURE OF THE SACRED LITURGY AND ITS IMPORTANCE IN THE LIFE OF THE CHURCH
Deus, qui omnes homines vult salvos fieri et ad agnitionem veritatis venire (l Tim. 2, 4), multifariam multisque modis olim loquens patribus in prophetis (He 1,1), ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Filium suum, Verbum carnem factum, Spiritu Sancto unctum, ad evangelizandum pauperibus, ad sanandos contritos corde, medicum carnalem et spiritualem, Mediatorem Dei et hominum.Ipsius namque humanitas, in unitate personae Verbi, fuit instrumentum nostrae salutis. Quare in Christo nostrae reconciliationis processit perfecta placatio, et divini cultus nobis est indita plenitudo.
This work of human redemption and perfect glorification of God, to which the divine marvels in the people of the Old Testament preluded, Christ the Lord accomplished especially through the paschal mystery of His blessed Passion, Resurrection from the dead, and glorious Ascension, by which, dying He destroyed our death, and rising He repaired life. For from the side of Christ sleeping on the cross arose the wonderful sacrament of the whole Church.
¶213
Hoc autem humanae Redemptionis et perfectae Dei glorificationis opus, cui divina magnalia in populo Veteris Testamenti praeluserant, adimplevit Christus Dominus, praecipue per suae beatae Passionis, ab inferis Resurrectionis et gloriosae Ascensionis paschale mysterium, quo mortem nostram moriendo destruxit, et vitam resurgendo reparavit. Nam de latere Christi in cruce dormientis ortum est totius Ecclesiae mirabile sacramentum.
Therefore, just as Christ was sent by the Father, so He also sent the Apostles, filled with the Holy Spirit, not only that, preaching the Gospel to every creature, they should announce that the Son of God by His death and resurrection had freed us from the power of Satan and death and transferred us into the kingdom of the Father, but also that they should exercise through sacrifice and sacraments, around which the whole liturgical life turns, the work of salvation which they announced. Thus through baptism men are inserted into the paschal mystery of Christ: comortified, consepulted, conresuscitated; they receive the spirit of adoption of sons, in which they cry: Abba, Father (Rom 8:15), and thus become true adorers, whom the Father seeks. Likewise, as often as they eat the Lord's supper, they announce the death of the Lord until He comes.
¶214
Ideoque,sicut Christus missus est a Patre, ita et ipse Apostolos, repletos Spiritu Sancto, misit, non solum ut, praedicantes Evangelium omni creaturae, annuntiarent Filium Dei morte sua et resurrectione nos a potestate satanae et a morte liberasse et in regnum Patris transtulisse, sed etiam ut, quod annuntiabant, opus salutis per Sacrificium et Sacramenta, circa quae tota vita liturgica vertit, exercerent. Sic per Baptismum homines paschali Christi mysterio inseruntur: commortui, consepulti, conresuscitati; spiritum accipiunt adoptionis filiorum, in quo clamamus: Abba, Pater (Rm 8,15), et ita fiunt veri adoratores, quos Pater quaerit. Similiter quotiescumque dominicam cenam manducant, mortem Domini annuntiant donec veniat.
Therefore, on the day of Pentecost itself, when the Church appeared to the world, those who received the word of Peter were baptized. "And they were persevering in the doctrine of the Apostles and in the communication of the breaking of bread and in prayers... praising God and having favor with all the people" (Acts 2:41-42, 47). From then on the Church never ceased to come together to celebrate the paschal mystery: reading those things which were concerning Him in all the Scriptures (Lk 24:27), celebrating the Eucharist in which the victory and triumph of His death are represented, and at the same time giving thanks to God for His unspeakable gift (2 Cor 9:15) in Christ Jesus, to the praise of His glory (Eph 1:12), through the power of the Holy Spirit.
¶215
Idcirco, ipso die Pentecostes, qua Ecclesia mundo apparuit, qui receperunt sermonem Petri baptizati sunt. Et erant perseverantes in doctrina Apostolorum et communicatione fractionis panis et orationibus... collaudantes Deum et habentes gratiam ad omnem plebem (Ac 2,41-42,47). Numquam exinde omisit Ecclesia quin in unum conveniret ad paschale mysterium celebrandum: legendo ea in omnibus Scripturis quae de ipso erant (Lc 24,27), Eucharistiam celebrando in qua mortis eius victoria et triumphus repraesentantur,19 et simul gratias agendo Deo super inenarrabili dono (2Co 9,15) in Christo Iesu, in laudem gloriae eius (Ep 1,12), per virtutem Spiritus Sancti.
To accomplish so great a work, Christ is always present to His Church, especially in liturgical actions. He is present in the Sacrifice of the Mass, both in the person of the minister, the same now offering through the ministry of priests who then offered Himself on the cross, and especially under the eucharistic species. He is present by His power in the sacraments, so that when anyone baptizes, Christ Himself baptizes. He is present in His word, since He Himself speaks when the sacred Scriptures are read in the Church. He is present finally when the Church prays and sings psalms, He who promised: "Where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them" (Mt 18:20).
¶216
Ad tantum vero opus perficiendum, Christus Ecclesiae suae semper adest, praesertim in actionibus liturgicis. Praesens adest in Missae Sacrificio cum in ministri persona, idem nunc offerens sacerdotum ministerio, qui seipsum tunc in cruce obtulit, tum maxime sub speciebus eucharisticis. Praesens adest virtute sua in Sacramentis, ita ut cum aliquis baptizat, Christus ipse baptizet. Praesens adest in verbo suo, siquidem ipse loquitur dum sacrae Scripturae in Ecclesia leguntur. Praesens adest denique dum supplicat et psallit Ecclesia, ipse qui promisit: Ubi sunt duo vel tres congregati in nomine meo, ibi sum in medio eorum (Mt 18,20).
Indeed, in so great a work, in which God is perfectly glorified and men are sanctified, Christ always associates with Himself His most beloved Spouse, the Church, who calls upon her Lord and through Him renders worship to the eternal Father. Therefore the Liturgy is justly held as the exercise of the priestly office of Jesus Christ, in which through sensible signs the sanctification of man is signified and brought about in a way proper to each, and the whole public worship is exercised by the mystical body of Jesus Christ, namely by the Head and its members. Hence every liturgical celebration, as the work of Christ the Priest and of His body, which is the Church, is a sacred action pre-eminently, whose efficacy no other action of the Church equals by the same title and in the same degree.
¶217
Reapse tanto in opere, quo Deus perfecte glorificatur et homines sanctificantur, Christus Ecclesiam, sponsam suam dilectissimam, sibi semper consociat, quae Dominum suum invocat et per ipsum Aeterno Patri cultum tribuit. Merito igitur Liturgia habetur veluti Iesu Christi sacerdotalis muneris exercitatio, in qua per signa sensibilia significatur et modo singulis proprio efficitur sanctificatio hominis, et a mystico Iesu Christi Corpore, Capite nempe eiusque membris, integer cultus publicus exercetur. Proinde omnis liturgica celebratio, utpote opus Christi sacerdotis eiusque Corporis, quod est Ecclesia, est actio sacra praecellenter, cuius efficacitatem eodem titulo eodemque gradu nulla alia actio Ecclesiae adaequat.
In the earthly liturgy we participate by foretaste in that heavenly liturgy which is celebrated in the holy city of Jerusalem, toward which we pilgrim as strangers, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God, a minister of the holy and the true tabernacle; we sing the hymn of glory to the Lord with all the host of the heavenly army; venerating the memory of the saints, we hope for some part and fellowship with them; we await the Savior, our Lord Jesus Christ, until He himself shall appear our life, and we shall appear with him in glory.
¶218
In terrena Liturgia caelestem illam praegustando participamus, quae in sancta civitate Ierusalem, ad quam peregrini tendimus, celebratur, ubi Christus est in dextera Dei sedens, sanctorum minister et tabernaculi veri; cum omni militia caelestis exercitus hymnum gloriae Domino canimus; memoriam Sanctorum venerantes partem aliquam et societatem cum iis speramus; Salvatorem expectamus Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, donec ipse apparebit vita nostra, et nos apparebimus cum ipso in gloria.
The sacred Liturgy does not exhaust the whole action of the Church; for before men can come to the Liturgy, they must be called to faith and conversion: "How shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? Or how shall they believe him whom they have not heard? And how shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach unless they be sent?" (Rom 10:14-15). Therefore the Church announces the herald of salvation to unbelievers, that all men may know the only true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent, and may be converted from their ways, doing penance. To believers, however, she must always preach faith and penance, she must also prepare them for the sacraments, teach them to observe all things whatsoever Christ commanded, and allure them to all works of charity, piety, and apostolate, by which works it is made manifest that Christ's faithful are not indeed of this world, but yet are the light of the world and glorify the Father before men.
¶219
II - DE LITURGICA INSTITUTIONE ET DE ACTUOSA PARTICIPATIONE PROSEQUENDIS
II - ON PROMOTING LITURGICAL INSTRUCTION AND ACTIVE PARTICIPATION
Sacra Liturgia non explet totam actionem Ecclesiae; nam antequam homines ad Liturgiam accedere possint, necesse est ut ad fidem et conversionem vocentur: Quomodo invocabunt in quem non crediderunt? Aut quomodo credent ei quem non audierunt? Quomodo autem audient sine praedicante? Quomodo vero praedicabunt nisi mittantur? (Rm 10,14-15). Quare Ecclesia non credentibus praeconium salutis annuntiat, ut omnes homines solum Deum verum et quem misit Iesum Christum cognoscant et a viis suis convertantur, paenitentiam agentes. Credentibus vero semper fidem et paenitentiam praedicare debet, eos praeterea debet ad Sacramenta disponere, docere servare omnia quaecumque mandavit Christus, et allicere ad omnia opera caritatis, pietatis et apostolatus, quibus operibus manifestum fiat christifideles de hoc mundo quidem non esse, sed tamen esse lucem mundi eosdemque Patrem glorificare coram hominibus.
Yet the Liturgy is the summit toward which the action of the Church tends and at the same time the source from which all her virtue flows. For apostolic labors are ordered to this, that all, made sons of God by faith and baptism, may come together, praise God in the midst of the Church, participate in the Sacrifice, and eat the Lord's supper. In turn, the Liturgy itself impels the faithful, filled with the paschal sacraments, to become one in piety; it prays that they may hold by living what they have perceived by faith; the renewal of the Lord's covenant with men in the Eucharist draws and kindles the faithful into urgent charity of Christ. From the Liturgy, therefore, especially from the Eucharist, as from a fountain, grace is poured forth upon us, and the sanctification of men and the glorification of God in Christ, toward which, as toward an end, all other works of the Church strive, is obtained with the greatest efficacy.
¶220
Attamen Liturgia est culmen ad quod actio Ecclesiae tendit et simul fons unde omnis eius virtus emanat. Nam labores apostolici ad id ordinantur ut omnes, per fidem et Baptismum filii Dei facti, in unum conveniant, in medio Ecclesiae Deum laudent, Sacrificium participent et cenam dominicam manducent. Vicissim, ipsa Liturgia impellit fideles ut sacramentis paschalibus satiati fiant pietate concordes; orat ut vivendo teneant quod fide perceperunt; renovatio vero foederis Domini cum hominibus in Eucharistia fideles in urgentem caritatem Christi trahit et accendit. Ex Liturgia ergo, praecipue ex Eucharistia, ut e fonte, gratia in nos derivatur et maxima cum efficacia obtinetur illa in Christo hominum sanctificatio et Dei glorificatio, ad quam, uti ad finem, omnia alia Ecclesiae opera contendunt.
That this full efficacy may be had, however, it is necessary that the faithful come to the sacred Liturgy with right dispositions of mind, accommodate their mind to the voice, and cooperate with heavenly grace, lest they receive it in vain. Therefore sacred pastors should watch that in the liturgical action not only the laws for valid and lawful celebration are observed, but that the faithful participate knowingly, actively, and fruitfully.
¶221
Ut haec tamen plena efficacitas habeatur, nesessarium est ut fideles cum recti animi dispositionibus ad sacram Liturgiam accedant, mentem suam voci accommodent, et supernae gratiae cooperentur, ne eam in vacuum recipiant. Ideo sacris pastoribus advigilandum est ut in actione liturgica non solum observentur leges ad validam et licitam celebrationem, sed ut fideles scienter, actuose et fructuose eandem participent.
Yet the spiritual life is not contained solely in participation in the sacred Liturgy. The Christian, called to pray in common, must nevertheless also enter into his chamber to pray to the Father in secret, indeed, the Apostle teaching, pray without ceasing. And we are taught by the same Apostle always to bear about the mortification of Jesus in our body, that the life also of Jesus may be made manifest in our mortal flesh. Therefore we pray to the Lord in the Sacrifice of the Mass that, the offering of the spiritual victim having been received, He may perfect us unto an eternal gift.
¶222
Vita tamen spiritualis non unius sacrae Liturgiae participatione continetur. Christianus enim ad communiter orandum vocatus, nihilominus debet etiam intrare in cubiculum suum ut Patrem in abscondito oret, immo, docente Apostolo, sine intermissione orare. Et ab eodem Apostolo docemur mortificationem Iesu semper circumferre in corpore nostro, ut et vita Iesu manifestetur in carne nostra mortali. Quapropter Dominum in Missae Sacrificio precamur ut, hostiae spiritualis oblatione suscepta, nosmetipsos sibi perficiat munus aeternum.
The pious practices of the Christian people, as long as they are conformable to the laws and norms of the Church, are strongly commended, especially when they are done by mandate of the Apostolic See. Also, the sacred practices of particular churches, which are celebrated by mandate of the bishops according to customs or books legitimately approved, enjoy special dignity. Yet in such a way, regard being had to the liturgical seasons, the same practices must be ordered that they be congruous to the sacred Liturgy, in some way derive from it, and lead the people to it, since it by its nature far excels them.
¶223
Pia populi christiani exercitia, dummodo legibus et normis Ecclesiae conformia sint, valde commendantur, praesertim cum de mandato Apostolicae Sedis fiunt. Speciali quoque dignitate gaudent sacra Ecclesiarum particularium exercitia, quae de mandato Episcoporum celebrantur, secundum consuetudines aut libros legitime approbatos. Ita vero, ratione habita temporum liturgicorum, eadem exercitia ordinentur oportet, ut sacrae Liturgiae congruant, ab ea quodammodo deriventur, ad eam populum manuducant, utpote quae natura sua iisdem longe antecellat.
Mother Church greatly desires that all the faithful be led to that full, conscious, and active participation in liturgical celebrations which is demanded by the nature of the Liturgy itself, and to which the Christian people, a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition (1 Pet 2:9; cf. 1 Pet 2:4-5), have a right and are bound by virtue of baptism. This full and active participation of the whole people, in restoring and fostering the sacred Liturgy, is to be most highly attended to: for it is the primary and indispensable source from which the faithful are to draw the truly Christian spirit; and therefore in the whole pastoral action, through due instruction, it is to be earnestly sought by the pastors of souls.
¶224
Valde cupit Mater Ecclesia ut fideles universi ad plenam illam, consciam atque actuosam liturgicarum celebrationum participationem ducantur, quae ab ipsius Liturgiae natura postulatur et ad quam populus christianus, genus electum, regale sacerdotium, gens sancta, populus adquisitionis (1P 2,9 cf. 1P 2,4-5), vi Baptismatis ius habet et officium. Quae totius populi plena et actuosa participatio, in instauranda et fovenda sacra Liturgia, summopere est attendenda: est enim primus, isque necessarius fons, e quo spiritum vere christianum fideles hauriant; et ideo in tota actione pastorali, per debitam institutionem, ab animarum pastoribus est sedulo adpetenda.
But because no hope shines for this to happen unless first the pastors of souls themselves are deeply imbued with the spirit and power of the Liturgy and become masters in it, therefore it is most necessary that the liturgical formation of the clergy be especially provided for. Wherefore the sacred Synod has decided to decree the following.
¶225
Sed quia, ut hoc evenire possit, nulla spes effulget nisi prius ipsi animarum pastores spiritu et virtute Liturgiae penitus imbuantur in eaque efficiantur magistri, ideo pernecesse est ut institutioni liturgicae cleri apprime consulatur. Quapropter Sacrosanctum Concilium ea quae sequuntur statuere decrevit.
Masters who are placed over teaching the discipline of sacred Liturgy in seminaries, religious houses of study, and theological faculties, are to be properly instituted for their office in institutes specially destined for this purpose with special care. The discipline of sacred Liturgy is to be held among the necessary and principal disciplines in seminaries and religious houses of study, and in theological faculties among the principal disciplines, and is to be taught under the theological, historical, spiritual, pastoral, and juridical aspect. Let the masters of other disciplines, especially of dogmatic theology, sacred Scripture, spiritual and pastoral theology, also take care that, from the intrinsic requirements of their own object, they so cultivate the mystery of Christ and the history of salvation that from it the connection of these with the Liturgy and the unity of priestly formation may clearly appear.
¶226
Magistri, qui sacrae Liturgiae disciplinam in seminariis, studiorum domibus religiosis et facultatibus theologicis docendae praeficiuntur, ad munus suum in institutis ad hoc speciali cura destinatis probe instituendi sunt. Disciplina de sacra Liturgia in seminariis et studiorum domibus religiosis inter disciplinas necessarias et potiores, in facultatibus autem theologicis inter disciplinas principales est habenda, et sub aspectu cum theologico et historico, tum spirituali, pastorali et iuridico tradenda. Curent insuper aliarum disciplinarum magistri, imprimis theologiae dogmaticae, sacrae Scripturae, theologiae spiritualis et pastoralis ita, ex intrinsecis exigentiis proprii uniuscuiusque obiecti, mysterium Christi et historiam salutis excolere, ut exinde earum connexio cum Liturgia et unitas sacerdotalis institutionis aperte clarescant.
Clerics, in seminaries and religious houses, are to acquire a liturgical formation of spiritual life, both by apt guidance by which they may be able to understand sacred rites and participate in them with their whole spirit, and by the very celebration of the sacred mysteries, as well as by other exercises of piety imbued with the spirit of the sacred Liturgy; likewise let them learn the observance of liturgical laws, so that life in seminaries and religious institutes may be thoroughly informed with the liturgical spirit.
¶227
Clerici, in seminariis domibusque religiosis, formationem vitae spiritualis liturgicam acquirant, cum apta manuductione qua sacros ritus intellegere et toto animo participare queant, tum ipsa sacrorum mysteriorum celebratione, necnon aliis pietatis exercitiis spiritu sacrae Liturgiae imbutis; pariter observantiam legum liturgicarum addiscant, ita ut vita in seminariis et religiosorum institutis liturgico spiritu penitus informetur.
Priests, both secular and religious, already laboring in the Lord's vineyard, should be helped by all opportune means that they may ever more fully understand what they do in sacred functions, may live the liturgical life, and communicate it to the faithful entrusted to them. Pastors of souls, according to the age, condition, manner of life, and grade of religious culture of the faithful, should sedulously and patiently pursue the liturgical instruction and the active participation of the faithful, both internal and external, fulfilling one of the principal duties of a faithful dispenser of the mysteries of God; and in this matter they should lead their flock not only by word but also by example.
¶228
Sacerdotes, sive saeculares sive religiosi, in vinea Domini iam operantes, omnibus mediis opportunis iuventur ut plenius semper quae in functionibus sacris agunt intellegant, vitam liturgicam vivant, eamque cum fidelibus sibi commissis communicent. Liturgicam institutionem necnon actuosam fidelium participationem, internam et externam, iuxta ipsorum aetatem, condicionem, vitae genus et religiosae culturae gradum, animarum pastores sedulo ac patienter prosequantur, unum e praecipuis fidelis mysteriorum Dei dispensatoris muneribus absolventes; et gregem suum hac in re non verbo tantum, sed etiam exemplo ducant.
Transmissions of sacred actions by means of radio and television, especially if it concerns the celebration of the Mass, are to be done discretely and decorously, under the direction and sponsorship of a suitable person designated for this office by the bishops.
¶229
Transmissiones actionum sacrarum ope radiophonica et televisifica, praesertim si agatur de Sacro faciendo, discrete ac decore fiant, ductu et sponsione personae idoneae, ad hoc munus ab Episcopis destinatae.
Pious Mother Church, that the Christian people may more securely obtain the abundance of graces in the sacred Liturgy, desires to sedulously take care for the general restoration of the Liturgy itself. For the Liturgy consists of an unchangeable part, as divinely instituted, and parts subject to change, which can vary or even ought to vary in the course of time, if perchance things which have crept in which less well respond to the intimate nature of the Liturgy itself, or have become less apt. In this restoration, texts and rites must be so ordered that they more clearly express the holy things which they signify, and that the Christian people, as far as possible, be able easily to perceive them and to participate in them with a full, active, and community-like celebration. Therefore the sacred Synod decrees the following more general norms.
¶230
III - DE SACRAE LITURGIAE INSTAURATIONE
III - ON THE RESTORATION OF THE SACRED LITURGY
Pia Mater Ecclesia, ut populus christianus in sacra Liturgia abundantiam gratiarum securius assequatur, ipsius Liturgiae generalem instaurationem sedulo curare cupit. Nam Liturgia constat parte immutabili, utpote divinitus instituta, et partibus mutationi obnoxiis, quae decursu temporum variare possunt vel etiam debent, si in eas forte irrepserint quae minus bene ipsius Liturgiae intimae naturae respondeant, vel minus aptae factae sint. Qua quidem instauratione, textus et ritus ita ordinari oportet, ut sancta, quae significant, clarius exprimant, eaque populus christianus, in quantum fieri potest, facile percipere atque plena, actuosa et communitatis propria celebratione participare possit. Quare Sacrosanctum Concilium generaliores has normas statuit.
- The regulation of the sacred Liturgy depends solely on the authority of the Church: which is with the Apostolic See and, according to the norm of law, with the bishop. 2. By the power granted by law, the regulation of liturgical matter also pertains, within established limits, to various competent territorial episcopal conferences legitimately constituted. 3. Wherefore, no other person at all, even if he be a priest, may add, remove, or change anything in the Liturgy on his own authority.
¶231
A) GENERAL NORMS
A) NORMAE GENERALES
1. Sacrae Liturgiae moderatio ab Ecclesiae auctoritate unice pendet: quae quidem est apud Apostolicam Sedem et, ad normam iuris, apud Episcopum. 2. Ex potestate a iure concessa, rei liturgicae moderatio inter limites statutos pertinet quoque ad competentes varii generis territoriales Episcoporum coetus legitime constitutos. 3. Quapropter nemo omnino alius, etiamsi sit sacerdos, quidquam proprio marte in Liturgia addat, demat, aut mutet.
That sound tradition may be retained and yet the way to legitimate progress be opened, an accurate theological, historical, and pastoral investigation must always precede the revision of each part of the Liturgy. Moreover, the general laws of the structure and mind of the Liturgy, as well as the experience from recent liturgical restoration and from indults granted here and there, are to be considered. Finally, innovations are not to be made unless the true and certain utility of the Church demands it, and with caution that new forms organically grow in a way from forms already existing. Let it also be taken care, as far as possible, that notable differences of rites between neighboring regions not be had.
¶232
Ut sana traditio retineatur et tamen via legitimae progressioni aperiatur, de singulis Liturgiae partibus recognoscendis accurata investigatio theologica, historica, pastoralis semper praecedat. Insuper considerentur cum leges generales structurae et mentis Liturgiae, tum experientia ex recentiore instauratione liturgica et ex indultis passim concessis promanans. Innovationes, demum, ne fiant nisi vera et certa utilitas Ecclesiae id exigat, et adhibita cautela ut novae formae ex formis iam exstantibus organice quodammodo crescant. Caveatur etiam, in quantum fieri potest, ne notabiles differentiae rituum inter finitimas regiones habeantur.
The greatest importance of sacred Scripture in celebrating the Liturgy is to be held. For from it the readings are read and explained in the homily, the psalms are sung, and from its inspiration and impulse prayers, liturgical songs and hymns have been poured forth, and from it actions and signs receive their meaning. Hence, to procure the restoration, progress, and adaptation of the sacred Liturgy, it is necessary to promote that sweet and living affection for sacred Scripture which the venerable tradition of both Eastern and Western rites testifies.
¶233
Maximum est sacrae Scripturae momentum in Liturgia celebranda. Ex ea enim lectiones leguntur et in homilia explicantur, psalmi canuntur, atque ex eius afflatu instinctuque preces, oratione et carmina liturgica effusa sunt, et ex ea significationem suam actiones et signa accipiunt. Unde, ad procurandam sacrae Liturgiae instaurationem, progressum et aptationem, oportet ut promoveatur ille suavis et vivus sacrae Scripturae affectus, quem testatur venerabilis rituum cum orientalium tum occidentalium traditio.
The liturgical books are to be revised as soon as possible, experts being employed and bishops consulted from various regions of the world.
¶234
Libri liturgici quam primum recognoscantur, peritis adhibitis et Episcopis consultis ex diversis orbis regionibus.
Liturgical actions are not private actions, but celebrations of the Church, which is the sacrament of unity, namely the holy people gathered and ordered under the bishops. Therefore they pertain to the whole body of the Church, manifest and affect it, but they touch individual members of it in a different way, according to the diversity of orders, offices, and actual participation.
¶235
B) NORMAE EX INDOLE LITURGIAE UTPOTE ACTIONIS HIERARCHICHAE ET COMMUNITATIS PROPRIAE
B) NORMS FROM THE NATURE OF THE LITURGY AS AN ACTION PROPER TO THE HIERARCHY AND THE COMMUNITY
Actiones liturgicae non sunt actiones privatae, sed celebrationes Ecclesiae, quae est unitatis sacramentum, scilicet plebs sancta sub Episcopis adunata et ordinata. Quare ad universum Corpus Ecclesiae pertinent illudque manifestant et afficiunt; singula vero membra ipsius diverso modo, pro diversitate ordinum, munerum et actualis participationis, attingunt.
As often as rites, according to the proper nature of each, carry with them a common celebration with the attendance and active participation of the faithful, let it be inculcated that this is to be preferred as far as possible to the individual and as it were private celebration of the same. Which is of special value for the celebration of Mass, the public and social nature of every Mass always being saved, and for the administration of the sacraments. In liturgical celebrations each one, whether minister or faithful, fulfilling his own office, should do all and only that which belongs to him from the nature of the matter and liturgical norms.
¶236
Quoties ritus, iuxta propriam cuiusque naturam, secum ferunt celebrationem communem, cum frequentia et actuosa participatione fidelium, inculcetur hanc, in quantum fieri potest, esse praeferendam celebrationi eorundem singulari et quasi privatae. Quod valet praesertim pro Missae celebratione, salva semper natura publica et sociali cuiusvis Missae, et pro Sacramentorum administratione. In celebrationibus liturgicis quisque, sive minister sive fidelis, munere suo fungens, solum et totum id agat, quod ad ipsum ex rei natura et normis liturgicis pertinet.
Even the ministers, lectors, commentators, and those who belong to the choir, exercise a true liturgical ministry. Therefore let them fulfill their office with such sincere piety and order that it becomes so great a ministry and that the people of God justly require it of them. Hence it is necessary that they be sedulously imbued with the spirit of the Liturgy, each in his own way, and be instituted for their parts to be performed rightly and orderly.
¶237
Etiam ministrantes, lectores, commentatores et ii qui ad scholam cantorum pertinent, vero ministerio liturgico funguntur. Propterea munus suum tali sincera pietate et ordine exerceant, quae tantum ministerium decent quaeque populus Dei ab eis iure exigit. Ideo oportet eos spiritu Liturgiae, suo cuiusque modo, sedulo imbui, et ad partes suas rite et ordinate obeundas institui.
To promote active participation, the acclamations of the people, responses, psalmody, antiphons, hymns, as well as actions or gestures and bodily posture are to be fostered. Sacred silence also is to be observed at its proper time. In revising the liturgical books, it is to be carefully attended that the rubrics also provide for the parts of the faithful. In the Liturgy, besides the distinction arising from liturgical office and sacred order, and besides the honors due to civil authorities according to the norms of liturgical laws, no acceptance of private persons or conditions, whether in ceremonies or in exterior pomp, is to be had.
¶238
Ad actuosam participationem promovendam, populi acclamationes, responsiones, psalmodia, antiphonae, cantica, necnon actiones seu gestus et corporis habitus foveantur. Sacrum quoque silentium suo tempore servetur. In libris liturgicis recognoscendis, sedulo attendatur ut rubricae etiam partes fidelium praevideant. In Liturgia, praeter distinctionem ex munere liturgico et Ordine sacro manantem, et praeter honores ad normam legum liturgicarum auctoritatibus civilibus debitos, nulla privatarum personarum aut condicionum, sive in caerimoniis, sive in exterioribus pompis, habeatur acceptio.
Although the sacred Liturgy is chiefly the worship of the divine majesty, it also contains great instruction for the faithful people. For in the Liturgy God speaks to his people; Christ still announces the Gospel. The people, however, respond to God both by hymns and by prayers. Indeed, the prayers directed to God by the priest, who presides over the assembly in the person of Christ, are said in the name of the whole holy people and of all present. Finally, the visible signs which the sacred Liturgy uses for signifying invisible divine things, have been chosen by Christ or the Church. Hence not only when those things are read which were written for our doctrine (Rom 15:4), but also when the Church prays or sings or acts, the faith of the participants is nourished, minds are raised to God to render Him a rational service, and His grace is received more abundantly.
¶239
C) NORMAE EX INDOLE DIDACTICA ET PASTORALI LITURGIAE
C) NORMS FROM THE DIDACTIC AND PASTORAL NATURE OF THE LITURGY
Etsi sacra Liturgia est praecipue cultus divinae maiestatis, magnam etiam continet populi fidelis eruditionem. In Liturgia enim Deus ad populum suum loquitur; Christus adhuc Evangelium annuntiat. Populus vero Deo respondet tum cantibus tum oratione. Immo, preces a sacerdote, qui coetui in persona Christi praeest, ad Deum directae, nomine totius plebis sanctae et omnium circumstantium dicuntur. Signa tandem visibilia, quibus utitur sacra Liturgia ad res divinas invisibiles significandas, a Christo vel Ecclesia delecta sunt. Unde non solum quando leguntur ea quae ad nostram doctrinam scripta sunt (Rm 15,4), sed etiam dum Ecclesia vel orat vel canit vel agit, participantium fides alitur, mentes in Deum excitantur ut rationabile obsequium Ei praestent, gratiamque Eius abundantius recipiant.
Hence, in the restoration to be done, the following general norms must be observed.
¶240
Exinde in instauratione facienda generales normae quae sequuntur observari debent.
Rites are to shine with noble simplicity, be brief and perspicuous, avoid useless repetitions, be accommodated to the capacity of the faithful, and generally not need many explanations. That it may clearly appear in the Liturgy that rite and word are intimately joined: 1) In sacred celebrations, a more abundant, varied, and apt reading of sacred Scripture is to be restored. 2) A more suitable place for the sermon, as part of the liturgical action, is to be indicated in the rubrics as the rite permits, and the ministry of preaching is to be most faithfully and rightly fulfilled. This, however, is to be drawn especially from the source of sacred Scripture and the Liturgy, as an announcement of the wonderful works of God in the history of salvation, that is, the mystery of Christ, which is always present and active in us, especially in liturgical celebrations. 3) Also a more directly liturgical catechesis is to be inculcated in every way; and in the rites themselves, if there be need, brief admonitions by the priest or a suitable minister, at only the more opportune moments, are to be foreseen, to be said in prescribed or similar words. 4) The sacred celebration of the word of God on the vigils of more solemn feasts, on some ferias of Advent and Lent, and on Sundays and feast days, especially in places lacking a priest, is to be fostered: in which case, the celebration is to be directed by a deacon or another delegated by the bishop.
¶241
Ritus nobili simplicitate fulgeant, sint brevitate perspicui et repetitiones inutiles evitent, sint fidelium captui accommodati, neque generatim multis indigeant explanationibus. Ut clare appareat in Liturgia ritum et verbum intime coniungi: 1) In celebrationibus sacris abundantior, varior et aptior lectio sacrae Scripturae instauretur. 2) Locus aptior sermonis, utpote partis actionis liturgicae, prout ritus patitur, etiam in rubricis notetur; et fidelissime ac rite adimpleatur ministerium praedicationis. Haec vero imprimis ex fonte sacrae Scripturae et Liturgiae hauriatur, quasi annuntiatio mirabilium Dei in historia salutis seu mysterio Christi, quod in nobis praesens semper adest et operatur, praesertim in celebrationibus liturgicis. 3) Etiam catechesis directius liturgica omnibus modis inculcetur; et in ipsis ritibus, si necessariae sint, breves admonitiones, a sacerdote vel competenti ministro, opportunioribus tantum momentis, praescriptis vel similibus verbis, dicendae, praevideantur. 4) Foveatur sacra verbi Dei celebratio in solemniorum festorum pervigiliis, in aliquibus feriis Adventus et Quadragesimae, atque in dominicis et diebus festis, maxime in locis quae sacerdote carent: quo in casu celebrationem diaconus vel alius ab Episcopo delegatus dirigat.
- The use of the Latin language, saving particular law, is to be preserved in the Latin rites. 2. Yet since the use of the vernacular language can often be very useful for the people both in the Mass, in the administration of the sacraments, and in other parts of the Liturgy, a wider place can be given to it, especially in the readings and admonitions, in some prayers and hymns, according to the norms which are established individually in the following chapters concerning this matter. 3. These norms having been observed, it pertains to the competent territorial ecclesiastical authority mentioned in art. 22, 2, and also, if the case requires, with the counsel of the bishops of neighboring regions of the same language, to decide concerning the use and manner of the vernacular language, the acts being approved or confirmed by the Apostolic See. 4. The translation of the Latin text into the vernacular language to be used in the Liturgy must be approved by the competent territorial ecclesiastical authority mentioned above.
¶242
1. Linguae latinae usus, salvo particulari iure, in ritibus latinis servetur. 2. Cum tamen, sive in Missa, sive in Sacramentorum administratione, sive in aliis Liturgiae partibus, haud raro linguae vernaculae usurpatio valde utilis apud populum exsistere possit, amplior locus ipsi tribui valeat, imprimis autem in lectionibus et admonitionibus, in nonnullis orationibus et cantibus, iuxta normas quae de hac re in sequentibus capitibus singillatim statuuntur. 3. Huiusmodi normis servatis, est competentis auctoritatis ecclesiasticae territorialis, de qua in art. 22 2, etiam, si casus ferat, consilio habito cum Episcopis finitimarum regionum eiusdem linguae, de usu et modo linguae vernaculae statuere, actis ab Apostolica Sede probatis seu confirmatis. 4. Conversio textus latini in linguam vernaculam in Liturgia adhibenda, a competenti auctoritate ecclesiastica territoriali, de qua supra, approbari debet.
The Church does not wish to impose rigid uniformity in the Liturgy even in those things which do not touch faith or the good of the whole community; nay rather, she cultivates and advances the ornaments and gifts of the spirit of various nations and peoples; and whatever is found in the customs of peoples that is not indissolubly attached to superstitions and errors, she kindly considers and, if possible, keeps whole and intact, indeed sometimes she even admits it into the Liturgy itself, provided it be congruous with the reasons of true and authentic liturgical spirit. The legitimate varieties and adaptations to various groups, regions, and peoples, especially in the Missions, are to be given place, the substantial unity of the Roman Rite being preserved, even when the liturgical books are revised; and this is to be opportunely kept before the eyes in establishing the structure of the rites and the rubrics. Within the limits set in the typical editions of the liturgical books, it will pertain to the competent territorial ecclesiastical authority mentioned in art. 22, 2 to define adaptations, especially concerning the administration of the sacraments, concerning sacramentals, processions, liturgical language, sacred music, and the arts, according, however, to the fundamental norms which are contained in this Constitution. Yet when in various places and circumstances a deeper adaptation of the Liturgy urgently presses, and therefore becomes more difficult: 1) The competent territorial ecclesiastical authority mentioned in art. 22, 2 shall sedulously and prudently consider what, in this matter, can be appropriately admitted into divine worship from the traditions and genius of individual peoples. Adaptations which are judged useful or necessary are to be proposed to the Apostolic See, to be introduced with its consent. 2) So that adaptation may be done with the necessary circumspection, the same ecclesiastical authority shall be granted by the Apostolic See the faculty, if the case requires, to permit and direct necessary preliminary experiments in certain groups suited for this and for a determined time. 3) Because liturgical laws, concerning adaptation, especially in the Missions, are accustomed to bring special difficulties, in establishing them let men skilled in the matter be at hand.
¶243
D) NORMAE APTATIONEM INGENIO ET TRADITIONIBUS POPULORUM PERFICIENDAM
D) NORMS FOR ADAPTATION TO THE GENIUS AND TRADITIONS OF PEOPLES
Ecclesia, in iis quae fidem aut bonum totius communitatis non tangunt, rigidam unius tenoris formam ne in Liturgia quidem imponere cupit; quinimmo, variarum gentium populorumque animi ornamenta ac dotes colit et provehit; quidquid vero in populorum moribus indissolubili vinculo superstitionibus erroribusque non adstipulatur, benevole perpendit ac, si potest, sartum tectumque servat, immo quandoque in ipsam Liturgiam admittit, dummodo cum rationibus veri et authentici spiritus liturgici congruat. Servata substantiali unitate Ritus romani, legitimis varietatibus et aptationibus ad diversos coetus, regiones, populos, praesertim in Missionibus, locus relinquatur, etiam cum libri liturgici recognoscuntur; et hoc in structura rituum et in rubricis instituendis opportune prae oculis habeatur. Intra limites in editionibus typicis librorum liturgicorum statutos, erit competentis auctoritatis ecclesiasticae territorialis, de qua in art. 22 2, aptationes definire, praesertim quoad administrationem Sacramentorum, quoad Sacramentalia, processiones, linguam liturgicam, musicam sacram et artes, iuxta tamen normas fundamentales quae hac in Constitutione habentur. Cum tamen variis in locis et adiunctis, profundior Liturgiae aptatio urgeat, et ideo difficilior evadat: 1) A competenti auctoritate ecclesiastica territoriali, de qua in art. 22 2, sedulo et prudenter consideretur quid, hoc in negotio, ex traditionibus ingenioque singulorum populorum opportune in cultum divinum admitti possit. Aptationes, quae utiles vel necessariae existimantur, Apostolicae Sedi proponantur, de ipsius consensu introducendae. 2) Ut autem aptatio cum necessaria circumspectione fiat, eidem auctoritati ecclesiasticae territoriali ab Apostolica Sede facultas tribuetur, si casus ferat, ut in quibusdam coetibus ad id aptis et per determinatum tempus necessaria praevia experimenta permittat et dirigat. 3) Quia leges liturgicae difficultates speciales, quoad aptationem, praesertim in Missionibus, secum ferre solent, in illis condendis praesto sint viri, in re de qua agitur, periti.
The bishop is to be regarded as the great priest of his flock, from whom the life of his faithful in Christ is in some way derived and depends. Therefore all ought to hold in great esteem the liturgical life of the diocese around the bishop, especially in the cathedral church, being persuaded that the principal manifestation of the Church is had in the full and active participation of the whole holy people of God in the same liturgical celebrations, especially in the same Eucharist, in one prayer, at one altar, over which the bishop presides, surrounded by his presbyterate and ministers.
¶244
IV - DE VITA LITURGICA IN DIOECESI ET IN PAROECIA FOVENDA
IV - ON FOSTERING LITURGICAL LIFE IN THE DIOCESE AND IN THE PARISH
Episcopus ut sacerdos magnus sui gregis habendus est, a quo vita suorum fidelium in Christo quodammodo derivatur et pendet. Quare omnes vitam liturgicam dioeceseos circa Episcopum, praesertim in ecclesia cathedrali, maximi faciant oportet: sibi persuasum habentes praecipuam manifestationem Ecclesiae haberi in plenaria et actuosa participatione totius plebis sanctae Dei in iisdem celebrationibus liturgicis, praesertim in eadem Eucharistia, in una oratione, ad unum altare cui praeest Episcopus a suo presbyterio et ministris circumdatus.
Since the bishop cannot always and everywhere preside over the whole flock in his own church, he must necessarily establish assemblies of the faithful, among which parishes, locally ordered under a pastor taking the bishop's place, stand out: for they in a certain way represent the visible Church constituted throughout the whole world. Therefore the liturgical life of the parish and its relation to the bishop is to be fostered in the mind and practice of the faithful and clergy; and it is to be labored that the sense of parish community, especially in the common celebration of the Sunday Mass, may flourish.
¶245
Cum Episcopus in Ecclesia sua ipsemet nec semper nec ubique universo gregi praeesse possit, necessario constituere debet fidelium coetus, inter quos paroeciae, localiter sub pastore vices gerente Episcopi ordinatae, eminent: nam quodammodo repraesentant Ecclesiam visibilem per orbem terrarum constitutam. Quare vita liturgica paroeciae eiusque relatio ad Episcopum in mente et praxi fidelium et cleri fovenda est; et adlaborandum ut sensus communitatis paroecialis, imprimis vero in communi celebratione Missae dominicalis, floreat.
The study of fostering and restoring the sacred Liturgy is justly regarded as a sign of the providential dispositions of God over our age, as a passage of the Holy Spirit in His Church; and it distinguishes its own life, indeed the whole manner of religious feeling and action of this our time, with a proper mark. Wherefore, to further promote this pastoral liturgical action in the Church, the sacred Synod decrees: It is expedient that by the competent territorial ecclesiastical authority mentioned in art. 22, 2 a Liturgical Commission be established, to be helped by men skilled in liturgical science, music, sacred art, and pastoral matters. To this Commission, as far as possible, let a certain Institute of Pastoral Liturgy give help, consisting of members, not excluding, if the matter requires, laity excelling in this matter. It will be of this Commission, under the direction of the territorial ecclesiastical authority mentioned above, to regulate pastoral liturgical action in its territory, and to promote studies and necessary experiments, as often as it concerns adaptations to be proposed to the Apostolic See. In the same way, in each diocese a Commission on sacred Liturgy is to be had, to promote liturgical action under the direction of the bishop. It can sometimes become opportune that several dioceses constitute one Commission, which, with counsel exchanged, may advance the liturgical matter.
¶246
V - DE ACTIONE PASTORALI LITURGICA PROMOVENDA
V - ON PROMOTING PASTORAL LITURGICAL ACTION
Sacrae Liturgiae fovendae atque instaurandae studium merito habetur veluti signum providentialium dispositionum Dei super nostra aetate, veluti transitus Spiritus Sancti in sua Ecclesia; et vitam ipsius, immo huius nostri temporis universam rationem religiose sentiendi et agendi, nota propria distinguit. Quapropter, ad hanc actionem pastoralem liturgicam ulterius in Ecclesia fovendam, Sacrosanctum Concilium decernit: A competenti auctoritate ecclesiastica territoriali, de qua in art 22 2, expedit ut instituatur Commissio liturgica, a viris in scientia liturgica, Musica, Arte sacra ac re pastorali peritis iuvanda. Cui Commissioni, in quantum fieri potest, opem ferat quoddam Institutum Liturgiae Pastoralis, constans sodalibus, non exclusis, si res ita ferat, laicis in hac materia praestantibus. Ipsius Commissionis erit, ductu auctoritatis ecclesiasticae territorialis, de qua supra, et actionem pastoralem liturgicam in sua dicione moderari, et studia atque necessaria experimenta promovere, quoties agatur de aptationibus Apostolicae Sedis proponendis. Eadem ratione, in singulis dioecesibus Commissio de sacra Liturgia habeatur, ad actionem liturgicam, moderante Episcopo, promovendam. Opportunum aliquando evadere potest ut plures dioeceses unam Commissionem constituant, quae, collatis consiliis, rem liturgicam provehat.
Besides the Commission on sacred Liturgy, in any diocese, as far as possible, Commissions on sacred music and sacred art are also to be constituted. It is necessary that these three Commissions labor with united forces; indeed, not seldom it will be fitting that they coalesce into one Commission.
¶247
Praeter Commissionem de sacra Liturgia, in quavis dioecesi constituantur, quantum fieri potest, etiam Commissiones de Musica sacra et de Arte sacra. Necessarium est ut hae tres Commissiones consociatis viribus adlaborent; immo non raro congruum erit ut in unam Commissionem coalescant.

UNITATIS REDINTEGRATIO
DECRETUM DE OECUMENISMO
DECREE ON ECUMENISM
UNITATIS REDINTEGRATIO inter universos Christianos promovenda unum est ex praecipuis Sacrae Oecumenicae Synodi Vaticanae Secundae propositis. Una enim atque unica a Christo Domino condita est Ecclesia, plures tamen christianae Communiones sese ut Iesu Christi veram haereditatem hominibus proponunt; discipulos quidem Domini omnes se esse profitentur at diversa sentiunt et per diversas ambulant vias, ac si Christus Ipse divisus sit. Quae sane divisio et aperte voluntati Christi contradicit et scandalo est mundo atque sanctissimae causae praedicandi Evangelium omni creaturae affert detrimentum.
The Lord of the ages, however, who wisely and patiently pursues the purpose of His grace toward us sinners, has recently begun to pour out more abundantly on Christians separated among themselves a compunction of spirit and a desire for union. Moved by this grace, very many men everywhere have been stirred, and also among our separated brothers a greater movement day by day, the grace of the Holy Spirit fostering, has arisen for restoring the unity of all Christians. This movement of unity, called ecumenical, is participated in by those who invoke the triune God and confess Jesus as Lord and Savior, not only singly separately, but also gathered in assemblies, in which they have heard the Gospel and which each calls his church and that of God. Almost all, however, although in a different way, aspire to the one and visible Church of God, which may be truly universal and sent to the whole world that the world may be converted to the Gospel and thus be saved to the glory of God.
¶249
Dominus vero saeculorum, qui propositum gratiae suae erga nos peccatores sapienter et patienter prosequitur, novissime in Christianos inter se disiunctos animi compunctionem et desiderium unionis abundantius effundere incepit. Qua gratia permulti ubique homines permoti sunt atque inter fratres quoque nostros seiunctos amplior in dies motus, Spiritus Sancti fovente gratia, exortus est ad omnium Christianorum unitatem restaurandam. Hunc autem unitatis motum, oecumenicum nuncupatum, participant qui Deum Trinum invocant atque Iesum confitentur Dominum et Salvatorem, nec modo singuli seiunctim, sed etiam in coetibus congregati, in quibus Evangelium audierunt quosque singuli Ecclesiam dicunt esse suam et Dei. Fere omnes tamen, etsi diverso modo, ad Ecclesiam Dei unam et visibilem adspirant, quae sit vere universalis et ad universum mundum missa ut mundus ad Evangelium convertatur et sic salvus fiat ad gloriam Dei.
Therefore this sacred Synod, joyfully considering all these things, having already declared the doctrine concerning the Church, moved by the desire for restoring unity among all Christ's disciples, wishes to set forth to all Catholics the aids, ways, and means by which they may be able to respond to this divine vocation and grace.
¶250
Haec igitur Sacra Synodus ea omnia laeto animo considerans, cum iam doctrinam de Ecclesia declaraverit, desiderio unitatis inter omnes Christi discipulos restaurandae permota, Catholicis omnibus proponere vult adiumenta, vias ac modos quibus ipsi possint huic divinae vocationi et gratiae respondere.
2 In this the charity of God has appeared in us, that the only-begotten Son of God was sent by the Father into the world, that, made man, He might regenerate the whole human race by redeeming it and gather it into one. Who before He would offer Himself as an immaculate victim on the altar of the cross, prayed to the Father for believers, saying: "that they all may be one, as you, Father, in me, and I in you, that they also may be one in us; that the world may believe that you have sent me" (Jn 17:21), and in His Church He instituted the wonderful sacrament of the Eucharist, by which the unity of the Church is both signified and brought about. He gave to His disciples a new commandment of mutual love, and promised the Paraclete Spirit who, the Lord and Giver of life, would abide with them forever.
¶251
CAPUT I. De catholicis oecumenismi principiis
CHAPTER I. ON CATHOLIC PRINCIPLES OF ECUMENISM
In hoc apparuit caritas Dei in nobis, quod Filius Dei unigenitus a Patre in mundum missus est, ut homo factus totum genus humanum redimendo regeneraret atque in unum congregaret. Qui antequam seipsum in ara crucis hostiam immaculatam offerret, Patrem pro credentibus oravit, dicens: ut omnes unum sint, sicut tu, Pater, in me, et ego in te, ut et ipsi in nobis unum sint; ut credat mundus quia tu me misisti (Jn 17,21), et in Ecclesia sua Eucharistiae mirabile sacramentum instituit, quo unitas Ecclesiae et significatur et efficitur. Discipulis suis novum mutui amoris mandatum dedit atque Paraclitum Spiritum promisit qui, Dominus et vivificans, cum eis maneret in aeternum.
Exalted on the cross and glorified, the Lord Jesus poured out the promised Spirit, through whom He called and gathered the people of the New Covenant, which is the Church, into the unity of faith, hope, and charity, as the Apostle teaches: "One body and one Spirit, as you are called in one hope of your calling. One Lord, one faith, one baptism" (Eph 4:4-5). For "all you who are baptized in Christ have put on Christ... For all you are one in Christ Jesus" (Gal 3:27-28). The Holy Spirit, who dwells in believers and fills and rules the whole Church, brings about that wonderful communion of the faithful and so intimately joins all in Christ that He is the Principle of the Church's unity. He works divisions of graces and ministries, enriching the Church of Jesus Christ with various gifts, "for the consummation of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the building up of the body of Christ" (Eph 4:12).
¶252
Exaltatus autem in cruce et glorificatus Dominus Iesus Spiritum promissum effudit, per quem Novi Foederis populum, qui est Ecclesia, in unitatem fidei, spei et caritatis vocavit et congregavit, sicut docet Apostolus: Unum corpus et unus Spiritus sicut vocati estis in una spe vocationis vestrae. Unus Dominus, una fides, unum baptisma (Ep 4,4-5). Etenim quicumque in Christo baptizati estis, Christum induistis... Omnes enim vos unus estis in Christo Iesu (Ga 3,27-28 gr.). Spiritus Sanctus, qui credentes inhabitat totamque replet atque regit Ecclesiam, miram illam communionem fidelium efficit et tam intime omnes in Christo coniungit, ut Ecclesiae unitatis sit Principium. Ille divisiones gratiarum et ministrationum operatur, variis muneribus Ecclesiam Iesu Christi ditans ad consummationem sanctorum in opus ministerii, in aedificationem corporis Christi (Ep 4,12).
To establish this holy Church of His everywhere on earth until the consummation of the world, Christ entrusted the office of teaching, ruling, and sanctifying to the College of the Twelve. Among them He chose Peter, upon whom after his confession of faith He decreed to build His Church; to him He promised the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and after the profession of his love He committed all the sheep to him to be confirmed in faith and fed in perfect unity, Christ Jesus Himself remaining the chief cornerstone and the shepherd of our souls forever.
¶253
Ad hanc autem sanctam suam Ecclesiam ubique terrarum usque ad consummationem saeculi stabiliendam, Christus munus docendi, regendi et sanctificandi Collegio Duodecim concredidit. Inter eos Petrum elegit, super quem post fidei confessionem Ecclesiam suam aedificare decrevit; cui claves regni caelorum promisit atque, post suae dilectionis professionem, universas oves in fide confirmandas et in perfecta unitate pascendas commisit, Ipso Christo Iesu summo angulari lapide et pastore animarum nostrarum in aeternum manente.
Jesus Christ wishes His people to grow and perfects their communion in unity through the faithful preaching of the Gospel by the Apostles and their successors, namely bishops with the successor of Peter as head, through the administration of the sacraments, and through governance in love, the Holy Spirit working: in the confession of one faith, in the common celebration of divine worship, and in the fraternal concord of the family of God. Thus the Church, the one flock of God, as a sign lifted up among the nations, ministering the Gospel of peace to the whole human race, pilgrimages in hope to the goal of the heavenly homeland.
¶254
Iesus Christus per Apostolorum eorumque successorum, nempe episcoporum cum Petri successore capite, fidelem Evangelii praedicationem sacramentorumque administrationem, et per gubernationem in dilectione, Spiritu Sancto operante, populum suum crescere vult eiusque communionem perficit in unitate: in confessione unius fidei, in divini cultus communi celebratione, necnon in familiae Dei fraterna concordia. Ita Ecclesia, unicus Dei grex, tamquam signum levatum in nationes, Evangelium pacis ministrans toti generi humano, peregrinatur in spe ad patriae supernae metam.
This is the sacred mystery of the unity of the Church, in Christ and through Christ, the Holy Spirit working the variety of gifts. The supreme exemplar and principle of this mystery is in the unity of the Persons of the one God the Father and the Son in the Holy Spirit.
¶255
Hoc est unitatis Ecclesiae sacrum mysterium, in Christo et per Christum, Spiritu Sancto munerum varietatem operante. Huius mysterii supremum exemplar et principium est in Trinitate Personarum unitas unius Dei Patris et Filii in Spiritu Sancto.
3 In this one and only Church of God, certain schisms arose from the very beginnings, which the Apostle gravely reprobates as to be condemned; in later centuries, however, greater dissensions were born, and not small communities were separated from the full communion of the Catholic Church, sometimes not without the fault of men on both sides. But those who are now born in such communities and are imbued with the faith of Christ cannot be accused of the sin of separation, and the Catholic Church embraces them with fraternal reverence and love.
¶256
In hac una et unica Dei Ecclesia iam a primordiis scissurae quaedam exortae sunt, quas ut damnandas graviter vituperat Apostolus; posterioribus vero saeculis ampliores natae sunt dissensiones, et Communitates haud exiguae a plena communione Ecclesiae catholicae seiunctae sunt, quandoque non sine hominum utriusque partis culpa. Qui autem nunc in talibus Communitatibus nascuntur et fide Christi imbuuntur, de separationis peccato argui nequeunt, eosque fraterna reverentia et dilectione amplectitur Ecclesia catholica.
For they who believe in Christ and have received baptism rightly, are constituted in a certain communion with the Catholic Church, albeit not perfect. Indeed, on account of the discrepancies existing in various ways between them and the Catholic Church both in doctrinal matters and sometimes in disciplinary matters as well as concerning the structure of the Church, not a few impediments, sometimes graver, oppose full ecclesiastical communion, to overcome which the ecumenical movement tends. Nevertheless, justified by faith in baptism, they are incorporated into Christ, therefore they are justly adorned with the Christian name, and are deservedly recognized by the sons of the Catholic Church as brothers in the Lord.
¶257
Hi enim qui in Christum credunt et baptismum rite receperunt, in quadam cum Ecclesia catholica communione, etsi non perfecta, constituuntur. Profecto, ob discrepantias variis modis vigentes inter eos et Ecclesiam catholicam tum in re doctrinali et quandoque etiam disciplinari tum circa structuram Ecclesiae, plenae ecclesiasticae communioni opponuntur impedimenta non pauca, quandoque graviora, ad quae superanda tendit motus oecumenicus. Nihilominus, iustificati ex fide in baptismate, Christo incorporantur, ideoque christiano nomine iure decorantur et a filiis Ecclesiae catholicae ut fratres in Domino merito agnoscuntur.
Moreover, from the elements or goods by which the Church itself is built up and given life, some, indeed many and excellent, can exist outside the visible walls of the Catholic Church: the written word of God, the life of grace, faith, hope, and charity, and other interior gifts of the Holy Spirit as well as visible elements. All these, which come from Christ and lead to Him, pertain by right to the one Church of Christ.
¶258
Insuper ex elementis seu bonis, quibus simul sumptis ipsa Ecclesia aedificatur et vivificatur, quaedam immo plurima et eximia exstare possunt extra visibilia Ecclesiae catholicae saepta: verbum Dei scriptum, vita gratiae, fides, spes et caritas, aliaque interiora Spiritus Sancti dona ac visibilia elementa: haec omnia, quae a Christo proveniunt et ad Ipsum conducunt, ad unicam Christi Ecclesiam iure pertinent.
Not a few also of the sacred actions of the Christian religion are performed among our separated brothers, which, in various ways according to the diverse condition of each Church or Community, without doubt can really generate the life of grace and are to be said to open access to the communion of salvation. Therefore the separated Churches and Communities themselves, although we believe they suffer defects, are by no means deprived of significance and weight in the mystery of salvation. For the Spirit of Christ does not refuse to use them as means of salvation, whose power derives from that fullness of grace and truth which is entrusted to the Catholic Church.
¶259
Non paucae etiam christianae religionis actiones sacrae apud fratres a nobis seiunctos peraguntur, quae variis modis secundum diversam condicionem uniuscuiusque Ecclesiae vel Communitatis procul dubio vitam gratiae reapse generare possunt atque aptae dicendae sunt quae ingressum in salutis communionem pandant. Proinde ipsae Ecclesiae et Communitates seiunctae, etsi defectus illas pati credimus, nequaquam in mysterio salutis significatione et pondere exutae sunt. Iis enim Spiritus Christi uti non renuit tamquam salutis mediis, quorum virtus derivatur ab ipsa plenitudine gratiae et veritatis quae Ecclesiae catholicae concredita est.
Nevertheless, our separated brothers, whether individually or their Communities and Churches, do not enjoy that unity which Jesus Christ willed to bestow on all those whom He regenerated and vivified together into one body and newness of life, and which the Sacred Scriptures and the venerable Tradition of the Church profess. For only through the Catholic Church of Christ, which is the general aid of salvation, can the fullness of all saving means be attained. For we believe the Lord entrusted all the goods of the New Covenant to the one apostolic college of which Peter is the head, to constitute the one body of Christ on earth, into which all who already in some way belong to the People of God must be fully incorporated. This people, during its earthly pilgrimage, although it remains obnoxious to sin in its members, grows in Christ and is gently led by God according to His hidden counsels, until it arrives joyfully at the whole fullness of eternal glory in the heavenly Jerusalem.
¶260
Attamen fratres a nobis seiuncti, sive singuli sive Communitates et Ecclesiae eorum, unitate illa non fruuntur, quam Iesus Christus iis omnibus dilargiri voluit quos in unum corpus et in novitatem vitae regeneravit et convivificavit, quamque Sacrae Scripturae et veneranda Ecclesiae Traditio profitentur. Per solam enim catholicam Christi Ecclesiam, quae generale auxilium salutis est, omnis salutarium mediorum plenitudo attingi potest. Uni nempe Collegio apostolico cui Petrus praeest credimus Dominum commisisse omnia bona Foederis Novi, ad constituendum unum Christi corpus in terris, cui plene incorporentur oportet omnes, qui ad populum Dei iam aliquo modo pertinent. Qui populus, durante sua terrestri peregrinatione, quamvis in membris suis peccato obnoxius remaneat, in Christo crescit et a Deo, secundum Eius arcana consilia, suaviter ducitur, usquedum ad totam aeternae gloriae plenitudinem in caelesti Ierusalem laetus perveniat.
4 Since today in many parts of the world, the grace of the Holy Spirit breathing, many efforts are made by prayer, word, and work to approach that fullness of unity which Jesus Christ wills, this sacred Synod exhorts all Catholic faithful that, recognizing the signs of the times, they participate skillfully in the ecumenical work.
¶261
Cum hodie in pluribus orbis partibus, afflante Spiritus Sancti gratia, oratione, verbo et opere multi conatus fiant accedendi ad illam plenitudinem unitatis, quam Iesus Christus vult, haec Sancta Synodus cunctos fideles catholicos hortatur ut, signa temporum agnoscentes, operi oecumenico sollerter participent.
By the ecumenical movement are understood the activities and undertakings which, for the various needs of the Church and opportunities of times, are aroused and ordered to foster the unity of Christians, such as: first, all efforts to eliminate words, judgments, and deeds which do not correspond to the condition of our separated brothers according to equity and truth, and therefore render mutual relations with them more difficult; then, in assemblies of Christians from different Churches or Communities ordered in a religious spirit, a dialogue entered into between experts suitably instructed, in which each explains the doctrine of his Communion more deeply and clearly presents its characteristics. For through this dialogue all acquire a truer knowledge and more equitable estimation of the doctrine and life of each Communion; then those Communions also obtain a wider collaboration in whatever offices demanded by the common good from every Christian conscience, and in unanimous prayer, where it is lawful, they meet. Finally, all examine their own fidelity to the will of Christ concerning the Church and, as is fitting, strenuously undertake the work of renewal and reformation.
¶262
Per motum oecumenicum intelliguntur activitates et incepta, quae pro variis Ecclesiae necessitatibus et opportunitatibus temporum ad Christianorum unitatem fovendam suscitantur et ordinantur, ut sunt: primum, omnes conatus ad eliminanda verba, iudicia et opera, quae fratrum seiunctorum condicioni secundum aequitatem et veritatem non respondeant, ideoque mutuas cum ipsis rationes difficiliores reddant; dein, in conventibus Christianorum ex diversis Ecclesiis vel Communitatibus in spiritu religioso ordinatis, dialogus inter peritos apte instructos initus, in quo unusquisque suae Communionis doctrinam profundius explicat eiusque characteres perspicue praesentat. Per hunc enim dialogum veriorem utriusque Communionis doctrinae vitaeque cognitionem et magis aequam aestimationem omnes acquirunt; tum etiam illae Communiones eam consequuntur ampliorem collaborationem in quibusvis officiis ad bonum commune ab omni conscientia christiana postulatis, et in oratione unanimi, sicubi licet, conveniunt. Denique omnes suam fidelitatem voluntati Christi circa Ecclesiam examinant atque, ut oportet, opus renovationis nec non reformationis strenue aggrediuntur.
All which, when they are carried out by the faithful of the Catholic Church prudently and patiently under the vigilance of the pastors, contribute to the good of equity and truth, of concord and collaboration, of fraternal spirit and union; that thus, obstacles impeding perfect ecclesiastical communion being gradually overcome, all Christians, in one celebration of the Eucharist, may be gathered into the unity of the one and only Church which Christ gave to His Church from the beginning, which we believe subsists indefectibly in the Catholic Church, and which we hope grows day by day until the consummation of the world.
¶263
Quae omnia, cum a fidelibus Ecclesiae catholicae sub pastorum vigilantia prudenter et patienter perficiuntur, ad bonum aequitatis et veritatis, concordiae et collaborationis, fraterni animi et unionis conferunt; ut hac via paulatim, superatis obstaculis perfectam communionem ecclesiasticam impedientibus, omnes Christiani, in una Eucharistiae celebratione, in unius unicacque Ecclesiae unitatem congregentur quam Christus ab initio Ecclesiae suae largitus est, quamque inamissibilem in Ecclesia catholica subsistere credimus et usque ad consummationem saeculi in dies crescere speramus.
It is clear, however, that the work of preparation and reconciliation of those individuals who desire full Catholic communion is by its nature distinct from the ecumenical undertaking; yet no opposition exists, since both proceed from the wonderful disposition of God.
¶264
Patet autem opus praeparationis ac reconciliationis eorum singulorum, qui plenam communionem catholicam desiderant, ab oecumenico incepto natura sua distingui; nulla tamen adest oppositio, cum utrumque ex Dei mirabili dispositione procedat.
Catholic faithful in ecumenical action must without doubt be solicitous for their separated brothers, praying for them, communicating with them concerning things of the Church, moving the first steps to them. But first of all they themselves with sincere and attentive mind ought to consider those things which are to be renewed and done in the Catholic Family itself, that its life may more faithfully and clearly bear witness to the doctrine and institutions handed down by Christ through the Apostles.
¶265
Fideles catholici in actione oecumenica sine dubio de fratribus seiunctis solliciti sint oportet, pro illis orando, de rebus Ecclesiae cum illis communicando, primos gressus ad illos movendo. In primis vero ipsimet sincero attentoque animo ea perpendere debent, quae in ipsa Familia catholica renovanda et efficienda sunt, ut eius vita fidelius et clarius testimonium reddat de doctrina institutisque a Christo per Apostolos traditis.
For although the Catholic Church is endowed with all revealed truth and all the means of grace, yet her members do not live from them with all the fervor they ought, so that the face of the Church shines less to our separated brothers and to the whole world, and the growth of the kingdom of God is retarded. Therefore all Catholics ought to tend to Christian perfection and, each according to his condition, strive that the Church, bearing the humility and mortification of Jesus in her body, may be cleansed and renewed day by day, until Christ presents her to Himself glorious, not having spot or wrinkle.
¶266
Quamvis enim Ecclesia catholica omni a Deo revelata veritate et omnibus mediis gratiae ditata sit, tamen membra eius non omni quo par est fervore inde vivunt, ita ut vultus Ecclesiae fratribus a nobis seiunctis et universo mundo minus affulgeat atque regni Dei incrementum retardetur. Quapropter Catholici omnes ad perfectionem christianam tendere debent atque, pro sua quisque condicione, eniti ut Ecclesia, humilitatem et mortificationem Iesu in corpore suo portans, de die in diem mundetur et renovetur, donec Christus eam sibi exhibeat gloriosam, non habentem maculam aut rugam.
Keeping unity in necessary things, let all in the Church, according to the office given to each, preserve due liberty in the various forms of spiritual life and discipline, in the diversity of liturgical rites, indeed also in the theological elaboration of revealed truth; but in all things let them cultivate charity. For by this manner of acting they will more and more fully manifest the true catholicity and apostolicity of the Church.
¶267
In necessariis unitatem custodientes, omnes in Ecclesia, secundum munus unicuique datum, cum in variis formis vitae spiritualis et disciplinae, tum in diversitate liturgicorum rituum, immo et in theologica veritatis revelatae elaboratione, debitam libertatem servent; in omnibus vero caritatem colant. Hac enim agendi ratione ipsi veri nominis catholicitatem simul et apostolicitatem Ecclesiae in dies plenius manifestabunt.
On the other hand, it is necessary that Catholics joyfully recognize and esteem the truly Christian goods, proceeding from the common patrimony, which are found among our separated brothers. To recognize the riches of Christ and the works of virtues in the life of others, who bear witness to Christ, sometimes even to the shedding of blood, is equitable and salutary: for God is always wonderful and admirable in His works.
¶268
Ex altera parte necessarium est Catholicos cum gaudio agnoscere et aestimare bona vere christiana, a communi patrimonio promanantia, quae apud fratres a nobis seiunctos inveniuntur. Divitias Christi et virtutum opera agnoscere in vita aliorum, qui pro Christo testimonium ferunt, quandoque usque ad sanguinis effusionem, aequum et salutare est: Deus enim semper mirabilis est et mirandus in operibus suis.
Nor is it to be passed over, that whatever is done by the grace of the Holy Spirit in our separated brothers can also contribute to our edification. Whatever is truly Christian is never opposed to the genuine goods of faith, but always can bring it about that the mystery of Christ and the Church itself is more perfectly attained.
¶269
Neque est praetereundum, quaecumque Spiritus Sancti gratia in fratribus seiunctis efficiuntur, eadem ad nostram quoque aedificationem conferre posse. Quodcumque vere christianum est, numquam germanis fidei bonis adversatur, immo semper efficere potest ut perfectius ipsum mysterium Christi et Ecclesiae attingatur.
Nevertheless, the divisions of Christians are an impediment to the Church, that she may bring to effect the fullness of catholicity proper to her in those sons who, indeed appointed to her by baptism, are separated from her full communion. Indeed, it becomes more difficult for the Church herself to express the fullness of catholicity in every respect in the reality of her life. This sacred Synod joyfully notices that the participation of Catholic faithful in ecumenical action is increasing day by day, and commends it to bishops everywhere, that it may be promoted skillfully and prudently directed by them.
¶270
Attamen divisiones Christianorum impedimento Ecclesiae sunt, quominus ipsa ad effectum deducat plenitudinem catholicitatis sibi propriam in iis filiis, qui sibi quidem baptismate appositi, sed a sua plena communione seiuncti sunt. Immo et pro ipsa Ecclesia difficilius fit plenitudinem catholicitatis sub omni respectu in ipsa vitae realitate exprimere. Haec Sacra Synodus cum gaudio advertit participationem fidelium catholicorum in actione oecumenica in dies augeri, eamque episcopis ubique terrarum commendat, ut eadem promoveatur sollerter, et prudenter ab ipsis dirigatur.
5 The solicitude for restoring union pertains to the whole Church, both to the faithful and to the pastors, and affects each according to his own virtue, whether in daily Christian life or in theological and historical investigations. This care already in a certain way manifests the fraternal union existing among all Christians, and leads to full and perfect unity according to God's benevolence.
¶271
CAPUT II. De oecumenismi exercitio
CHAPTER II. ON THE PRACTICE OF ECUMENISM
Ad totam Ecclesiam sollicitudo unionis instaurandae spectat, tam ad fideles quam ad pastores, et unumquemque secundum propriam virtutem afficit, sive in vita christiana quotidiana sive in theologicis et historicis investigationibus. Haec cura fraternam coniunctionem inter omnes Christianos existentem iam quodammodo manifestat, atque ad plenam perfectamque unitatem secundum Dei benevolentiam conducit.
6 Since every renewal of the Church essentially consists in increased fidelity to her own vocation, it is without doubt the reason why the movement toward unity tends. The pilgrim Church is called by Christ to this perennial reformation, which she, as a human and earthly institution, perpetually needs; so that if certain things, according to circumstances of times and places, whether in morals, or in ecclesiastical discipline, or even in the manner of enunciating doctrine (which must be sedulously distinguished from the deposit of faith itself), have been less accurately kept, they be rightly and duly restored at the opportune time. This renovation, therefore, obtains notable ecumenical importance. The various ways of life of the Church, by which this renovation is already effected—such as the biblical and liturgical movements, the preaching of the word of God and catechesis, the apostolate of the laity, new forms of religious life, the spirituality of matrimony, the doctrine and activity of the Church in social matters—are to be held as certain pledges and auspices that happily portend future progress of ecumenism.
¶272
Cum omnis renovatio Ecclesiae essentialiter in aucta fidelitate erga vocationem eius consistat, ea procul dubio ratio est cur motus versus unitatem contendat. Ecclesia in via peregrinans vocatur a Christo ad hanc perennem reformationem qua ipsa, qua humanum terrenumque institutum, perpetuo indiget; ita ut si quae, pro rerum temporumque adiunctis, sive in moribus, sive in ecclesiastica disciplina, sive etiam in doctrinae enuntiandae modo — qui ab ipso deposito fidei sedulo distingui debet — minus accurate servata fuerint, opportuno tempore recte debiteque instaurentur. Haec igitur renovatio insigne obtinet momentum oecumenicum. Varii autem illi modi vitae Ecclesiae, per quos haec renovatio iam efficitur — ut sunt motus biblicus et liturgicus, praedicatio verbi Dei atque catechesis, laicorum apostolatus, vitae religiosae novae formae, matrimonii spiritualitas, doctrina et activitas Ecclesiae in re sociali — tamquam pignora et auspicia quaedam sunt habenda, quae futuros oecumenismi progressus fauste portendunt.
7 True ecumenism does not exist without interior conversion. For the desires of unity arise and mature from newness of mind, from self-denial, and from the most free outpouring of charity. Therefore we must implore from the divine Spirit the grace of sincere self-denial, humility, and meekness in serving, and of fraternal generosity of spirit toward others. "I therefore, a prisoner in the Lord, beseech you that you walk worthy of the vocation in which you are called, with all humility and meekness, with patience supporting one another in charity, careful to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace" (Eph 4:1-3). This exhortation especially regards those who are raised to the sacred order for the purpose of continuing the mission of Christ, who came not to be ministered unto but to minister (Mt 20:28). The testimony of St. John also holds good concerning faults against unity: "If we say that we have not sinned, we make Him a liar, and His word is not in us" (1 Jn 1:10). Therefore with humble prayer we ask pardon from God and from our separated brothers, just as we forgive our debtors.
¶273
Oecumenismus veri nominis sine interiore conversione non datur. Etenim ex novitate mentis, ex sui ipsius abnegatione atque ex caritatis liberrima effusione proficiscuntur et maturescunt desideria unitatis. Ideo a Spiritu divino imploranda nobis est gratia sincerae abnegationis, humilitatis et mansuetudinis in serviendo, atque fraternae in alios animi liberalitatis. Obsecro itaque vos, ait Apostolus gentium, ego vinctus in Domino, ut digne ambuletis vocatione, qua vocati estis, cum omni humilitate et mansuetudine, cum patientia supportantes invicem in caritate, solliciti servare unitatem Spiritus in vinculo pacis (Ep 4,1-3). Exhortatio haec illos praesertim spectat, qui ad sacrum ordinem eo consilio evecti sunt, ut continuetur missio Christi, qui inter nos non venit ministrari, sed ministrare (Mt 20,28). De culpis etiam adversus unitatem valet testimonium S. Ioannis: Si dixerimus quoniam non peccavimus, mendacem facimus eum, et verbum eius non est in nobis (1Jn 1,10). Humili igitur prece veniam petimus a Deo et a fratribus seiunctis, sicut et nos dimittimus debitoribus nostris.
Let all Christ's faithful be mindful that they promote, indeed exercise, the union of Christians the better, the purer life they strive to lead according to the Gospel. For by how much the more intimately they are united with the Father, the Word, and the Spirit, by so much the more intimately and easily they will be able to increase mutual fraternity.
¶274
Meminerint omnes christifideles se Christianorum unionem eo melius promovere, immo exercere, quo puriorem secundum Evangelium vitam degere studeant. Quo enim arctiore communione cum Patre, Verbo et Spiritu unientur, eo intimius atque facilius mutuam fraternitatem augere valebunt.
8 This conversion of heart and holiness of life, together with private and public supplications for the unity of Christians, are to be esteemed as the soul of the whole ecumenical movement, and can be rightly called spiritual ecumenism.
¶275
Haec cordis conversio vitaeque sanctitas, una cum privatis et publicis supplicationibus pro Christianorum unitate, tamquam anima totius motus oecumenici existimandae sunt et merito oecumenismus spiritualis nuncupari possunt.
For it is solemn for Catholics frequently to meet for that prayer for the unity of the Church, which the Savior Himself on the eve of His death asked from the Father most fervently: "that all may be one" (Jn 17:21). In certain peculiar circumstances of matters, such as the prayers which are indicated for unity, and in ecumenical gatherings, it is lawful and indeed desirable that Catholics join in prayer with separated brothers. Common prayers of this kind are a most effective means of obtaining the grace of unity, and a genuine signification of the bonds by which Catholics are still joined with separated brothers: "For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them" (Mt 18:20).
¶276
Sollemne enim est Catholicis frequenter ad illam orationem convenire pro Ecclesiae unitate, quam Salvator Ipse in pervigilio mortis a Patre flagranter precatus est: ut omnes unum sint (Jn 17,21). In quibusdam peculiaribus rerum adiunctis, cuiusmodi sunt precationes quae pro unitate indicuntur, atque in oecumenicis conventibus licitum est immo et optandum, ut Catholici cum fratribus seiunctis in oratione consocientur. Communes eiusmodi preces perefficax sane medium sunt gratiae unitatis impetrandae et genuina significatio vinculorum, quibus Catholici cum fratribus seiunctis adhuc coniunguntur: Ubi enim sunt duo vel tres congregati in nomine meo, ibi sum in medio eorum (Mt 18,20).
Nevertheless, communication in sacred things is not to be considered as a means indiscriminately to be used for restoring the unity of Christians. This communication depends chiefly on two principles: on signifying the unity of the Church, and on participation in the means of grace. The signification of unity most often forbids communication. The procuring of grace sometimes commends it. Concerning the concrete manner of acting, all circumstances of times, places, and persons having been attended to, let the local episcopal authority prudently decide, unless something else is decided by the episcopal conference according to its own statutes, or by the Holy See.
¶277
Attamen communicationem in sacris considerare non licet velut medium indiscretim adhibendum ad Christianorum unitatem restaurandam. Quae communicatio a duobus principiis praecipue pendet: ab unitate Ecclesiae significanda, et a participatione in mediis gratiae. Significatio unitatis plerumque vetat communicationem. Gratia procuranda quandoque illam commendat. De modo concreto agendi, attentis omnibus circumstantiis temporum, locorum et personarum, prudenter decernat auctoritas episcopalis localis, nisi aliud a conferentia episcopali, ad normam propriorum statutorum, vel a Sancta Sede statuatur.
9 It is necessary to know the mind of our separated brothers. To this end a study is necessarily required, which must be carried out according to truth and with a benevolent spirit. Catholics, duly prepared, must acquire a better knowledge of the doctrine and history, the spiritual and cultual life, the religious psychology and culture, which is proper to the brothers. To accomplish this, meetings from each side for treating especially theological questions, where each acts as an equal with an equal, provided that those who take part in them are truly skilled under the vigilance of the prelates, greatly help. From such dialogue the condition of the Catholic Church will also become more clearly known. By this way the mind of our separated brothers will be better known and our faith more aptly set forth to them.
¶278
Fratrum seiunctorum animum cognoscere oportet. Ad hoc necessario requiritur studium, quod secundum veritatem et animo benevolo peragendum est. Catholici debito modo praeparati acquirant necesse est meliorem cognitionem doctrinae et historiae, vitae spiritualis et cultualis, psychologiae religiosae et culturae, quae fratribus propria est. Ad id assequendum multum iuvant ex utraque parte conventus ad theologicas praesertim quaestiones tractandas, ubi unusquisque par cum pari agat, dummodo qui in illis partem habent sub praesulum vigilantia vere periti sint. Ex tali dialogo etiam clarius innotescet, quae sit revera Ecclesiae catholicae condicio. Hac via et seiunctorum fratrum mens melius cognoscetur eisque fides nostra aptius exponetur.
10 The institutions of sacred theology and other disciplines, especially historical, must be taught also under an ecumenical aspect, that they may correspond ever more accurately to the truth of things. For it is of great interest that future pastors and priests excel in theology thus accurately elaborated, not polemically, especially in those things which concern the relations of separated brothers toward the Catholic Church. For from the formation of priests the necessary instruction and spiritual formation of the faithful and religious mostly depends. Also Catholics engaged in missionary work in the same lands as other Christians today especially ought to know the questions and fruits which arise from ecumenism in their apostolate.
¶279
Sacrae theologiae institutiones et aliae disciplinae praesertim historicae tradantur oportet etiam sub aspectu oecumenico, ut usque accuratius rerum veritati respondeant. Multum enim interest futuros pastores et sacerdotes pollere theologia hoc modo adamussim elaborata, non polemice, in iis imprimis, quae fratrum seiunctorum erga Ecclesiam catholicam relationes spectant. A sacerdotum enim formatione necessaria institutio ac formatio spiritualis fidelium et religiosorum quam maxime pendet. Etiam Catholici in missionalia opera in iisdem terris ac alii Christiani incumbentes hodie praesertim cognoscant oportet quaestiones et fructus, quae ex oecumenismo in eorum apostolatu oriuntur.
11 The manner and way of expressing the catholic faith must by no means become an obstacle to dialogue with the brothers. The whole doctrine must be clearly set forth absolutely. Nothing is so alien from ecumenism as that false irenicism by which the purity of catholic doctrine suffers detriment and its genuine and certain sense is obscured. At the same time, the catholic faith is to be explained more profoundly and rightly, in a manner and language which can also be truly understood by separated brothers.
¶280
Modus ac ratio fidem catholicam exprimendi nullatenus obstaculum fieri debet dialogo cum fratribus. Integra doctrina lucide exponatur omnino oportet. Nil ab oecumenismo tam alienum est quam ille falsus irenismus, quo puritas doctrinae catholicae detrimentum patitur et eius sensus genuinus et certus obscuratur. Simul fides catholica et profundius et rectius explicanda est, modo et sermone qui etiam a fratribus seiunctis possit vere comprehendi.
Moreover, in ecumenical dialogue, catholic theologians, adhering to the doctrine of the Church, investigating the divine mysteries together with separated brothers, must proceed with love of truth, charity, and humility. In comparing doctrines, let them be mindful that there exists an order or hierarchy of truths of catholic doctrine, since their connection with the foundation of the Christian faith is different. Thus the way will be paved by which, through this fraternal emulation, all are incited to deeper knowledge and clearer manifestation of the unsearchable riches of Christ.
¶281
Insuper in dialogo oecumenico theologi catholici, doctrinae Ecclesiae inhaerentes, una cum fratribus seiunctis investigationem peragentes de divinis mysteriis, cum veritatis amore, caritate et humilitate progredi debent. In comparandis doctrinis meminerint existere ordinem seu hierarchiam veritatum doctrinae catholicae, cum diversus sit earum nexus cum fundamento fidei christianae. Sic via sternetur qua per fraternam hanc aemulationem omnes incitentur ad profundiorem cognitionem et clariorem manifestationem investigabilium divitiarum Christi.
12 Before all nations, let all Christians profess faith in the one and triune God, in the incarnate Son of God, our Redeemer and Lord, and with common effort, in mutual esteem, bear witness to our hope, which does not confound. Since in modern times cooperation in social matters is most widely established, absolutely all men are called to common work, but especially those who believe in God, and most of all all Christians as marked with the name of Christ. The cooperation of all Christians vividly expresses that union by which they are already joined together among themselves, and sets the face of the servant of Christ in fuller light. This cooperation, already established in not a few nations, must be more and more perfected, especially in regions where social or technical development is accomplished, whether in rightly estimating the dignity of the human person, in promoting the good of peace, in pursuing the social application of the Gospel, in advancing sciences and arts in a Christian spirit, or also in applying remedies of any kind against the afflictions of our age, such as famine and calamities, illiteracy and poverty, shortage of housing and unequal distribution of goods. By this cooperation all who believe in Christ can easily learn how others can better know and esteem one another, and the way to the unity of Christians is paved.
¶282
Coram omnibus gentibus Christiani universi fidem in Deum unum et trinum, in Filium Dei incarnatum, Redemptorem et Dominum nostrum profiteantur et communi conatu in mutua aestimatione testimonium reddant spei nostrae, quae non confundit. Cum hodiernis temporibus in re sociali cooperatio latissime instauretur, omnes prorsus homines ad communem operam vocantur, potiore vero ratione ii, qui in Deum credunt, maxime autem omnes Christiani utpote Christi nomine insigniti. Omnium Christianorum cooperatio coniunctionem illam qua iam inter se uniuntur vivo modo exprimit atque Christi servi vultum in pleniorem lucem ponit. Cooperatio haec, in non paucis nationibus iam instaurata, magis magisque perficiatur oportet, in regionibus praesertim, ubi evolutio socialis vel technica peragitur, sive in dignitate personae humanae recte aestimanda, sive in bono pacis promovendo, sive in sociali Evangelii applicatione prosequenda, sive in scientiis et artibus spiritu christiano provehendis, sive etiam in cuiusvis generis remediis adhibendis contra nostrae aetatis aerumnas, cuiusmodi sunt fames et calamitates, analphabetismus et inopia, penuria habitationum et non acqua bonorum distributio. Hac cooperatione omnes qui in Christum credunt facile addiscere possunt, quomodo alii alios melius cognoscere et pluris aestimare queant atque ad unitatem Christianorum via sternatur.
13 We turn our eyes to the two principal categories of schisms affecting the seamless tunic of Christ. The first of these occurred in the East, either by the contestation of dogmatic formulas of the Councils of Ephesus and Chalcedon, or, at a later time, by the dissolution of ecclesiastical communion between the Eastern Patriarchates and the Roman See. Others, then, after more than four centuries, arose in the West from the events which commonly come under the name of the Reformation. From then on, several Communities, whether national or confessional, were separated from the Roman See. Among these, in which traditions and catholic structures continue partly to subsist, the Anglican Communion holds a special place. These diverse divisions, however, differ greatly among themselves not only by reason of origin, place, and time, but especially by the nature and gravity of questions pertaining to faith and ecclesiastical structure.
¶283
CAPUT III. De Ecclesiis et de Communitatibus ecclesialibus a Sede Apostolica Romana seiunctis
CHAPTER III. ON CHURCHES AND ECCLESIAL COMMUNITIES SEPARATED FROM THE ROMAN APOSTOLIC SEE
Ad duas praecipuas scissionum categorias inconsutilem tunicam Christi afficientium oculos convertimus. Primae earum in Oriente evenerunt, sive contestatione dogmaticarum formularum Conciliorum Ephesini et Chalcedonensis, sive, posteriore tempore, per solutionem ecclesiasticae communionis inter Patriarchatus orientales et Sedem Romanam. Aliae dein, post amplius quattuor saecula, in Occidente ortae sunt ex eventibus qui sub nomine Reformationis communiter veniunt. Exinde a Sede Romana plures Communiones sive nationales sive confessionales seiunctae sunt. Inter eas, in quibus traditiones et structurae catholicae ex parte subsistere pergunt, locum specialem tenet Communio anglicana. Hae tamen diversae divisiones inter se valde differunt non solum ratione originis, loci et temporis, sed praesertim natura et gravitate quaestionum ad fidem et structuram ecclesiasticam pertinentium.
Wherefore, this sacred Synod, neither despising the diverse conditions of the various Christian groups, nor passing over the surviving bonds between them, notwithstanding division, has decided to propose the following considerations for the prudent exercise of ecumenical action.
¶284
Quam ob rem, haec Sancta Synodus, nec condiciones diversas diversorum Coetuum christianorum parvipendens, neque superstites, non obstante divisione, nexus inter eos praeteriens, ad prudentem actionem occumenicam exercendam sequentes considerationes proponere decernit.
14 The Churches of the East and the West followed their own proper way for not a few centuries, yet joined by fraternal communion of faith and sacramental life, the Roman See moderating common consent, if dissensions concerning faith or discipline arose between them. It is pleasing to the sacred Synod, among other grave matters, to call to mind for all that several particular or local churches flourish in the East, among which the Patriarchal Churches hold the first place, and not a few of them glory to have origin from the Apostles themselves. Hence among the Easterns the solicitude and care of preserving those fraternal relations in the communion of faith and charity, which must flourish between local churches as between sisters, has prevailed and prevails. It is not to be passed over that the Churches of the East from the beginning have a treasure, from which the Church of the West has drawn many things in liturgical matters, in spiritual tradition, and in juridical order. Nor is it to be made little of that the fundamental dogmas of the Christian faith concerning the Trinity and the Word of God, incarnate of the Virgin Mary, were defined in ecumenical councils celebrated in the East. To preserve this faith, those Churches have suffered and suffer many things. The heritage handed down from the Apostles has been accepted in diverse forms and ways, and from the very beginnings of the Church has been variously explained here and there on account of the diversity of genius and conditions of life. All which, besides external causes, on account also of the defect of mutual understanding and charity, have furnished an occasion for separations.
¶285
I. De Ecclesiarum Orientalium peculiari consideratione
I. ON THE PECULIAR CONSIDERATION OF THE EASTERN CHURCHES
Ecclesiae Orientis et Occidentis per non pauca saecula suam propriam viam, fraterna tamen communione fidei et vitae sacramentalis coniunctae, secutae sunt, Sede Romana moderante communi consensu, si dissensiones circa fidem vel disciplinam inter eas orirentur. Sacrosanctae Synodo gratum est, inter cetera gravis momenti, omnibus in mentem revocare plures in Oriente florere particulares seu locales Ecclesias, inter quas primum locum tenent Ecclesiae Patriarchales, et ex quibus non paucae ab ipsis Apostolis ortum habere gloriantur. Proinde apud Orientales praevaluit atque praevalet sollicitudo et cura servandi fraternas illas in fidei caritatisque communione necessitudines, quae inter Ecclesias locales, ut inter sorores, vigere debent. Praetermittendum pariter non est Ecclesias Orientis ab origine habere thesaurum, ex quo plura in rebus liturgicis, in traditione spirituali et in ordine iuridico Ecclesia Occidentis deprompsit. Neque illud parvi faciendum est fundamentalia dogmata christianae fidei de Trinitate et de Verbo Dei, ex Virgine Maria incarnato, in Conciliis Oecumenicis in Oriente celebratis definita esse. Ad hanc fidem servandam illae Ecclesiae multa et passae sunt et patiuntur. Tradita autem ab Apostolis haereditas diversis formis et modis acceptata est et inde ab ipsis Ecclesiae primordiis hic et illic varie explicata ob diversitatem quoque ingenii et vitae condicionum. Quae omnia, praeter causas externas, propter defectum etiam mutuae comprehensionis et caritatis separationibus ansam praebuerunt.
Wherefore the sacred Synod exhorts all, but especially those who intend to strain for the restoration of the desired full communion between the Eastern Churches and the Catholic Church, that they have due consideration for this peculiar condition of the nascent and growing Churches of the East and for the nature of the relations which flourished between them and the Roman See before the separation, and form for themselves a right estimation concerning all these things. These things accurately kept will most greatly contribute to the intended dialogue.
¶286
Quamobrem Sacrosancta Synodus omnes quidem, sed praesertim eos exhortatur qui in instaurationem plenae communionis optatae inter Ecclesias orientales et Ecclesiam catholicam incumbere intendunt, ut debitam considerationem habeant de hac peculiari condicione nascentium crescentiumque Ecclesiarum Orientis et de indole relationum, quae inter eas et Sedem Romanam ante separationem vigebant atque rectam de his omnibus existimationem sibi efforment. Haec accurate servata ad dialogum intentum summopere conferent.
15 It is also known to all with how great love Eastern Christians perform sacred liturgical actions, especially the eucharistic celebration, the source of the life of the Church and the pledge of future glory, through which the faithful, united with the bishop, having access to God the Father through the Son the incarnate Word, suffering and glorified, in the outpouring of the Holy Spirit, attain communion with the most holy Trinity, made partakers of the divine nature (2 Pet 1:4). Hence through the celebration of the Eucharist of the Lord in each of these Churches, the Church of God is built up and grows, and through concelebration their communion is manifested. In this liturgical cult, the Easterns magnify the ever-Virgin Mary, whom the Ecumenical Council of Ephesus solemnly proclaimed the most holy Mother of God, that truly and properly Christ, the Son of God and the Son of Man, according to the Scriptures, might be acknowledged, with most beautiful hymns, and they also praise many saints, among whom the Fathers of the universal Church.
¶287
Omnibus quoque notum est quanto cum amore Christiani orientales liturgica Sacra peragant, praesertim celebrationem eucharisticam, fontem vitae Ecclesiae et pignus futurae gloriae, qua fideles cum episcopo uniti accessum ad Deum Patrem habentes per Filium Verbum incarnatum, passum et glorificatum, in effusione Sancti Spiritus, communionem cum Sanctissima Trinitate consequuntur, divinae consortes naturae (2P 1,4) effecti. Proinde per celebrationem Eucharistiae Domini in his singulis Ecclesiis, Ecclesia Dei aedificatur et crescit, et per concelebrationem communio earum manifestatur. In cultu hoc liturgico Mariam semper Virginem, quam Oecumenica Synodus Ephesina sollemniter Deiparam Sanctissimam proclamavit ut vere et proprie Christus Filius Dei et Filius Hominis secundum Scripturas agnosceretur, Orientales pulcherrimis hymnis magnificant et multos quoque Sanctos, inter quos Patres universalis Ecclesiae, collaudant.
Since, however, those Churches, although separated, have true sacraments, especially, by virtue of apostolic succession, the priesthood and the Eucharist, by which they are still joined to us by a most close relationship, a certain communication in sacred things, given opportune circumstances and the ecclesiastical authority approving, is not only possible but also to be recommended.
¶288
Cum autem illae Ecclesiae, quamvis seiunctae, vera sacramenta habeant, praecipue vero, vi successionis apostolicae, Sacerdotium et Eucharistiam, quibus arctissima necessitudine adhuc nobiscum coniunguntur, quaedam communicatio in sacris, datis opportunis circumstantiis et approbante auctoritate ecclesiastica, non solum possibilis est sed etiam suadetur.
In the East also are found the riches of those spiritual traditions, which especially monasticism expressed. For there from the glorious times of the holy Fathers, that monastic spirituality flourished, which afterward flowed to the Western parts, and from which the religious institute of the Latins, as from its source, derived its origin and afterwards repeatedly received new vigor. Wherefore it is strongly recommended that Catholics more frequently approach these spiritual riches of the Eastern Fathers, which lift up the whole man to the contemplation of divine things. Let all know that to know, venerate, preserve, and foster the most rich liturgical and spiritual patrimony of the Easterns is of the greatest importance for faithfully preserving the fullness of the Christian tradition and for perfecting the reconciliation of Eastern and Western Christians.
¶289
In Oriente quoque inveniuntur divitiae illarum traditionum spiritualium, quas praesertim monachismus expressit. Ibi enim inde a gloriosis Sanctorum Patrum temporibus floruit spiritualitas illa monastica, quae dein ad occidentales partes manavit et ex qua religiosum latinorum institutum tamquam e suo fonte originem duxit ac deinceps novum vigorem identidem accepit. Quapropter enixe commendatur ut Catholici frequentius accedant ad has spirituales Patrum Orientalium divitias quae hominem totum ad divina contemplanda evehunt. Ditissimum Orientalium patrimonium liturgicum et spirituale cognoscere, venerari, conservare et fovere omnes sciant maximi esse momenti ad plenitudinem traditionis christianae fideliter custodiendam et ad reconciliationem orientalium et occidentalium Christianorum perficiendam.
16 Moreover, from the first times the Churches of the East followed their own disciplines, sanctioned by the holy Fathers and Synods, even ecumenical. Since, however, a certain diversity of customs and practices does not stand against the unity of the Church, indeed increases its beauty and contributes not a little to fulfilling its mission, as is mentioned above, the sacred Synod, to remove every doubt, declares that the Churches of the East, mindful of the necessary unity of the whole Church, have the faculty of governing themselves according to their own disciplines, as being more congruent to the character of their faithful and more suitable for providing for the good of souls. The perfect observance of this traditional principle, not always indeed kept, pertains to those things which are altogether required as a condition for restoring union.
¶290
Praeterea a primis iam temporibus Ecclesiae Orientis disciplinas proprias a Sanctis Patribus atque Synodis, etiam Oecumenicis, sancitas sequebantur. Cum autem unitati Ecclesiae minime obstet, immo decorem eius augeat et ad missionem eius implendam non parum conferat quaedam morum consuetudinumque diversitas, uti supra memoratur, Sacra Synodus, ad omne dubium tollendum, declarat Ecclesias Orientis, memores necessariae unitatis totius Ecclesiae, facultatem habere se secundum proprias disciplinas regendi, utpote indoli suorum fidelium magis congruas atque bono animorum consulendo aptiores. Perfecta huius traditionalis principii observantia, non semper quidem servata, ad ea pertinet quae ad unionem restaurandam tamquam praevia condicio omnino requiruntur.
17 What has been said above concerning legitimate diversity, it is pleasing also to declare concerning the diverse theological enunciation of doctrines. For in the investigation of revealed truth, diverse methods and steps toward knowing and confessing God have been used in the East and in the West. Hence it is no wonder that certain aspects of the revealed mystery are sometimes more fittingly perceived and brought into better light by one than by the other, so that then those various theological formulas are often to be said to complement one another rather than to oppose. Concerning the authentic theological traditions of the Easterns, it is to be recognized that they are rooted in an excellent way in the sacred Scriptures, fostered and expressed by the liturgical life, nourished by the living apostolic tradition and the writings of the Eastern Fathers and spiritual authors, and tend to right ordering of life, indeed to the full contemplation of Christian truth. This sacred Synod, giving thanks to God that many Eastern sons of the Catholic Church who preserve this patrimony and wish to live it more purely and more fully already live in full communion with their brothers cultivating the Western tradition, declares that this whole spiritual and liturgical, disciplinary and theological patrimony in its diverse traditions pertains to the full catholicity and apostolicity of the Church.
¶291
Quae supra de legitima diversitate dicta sunt, eadem placet etiam de diversa theologica doctrinarum enuntiatione declarare. Etenim in veritatis revelatae exploratione methodi gressusque diversi ad divina cognoscenda et confitenda in Oriente et in Occidente adhibiti sunt. Unde mirum non est quosdam aspectus mysterii revelati quandoque magis congrue percipi et in meliorem lucem poni ab uno quam ab altero, ita ut tunc variae illae theologicae formulae non raro potius inter se compleri dicendae sint quam opponi. Ad traditiones theologicas authenticas Orientalium quod attinet, agnoscendum est eas eximio quidem modo in Sacris Scripturis radicatas esse, vita liturgica foveri et exprimi, viva apostolica traditione scriptisque Orientalium Patrum ac spiritualium auctorum nutriri, ad rectam vitae institutionem, immo ad christianam veritatem plene contemplandam tendere. Haec Sancta Synodus, gratias agens Deo quod multi orientales Ecclesiae catholicae filii, qui hoc patrimonium custodiunt et illud purius pleniusque vivere cupiunt, iam cum fratribus traditionem occidentalem colentibus in plena communione vivunt, declarat, totum hoc patrimonium spirituale ac liturgicum, disciplinare ac theologicum in diversis suis traditionibus ad plenam catholicitatem et apostolicitatem Ecclesiae pertinere.
18 All these things having been well considered, this sacred Synod renews what has been declared by the sacred councils of the past and also by the Roman Pontiffs, namely that to restore or preserve communion and unity nothing more is necessary than to impose... a burden beyond... what is necessary (Acts 15:28). It also most strongly desires that for gradually attaining it all efforts henceforth intend in various institutes and forms of the life of the Church, especially by prayer and fraternal dialogue concerning doctrine and the more urgent needs of pastoral office in our age. In the same way, it commends to the pastors and faithful of the Catholic Church relations with those who live no longer in the East but far from their homeland, that fraternal collaboration with them in the spirit of charity and all spirit of contentious emulation being removed may grow. But if this work be promoted with whole spirit, the sacred Synod hopes that, the wall dividing the Eastern and Western Church being removed, there may at last become one mansion founded on the cornerstone, Christ Jesus, who will make both one.
¶292
His omnibus bene perspectis, haec Sacrosancta Synodus renovat id quod a Sacris praeteritis Conciliis nec non a Romanis Pontificibus declaratum est, nempe ad communionem et unitatem restaurandam vel servandam opus esse nihil ultra imponere... oneris quam... necessaria ( I5, 28). Vehementer etiam exoptat ut ad eam paulatim consequendam omnes conatus exinde intendant in variis institutis et formis vitae Ecclesiae, praesertim oratione et fraterno dialogo circa doctrinam et muneris pastoralis urgentiores aetatis nostrae necessitates. Eodem modo pastoribus et fidelibus Ecclesiae catholicae commendat necessitudines cum eis qui non iam in Oriente, sed procul a patria vitam degunt, ut fraterna collaboratio cum eis in spiritu caritatis et secluso omni spiritu contentiosae aemulationis crescat. Quod si hoc opus toto animo promoveatur, Sacrosancta Synodus sperat fore, ut sublato pariete occidentalem orientalemque Ecclesiam dividente, unica tandem fiat mansio angulari firmata lapide, Christo Iesu, qui faciet utraque unum.
19 The Churches and ecclesial Communities which, either in that most grave crisis of affairs which began in the West from the end of the Middle Ages, or in later times, have been separated from the Roman Apostolic See, are joined with the Catholic Church by a peculiar affinity and relationship on account of the long life of the Christian people in past centuries spent in ecclesiastical communion. Since, however, these Churches and ecclesial Communities, on account of the diversity of origin, doctrine, and spiritual life, differ not a little from us, but also among themselves, it is a most difficult work to describe them equally, which we do not intend to attempt here. Although the ecumenical movement and the desire for peace with the Catholic Church have not yet prevailed everywhere, there is hope for us that in all the ecumenical sense and mutual esteem may gradually grow. Yet it must be recognized that between these Churches and Communities and the Catholic Church there are weighty discrepancies, not only of historical, sociological, psychological, cultural nature, but especially of the interpretation of revealed truth. So that, notwithstanding those differences, dialogue may be more easily established, in what follows we wish to bring forth certain things which can and ought to be the foundation and incitement of this dialogue.
¶293
II. De Ecclesiis et Communitatibus ecclesialibus in Occidente seiunctis
II. ON CHURCHES AND ECCLESIAL COMMUNITIES SEPARATED IN THE WEST
Ecclesiae et Communitates ecclesiales, quae vel in gravissimo illo rerum discrimine, quod in Occidente iam ab exeunte medio aevo initium sumpsit, vel posterioribus temporibus ab Apostolica Sede Romana separatae sunt, cum Ecclesia catholica peculiari affinitate ac necessitudine iunguntur ob diuturnam populi christiani vitam praeteritis saeculis in ecclesiastica communione peractam. Cum hae vero Ecclesiae et Communitates ecclesiales propter diversitatem originis, doctrinae et vitae spiritualis non tantum a nobis, sed etiam inter se non parum differant, eas aeque describere opus perdifficile est, quod hic aggredi non intendimus. Quamvis motus oecumenicus et desiderium pacis cum Ecclesia catholica nondum ubique invaluerint, spes nobis est fore ut in omnibus sensus oecumenicus et mutua aestimatio paulatim crescant. Attamen agnoscendum est inter has Ecclesias et Communitates atque Ecclesiam catholicam magni ponderis discrepantias adesse, non tantum indolis historicae, sociologicae, psychologicae, culturalis, sed imprimis interpretationis revelatae veritatis. Quo autem facilius, non obstantibus illis differentiis, dialogus oecumenicus instaurari possit, in sequentibus quaedam efferre volumus quae fundamentum huius dialogi atque incitamentum esse possunt ac debent.
20 Our mind especially regards those Christians who openly confess Jesus Christ as God and Lord and the one mediator between God and men, to the glory of the one God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. We know indeed that there are not light discrepancies from the doctrine of the Catholic Church even concerning Christ the incarnate Word of God and the work of redemption, consequently concerning the mystery and ministry of the Church and the office of Mary in the work of salvation. Yet we rejoice seeing our separated brothers intend Christ as the source and center of ecclesiastical communion. Touched with the desire of union with Christ, they are compelled to seek unity more and more, and also to bear witness to their faith everywhere among the nations.
¶294
Mens nostra eos imprimis Christianos respicit, qui Iesum Christum ut Deum ac Dominum et unicum mediatorem inter Deum et homines palam confitentur ad unius gloriam Dei, Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti. Non leves scimus quidem existere discrepantias a doctrina catholicae Ecclesiae etiam de Christo Verbo Dei incarnato et de opere redemptionis, proinde de mysterio ministerioque Ecclesiae et munere Mariae in opere salutis. Laetamur tamen videntes fratres seiunctos in Christum tamquam fontem et centrum communionis ecclesiasticae intendere. Desiderio tacti unionis cum Christo compelluntur ad unitatem magis magisque quaerendam atque etiam ad testimonium fidei suae ubique apud gentes reddendum.
21 The love and veneration, indeed almost cult, of the sacred Scriptures leads our brothers to constant and skillful study of sacred page: for "the Gospel is the power of God for salvation to every believer, to the Jew first and to the Greek" (Rom 1:16). Invoking the Holy Spirit, they seek God in the sacred Scriptures themselves as speaking to them in Christ, foretold by the prophets, the Word of God incarnate for us. In them they contemplate the life of Christ and those things which the divine Master taught and did for the salvation of men, especially the mysteries of His death and resurrection. But since the Christians separated from us affirm the divine authority of the sacred books, they think differently—indeed diversely—from us concerning the relation between Scripture and the Church, in which according to catholic faith the authentic magisterium holds a special place in expounding and preaching the written word of God. Nevertheless, the sacred Scriptures are excellent instruments in the powerful hand of God for attaining that unity which the Savior offers to all men.
¶295
Sacrarum Scripturarum amor et veneratio ac prope cultus fratres nostros ad constans et sollers Sacrae Paginae studium adducunt: Evangelium virtus enim Dei est in salutem omni credenti, Iudaeo prinum et Graeco (Rm 1,16). Spiritum Sanctum invocantes, in ipsis Sacris Scripturis Deum inquirunt quasi sibi loquentem in Christo, a Prophetis praenuntiato, Verbo Dei pro nobis incarnato. In iis vitam Christi contemplantur et ea quae Divinus Magister ad salutem hominum docuit et peregit, mysteria praesertim mortis eius et resurrectionis. At cum Christiani a nobis seiuncti auctoritatem divinam Sacrorum Librorum affirmant, aliter ac nos — diversi quidem diverse — sentiunt de habitudine Scripturas inter et Ecclesiam, in qua secundum fidem catholicam magisterium authenticum peculiarem obtinet locum in verbo Dei scripto exponendo et praedicando. Nihilominus Sacra Eloquia in ipso dialogo eximia sunt instrumenta in potenti manu Dei ad illam unitatem adipiscendam, quam Salvator omnibus hominibus exhibet.
22 By the sacrament of baptism, whenever it is conferred rightly according to the Lord's institution and received with due disposition of mind, a man is truly incorporated into the crucified and glorified Christ, and is regenerated to the participation of divine life according to the Apostle: "Buried with him in baptism, in which you also rose again by the faith of the operation of God, who raised him from the dead" (Col 2:12). Baptism therefore constitutes a sacramental bond of unity existing among all who are regenerated through it. Nevertheless, baptism of itself is only a beginning and an exordium, since it tends wholly to acquire the fullness of life in Christ. Therefore baptism is ordered to the whole profession of faith, to the whole incorporation into the institution of salvation, as Christ Himself willed it, to the whole insertion into eucharistic communion finally.
¶296
Baptismi sacramento, quandocumque iuxta Domini institutionem rite confertur ac debita animi dispositione accipitur, homo vere Christo crucifixo et glorificato incorporatur atque ad vitae divinae consortium regeneratur iuxta illud Apostoli: consepulti ei in baptismo, in quo et resurrexistis per fidem operationis Dei, qui suscitavit illum a mortuis (Col 2,12). Baptismus igitur vinculum unitatis sacramentale constituit vigens inter omnes qui per illum regenerati sunt. Attamen baptismus per se dumtaxat initium et exordium est, quippe qui totus in acquirendam tendit plenitudinem vitae in Christo. Itaque baptismus ordinatur ad integram fidei professionem, ad integram incorporationem in salutis institutum, prout ipse Christus illud voluit, ad integram denique in communionem eucharisticam insertionem.
The ecclesial Communities separated from us, although the full unity with us flowing from baptism is lacking in them, and although we believe that they, especially on account of the defect of the sacrament of Order, have not preserved the genuine and whole substance of the eucharistic mystery, yet when they make the memory of the Lord's death and resurrection in the Holy Supper, they profess that life in communion with Christ is signified, and await His glorious coming. Therefore the doctrine concerning the Lord's Supper, the other sacraments and cult, and the ministries of the Church ought to become the object of dialogue.
¶297
Communitates ecclesiales a nobis seiunctae, quamvis deficiat earum plena nobiscum unitas ex baptismate profluens, et quamvis credamus illas, praesertim propter sacramenti Ordinis defectum, genuinam atque integram substantiam Mysterii eucharistici non servasse, tamen, dum in Sancta Coena mortis et resurrectionis Domini memoriam faciunt, vitam in Christi communione significari profitentur atque gloriosum Eius adventum exspectant. Quapropter doctrina circa Coenam Domini, cetera sacramenta et cultum ac Ecclesiae ministeria obiectum dialogi constituat oportet.
23 The Christian life of these brothers is nourished by faith in Christ, fostered by the grace of baptism and the hearing of the word of God. It is manifested indeed in private prayer, in biblical meditation, in the life of the Christian family, in the cult of the community gathered for the praise of God. Moreover, their cult sometimes bears conspicuous elements of the common ancient liturgy. The faith by which Christ is believed produces fruit in praise and thanksgiving for benefits divinely received; there is added a living sense of justice and sincere charity toward neighbor. This active faith has also produced not a few institutions for relieving spiritual and corporal misery, for cultivating the education of youth, for making social conditions of life more humane, for universally establishing peace.
¶298
Christiana horum fratrum conversatio fide in Christum alitur, baptismatis gratia et verbo Dei audito fovetur. Manifestatur quidem in oratione privata, in meditatione biblica, in familiae christianae vita, in cultu communitatis ad laudem Dei congregatae. Ceteroquin cultus eorum nonnumquam elementa conspicua communis antiquae liturgiae prae se fert. Fides qua Christo creditur fructus edit in laude et gratiarum actione pro beneficiis divinitus acceptis; accedit vivus iustitiae sensus et sincera caritas in proximum. Haec autem operosa fides haud pauca etiam instituta ad miseriam spiritualem et corporalem sublevandam, ad iuventutis educationem excolendam, ad sociales vitae condiciones humaniores reddendas, ad pacem universim constabiliendam protulit.
But if among Christians many do not always understand the Gospel in the same way as Catholics in moral matters, nor admit the same solutions of the more difficult questions of modern society, nevertheless they wish, like us, to adhere to the word of Christ as the source of Christian virtue, and to obey the apostolic precept: "All whatsoever you do in word or in work, all things in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, giving thanks to God and the Father through him" (Col 3:17). Hence ecumenical dialogue concerning the moral application of the Gospel can begin from this.
¶299
Quod si inter Christianos multi non semper eadem ratione atque Catholici Evangelium in re morali intelligunt neque easdem solutiones difficiliorum hodiernae societatis quaestionum admittunt, nihilominus ut nos volunt verbo Christi ut fonti christianae virtutis haerere et apostolico oboedire praecepto: Omne, quodcumque facitis in verbo aut in opere, omnia in nomine Domini Iesu Christi, gratias agentes Deo et Patri per ipsum (Col 3,1 Col 3,7). Hinc dialogus oecumenicus de morali Evangelii applicatione initium sumere potest.
24 Thus, having briefly set forth the conditions in which it happens that ecumenical action is exercised, and the principles by which it is to be moderated, we confidently turn our eyes to the future. This sacred Synod exhorts the faithful that they abstain from any levity or imprudent zeal, which can harm the progress of unity. For their ecumenical action cannot be other than fully and sincerely catholic, namely faithful to the truth which we have received from the Apostles and the Fathers, and consonant with the faith which the Catholic Church has always professed, and at the same time tending toward that fullness in which the Lord wishes His body to be increased in the course of times.
¶300
Sic post breviter expositas condiciones, quibus exerceri contingit actio oecumenica, et principia quibus moderanda est, fidenter oculos ad futura convertimus. Haec Sacrosancta Synodus hortatur fideles, ut a quavis levitate vel imprudenti zelo se abstineant, quae vero progressui unitatis nocere possint. Eorum enim oecumenica actio non potest esse nisi plene sincereque catholica, fidelis nempe veritati, quam ab Apostolis et Patribus accepimus, et consentanea fidei, quam Ecclesia catholica semper professa est, ac simul in eam plenitudinem tendens, qua Dominus decursu temporum, Corpus Suum vult augeatur.
25 This sacred Synod earnestly desires that the undertakings of the sons of the Catholic Church, joined with the undertakings of our separated brothers, may progress, without any obstacle being placed in the ways of Providence and without prejudice to future impulses of the Holy Spirit. Moreover, it declares itself conscious that this holy purpose of reconciling all Christians in the unity of the one and only Church of Christ exceeds human forces and gifts. Wherefore it places its hope completely in the prayer of Christ for the Church, in the love of the Father for us, in the power of the Holy Spirit. But hope does not confound, because the charity of God is poured forth in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who is given to us (Rom 5:5).
¶301
Haec Sacrosancta Synodus instanter exoptat ut filiorum Ecclesiae catholicae incepta cum inceptis fratrum seiunctorum coniuncta progrediantur, quin Providentiae viis ullum ponatur obstaculum et quin futuris Spiritus Sancti impulsionibus praeiudicetur. Insuper se consciam esse declarat hoc sanctum propositum reconciliandi Christianos omnes in unitate unius unicaeque Ecclesiae Christi humanas vires et dotes excedere. Quapropter spem suam in oratione Christi pro Ecclesia, in amore Patris erga nos, in virtute Spiritus Sancti penitus ponit. Spes autem non confundit, quia caritas Dei diffusa est in cordibus nostris per Spiritum Sanctum, qui datus est nobis (Rm 5,5).
1 The Catholic Church holds in high esteem the institutions, liturgical rites, ecclesiastical traditions, and the discipline of Christian life of the Eastern Churches. For in them, as they are distinguished by venerable antiquity, shines that tradition which has been handed down from the Apostles through the Fathers, and which constitutes part of the divinely revealed and undivided patrimony of the universal Church. Therefore, having care for the Eastern Churches which are living witnesses of this tradition, this holy and ecumenical Synod, wishing that they may flourish and carry out with new apostolic strength the office entrusted to them, besides those things which pertain to the universal Church, has decided to decree certain points, the rest being remitted to the providence of the Eastern synods and the Apostolic See.
¶302
ORIENTALIUM ECCLESIARUM
DECRETUM DE ECCLESIIS ORIENTALIBUS CATHOLICIS
DECREE ON THE EASTERN CATHOLIC CHURCHES
ORIENTALIUM ECCLESIARUM instituta, ritus liturgicos, traditiones ecclesiasticas atque vitae christianae disciplinam Ecclesia catholica magni facit. In eis enim, utpote veneranda antiquitate praeclaris, elucet ea quae ab Apostolis per Patres est traditio, quaeque partem constituit divinitus revelati atque indivisi universae Ecclesiae patrimonii. Ecclesiarum igitur Orientalium, quae huius traditionis testes sunt vivi, sollicitudinem gerens, haec Sancta et Oecumenica Synodus cupiens ut eaedem floreant et novo robore apostolico concreditum sibi munus absolvant, praeter ea quae ad universam spectant Ecclesiam, capita quaedam statuere decrevit, ceteris ad providentiam Synodorum orientalium nec non Sedis Apostolicae remissis.
2 The holy and catholic Church, which is the mystical body of Christ, consists of the faithful, who are organically united in the Holy Spirit by the same faith, the same sacraments, and the same governance, and who, coalescing into various groups joined hierarchically, constitute particular churches or rites. Among them there flourishes a wonderful communion, so that variety in the Church not only does not harm its unity, but rather declares it; for the Catholic Church has this purpose, that the traditions of each particular church or rite remain whole and intact, and at the same time it wishes to adapt its own manner of life to the various needs of times and places.
¶303
DE ECCLESIIS PARTICULARIBUS SEU RITIBUS
ON PARTICULAR CHURCHES OR RITES
Sancta et catholica Ecclesia, quae est corpus Christi Mysticum, constat ex fidelibus, qui eadem fide, iisdem sacramentis et eodem regimine in Spiritu Sancto organice uniuntur, quique in varios coetus hierarchia iunctos coalescentes, particulares Ecclesias seu ritus constituunt. Inter eas mirabilis viget communio, ita ut varietas in Ecclesia nedum eiusdem noceat unitati, eam potius declaret; Ecclesiae enim catholicae hoc propositum est, ut salvae et integrae maneant uniuscuiusque particularis Ecclesiae seu ritus traditiones, eademque pariter vult suam vitae rationem aptare variis temporum locorumque necessitatibus.
3 Such particular churches, both of the East and of the West, although they differ in part among themselves in rites, namely liturgy, ecclesiastical discipline, and spiritual patrimony, are nevertheless equally entrusted to the pastoral governance of the Roman Pontiff, who by divine succession succeeds Blessed Peter in primacy over the universal Church. Therefore they enjoy equal dignity, so that none of them excels the others by reason of rite, and they enjoy the same rights and are bound by the same obligations, even concerning the preaching of the Gospel to the whole world (cf. Mk 16:15), under the direction of the Roman Pontiff.
¶304
Huiusmodi particulares Ecclesiae, tum Orientis tum Occidentis, licet ritibus, ut aiunt, nempe liturgia, ecclesiastica disciplina et patrimonio spirituali partim inter se differant, aequali tamen modo concreduntur pastorali gubernio Romani Pontificis, qui Beato Petro in primatu super universam Ecclesiam divinitus succedit. Eaedem proinde pari pollent dignitate, ita ut nulla earum ceteris praestet ratione ritus, atque iisdem fruuntur iuribus et tenentur obligationibus, etiam quod attinet ad Evangelium praedicandum in universum mundum (cf. Mc 16,15), sub moderamine Romani Pontificis.
4 Provision is therefore to be made everywhere for the protection and increase of all particular churches, and for this purpose parishes and a proper hierarchy are to be constituted, where the spiritual good of the faithful demands it. However, the hierarchs of various particular churches obtaining jurisdiction in the same territory, let them foster unity of action with counsel exchanged in periodic meetings, and, with united forces, help common works for expediting the good of religion and more effectively protecting the discipline of the clergy. Let all clerics and those ascending to sacred orders be well instructed concerning rites and especially the practical norms in interritual matters, indeed also let laity in catechetical instruction be taught concerning rites and their norms. Finally, all and singular Catholics, and also the baptized of any acatholic Church or community coming to the fullness of catholic communion, retain their own rite everywhere, and cultivate it and observe it according to their strength; saving the right of recourse to the Apostolic See in particular cases of persons, communities, or regions, which, as the supreme arbiter of interecclesial relations, will provide for necessities in an ecumenical spirit, either itself or through other authorities, giving opportune norms, decrees, or rescripts.
¶305
Provideatur igitur ubique terrarum tuitioni atque incremento omnium Ecclesiarum particularium ac propterea constituantur paroeciae atque propria hierarchia, ubi id postulat bonum spirituale fidelium. Hierarchae vero variarum Ecclesiarum particularium in eodem territorio iurisdictionem obtinentes, curent, collatis consiliis in periodicis conventibus, unitatem actionis fovere, et, viribus unitis, communia adiuvare opera, ad bonum religionis expedite promovendum et cleri disciplinam efficacius tuendam. Clerici omnes et ascendentes ad ordines sacros bene instruantur de ritibus et praesertim de normis practicis in materiis interritualibus, imo et laici in institutione catechetica de ritibus eorumque normis doceantur. Omnes denique et singuli catholici, necnon baptizati cuiusvis Ecclesiae vel communitatis acatholicae ad plenitudinem communionis catholicae convenientes, proprium ubique terrarum retineant ritum eumque colant et pro viribus observent; salvo iure recurrendi ad Sedem Apostolicam in casibus peculiaribus personarum, communitatum, vel regionum, quae, uti suprema relationum interecclesialium arbitra, providebit necessitatibus in spiritu oecumenico, ipsa vel per alias auctoritates, datis opportunis normis, decretis vel rescriptis.
5 History, traditions, and very many ecclesiastical institutions splendidly testify how greatly the Eastern Churches have deserved of the universal Church. Therefore the holy Synod not only pursues this ecclesiastical and spiritual patrimony with due esteem and just praise, but also firmly considers it as the patrimony of the whole Church of Christ. Wherefore it solemnly declares that the Churches of the East, as well as the West, have the right and the duty to govern themselves according to their own particular disciplines, as being commended by venerable antiquity, more congruent to the morals of their faithful, and seen as more suitable for providing for the good of souls.
¶306
DE SPIRITUALI ECCLESIARUM ORIENTALIUM PATRIMONIO SERVANDO
ON PRESERVING THE SPIRITUAL PATRIMONY OF THE EASTERN CHURCHES
Historia, traditiones et plurima ecclesiastica instituta praeclare testantur quantopere de universa Ecclesia Orientales Ecclesiae meritae sint. Quapropter Sancta Synodus patrimonium hoc ecclesiasticum et spirituale non solum aestimatione debita et iusta laude prosequitur, sed etiam tamquam patrimonium universae Christi Ecclesiae firmiter considerat. Quamobrem sollemniter declarat, Ecclesias Orientis sicut et Occidentis iure pollere et officio teneri se secundum proprias disciplinas peculiares regendi, utpote quae veneranda antiquitate commendentur, moribus suorum fidelium magis sint congruae atque ad bonum animarum consulendum aptiores videantur.
6 Let all Easterns know and have for certain that they can and ought always to preserve their own legitimate liturgical rites and their own discipline, and that changes are to be introduced only by reason of their own organic progress. All these things, therefore, are to be observed with the greatest fidelity by the Easterns themselves; who indeed must acquire a greater knowledge and more perfect use of these things day by day, and if they have unduly fallen away from them on account of circumstances of times or persons, they should strive to return to their ancestral traditions. But those who, by reason of office or apostolic ministry, have frequent intercourse with the Eastern Churches or with their faithful, are to be accurately instructed in the knowledge and cult of the rites, discipline, doctrine, history, and character of the Easterns, according to the gravity of the office which they hold. It is also strongly recommended to religions and associations of the Latin rite, which work in Eastern regions or among Eastern faithful, that for greater efficacy of apostolate they constitute, as far as possible, houses or even provinces of the Eastern rite.
¶307
Sciant ac pro certo habeant omnes Orientales, se suos legitimos ritus liturgicos suamque disciplinam semper servare posse et debere, ac nonnisi ratione proprii et organici progressus mutationes inducendas esse. Haec omnia, igitur, maxima fidelitate ab ipsis Orientalibus observanda sunt; qui quidem harum rerum cognitionem in dies maiorem usumque perfectiorem acquirere debent, et, si ab iis ob temporum vel personarum adiuncta indebite defecerint, ad avitas traditiones redire satagant. Illi vero qui ratione sive muneris, sive apostolici ministerii frequens cum Orientalibus Ecclesiis aut cum earum fidelibus habeant commercium, in cognitione et cultu rituum, disciplinae, doctrinae, historiae atque indolis Orientalium accurate, pro gravitate officii quod gerunt, instituantur. Religionibus vero et associationibus latini ritus, quae in regionibus orientalibus vel inter fideles orientales operam praestant, enixe commendatur, ut, ad maiorem apostolatus efficaciam, domos aut etiam provincias orientalis ritus, quantum fieri potest, constituant.
7 From the most ancient times the patriarchal institution has flourished in the Church, already recognized by the first ecumenical councils. By the name of Eastern patriarch comes a bishop, to whom belongs jurisdiction over all bishops, not excepting metropolitans, the clergy, and the people of his own territory or rite, according to the norm of law and saving the primacy of the Roman Pontiff. Wherever a hierarch of some rite is constituted outside the boundaries of the patriarchal territory, he remains aggregated to the hierarchy of the patriarchate of the same rite according to the norm of law.
¶308
DE PATRIARCHIS ORIENTALIBUS
ON EASTERN PATRIARCHS
Ab antiquissimis temporibus in Ecclesia viget institutio patriarchalis, iam a primis Synodis Oecumenicis agnita. Nomine vero Patriarchae orientalis venit episcopus, cui competit iurisdictio in omnes episcopos, haud exceptis metropolitis, clerum et populum proprii territorii vel ritus, ad normam iuris et salvo primatu Romani Pontificis. Ubicumque Hierarcha alicuius ritus extra fines territorii patriarchalis constituitur, manet aggregatus hierarchiae patriarchatus eiusdem ritus ad normam iuris.
8 Patriarchs of the Eastern Churches, although later in time one than another, are nevertheless all equal by reason of patriarchal dignity, saving the precedence of honor legitimately established among them.
¶309
Patriarchae Ecclesiarum Orientalium, licet alii aliis tempore posteriores, omnes tamen aequales sunt ratione dignitatis patriarchalis, salva inter eos praecedentia honoris legitime statuta.
9 According to the most ancient tradition of the Church, the patriarchs of the Eastern Churches are to be pursued with singular honor, since each presides over his own patriarchate as father and head. Therefore this holy Synod decrees that their rights and privileges be restored, according to the ancient traditions of each Church and the decrees of the ecumenical councils. These rights and privileges, however, are those which flourished at the time of the union of East and West, even if they are to be somewhat adapted to modern conditions. Patriarchs with their synods constitute the superior instance for any affairs of the patriarchate, not excluding the right of constituting new eparchies and of naming bishops of their own rite within the boundaries of the patriarchal territory, saving the inalienable right of the Roman Pontiff to intervene in individual cases.
¶310
Secundum antiquissimam Ecclesiae traditionem, singulari honore prosequendi sunt Ecclesiarum Orientalium patriarchae, quippe qui suo quisque patriarchatui tamquam pater et caput praesint. Ideo statuit haec Sancta Synodus, ut eorum iura atque privilegia instaurentur, iuxta antiquas traditiones uniuscuiusque Ecclesiae et Synodorum Oecumenicarum decreta. Haec autem iura et privilegia sunt illa, quae tempore unionis Orientis et Occidentis viguerunt, etsi ad hodiernas conditiones aliquantum aptanda sint. Patriarchae cum suis synodis superiorem constituunt instantiam pro quibusvis negotiis patriarchatus, non secluso iure constituendi novas eparchias atque nominandi episcopos sui ritus intra fines territorii patriarchalis, salvo inalienabili Romani Pontificis iure in singulis casibus interveniendi.
10 What has been said concerning patriarchs also holds good, according to the norm of law, for major archbishops, who preside over a certain whole particular church or rite.
¶311
Quae de Patriarchis sunt dicta, valent etiam, ad normam iuris, de Archiepiscopis maioribus, qui universae cuidam Ecclesiae particulari seu ritui praesunt.
11 Since the patriarchal institution in the Eastern Churches is a traditional form of governance, the holy ecumenical Synod desires that, where necessary, new patriarchates be erected, the constitution of which is reserved to the ecumenical Synod or the Roman Pontiff.
¶312
Cum institutum patriarchale in Ecclesiis Orientalibus sit forma regiminis traditionalis, Sancta et Oecumenica Synodus exoptat ut, ubi opus sit, novi erigantur patriarchatus, quorum constitutio Synodo Oecumenicae vel Romano Pontifici reservatur.
12 The holy ecumenical Synod confirms and praises the ancient discipline concerning the sacraments existing among the Eastern Churches, and likewise the practice which pertains to their celebration and administration, and, if the case requires, desires that the same be restored.
¶313
DE DISCIPLINA SACRAMENTORUM
ON THE DISCIPLINE OF THE SACRAMENTS
Veterem disciplinam de sacramentis apud Orientales Ecclesias vigentem, itemque praxim quae ad eorum celebrationem et ministrationem spectat, Sancta Oecumenica Synodus confirmat et laudat, et, si casus ferat, exoptat ut eadem instauretur.
13 The discipline concerning the minister of holy chrism existing among the Easterns from the most ancient times is fully to be restored. Therefore, presbyters can confer this sacrament, using chrism blessed by a patriarch or bishop.
¶314
Disciplina de ministro S. Chrismatis inde ab antiquissimis temporibus apud Orientales vigens plene instauretur. Idcirco presbyteri hoc sacramentum conferre valent, adhibito Chrismate a patriarcha vel episcopo benedicto.
14 All Eastern presbyters can validly confer this sacrament, whether together with baptism or separately, to all the faithful of any rite, not excluding the Latin, the prescriptions of both common and particular law as to liceity being observed. Latin rite presbyrators also, according to the faculties which they enjoy concerning the administration of this sacrament, can validly administer it also to the faithful of the Eastern Churches, without prejudice to the rite, observing as to liceity the prescriptions of law, whether common or particular.
¶315
Presbyteri omnes orientales hoc sacramentum, sive una cum Baptismo sive separatim, valide conferre possunt omnibus fidelibus cuiusvis ritus, latino haud excluso, servatis ad liceitatem praescriptis iuris tum communis tum particularis. Presbyteri quoque latini ritus, secundum facultates quibus gaudent circa ministrationem huius sacramenti, valent illud etiam fidelibus Ecclesiarum Orientalium ministrare, sine praeiudicio ritui, servatis quoad liceitatem praescriptis iuris sive communis sive particularis.
15 The faithful are bound to attend divine liturgy on Sundays and feast days, or, according to the prescription or custom of their own rite, the celebration of divine praises. So that the faithful may more easily be able to fulfill this obligation, it is decreed that the useful time for fulfilling this precept runs from the vespers of the vigil to the end of the Sunday or feast day. It is strongly recommended to the faithful that they receive the holy Eucharist on these days, indeed more frequently and even daily.
¶316
Fideles obligatione tenentur diebus dominicis et festis interesse divinae liturgiae aut, iuxta praescripta vel consuetudinem proprii ritus, celebrationi divinarum laudum. Quo facilius fideles hanc obligationem adimplere valeant, statuitur tempus utile, pro hoc praecepto adimplendo, decurrere inde a vesperis vigiliae usque ad finem diei Dominicae vel festi. Enixe commendatur fidelibus, ut his diebus, imo frequentius ac vel etiam quotidie, Sacram Eucharistiam suscipiant.
16 On account of the daily mixture of faithful of different particular churches in the same region or Eastern territory, the faculty of priests of any rite to hear confessions, duly and without any restriction granted by their own hierarchs, is extended to the whole territory of the conceding and to the places and faithful of any rite in the same territory, unless the hierarch of the place, as to the places of his own rite, has expressly refused.
¶317
Ob quotidianam permixtionem fidelium diversarum particularium Ecclesiarum in eadem regione vel territorio orientali, facultas presbyterorum cuiuscumque ritus excipiendi confessiones, a propriis hierarchis rite et sine ulla restrictione concessa, ad totum territorium extenditur concedentis necnon ad loca et fideles cuiuscumque ritus in eodem territorio, nisi hierarcha loci, quoad loca sui ritus, expresse renuerit.
17 So that the ancient discipline of the sacrament of Order in the Eastern Churches may flourish again, this holy Synod desires that the institution of the permanent diaconate, where it has fallen into disuse, be restored. As to the subdiaconate and the lower orders and their rights and obligations, let the legislative authority of each particular church provide.
¶318
Ut antiqua sacramenti Ordinis disciplina in Ecclesiis Orientalibus iterum vigeat, exoptat haec Sancta Synodus, ut institutum diaconatus permanentis ubi in desuetudinem venerit, instauretur. Quoad subdiaconatum vero et Ordines inferiores eorumque iura et obligationes, provideat Auctoritas legislativa uniuscuiusque Ecclesiae particularis.
18 To prevent invalid marriages when Eastern Catholics marry with baptized Eastern acatholics, and to consult the firmness and sanctity of marriage and domestic peace, the holy Synod decrees that the canonical form of celebration obliges for liceity only for these marriages; for validity, the presence of a sacred minister suffices, other things to be observed according to law.
¶319
Ad praecavenda matrimonia invalida, quando catholici orientales cum acatholicis orientalibus baptizatis matrimonium ineunt, et ad consulendum nuptiarum firmitati et sanctitati nec non domesticae paci, Sancta Synodus statuit formam canonicam celebrationis pro his matrimoniis obligare tantum ad liceitatem; ad validitatem sufficere praesentiam ministri sacri, servatis aliis de iure servandis.
19 The establishing, transferring, or suppressing of feast days common to all Eastern Churches pertains in the future only to the ecumenical Synod or the Apostolic See. However, establishing, transferring, or suppressing feast days for individual particular churches, besides the Apostolic See, pertains to the patriarchal or archiepiscopal synods, due regard being had to the whole region and the other particular churches.
¶320
DE CULTU DIVINO
ON DIVINE WORSHIP
Dies festos pro omnibus Ecclesiis Orientalibus communes in posterum constituere, transferre aut supprimere unius est Synodi Oecumenicae vel Sedis Apostolicae. Dies vero festos pro singulis Ecclesiis particularibus constituere, transferre aut supprimere, praeter Apostolicam Sedem, competit Synodis patriarchalibus aut archiepiscopalibus, debita tamen ratione habita totius regionis ceterarumque Ecclesiarum particularium.
20 Until the desired agreement among all Christians concerning the one day on which the feast of Easter is to be celebrated by all is attained, in the meantime for fostering unity among Christians living in the same region or nation, it is committed to the Patriarchs or the Supreme Ecclesiastical Authorities in the place, that, with unanimous consent and counsel exchanged with those whose interest it is, they agree concerning the feast of Easter to be celebrated on the same Sunday.
¶321
Donec perveniatur ad optatam inter omnes christianos conventionem de unico die, quo festivitas Paschatis ab omnibus celebretur, interim ad unitatem inter christianos in eadem regione vel natione degentes fovendam, Patriarchis vel Supremis in loco Auctoritatibus ecclesiasticis committitur, ut, unanimi consensu et collatis consiliis cum iis quorum interest, de festo Paschatis eadem die Dominica celebrando conveniant.
21 Individual faithful staying outside the region or territory of their own rite can fully conform themselves, as to the law of sacred times, to the discipline prevailing in the place where they live. In families of mixed rite, it is lawful to observe this law according to one and the same rite.
¶322
Singuli fideles extra regionem vel territorium proprii ritus versantes, quoad legem de temporibus sacris, ad disciplinam in loco ubi degunt vigentem se plene conformare possunt. In familiis mixti ritus hanc legem servare licet iuxta unum eundemque ritum.
22 Eastern clerics and religious should celebrate the Divine Praises according to the prescriptions of their own discipline and traditions, which from ancient age have been in great honor among all Eastern Churches. The faithful also, following the example of their fathers, should apply themselves to the Divine Praises according to their strength and with devotion.
¶323
Clerici et religiosi orientales celebrent iuxta propriae disciplinae praescripta et traditiones Laudes Divinas, quae inde ab antiqua aetate magno in honore fuerunt apud omnes Ecclesias Orientales. Fideles quoque, exempla maiorum secuti, in Divinas Laudes pro viribus et devote incumbant.
23 The right of moderating the use of languages in sacred liturgical actions pertains to the Patriarch with the Synod or to the Supreme Authority of each Church with the Council of hierarchs, and also, having made relation to the Apostolic See, of approving translations of texts into the vernacular language.
¶324
Ad Patriarcham cum Synodo vel ad Supremam cuiusque Ecclesiae Auctoritatem cum Consilio hierarcharum, ius pertinet moderandi usum linguarum in sacris actionibus liturgicis nec non, facta relatione ad Sedem Apostolicam, probandi versiones textuum in linguam vernaculam.
24 It pertains to the Eastern Churches in communion with the Roman Apostolic See a particular office of fostering the unity of all Christians, especially of the Easterns, according to the principles of the decree of this holy Synod on Ecumenism, first of all by prayer, by the example of life, by religious fidelity toward the ancient Eastern traditions, by mutual and better knowledge, by collaboration, and by fraternal estimation of matters and spirits.
¶325
DE CONVERSATIONE CUM FRATRIBUS ECCLESIARUM SEIUNCTARUM
ON CONVERSATION WITH BROTHERS OF THE SEPARATED CHURCHES
Ad Ecclesias Orientales, communionem Cum Sede Apostolica Romana habentes, peculiare pertinet munus omnium christianorum unitatem, orientalium praesertim, fovendi, iuxta principia decreti huius S. Synodi De Oecumenismo, precibus imprimis, vitae exemplis, religiosa erga antiquas traditiones orientales fidelitate, mutua et meliore cognitione, collaboratione ac fraterna rerum animorumque aestimatione.
25 From Easterns separated, coming into catholic unity under the influx of the grace of the Holy Spirit, let nothing more be required than what the simple profession of catholic faith demands. And since among them the priesthood has been preserved valid, to Eastern clerics coming into catholic unity the faculty is to exercise their own Order, according to the norms established by competent Authority.
¶326
Ab Orientalibus seiunctis, in unitatem catholicam sub influxu gratiae Sancti Spiritus convenientibus, ne plus exigatur quam quod simplex fidei catholicae professio exigit. Et cum apud eos sacerdotium validum servatum sit, clericis orientalibus, in unitatem catholicam convenientibus, facultas est proprium Ordinem exercendi, iuxta normas a competenti Auctoritate statutas.
26 Communication in sacred things, which offends the unity of the Church or includes formal adherence to error or peril of deviation in the end, scandal and indifferentism, is prohibited by divine law. But pastoral practice demonstrates that, as to the Eastern brothers, various circumstances of individual persons can be considered and ought to be, in which neither the unity of the Church is harmed, nor the perils to be avoided are present, but the necessity of salvation and the spiritual good of souls urge. Therefore the Catholic Church, according to the circumstances of times, places, and persons, has often used and uses a milder manner of acting, offering to all the means of salvation and the testimony of charity among Christians, through participation in the sacraments and other functions and sacred things. These things having been attended to, the holy Synod, lest we be an impediment by the severity of judgment to those who are saved and to more foster the union with the Eastern Churches separated from us, decrees the following manner of acting.
¶327
Communicatio in sacris, quae unitatem Ecclesiae offendit aut formalem errori adhaesionem vel periculum aberrationis in fine, scandali et indifferentismi includit, lege divina prohibetur. Praxis vero pastoralis demonstrat, ad fratres orientales quod spectat, varia considerari posse et debere singularum personarum adiuncta, in quibus nec unitas Ecclesiae laeditur, nec pericula vitanda adsunt, sed necessitas salutis et bonum spirituale animarum urgent. Ideo Ecclesia catholica, pro temporum, locorum et personarum adiunctis, mitiorem saepe adhibuit et adhibet rationem agendi, salutis media et testimonium caritatis inter christianos omnibus praebens, per participationem in sacramentis aliisque in functionibus et rebus sacris. His attentis, Sancta Synodus, ne impedimento propter sententiae severitatem simus iis qui salvantur et ad magis fovendam unionem cum Ecclesiis Orientalibus a nobis seiunctis, sequentem agendi rationem statuit.
27 The principles being set down, to Easterns who are found in good faith separated from the Catholic Church, if they spontaneously ask and are rightly disposed, the sacraments of Penance, Eucharist, and Anointing of the Sick can be conferred; indeed, even to Catholics it is lawful to ask the same sacraments from acatholic ministers, in whose Church valid sacraments are had, as often as necessity or true spiritual utility persuades, and access to a catholic priest becomes physically or morally impossible.
¶328
Positis memoratis principiis, Orientalibus, qui bona fide seiuncti inveniuntur ab Ecclesia catholica, si sponte petant et rite sint dispositi, sacramenta Poenitentiae, Eucharistiae et Unctionis Infirmorum conferri possunt; imo, etiam catholicis eadem sacramenta licet petere ab iis ministris acatholicis, in quorum Ecclesia habentur valida sacramenta, quotiescumque id necessitas aut vera spiritualis utilitas suadeat, et accessus ad sacerdotem catholicum physice vel moraliter impossibilis evadat.
28 Likewise, the same principles being set down, communication in sacred functions, things, and places between Catholics and separated Eastern brothers is permitted for a just cause.
¶329
Item, positis iisdem principiis, communicatio in sacris functionibus, rebus et locis inter catholicos et fratres seiunctos orientales iusta de causa permittitur.
29 This milder manner of communication in sacred things with brothers of the separated Eastern Churches is committed to the vigilance and moderation of the local hierarchs, that, counsel exchanged among themselves, and, if the case requires, the hierarchs of the separated Churches also having been heard, they may moderate the conversation of Christians with opportune and effective precepts and norms.
¶330
Haec mitior communicationis in sacris cum fratribus Ecclesiarum Orientalium seiunctarum ratio vigilantiae et moderamini hierarcharum locorum committitur, ut, collatis inter se consiliis, et, si casus ferat, auditis etiam hierarchis Ecclesiarum seiunctarum, opportunis efficacibusque praeceptis et normis christianorum moderentur conversationem.
30 The holy Synod greatly rejoices over the fruitful and active collaboration of the Eastern and Western Catholic Churches, and at the same time declares: all these dispositions of law are established for the present conditions, until the Catholic Church and the separated Eastern Churches come to the fullness of communion. In the meantime, however, all Christians, Eastern as well as Western, are earnestly asked that they pour forth fervent and assiduous, indeed daily, prayers to God that, the most holy Mother of God helping, all may become one. Let them also pray that the fullness of the Paraclete Spirit of strengthening and solace may flow to so many Christians of every Church, who, strenuously professing the name of Christ, suffer and are in anguish. Let us all love one another with the charity of fraternity, preventing one another with honor.
¶331
Magnopere laetatur Sancta Synodus de fructuosa et actuosa collaboratione Orientalium et Occidentalium Ecclesiarum Catholicarum, simulque declarat: hae omnes iuris dispositiones pro praesentibus conditionibus statuuntur, usquedum Ecclesia catholica et Ecclesiae Orientales seiunctae ad plenitudinem communionis convenient. Interim tamen omnes christiani, Orientales necnon Occidentales, enixe rogantur, ut ferventes atque assiduas, imo quotidianas preces Deo fundant ut, Sanctissima Deipara auxiliante, omnes unum fiant. Orent quoque, ut tot christianis cuiuscumque Ecclesiae, qui, strenue profitentes Christi nomen, patiuntur et angustiantur, Spiritus Sancti Paracliti adfluat plenitudo confortationis et solatii. Omnes caritate fraternitatis invicem diligamus, honore invicem praevenientes.
1 Hearing the word of God religiously and proclaiming it confidently, the sacred Synod follows the words of St. John saying: "We announce to you the eternal life, which was with the Father and appeared to us: what we have seen and heard we announce to you, that you also may have fellowship with us, and our fellowship may be with the Father and with His Son Jesus Christ" (1 Jn 1:2-3). Therefore, following in the footsteps of the Councils of Trent and Vatican I, it intends to set forth the genuine doctrine concerning divine revelation and its transmission, that the whole world may, by hearing, believe, by believing, hope, by hoping, love.
