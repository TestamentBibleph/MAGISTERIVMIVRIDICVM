Part 1 of 6: Concilium Oecumenicum Vaticanum II Sollemniter Inchoatur — to — Venerable Brothers,

Editorial note

Correctly placed in Section B — the council produced no new anathemas and described itself explicitly as pastoral in intent. However, the four constitutions must be differentiated by dogmatic weight within the chapter:

Constitutions with genuine dogmatic content:

  • Dei Verbum — on divine revelation, Scripture, and Tradition. Its treatment of the relationship between Scripture and Tradition develops and refines (without contradicting) Trent's position. LG 25 on the ordinary universal magisterium as a source of infallible teaching is a significant doctrinal development
  • Lumen Gentium — on the Church. LG 22–23 on episcopal collegiality in communion with Rome, and LG 25 on magisterial authority, carry substantive doctrinal content. LG 8's subsistit in language on the Church of Christ and the Catholic Church requires explicit treatment — it is the most contested doctrinal formulation of the council

Constitutions primarily pastoral:

  • Gaudium et Spes — pastoral constitution on the Church in the modern world. Doctrinally significant but not magisterially binding in the same mode
  • Sacrosanctum Concilium — liturgical constitution. Disciplinary and pastoral rather than dogmatic

Treating all four constitutions uniformly as "pastoral but doctrinally significant" flattens a distinction the chapter must preserve to be credible with a serious reader.

CONCILIUM OECUMENICUM VATICANUM II SOLLEMNITER INCHOATUR
THE SECOND VATICAN ECUMENICAL COUNCIL IS SOLEMNLY BEGUN
ALLOCUTIO IOANNIS PP. XXIII IN SOLLEMNI SS. CONCILII INAUGURATIONE
Die 11 octobris mensis a. 1962
ALLOCUTION OF POPE JOHN XXIII AT THE SOLEMN INAUGURATION OF THE SECOND VATICAN COUNCIL
On the 11th day of the month of October, 1962
- Mother Church rejoices that, by the singular gift of Divine Providence, the longed-for day has at last dawned when, under the auspices of the Virgin Mother of God, whose maternal dignity is commemorated today in festal rite, here at the tomb of blessed Peter the Second Vatican Ecumenical Council solemnly begins.
¶1
Venerable Brothers,
Venerabiles Fratres,
Gaudet Mater Ecclesia quod, singulari Divinae Providentiae munere, optatissimus iam dies illuxit, quo, auspice Deipara Virgine, cuius materna dignitas hodie festo ritu recolitur, hic ad Beati Petri sepulcrum Concilium Oecumenicum Vaticanum Secundum sollemniter initium capit.
¶2
Ecumenical Councils in the Church
Universa Concilia — sive viginti Oecumenica sive innumera eaque non parvi pendenda Provincialia et Regionalia — quae per succedentia tempora sunt celebrata, manifesto vigorem Ecclesiae Catholicae comprobant et quasi fulgentia lumina in eius annalibus recensentur.
The most recent and humble successor of the same Prince of the Apostles, who is addressing you, by indicting this most ample assembly, proposed this to himself: that the ecclesiastical Magisterium, never failing and persevering until the end of time, might be affirmed again; which Magisterium, having regard for the errors, necessities, and opportunities of our age, is exhibited in an extraordinary way in the present through this council itself to all men, as many as are in the whole world.
¶3
Recentiorem humilisque eiusdem Principis Apostolorum Successor, qui vos alloquitur, amplissimum hunc Coetum indicens, id sibi proposuit, ut iterum Magisterium Ecclesiasticum, numquam deficiens et ad finem usque temporum perseverans, affirmaretur; quod quidem Magisterium rationem habens errorum, necessitatum, rerum opportunarum nostrae aetatis, per hoc ipsum Concilium omnibus hominibus, quotquot in orbe terrarum sunt, extraordinario modo, in praesenti exhibetur.
Therefore, beginning this universal Synod, the Vicar of Christ, who speaks to you, as is clear, looks back upon past times and listens, as it were, to their voice, alert and confirming the spirit: for he gladly remembers the well-deserving Supreme Pontiffs who were in the most ancient and recent age, and who from the assemblies of councils, whether held in the East or in the West from the fourth century to the Middle Ages and recent times, transmitted testimonies of such venerable and weighty voice. These, with unceasing study, extol the triumph of that divine and human Society, namely the Church of Christ, which receives its name, the gifts of grace, and its whole strength from the Divine Redeemer.
¶4
Hanc igitur universalem incohans Synodum, Christi Vicarius, qui ad vos verba facit, praeterita tempora, ut liquet, respicit eorumque veluti vocem, alacrem animosque confirmantem, auscultat: libenter enim meminit bene meritorum Summorum Pontificum, qui antiquissima et recentiore aetate fuerunt; et qui e Conciliorum conventibus, sive in Oriente sive in Occidente a saeculo quarto ad Medium usque Aevum et recentia tempora actis, venerandae gravisque huiusmodi vocis testimonia transmiserunt. Haec non intermisso studio triumphum divinae et humanae illius Societatis extollunt, scilicet Ecclesiae Christi, quae a Divino Redemptore nomen, gratiae munera, vimque accipit totam.
But if these are causes of spiritual joy, we cannot, however, deny that many sorrows and bitternesses through the long series of nineteen centuries have darkened this history. For there existed and exists what once the aged Simeon said prophetically to Mary the Mother of Jesus: "Behold, this child is set for the fall and for the resurrection of many... and for a sign which shall be contradicted" (Lk 2:34). And Jesus Himself, more mature in age, clearly showed how men through the ages would conduct themselves against Him, uttering these mysterious words: "He who hears you, hears me" (Lk 10:16). Moreover, He spoke these words, as we see written in St. Luke, who also relates the above words: "He who is not with me is against me, and he who does not gather with me scatters" (Lk 11:23).
¶5
Quodsi haec spiritualis laetitiae causa sunt, negare tamen non possumus dolores et acerbitates plurimas per longam undeviginti saeculorum seriem hanc historiam obscuravisse. Verum enim exstitit et exstat, quod olim Simeon senex ad Mariam Matrem Iesu ore prophetico dixit: "Ecce positus est hic in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum... et in signum cui contradicetur" (Lc 2,34). Atque Iesus ipse, aetate maturior, perspicue ostendit, quomodo homines per tempora adversus Ipsum se essent gesturi, arcana haec proferens verba: "Qui vos audit, me audit" (Lc 10,16). Praeterea haec effatus est, ut apud Sanctum Lucam, qui superiora quoque verba refert, scriptum videmus: "Qui non est mecum, contra me est, et qui non colligit mecum, dispergit" (Lc 11,23).
Truly grave matters and questions to be resolved by the human race, after nearly twenty centuries, do not change; for Jesus Christ always holds, as it were, the central place in history and in life: men either adhere to Him and His Church, and thus enjoy the goods of light, sweetness, right order, and peace; or they live without Him or act against Him and deliberately remain outside the Church, whence confusion occurs among them, mutual relations become harsh, and the danger of bloody wars impends.
¶6
Gravissimae sane res et quaestiones ab humano genere dissolvendae, post viginti fere saecula non mutantur; etenim Christus Iesus semper medium quasi locum tenet historiae et vitae: homines aut ipsi ipsiusque Ecclesiae adhaerent, atque adeo lucis, suavitatis, recti ordinis pacisque bonis fruuntur; aut sine ipso vivunt vel contra ipsum agunt et consulto extra Ecclesiam commorantur, quo fit ut confusio inter eos habeatur, mutuae rationes asperae efficiantur, cruentorum bellorum impendeat periculum.
This union with Christ and His Church, the Ecumenical Councils, whenever it happens that they are celebrated, proclaim in a solemn manner and send forth the light of truth in every direction, direct the life of individuals, domestic life, and society into straight paths, excite and strengthen spiritual forces, and constantly lift up minds to true and everlasting goods.
¶7
Hanc coniunctionem cum Christo eiusque Ecclesia Concilia Oecumenica, quotiescumque ea celebrari contingit, sollemni quodam modo praedicant et veritatis lucem quoquoversus emittunt, vitam singulorum hominum, domestici convictus, societatis in rectas semitas dirigunt, spirituales vires excitant atque stabiliunt, ad vera et sempiterna bona continenter animos erigunt.
The testimonies of this extraordinary Magisterium of the Church, namely of universal Synods, are before our eyes, while we look upon the various ages of men through these twenty centuries of the Christian era. These documents are contained in many volumes of great weight and are to be esteemed as a sacred treasure, which is hidden in the archives of the city of Rome and in the most celebrated libraries of the whole world.
¶8
Testimonia extraordinarii huius Magisterii Ecclesiae, scilicet universalium Synodorum, ob oculos Nostros versantur, dum varias hominum aetates per haec viginti saecula christiani aevi intuemur. Quae documenta pluribus magnique ponderis voluminibus continentur ac veluti sacer thesaurus sunt aestimanda, qui in tabulariis Urbis Romae ac totius orbis terrarum celebratissimis bibliothecis est reconditus.
¶9
The Origin and Cause of the Second Vatican Ecumenical Council
Quod autem ad originem et causam attinet maximi huius eventus, propter quem placuit Nobis vos hic congregare, satis est iterum afferre testimonium humile quidem, sed quod Nos ipsi experientia possumus probare: primo enim paene ex inopinato hoc Concilium mente concepimus, ac deinde simplicibus verbis enuntiavimus coram Sacro Purpuratorum Patrum Collegio fausto die illo vicesimo quinto mensis Ianuarii anno millesimo nongentesimo quinquagesimo nono, in festo Conversionis Sancti Pauli Apostoli, in ipsa eius Patriarchali Basilica ad viam Ostiensem.
Immediately the minds of those present were suddenly touched, as if sparkling with a ray of supernal light, and all were gently affected in face and eyes. At the same time, a vehement zeal burned throughout the whole world, and all men began to expect the celebration of the council with eager desire.
¶10
Statim adstantium animi subito tacti sunt, quasi supernae lucis radio coruscante, et suaviter omnes affecti in vultu oculisque. Simul vero vehemens studium toto terrarum orbe exarsit, cunctique homines Concilii celebrationem studiose exspectare coeperunt.
In the meantime, three years of laborious work were accomplished for preparing the council, which pertained to investigating more accurately and more fully with what honor the Faith, the practice of religion, the vigor of the Christian and especially the Catholic assembly, should be held in our age.
¶11
Interea tres annos ad apparandum Concilium laboriosum est opus patratum, quod eo pertinebat, ut accuratius et amplius inquireretur, quo honore essent hac nostra aetate Fides, religionis usus, vigor coetus christiani ac praesertim catholici.
Indeed, it seems to us not undeservedly that this time spent in preparing the Ecumenical Council has been a first sign and gift of heavenly grace.
¶12
Profecto haud immerito Nobis videtur hoc tempus in apparando Concilio Oecumenico insumptum primum quoddam signum ac donum gratiae caelestis fuisse.
Therefore, illuminated by the light of this Council, the Church will be enriched with spiritual riches, as we trust, and, drawing from it the strength of new powers, will look forward without fear to future times. For, by opportune emendations introduced and mutual helpful work wisely instituted, the Church will bring it about that men, families, and nations truly turn their minds to those things which are above.
¶13
Huius ergo Concilii lumine illustrata, Ecclesia spiritualibus divitiis, ut confidimus, augebitur atque, novarum virium robur ex illo hauriens, intrepide futura prospiciet tempora. Nam, opportunis inductis emendationibus ac mutua auxiliatrice opera sapienter instituta, Ecclesia efficiet, ut homines, familiae, nationes reapse ad ea, quae supra sunt, mentes convertant.
Thus, on account of the celebration of this Council, the greatest thanks are to be rendered to the Supreme Giver of all goods from the duty of conscience, and the glory of Christ the Lord is to be proclaimed with exultation, who is the invincible and immortal King of the ages and of the nations.
¶14
Itaque propter Concilii huius celebrationem Summo Largitori omnium bonorum gratiae maximae ex conscientiae officio sunt reddendae, et gloria Christi Domini cum exsultatione praedicanda, qui est Rex invictus et immortalis saeculorum ac gentium.
Namely, it is permissible for us, in the inflammation of holy joy which at this solemn hour affects our spirits, to declare openly in this most ample assembly that the beginning of this Ecumenical Synod happens at opportune circumstances of time.
¶15
Aliud praeterea est, Venerabiles Fratres, quod hac in re iuvat vobis ad considerandum proponere.
The Opportunity of Celebrating the Council
Scilicet, in accensionem laetitiae sanctae, quae sollemni hac hora animos afficit nostros, liceat Nobis hoc in amplissimo consessu palam asseverare, initium Oecumenicae huius Synodi opportunis sane temporis adiunctis contingere.
For it often happens, as we have come to know in the daily pursuit of the apostolic ministry, that certain voices are brought to our hearing, not without offense to our ears, of those who, although inflamed with religious zeal, nevertheless do not weigh things with sufficiently equitable estimation and prudent judgment. For these, in the present conditions of human society, can see nothing but ruins and calamities; they continually say that our times, if compared with past centuries, have gone completely for the worse; and they behave as if from history, which is the teacher of life, they have nothing to learn, and as if at the time of previous councils everything proceeded prosperously and rightly with respect to Christian doctrine, morals, and the just liberty of the Church.
¶16
Saepe quidem accidit, quemadmodum in cotidiano obeundo apostolico ministerio comperimus, ut non sine aurium Nostrarum offensione quorundam voces ad Nos perferantur, qui, licet religionis studio incensi, non satis tamen aequa aestimatione prudentique iudicio res perpendunt. Hi enim, in praesentibus humanae societatis condicionibus, nonnisi ruinas calamitatesque cernere valent; dictitant nostra tempora, si cum elapsis saeculis comparentur, prorsus in peius abiisse; atque adeo ita se habent, quasi ex historia, quae vitae magistra est, nihil habeant quod discant, ac veluti si, superiorum Conciliorum tempore, quoad christianam doctrinam, quoad mores, quoad iustam Ecclesiae libertatem, omnia prospere ac recte processerint.
But it seems to us that we must completely disagree with these prophets of misfortune, who always announce worse things, as if the end of the world were at hand.
¶17
At Nobis plane dissentiendum esse videtur ab his rerum adversarum vaticinatoribus, qui deteriora semper praenuntiant, quasi rerum exitium instet.
In the present course of human events, in which human society seems to be entering a new order of things, the hidden counsels of Divine Providence must rather be acknowledged, which, through succeeding times, through the work of men, and often beyond their expectation, attain their outcome, and wisely dispose all things, even adverse human events, for the good of the Church.
¶18
In praesenti humanorum eventuum cursu, quo hominum societas novum rerum ordinem ingredi videtur, potius arcana Divinae Providentiae consilia agnoscenda sunt, quae per tempora succedentia, hominum opera, ac plerumque praeter eorum exspectationem, suum exitum consequuntur, atque omnia, adversos etiam humanos casus, in Ecclesiae bonum sapienter disponunt.
This can be easily seen if one attentively considers the most grave questions of politics and economics and the crises which exist today. All of which hold men so anxious that they turn their cares and thoughts away from religious affairs which pertain to the sacred Magisterium of the Church. This manner of acting is certainly not without fault, and is justly to be disapproved. Yet no one can deny that these new conditions of things have at least this advantage, that countless impediments are now removed by which the sons of the world used to hinder the free action of the Church. For it is enough to run cursorily through ecclesiastical annals to see immediately clearly that even the Ecumenical Councils themselves—whose vicissitudes are recorded in golden letters in the fasti of the Church—were often celebrated not without great difficulties and causes of sorrow, on account of the undue interposed authority of the civil power. For the Princes of this world sometimes indeed proposed to themselves with sincere heart to undertake the patronage of the Church; but this was done for the most part not without spiritual detriment and peril, since they were often led by political reasons and were too studious of their own advantages.
¶19
Hoc facile cernere est, si attento animo perpendantur gravissimae de re politica et oeconomica quaestiones atque discrimina, quae hodie sunt. Quae omnia tam sollicitos homines tenent, ut eorum curas cogitationesque a religiosis negotiis avertant, quae ad sacrum pertinent Ecclesiae Magisterium. Huiusmodi agendi ratio certe vitio non caret, ac merito est improbanda. Nemo tamen negare potest, has novas inductas rerum condiciones, id saltem commodi habere, ut e medio innumera illa impedimenta iam auferantur, quibus olim saeculi filii liberam Ecclesiae actionem praepedire consueverant. Etenim satis est cursim ecclesiasticos annales pervolvere, ut statim manifesto pateat, vel ipsa Oecumenica Concilia, quorum vicissitudines aureis litteris Ecclesiae fastis consignatae sunt, saepe non sine gravissimis difficultatibus ac doloris causis, ob indebitam civilis potestatis interpositam auctoritatem, celebrata fuisse. Huius enim mundi Principes interdum sibi quidem proponebant sincero animo Ecclesiae patrocinium suscipere; quod tamen plerumque non sine spirituali detrimento ac periculo fiebat, cum iidem saepius rationibus politicis ducerentur suisque utilitatibus nimium studerent.
We confess indeed that today we are moved with vehement sorrow that among you many Pastors of the Church are lacking, most dear to us, who are detained in chains on account of the faith of Christ or hindered by other impediments, whose memory moves us to offer most fervent prayers to God for them; yet we see that it has come about today not without hope and great consolation to us that the Church, at length freed from so many profane impediments of the past age, from this Vatican Temple, as if from another Upper Room of the Apostles, through you can lift up her voice, full of majesty and gravity.
¶20
Fatemur quidem hodie Nos vehementi dolore affici, quod inter vos complures Ecclesiae Pastores desiderantur, Nobis sane carissimi, qui ob Christi Fidem in vinculis detinentur vel aliis impedimentis praepediuntur, et quorum recordatio Nos permovet ut pro ipsis flagrantissimas Deo preces admoveamus; attamen non sine spe ac magno solacio Nostro hodie factum esse cernimus, ut Ecclesia, tot profanis praeteritae aetatis impedimentis tandem expedita, ex hoc Vaticano Templo, veluti altero Apostolorum Cenaculo, per vos vocem suam, maiestatis gravitatisque plenam, attollere possit.
This doctrine embraces the whole man, composed of body and soul, and commands us, who are inhabitants of this earth, to press forward as pilgrims to the heavenly homeland.
¶21
Quod Concilii Oecumenici maxime interest, hoc est, ut sacrum christianae doctrinae depositum efficaciore ratione custodiatur atque proponatur.
The Principal Office of the Council: Doctrine to Be Defended and Promoted
Haec doctrina integrum complectitur hominem, corpore et animo compositum, eademque iubet nos, qui huius terrae incolae sumus, ad caelestem patriam peregrinos contendere.
And it shows how this mortal life is to be ordered, so that, satisfying the duties by which we are bound to the earthly and the heavenly City, we may be able to attain the end established for us by God. Namely, all men absolutely, whether individually or united in society, are without interruption held by the duty to pursue heavenly goods as long as this life lasts, and for attaining this, to use earthly things, yet in such a way that the use of temporal goods does not bring their eternal beatitude into peril.
¶22
Id autem ostendit, qua ratione mortalis haec vita componenda sit, ut nos officiis satisfacientes, quibus erga terrestrem et caelestem Civitatem obstringimur, finem nobis a Deo praestitutum assequi valeamus. Scilicet, omnes prorsus homines, sive singuli sive inter se societate coniuncti, sine intermissione officio tenentur caelestia bona consectandi, donec haec vita suppetit, atque ad hoc assequendum, terrenas res adhibendi, ita tamen ut temporalium bonorum usus sempiternam eorum beatitatem in discrimen non adducat.
It is truly true that Christ the Lord pronounced this sentence: "Seek first the kingdom of God and His justice" (Mt 6:33). This word "first" declares to what especially our strength and thoughts ought to be directed; nevertheless, the other words of this Lord's precept which follow are by no means to be neglected: "and all these things shall be added unto you" (Mt 6:34).
¶23
Verum profecto est, Christum Dominum hanc pronuntiasse sententiam: "Quaerite primum regnum Dei et iustitiam eius" (Mt 6,33). Quae vox "primum" declarat, quo potissimum vires et cogitationes nostras dirigi oporteat; nihilominus minime neglegenda sunt cetera huius Dominici praecepti verba, quae subsequuntur: "et haec omnia adicientur vobis" (Mt 6,34).
And indeed there have always been and are in the Church those who, while they strive with all study to attain evangelical perfection, at the same time contribute their helping work to civil culture, since from the examples of their life and from their salutary undertakings of charity, those things which are more excellent and noble in human society receive no small strength and increase.
¶24
Ac revera semper fuerunt ac sunt in Ecclesia qui, dum evangelicae perfectioni adipiscendae omni studio vacant, simul ad civilem cultum adiutricem operam conferunt, cum ex eorum vitae exemplis, ex eorumque salutaribus caritatis inceptis, ea, quae in humana societate praestantiora ac nobiliora sunt, haud parum accipiant roboris atque incrementi.
So that this doctrine may touch the manifold fields of human activity which pertain to individuals, to domestic life, and to social life, it is first necessary that the Church never turn her eyes away from the sacred patrimony of truth received from the fathers; but at the same time she must also look upon the present times, which have introduced new conditions of things, new forms of living, and have opened new paths for the catholic apostolate.
¶25
Ut autem haec doctrina multiplices attingat humanae navitatis campos, qui ad singulos homines, ad domesticum convictum, ad socialem vitam pertinent, in primis necesse est, ne Ecclesia oculos a sacro veritatis patrimonio a maioribus accepto umquam avertat; at simul necesse habet, ut praesentia quoque aspiciat tempora, quae novas induxerunt rerum condiciones, novasque vivendi formas, atque nova catholico apostolatui patefecerunt itinera.
For this reason the Church has not been inert in the presence of those wonderful discoveries of the human mind and those advances of doctrines which we use today, nor has she proved incapable of duly evaluating them; but, pursuing all these things with most watchful care, she does not cease to admonish men to turn their eyes, above the very appearance of visible things, to God the fountain of all wisdom and beauty, lest they—to whom it was said: "Subdue the earth and have dominion"—ever forget that most grave precept: "Thou shalt adore the Lord thy God and Him only shalt thou serve" (Mt 4:10; Lk 4:8), nor let the fleeting fascination of things impede progress in the true sense.
¶26
Hac de causa Ecclesia mirandis illis humani ingenii inventis doctrinarumque incrementis, quibus hodie utimur, non iners affuit, neque iisdem recte aestimandis impar evasit; sed haec omnia pervigili prosequens cura, homines admonere non desinit, ut super ipsam rerum aspectabilium speciem, oculos ad Deum omnis sapientiae et pulchritudinis fontem convertant, ne iidem, quibus dictum fuit: "Subicite terram et dominamini", obliviscantur umquam gravissimi illius praecepti: "Dominum Deum tuum adorabis et illi soli servies (Mt 4,10; Lc 4,8), neu fluxa rerum fascinatio veri nominis progressionem impediat.
Namely, the first and twentieth Ecumenical Council—which uses the effective and greatly estimable help of those who excel in the knowledge of sacred disciplines, in the exercise of the apostolate, and in the right ordering of things—wishes to hand on the catholic doctrine whole, undiminished, not distorted, which, although placed among difficulties and contentions, has become as a common patrimony of men. This is not indeed pleasing to all, nevertheless it is set forth as a most abundant treasure ready for all who are endowed with good will.
¶27
Hisce positis, satis manifestae sunt, Venerabiles Fratres, partes quae, ad doctrinam quod attinet, Concilio Oecumenico sunt demandatae.
How the Doctrine Is to Be Promoted Today
Scilicet Concilium Oecumenicum primum et vicesimum - quod efficaci magnique aestimando auxilio utitur eorum, qui scientia sacrarum disciplinarum, apostolatus exercendi resque recto ordine agendi excellunt - integram, non imminutam, non detortam tradere vult doctrinam catholicam, quae, licet inter difficultates et contentiones, veluti patrimonium commune hominum evasit. Hoc non omnibus quidem gratum est, tamen cunctis, qui bona voluntate sunt praediti, quasi paratus thesaurus uberrimus proponitur.
But it is not our part to guard this precious treasure only, as if we were devoted only to antiquity; but with alacrity, without fear, let us now insist upon the work which our age requires, continuing the journey which the Church has made for nearly twenty centuries.
¶28
Attamen nostrum non est pretiosum hunc thesaurum solum custodire, quasi uni antiquitati studeamus; sed alacres, sine timore, operi, quod nostra exigit aetas, nunc insistamus, iter pergentes, quod Ecclesia a viginti fere saeculis fecit.
Nor does our work, as if for a primary end, look to this, that there be disputation concerning certain principal chapters of ecclesiastical doctrine, and that those things which the fathers and ancient and recent theologians have handed down—which we rightly think not to be unknown to you but to inhere in your minds—be more fully repeated.
¶29
Neque opus nostrum, quasi ad finem primarium, eo spectat, ut de quibusdam capitibus praecipuis doctrinae ecclesiasticae disceptetur, atque adeo fusius repetantur ea, quae Patres ac theologi veteres et recentiores tradiderunt, et quae a vobis non ignorari sed in mentibus vestris inhaerere merito putamus.
For indeed, for such disputations alone to be held it would not have been necessary to indict an Ecumenical Council. Nevertheless at present it is fitting that the whole Christian doctrine, no part subtracted from it, be received here in our times by all with new study, with serene and peaceful minds, handed down with that accurate manner of conceiving and reducing to form words which is especially evident from the acts of the Council of Trent and the First Vatican Council; it is fitting that, as all sincere supporters of the Christian, catholic, apostolic religion vehemently desire, the same doctrine be known more widely and more deeply and that minds be more fully imbued and formed by it; it is fitting that this doctrine, certain and immutable, to which faithful obedience is to be rendered, be investigated and set forth in that manner which our times demand. For it is one thing that the deposit of Faith itself, namely the truths which are contained in our venerable doctrine, is another thing the manner in which they are enunciated, preserving the same sense and the same meaning. Indeed, great importance must be given to this manner, and, if necessary, one must labor patiently in it; namely, those ways of setting forth matters must be introduced which are more congruent with the magisterium, whose character is especially pastoral.
¶30
Etenim ad huiusmodi tantum disputationes habendas non opus erat, ut Concilium Oecumenicum indiceretur. Verumtamen in praesenti oportet ut universa doctrina christiana, nulla parte inde detracta, hic temporibus nostris ab omnibus accipiatur novo studio, mentibus serenis atque pacatis, tradita accurata illa ratione verba concipiendi et in formam redigendi, quae ex actis Concilii Tridentini et Vaticani Primi praesertim elucet; oportet ut, quemadmodum cuncti sinceri rei christianae, catholicae, apostolicae fautores vehementer exoptant, eadem doctrina amplius et altius cognoscatur eaque plenius animi imbuantur atque formentur; oportet ut haec doctrina certa et immutabilis, cui fidele obsequium est praestandum, ea ratione pervestigetur et exponatur, quam tempora postulant nostra. Est enim aliud ipsum depositum Fidei, seu veritates, quae veneranda doctrina nostra continentur, aliud modus, quo eaedem enuntiantur, eodem tamen sensu eademque sententia. Huic quippe modo plurimum tribuendum erit et patienter, si opus fuerit, in eo elaborandum; scilicet eae inducendae erunt rationes res exponendi, quae cum magisterio, cuius indoles praesertim pastoralis est, magis congruant.
For indeed, while age succeds age, we see uncertain opinions of men succeed one another, and emerging errors often quickly vanish like a mist driven away by the sun.
¶31
Ineunte Concilio Oecumenico Vaticano Secundo, manifesto ut alias numquam patet, veritatem Domini in aeternum manere.
How Errors Are to Be Repressed
Siquidem, dum aetas aetati succedit, incertas cernimus hominum opinationes alias alias excipere, atque enascentes errores saepe cito velut nebulam sole depulsam evanescere.
The Church has opposed these errors at all times, and has often even condemned them, and indeed with the firmest severity. As for the present time, it pleases the Spouse of Christ to apply the medicine of mercy rather than to take up the arms of severity; she thinks that present needs are to be consulted more by explaining the force of her doctrine more abundantly than by condemning. Not that fallacious doctrines, opinions, perils to be guarded against and dissipated are lacking; but because all these things so openly fight against the principles of right honesty, and have produced such deadly fruits, that today men themselves seem to be beginning to condemn them on their own, and especially those forms of living which put God and His laws last, excessive confidence placed in the progress of technical art, prosperity based solely on the conveniences of life. They themselves more and more know that the dignity of the human person and its fitting perfection is a matter of great importance and very difficult to accomplish. But what is of greatest importance, they have finally learned by experience that external force imposed on others, the power of arms, political domination are by no means sufficient to resolve successfully the most grave questions which afflict them.
¶32
Quibus erroribus Ecclesia nullo non tempore obstitit, eos saepe etiam damnavit, et quidem severitate firmissima. Ad praesens tempus quod attinet, Christi Sponsae placet misericordiae medicinam adhibere, potius quam severitatis arma suscipere; magis quam damnando, suae doctrinae vim uberius explicando putat hodiernis necessitatibus esse consulendum. Non quod desint fallaces doctrinae, opinationes, pericula praecavenda atque dissipanda; sed quia haec omnia tam aperte pugnant cum rectis honestatis principiis, ac tam exitiales peperere fructus, ut hodie homines per se ipsi ea damnare incipere videantur, ac nominatim illas vivendi formas, quae Deum eiusque leges posthabeant, nimiam in technicae artis progressibus positam confidentiam, prosperitatem unice vitae commodis innixam. Ipsi magis magisque norunt, humanae personae dignitatem eiusque congruam perfectionem negotium esse magni momenti et ad expediendum perarduum. Quod autem maxime interest, iidem experiendo tandem didicerunt, externam vim aliis impositam, armorum potentiam, politicum dominatum minime satis esse, ad gravissimas, quae eos angunt, quaestiones feliciter dissolvendas.
In these circumstances of things, the Catholic Church, while through this Ecumenical Council she lifts up the torch of religious truth, wishes to show herself as the most loving mother of all, benign, patient, and moved with mercy and goodness toward sons separated from herself. To the human race, laboring under so many difficulties, she herself, like Peter once to that poor man who asked alms from him, says: "Silver and gold I have none; but what I have I give you: in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, arise and walk" (Acts 3:6). Namely, to the men of our times the Church does not hand over fleeting riches, nor does she promise mere earthly beatitude; but she imparts the goods of heavenly grace, which, as they raise men to the dignity of sons of God, are so strong an aid and help for making their life more humane; she opens the more abundant fountains of her doctrine, by which men, illuminated by the light of Christ, are able to understand deeply what they themselves really are, with what dignity they excel, what end they ought to pursue; finally, through her sons, she everywhere extends the spaces of Christian charity, than which nothing is more apt for pulling up the seeds of dissension, and nothing is more efficacious for promoting concord, just peace, and fraternal unity of all.
¶33
Hisce in rerum adiunctis, Catholica Ecclesia, dum per Oecumenicum hoc Concilium religiosae veritatis facem attollit, amantissimam omnium matrem se vult praebere, benignam, patientem, atque erga filios a se seiunctos misericordia ac bonitate permotam. Humano generi, tot difficultatibus laboranti, ipsa, ut olim Petrus misero illi qui ab eo stipem rogaverat, dicit: "Argentum et aurum non est mihi: quod autem habeo hoc tibi do; in nomine Iesu Christi Nazareni surge et ambula" (Act 3,6). Scilicet nostrorum temporum hominibus Ecclesia non caducas porrigit divitias, neque beatitatem dumtaxat terrenam pollicetur; sed supernae gratiae bona impertit, quae cum homines ad filiorum Dei dignitatem erigant, tam valido praesidio et adiumento sunt ad humaniorem efficiendam eorum vitam; uberioris doctrinae suae fontes aperit, qua homines luce Christi collustrati penitus intelligere valent, quid ipsi revera sint, qua dignitate excellant, quem finem prosequi debeant; per filios denique suos ubique christianae caritatis spatia dilatat, qua ad dissensionum semina evellenda nihil est aptius, et ad provehendam concordiam, iustam pacem fraternamque omnium unitatem nihil est efficacius.
¶34
Unity in the Christian and Human Family to Be Fostered
Huiusmodi Ecclesiae sollicitudo de veritate promovenda atque tuenda, ex eo proficiscitur, quod, secundum Dei consilium, "qui omnes homines vult salvos fieri, et ad agnitionem veritatis venire" (1Tim 2,4), homines nonnisi integrae revelatae doctrinae praesidio aucti, ad absolutam firmissimamque animorum unitatem pervenire possunt, quacum veri nominis pax sempiternaque salus coniungitur.
But this visible unity in truth, alas, the whole family of Christians has not yet fully and perfectly attained. Yet the Catholic Church judges it to be her duty to contribute sedulous work to accomplish the great mystery of that unity which Christ Jesus, as His sacrifice impended, asked from the Heavenly Father with most fervent prayers; and she enjoys most sweet peace, knowing herself to be most closely joined with these prayers of Christ; nay, she rejoices with sincere heart when she notices that these prayers are producing salutary and more abundant fruits even among those who are outside her fold. For, if we consider the matter rightly, this unity itself, which Jesus Christ obtained for His Church, seems to shine as if with a threefold ray of supernal and salutary light, to which correspond: the unity of Catholics among themselves, which must be kept most firm and shining as an example; moreover, unity consisting of pious prayers and most fervent vows, by which Christians separated from this Apostolic See seek to be joined with us; finally, unity based on esteem and reverence for the Catholic Church, exhibited by those who profess diverse non-Christian forms of religion.
¶35
Hanc autem visibilem unitatem in veritate, nondum, pro dolor, universa christianorum familia plene perfecteque assecuta est. At Ecclesia Catholica officii sui esse ducit sedulam conferre operam ad magnum complendum mysterium illius unitatis, quam Christus Iesus, imminente sacrificio suo, a Caelesti Patre flagrantissimis rogavit precibus; atque suavissima fruitur pace, sciens se cum hisce Christi precibus arctissime esse coniunctam; quin etiam sincero animo gaudet, cum animadvertit has precationes etiam inter eos, qui extra eiusdem saepta versantur, salutares atque uberiores usque multiplicare fructus. Etenim, si rem probe consideramus, haec ipsa unitas, quam Iesus Christus pro Ecclesia sua impetravit, quasi triplici supernae salutarisque lucis radio coruscare videtur, cui respondent: unitas catholicorum inter se, quae firmissima atque in exemplum praelucens servetur oportet; unitas praeterea ex piis precibus et flagrantissimis votis constans, qua christiani ab hac Apostolica Sede seiuncti expetunt ut nobiscum coniungantur; unitas denique existimatione atque observantia erga Ecclesiam Catholicam innixa, ab iis exhibita, qui diversas religionis formas adhuc non christianas profitentur.
Concerning which matter, it is greatly to be mourned that a huge part of the human race—although all men who are born are redeemed also by the blood of Christ—does not yet participate in the sources of heavenly grace which are held in the Catholic Church. Hence it comes about that these splendid praises of St. Cyprian aptly apply to the Catholic Church, whose light illuminates all things and whose power of supernatural unity redounds to the progress of the whole human family: "The Church of the Lord, bathed in light, stretches forth her rays throughout the whole world; yet it is one light which is diffused everywhere, and the unity of the body is not separated. She extends her branches in abundant fertility over the whole earth, more widely spreads her flowing streams; yet there is one head and one source and one mother, abundant in the successes of her fecundity; by her offspring we are born, by her milk we are nourished, by her spirit we are given life."
¶36
Quam ad rem, est maxime lugendum, quod ingens adhuc humani generis pars - quamvis omnes homines qui nascuntur ipsi quoque Christi Sanguine sint redempti - nondum participet supernae gratiae fontes, qui in Ecclesia Catholica habentur. Quo fit ut de Ecclesia Catholica, cuius lux omnia illuminat et cuius supernaturalis unitatis vis in universae hominum familiae profectum redundat, apte cadant haec Sancti Cypriani praeclara praeconia: "Ecclesia Domini luce perfusa per orbem totum radios suos porrigit; unum tamen lumen est quod ubique diffunditur nec unitas corporis separatur. Ramos suos in universam terram copia ubertatis extendit, profluentes largiter rivos latius pandit: unum tamen caput est et origo una et una mater fecunditatis successibus copiosa: illius fetu nascimur, illius lacte nutrimur, spiritu eius animamur".
This is what the Second Vatican Ecumenical Synod proposes to itself; which, while it gathers the principal forces of the Church into one, and earnestly strives that the message of salvation may be more willingly received by men, as if paves and fortifies the way for achieving that unity of the human race which is as a necessary foundation, so that the earthly City may be ordered after the likeness of the heavenly City, "whose King is truth, whose law is love, whose measure is eternity."
¶37
Venerabiles Fratres,
Venerable Brothers,
Hoc sibi proponit Oecumenica Synodus Vaticana Secunda; quae, dum praecipuas Ecclesiae vires in unum congregat, atque enixe studet, ut salutis nuntium ab hominibus libentius excipiatur, quasi viam sternit ac munit ad efficiendam illam humani generis unitatem, quae veluti necessarium fundamentum est, ut terrena Civitas in similitudinem componatur Civitatis caelestis, "cuius rex veritas, cuius lex caritas, cuius modus aeternitas".
Behold, we are now gathered together in this Vatican Basilica, where the hinge of the history of the Church turns, where now Heaven and earth are joined in a most close bond; here at the tomb of St. Peter, at the tombs of so many holy predecessors of ours, whose ashes at this solemn hour seem to exult as if with a mysterious murmuring.
