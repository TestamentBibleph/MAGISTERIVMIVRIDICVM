Conclusion
Ecce nos iam in unum congregatos in hac Vaticana Basilica, ubi Ecclesiae historiae cardo vertitur, ubi nunc Caelum et terra artissimo coniunguntur foedere; hic ad Sancti Petri sepulcrum, ad tot Sanctorum Decessorum Nostrorum tumulos, quorum cineres hac sollemni hora quasi arcano quodam fremitu exsultare videntur.
What is being begun, the Council, rises like a day shining with most splendid light. It is only dawn; and already the first rays of the rising sun how sweetly they affect our spirits! All here breathes holiness, excites joy. For we contemplate the stars with their brightness increasing the majesty of this temple, which, as the Apostle John testifies, are you; and through you, as it were, golden candlesticks shining around the tomb of the Prince of the Apostles, which are the churches entrusted to you. At the same time we see men of most ample dignity, who have come to Rome from the five continents, about to act for their nations, and who are present here with all reverence and most humane expectation.
¶39
Quod incohatur Concilium, veluti dies in Ecclesia oritur splendidissima luce refulgens. Tantum aurora est; et iam primi orientis solis radii quam suaviter animos afficiunt nostros! Omnia hic sanctitatem spirant, laetitiam excitant. Contemplamur enim stellas claritate sua maiestatem huius templi adaugere, quae, teste Ioanne Apostolo, vos estis; et per vos quasi aurea lucere candelabra circa Principis Apostolorum sepulcrum, quae sunt Ecclesiae vobis concreditae. Simul cernimus viros dignitate amplissimos, qui e quinque continentibus terris Romam convenerunt, suarum Nationum personam acturi, quique omni cum reverentia atque humanissima cum exspectatione hic adsunt.
Wherefore it must be plainly said that the heavenly citizens and men confer united work for celebrating the Council. The part of the blessed citizens is to protect our labors; that of Christ's faithful, to continue to pour forth fervent prayers to God; that of all of you, to give prompt obedience to the supernal impulses of the Holy Spirit, to give alacritous work so that your labors may excellently respond to the wishes and necessities of various nations. So that these things may happen, serene peace of spirits, fraternal concord, temperateness of undertakings, dignity of disputations, wisdom of all deliberations are required of you.
¶40
Quare plane dicendum est, Caelites et homines ad celebrandum Concilium consociatam conferre operam. Beatorum Caelitum partes hae sunt, ut labores tueantur nostros; christifidelium, ut flagrantes preces ad Deum fundere pergant; vestrum omnium, ut supernis Spiritus Sancti impulsionibus prompte obsecuti, alacriter detis operam, ut labores vestri variarum gentium optatis ac necessitatibus apprime respondeant. Haec ut contingant, a vobis postulantur serena animorum pax, fraterna concordia, coeptorum temperantia, disceptationum dignitas, deliberationum omnium sapientia.
May it be that your studies and works, upon which not only the eyes of peoples but also the hope of the whole world are turned, may abundantly fulfill expectation.
¶41
Utinam studia atque opera vestra, in quae non tantum populorum oculi sed spes quoque universi orbis sunt conversae, exspectationem cumulate expleant.
Omnipotent God, in You, distrusting our own strength, we place all our confidence. Look benignly upon these pastors of Your Church. May the light of Your heavenly grace be with us in taking counsel, be with us in making laws; and hear willingly the prayers which we pour forth to You with one faith, one mouth, one spirit.
¶42
Omnipotens Deus, in Te, nostris diffisi viribus, fiduciam totam reponimus. Super hos Ecclesiae tuae Pastores benignus respice. Supernae tuae gratiae lumen Nobis adsit consilium capientibus, adsit leges ferentibus; et quas una Fide, uno ore, uno animo ad Te preces fundimus, libenter exaudi.
O Mary, Help of Christians, Help of Bishops, whose love we recently in a particular manner experienced in your Lauretan temple, where it pleased us to venerate the mystery of the Incarnation, dispose all things by your help to a happy, favorable, prosperous outcome; and you, together with your Spouse St. Joseph, with the holy Apostles Peter and Paul, with St. John the Baptist and the Evangelist, intercede for us before God.
¶43
O Maria, Auxilium Christianorum, Auxilium Episcoporum, cuius amorem nuper in Lauretano templo tuo, ubi Incarnationis mysterium venerari placuit, peculiari modo experti sumus, omnia ad laetum, faustum, prosperum exitum tua ope dispone; tuque una cum Sancto Ioseph Sponso tuo, cum Sanctis Petro et Paulo Apostolis, Sanctis Ioanne Baptista et Evangelista, apud Deum intercede pro nobis.
To Jesus Christ, our most loving Redeemer, immortal King of peoples and of times, be love, power, and glory forever and ever. Amen.
¶44
Iesu Christo, Redemptori nostro amantissimo, Regi immortali populorum et temporum, amor, potestas et gloria in saecula saeculorum. Amen.
Since Christ is the Light of the Nations, this sacred Synod, gathered in the Holy Spirit, greatly desires that, by proclaiming the Gospel to every creature (cf. Mk 16:15), it may illuminate all men with the clarity of Him who shines forth on the face of the Church. And since the Church is in Christ as a sacrament, that is, a sign and instrument of intimate union with God and of the unity of the whole human race, it intends, following upon the subject matter of previous councils, to declare more precisely to its faithful and to the whole world the nature and universal mission of the Church. The conditions of this time add greater urgency to this duty of the Church, namely that all men, now more closely joined together by various social, technical, and cultural bonds, may also attain full unity in Christ.

LUMEN GENTIUM
DOGMATIC CONSTITUTION ON THE CHURCH
CAPUT I. DE ECCLESIAE MYSTERIO
CHAPTER I. ON THE MYSTERY OF THE CHURCH
LUMEN GENTIUM cum sit Christus, haec Sacrosancta Synodus, in Spiritu Sancto congregata, omnes homines claritate Eius, super faciem Ecclesiae resplendente, illuminare vehementer exoptat, omni creaturae Evangelium annuntiando (cf. Mc 16,15). Cum autem Ecclesia sit in Christo veluti sacramentum seu signum et instrumentum intimae cum Deo unionis totiusque generis humani unitatis, naturam missionemque suam universalem, praecedentium Conciliorum argumento instans, pressius fidelibus suis et mundo universo declarare intendit. Condiciones huius temporis huic Ecclesiae officio urgentiorem vim addunt, ut nempe homines cuncti, variis hodie vinculis socialibus, technicis, culturalibus arctius coniuncti, plenam etiam unitatem in Christo consequantur.
The eternal Father, by the most free and hidden counsel of His wisdom and goodness, created the whole world, decreed to raise men to a participation in divine life, and did not abandon them when they had fallen in Adam, always providing them with aids for salvation in view of Christ the Redeemer, who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature (Col 1:15). All the elect the Father foreknew and predestined from before the ages to become conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brothers (Rom 8:29). He decreed to call those who believe in Christ into the holy Church, which, prefigured from the beginning of the world, wonderfully prepared in the history of the people of Israel and in the old covenant, established in the last times, manifested by the outpouring of the Spirit, will be gloriously consummated at the end of the ages. Then, as is read in the holy fathers, all the just from Adam, from righteous Abel to the last elect, will be gathered together in the universal Church before the Father.
¶46
Aeternus Pater, liberrimo et arcano sapientiae ac bonitatis suae consilio, mundum universum creavit, homines ad participandam vitam divinam elevare decrevit, eosque lapsos in Adamo non dereliquit, semper eis auxilia ad salutem praebens, intuitu Christi, Redemptoris, qui est imago Dei invisibilis, primogenitus omnis creaturae (Col 1,15). Omnes autem electos Pater ante saecula praescivit et praedestinavit conformes fieri imaginis Filii sui, ut sit Ipse primogenitus in multis fratribus (Rm 8,29). Credentes autem in Christum convocare statuit in sancta Ecclesia, quae iam ab origine mundi praefigurata, in historia populi Israel ac foedere antiquo mirabiliter praeparata, in novissimis temporibus constituta, effuso Spiritu est manifestata, et in fine saeculorum gloriose consummabitur. Tunc autem, sicut apud sanctos Patres legitur, omnes iusti inde ab Adam, ab Abel iusto usque ad ultimum electum in Ecclesia universali apud Patrem congregabuntur.
Therefore the Son came, sent by the Father, who chose us in Him before the foundation of the world and predestined us to the adoption of sons, because in Him it pleased Him to restore all things (cf. Eph 1:4-5, 10). Christ therefore, to fulfill the will of the Father, inaugurated the kingdom of heaven on earth and revealed His mystery to us, and by His obedience He accomplished redemption. The Church, that is, the kingdom of Christ already present in mystery, grows visibly in the world through the power of God. This beginning and growth are signified by the blood and water flowing from the open side of crucified Jesus (cf. Jn 19:34), and are foretold by the Lord's words concerning His death on the cross: "And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to myself" (Jn 12:32). As often as the sacrifice of the cross, in which Christ our pasch was sacrificed (1 Cor 5:7), is celebrated on the altar, the work of our redemption is exercised. At the same time, by the sacrament of the eucharistic bread, the unity of the faithful, who constitute one body in Christ (cf. 1 Cor 10:17), is represented and brought about. All men are called to this union with Christ, who is the light of the world, from whom we proceed, through whom we live, toward whom we tend.
¶47
Venit igitur Filius, missus a Patre, qui nos in Eo ante mundi constitutionem elegit ac in adoptionem filiorum praedestinavit, quia in Eo omnia instaurare sibi complacuit (cf. Ep 1,4-5 Ep 1,10). Christus ideo, ut voluntatem Patris impleret, regnum caelorum in terris inauguravit nobisque Eius mysterium revelavit, atque oboedientia sua redemptionem effecit. Ecclesia, seu regnum Christi iam praesens in mysterio, ex virtute Dei in mundo visibiliter crescit. Quod exordium et incrementum significantur sanguine et aqua ex aperto latere Iesu crucifixi exeuntibus (cf. Jn 19,34), ac praenuntiantur verbis Domini de morte sua in cruce: Et Ego, si exaltatus fuero a terra, omnes traham ad Meipsum (Jn 12,32 gr.). Quoties sacrificium crucis, quo Pascha nostrum immolatus est Christus (1Co 5,7), in altari celebratur, opus nostrae redemptionis exercetur. Simul sacramento panis eucharistici repraesentatur et efficitur unitas fidelium, qui unum corpus in Christo constituunt (cf. 1Co 10,17). Omnes homines ad hanc vocantur unionem cum Christo, qui est lux mundi, a quo procedimus, per quem vivimus, ad quem tendimus.
When the work was accomplished which the Father gave the Son to do on earth (cf. Jn 17:4), the Holy Spirit was sent on the day of Pentecost, that He might continually sanctify the Church, and thus believers, through Christ and in one Spirit, might have access to the Father (cf. Eph 2:18). He is the Spirit of life, that is, the fountain of water springing up unto eternal life (cf. Jn 4:14; 7:38-39), through whom the Father gives life to men, dead through sin, until He raises up their mortal bodies in Christ (cf. Rom 8:10-11). The Spirit dwells in the Church and in the hearts of the faithful as in a temple (cf. 1 Cor 3:16; 6:19), and in them He prays and bears witness to the adoption of sons (cf. Gal 4:6; Rom 8:15-16, 26). He leads the Church into all truth (cf. Jn 16:13), unifies her in communion and ministry, instructs and directs her with diverse hierarchical and charismatic gifts, and adorns her with His fruits (cf. Eph 4:11-12; 1 Cor 12:4; Gal 5:22). By the power of the Gospel He makes the Church young again, perpetually renews her, and leads her to consummated union with her Spouse. For the Spirit and the Spouse say to the Lord Jesus, "Come!" (cf. Rev 22:17).
¶48
Opere autem consummato, quod Pater Filio commisit in terra faciendum (cf. Jn 17,4), missus est Spiritus Sanctus die Pentecostes, ut Ecclesiam iugiter sanctificaret, atque ita credentes per Christum in uno Spiritu accessum haberent ad Patrem (cf. Ep 2,18). Ipse est Spiritus vitae seu fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam (cf. Jn 4,14 Jn 7,38-39), per quem Pater homines, peccato mortuos, vivificat, donec eorum mortalia corpora in Christo resuscitet (cf. Rm 8,10-11). Spiritus in Ecclesia et in cordibus fidelium tamquam in templo habitat (cf. 1Co 3,16 1Co 6,19), in eisque orat et testimonium adoptionis filiorum reddit (cf. Ga 4,6 Rm 8,15-16 Rm 8,26). Ecclesiam quam in omnem veritatem inducit (cf. Jn 16,13) et in communione et ministratione unificat, diversis donis hierarchicis et charismaticis instruit ac dirigit, et fructibus suis adornat (cf. Ep 4,11-12 1Co 12,4 Ga 5,22). Virtute Evangelii iuvenescere facit Ecclesiam eamque perpetuo renovat et ad consummatam cum Sponso suo unionem perducit.Nam Spiritus et Sponsa ad Dominum Iesum dicunt Veni! (cf. Ap 22,17).
Thus the whole Church appears as a people united from the unity of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit.
¶49
Sic apparet universa Ecclesia sicuti de unitate Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti plebs adunata.
The mystery of the holy Church is manifested in its foundation. For the Lord Jesus made a beginning of His Church by preaching the good news, namely the advent of the Kingdom of God promised from of old in the Scriptures: "The time is fulfilled, and the Kingdom of God is at hand" (Mk 1:15; cf. Mt 4:17). This Kingdom truly shines forth before men in the word, works, and presence of Christ. For the word of the Lord is compared to a seed sown in the field (Mk 4:14): those who hear it with faith and are numbered among the little flock of Christ (Lk 12:32) have received the Kingdom itself; then by its own power the seed germinates and grows until the harvest time (cf. Mk 4:26-29). The miracles of Jesus also prove that the Kingdom has already arrived on earth: "If I by the finger of God cast out devils, then surely the Kingdom of God has come upon you" (Lk 11:20; cf. Mt 12:28). Above all, however, the Kingdom is manifested in the very Person of Christ, the Son of God and the Son of Man, who came "to minister and to give His life as a redemption for many" (Mk 10:45).
¶50
Ecclesiae sanctae mysterium in eiusdem fundatione manifestatur. Dominus enim Iesus Ecclesiae suae initium fecit praedicando faustum nuntium, adventum scilicet Regni Dei a saeculis in Scripturis promissi: Quoniam impletum est tempus, et appropinquavit Regnum Dei (Mc 1,15 cf. Mt 4,17). Hoc vero Regnum in verbo, operibus et praesentia Christi hominibus elucescit. Verbum nempe Domini comparatur semini, quod in agro seminatur (Mc 4,14): qui illud cum fide audiunt et Christi pusillo gregi (Lc 12,32) adnumerantur, Regnum ipsum susceperunt; propria dein virtute semen germinat et increscit usque ad tempus messis (cf. Mc 4,26-29). Miracula etiam Iesu Regnum iam in terris pervenisse comprobant: Si in digito Dei eiicio daemonia, profecto pervenit in vos Regnum Dei (Lc 11,20 cf. Mt 12,28). Ante omnia tamen Regnum manifestatur in ipsa Persona Christi, Filii Dei et Filii hominis, qui venit ut ministraret, et daret animam suam redemptionem pro multis (Mc 10,45).
When Jesus, having suffered the death of the cross for men, had risen, He appeared as the Lord and Christ and Priest constituted forever (cf. Acts 2:36; Heb 5:6; 7:17-21), and He poured out upon His disciples the Spirit promised by the Father (cf. Acts 2:33). Hence the Church, instructed by the gifts of her Founder and faithfully keeping His precepts of charity, humility, and self-denial, receives the mission to announce and to establish the Kingdom of Christ and of God among all nations, and she constitutes on earth the seed and beginning of this Kingdom. In the meantime, while she grows gradually, she yearns for the consummated Kingdom, and with all her strength hopes and longs to be united with her King in glory.
¶51
Cum autem Iesus, mortem crucis pro hominibus passus, resurrexerit, tamquam Dominus et Christus Sacerdosque in aeternum constitutus apparuit (cf. Ac 2,36 He 5,6 He 7,17-21), atque Spiritum a Patre promissum in discipulos suos effudit (cf. Ac 2,33). Unde Ecclesia, donis sui Fundatoris instructa fideliterque eiusdem praecepta caritatis, humilitatis et abnegationis servans, missionem accipit Regnum Christi et Dei annuntiandi et in omnibus gentibus instaurandi, huiusque Regni in terris germen et initium constituit. Ipsa interea, dum paulatim increscit, ad Regnum consummatum anhelat, ac totis viribus sperat et exoptat cum Rege suo in gloria coniungi.
Just as in the Old Testament the revelation of the Kingdom is often proposed under figures, so now also the intimate nature of the Church is made known to us through various images, which, whether drawn from pastoral life or agriculture, from building or even from family and betrothal, are prepared in the books of the Prophets.
¶52
Sicut in Vetere Testamento revelatio Regni saepe sub figuris proponitur, ita nunc quoque variis imaginibus intima Ecclesiae natura nobis innotescit, quae sive a vita pastorali vel ab agricultura, sive ab aedificatione aut etiam a familia et sponsalibus desumptae, in libris Prophetarum praeparantur.
For the Church is a sheepfold, whose one and necessary door is Christ (Jn 10:1-10). It is also a flock, of which God Himself foretold He would be the shepherd (cf. Is 40:11; Ez 34:11 ff.), and whose sheep, although governed by human shepherds, are nevertheless unceasingly led and nourished by Christ Himself, the Good Shepherd and Prince of shepherds (cf. Jn 10:11; 1 Pet 5:4), who gave His life for the sheep (cf. Jn 10:11-15).
¶53
Est enim Ecclesia ovile, cuius ostium unicum et necessarium Christus est (Jn 10,1-10). Est etiam grex, cuius ipse Deus pastorem se fore praenuntiavit (cf. Is 40,11 Ez 34,11 ss.), et cuius oves, etsi a pastoribus humanis gubernantur, indesinenter tamen deducuntur et nutriuntur ab ipso Christo. bono Pastore Principeque pastorum (cf. Jn 10,11 1P 5,4), qui vitam suam dedit pro ovibus (cf. Jn 10,11-15).
The Church is the farm or field of God (1 Cor 3:9). In that field grows the ancient olive tree, whose holy root were the Patriarchs, and in which the reconciliation of Jews and Gentiles has taken place and will take place (Rom 11:13-26). It was planted by the heavenly Farmer as a chosen vine (Mt 21:33-43 par.; cf. Is 5:1 ff.). Christ is the true vine, giving life and fertility to the branches, that is, to us, who through the Church remain in Him, and without whom we can do nothing (Jn 15:1-5).
¶54
Est Ecclesia agricultura seu ager Dei (1Co 3,9). In illo agro crescit antiqua oliva, cuius radix sancta fuerunt Patriarchae, et in qua Iudaeorum et Gentium reconciliatio facta est et fiet (Rm 11,13-26). Ipsa plantata est a caelesti Agricola tamquam vinea electa (Mt 21,33-43 par.; cf. Is 5,1 ss.). Vitis vera Christus est, vitam et fecunditatem tribuens palmitibus, scilicet nobis, qui per Ecclesiam in ipso manemus, et sine quo nihil possumus facere (Jn 15,1-5).
The Church is also often called the building of God (1 Cor 3:9). The Lord Himself compared Himself to the stone which the builders rejected, but which was made the head of the corner (Mt 21:42 par.; cf. Acts 4:11; 1 Pet 2:7; Ps 117:22). Upon that foundation the Church is built by the Apostles (cf. 1 Cor 3:11), and from it she receives firmness and cohesion. This building is adorned with various names: the house of God (1 Tim 3:15), in which indeed His family dwells, the dwelling of God in the Spirit (Eph 2:19-22), the tabernacle of God with men (Rev 21:3), and especially the holy temple, which, represented in stone sanctuaries, is praised by the holy Fathers, and in the Liturgy is not undeservedly assimilated to the holy City, the new Jerusalem. For in her we are built as living stones here on earth (1 Pet 2:5). John contemplates this holy City, at the renewal of the world, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband (Rev 21:1-2).
¶55
Saepius quoque Ecclesia dicitur aedificatio Dei (1Co 3,9). Dominus ipse se comparavit lapidi, quem reprobaverunt aedificantes, sed qui factus est in caput anguli (Mt 21,42 par.; cf. Ac 4,11 1P 2,7 Ps 117,22). Super illud fundamentum Ecclesia ab Apostolis exstruitur (cf. 1Co 3,11), ab eoque firmitatem et cohaesionem accipit. Quae constructio variis appellationibus decoratur: domus Dei (1Tm 3,15), in qua nempe habitat eius familia, habitaculum Dei in Spiritu (Ep 2,19-22), tabernaculum Dei cum hominibus (Ap 21,3), et praesertim templum sanctum, quod in lapideis sanctuariis repraesentatum a Sanctis Patribus laudatur, et in Liturgia non immerito assimilatur Civitati sanctae, novae Ierusalem. In ipsa enim tamquam lapides vivi his in terris aedificamur (1P 2,5). Quam sanctam civitatem Ioannes contemplatur, in renovatione mundi descendentem de caelis a Deo, paratam sicut sponsam ornatam viro suo (Ap 21,1-2).
The Church, also, which is the Jerusalem above and is called our mother (Gal 4:26; cf. Rev 12:17), is described as the immaculate spouse of the immaculate Lamb (Rev 19:7; 21:2, 9; 22:17), whom Christ loved and delivered Himself up for her, that He might sanctify her (Eph 5:26), whom He joined to Himself by an indissoluble covenant, and continually nourishes and cherishes (Eph 5:29), and whom, purified, He willed to be joined to Himself and subject to Him in love and fidelity (cf. Eph 5:24), and finally enriched with heavenly goods forever, that we may comprehend the charity of God and of Christ toward us, which surpasses all knowledge (cf. Eph 3:19). While, however, the Church pilgrimages here on earth away from the Lord (cf. 2 Cor 5:6), she lives as an exile, so that she seeks and savors the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God, where the life of the Church is hidden with Christ in God, until she appears with her Spouse in glory (cf. Col 3:1-4).
¶56
Ecclesia etiam, quae sursum est Ierusalem et mater nostra appellatur (Ga 4,26 cf. Ap 12,17), describitur ut sponsa immaculata Agni immaculati (Ap 19,7 Ap 21,2 cf. et9; 22, 17), quam Christus dilexit, et seipsum tradidit pro ea, ut illam sanctificaret (Ep 5,26), quam sibi foedere indissolubili sociavit et indesinenter nutrit et fovet (Ep 5,29), et quam mundatam sibi voluit coniunctam et in dilectione ac fidelitate subditam (cf. Ep 5,24), quam tandem bonis caelestibus in aeternum cumulavit, ut Dei et Christi erga nos caritatem, quae omnem scientiam superat, comprehendamus (cf. Ep 3,19). Dum vero his in terris Ecclesia peregrinatur a Domino (cf. 2Co 5,6), tamquam exsulem se habet, ita ut quae sursum sunt quaerat et sapiat, ubi Christus est in dextera Dei sedens, ubi vita Ecclesiae abscondita est cum Christo in Deo, donec cum Sponso suo appareat in gloria (cf. Col 3,1-4).
The Son of God, in the human nature united to Himself, by His death and resurrection overcame death, redeemed man, and transformed him into a new creation (cf. Gal 6:15; 2 Cor 5:17). For by communicating His Spirit, He mystically constituted His brothers, called from all nations, as His own body.
¶57
Dei Filius, in natura humana Sibi unita, morte et resurrectione sua mortem superando, hominem redemit et in novam creaturam transformavit (cf. Ga 6,15 2Co 5,17). Communicando enim Spiritum suum, fratres suos, ex omnibus gentibus convocatos, tamquam corpus suum mystice constituit.
In that body the life of Christ is poured out into believers, who through the sacraments are united to Christ, suffering and glorified, in an hidden and real way. For through baptism we are conformed to Christ: "For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body" (1 Cor 12:13). By this sacred rite the association with the death and resurrection of Christ is represented and brought about: "For we were buried with Him by baptism into death; if we have been planted together in the likeness of His death, we shall be also in the likeness of His resurrection" (Rom 6:4-5). In the breaking of the eucharistic bread, really partaking of the Lord's body, we are raised to communion with Him and among ourselves. "Because there is one bread, we, many, are one body, all we who partake of one bread" (1 Cor 10:17). Thus we all become members of that body (cf. 1 Cor 12:27), each members one of another (Rom 12:5).
¶58
In corpore illo vita Christi in credentes diffunditur, qui Christo passo atque glorificato, per sacramenta arcano ac reali modo uniuntur. Per baptismum enim Christo conformamur: Etenim in uno Spiritu omnes nos in unum corpus baptizati sumus (l Cor. 12, 13). Quo sacro ritu consociatio cum morte et resurrectione Christi repraesentatur et efficitur: Consepulti enim sumus cum Illo per baptismum in mortem; si autem complantati facti sumus similitudini mortis Eius: simul et resurrectionis erimus (Rm 6,4-5). In fractione panis eucharistici de Corpore Domini realiter participantes, ad communionem cum Eo ac inter nos elevamur. Quoniam unus corpus multi sumus, omnes, qui de uno pane participamus (1Co 10,17). Ita nos omnes membra illius Corporis efficimur (cf. 1Co 12,27), singuli autem alter alterius membra (Rm 12,5).
Just as all the members of the human body, although many, form one body, so the faithful in Christ (cf. 1 Cor 12:12). Diversity of members and functions also flourishes in the building up of the body of Christ. There is one Spirit, who distributes His varied gifts, according to His riches and the necessities of ministries, for the utility of the Church (cf. 1 Cor 12:1-11). Among these gifts stands out the grace of the Apostles, to whose authority even the charismatic persons are subject by the Spirit Himself (cf. 1 Cor 14). The same Spirit, by Himself and by His power and by the internal connection of the members, unifying the body, produces and urges charity among the faithful. Hence, if one member suffers anything, all the members suffer with it; or if one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it (cf. 1 Cor 12:26).
¶59
Sicut vero omnia corporis humani membra, licet multa sint, unum tamen corpus efformant, ita fideles in Christo (cf. 1Co 12,12). Etiam in aedificatione corporis Christi diversitas viget membrorum et officiorum. Unus est Spiritus, qui varia sua dona, secundum divitias suas atque ministeriorum necessitates, ad Ecclesiae utilitatem dispertit (cf. 1Co 12,1-11). Inter quae dona praestat gratia Apostolorum, quorum auctoritati ipse Spiritus etiam charismaticos subdit (cf. 1Co 14). Idem Spiritus per Se suaque virtute atque interna membrorum connexione corpus unificans, caritatem inter fideles producit et urget. Unde, si quid patitur unum membrum, compatiuntur omnia membra; sive si unum membrum honoratur, congaudent omnia membra (cf. 1Co 12,26).
The Head of this body is Christ. He is the image of the invisible God, and in Him all things were created. He is before all, and all things hold together in Him. He is the head of the body, the Church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that He might hold first place in everything (cf. Col 1:15-18). By the greatness of His power He rules over heavenly and earthly things, and by His surpassing perfection and action He fills the whole body with the riches of His glory (cf. Eph 1:18-23).
¶60
Huius corporis Caput est Christus. Ipse est imago Dei invisibilis, in Eoque condita sunt universa. Ipse est ante omnes et omnia in Ipso constant. Ipse est caput corporis quod est Ecclesia. Ipse est principium, primogenitus ex mortuis, ut sit in omnibus primatum tenens (cf. Col 1,15-18). Magnitudine virtutis suae caelestibus et terrestribus dominatur, et supereminenti perfectione et operatione sua totum corpus gloriae suae divitiis replet (cf. Ep 1,18-23).
All members must be conformed to Him, until Christ be formed in them (cf. Gal 4:19). Therefore we are assumed into the mysteries of His life, configured with Him, comortified and conresuscitated, until we reign with Him (cf. Phil 3:21; 2 Tim 2:11; Eph 2:6; Col 2:12, etc.). Still pilgrimaging on earth, pressing His footprints in tribulation and persecution, we are consociated with His passions as the body to the Head, suffering with Him, that we may be glorified with Him (cf. Rom 8:17).
¶61
Omnia membra Ei conformari oportet, donec Christus formetur in eis (cf. Ga 4,19). Quapropter in vitae Eius mysteria adsumimur, cum Eo configurati, commortui et conresuscitati, donec cum Eo conregnemus (cf. Ph 3,21 2Tm 2,11 Ep 2,6 Col 2,12 etc.). In terris adhuc peregrinantes, Eiusque vestigia in tribulatione et persecutione prementes, Eius passionibus tamquam corpus Capiti consociamur, Ei compatientes, ut cum Eo conglorificemur (cf. Rm 8,17).
From Him the whole body, supplied and built up through joints and connections, grows into the increase of God (Col 2:19). He constantly disposes in His body, that is, the Church, the gifts of ministries, by which through His power we render services to one another for salvation, so that doing truth in charity, we may grow into Him in all things, who is our Head (cf. Eph 4:11-16). So that we may be continually renewed in Him (cf. Eph 4:23), He gave us of His Spirit, who, being one and the same in Head and members, so vivifies, unifies, and moves the whole body that His function could be compared by the holy Fathers to the office which the principle of life, that is, the soul, fulfills in the human body.
¶62
Ex Eo totum corpus, per nexus et coniunctiones subministratum et constructum, crescit in augmentum Dei (Col 2,19). Ipse in corpore suo, scilicet Ecclesia, dona ministrationum iugiter disponit, quibus Ipsius virtute nobis invicem ad salutem servitia praestamus, ut veritatem facientes in caritate, crescamus in Illum per omnia, qui est Caput nostrum (cf. Ep 4,11-16 gr.) . Ut autem in Illo incessanter renovemur (cf. Ep 4,23), dedit nobis de Spiritu suo, qui unus et idem in Capite et in membris exsistens, totum corpus ita vivificat, unificat et movet, ut Eius officium a sanctis Patribus comparari potuerit cum munere, quod principium vitae seu anima in corpore humano adimplet.
Christ indeed loves the Church as His spouse, having become the pattern of the man who loves his wife as his own body (cf. Eph 5:25-28); the Church herself is subject to her Head (ibid. 23-24). Because in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily (Col 2:9), He fills the Church, which is His body and fullness, with His divine gifts (cf. Eph 1:22-23), that she may extend and attain to all the fullness of God (cf. Eph 3:19).
¶63
Christus vero diligit Ecclesiam ut sponsam suam, exemplar factus viri diligentis uxorem suam ut corpus suum (cf. Ep 5,25-28); ipsa vero Ecclesia subiecta est Capiti suo (ibid. Ep 23-24). Quia in Ipso inhabitat omnis plenitudo divinitatis corporaliter (Col 2,9), Ecclesiam, quae corpus et plenitudo Eius est, divinis suis donis replet (cf. Ep 1,22-23), ut ipsa protendat et perveniat ad omnem plenitudinem Dei (cf. Ep 3,19).
Christ, the one Mediator, constituted and unceasingly sustains His holy Church, the community of faith, hope, and charity, here on earth as a visible structure, through which He diffuses truth and grace to all. But the society furnished with hierarchical organs and the mystical Body of Christ, the visible assembly and the spiritual community, the earthly Church and the Church enriched with heavenly goods, are not to be considered as two things, but form one complex reality which coalesces from the human and the divine element. Therefore it is assimilated, for no small analogy, to the mystery of the incarnate Word. For just as the assumed nature serves the divine Word as a living organ of salvation, indissolubly united to Him, not in a dissimilar manner does the social structure of the Church serve the Spirit of Christ, who vivifies her, for the increase of the body (cf. Eph 4:16).
¶64
Unicus Mediator Christus Ecclesiam suam sanctam, fidei, spei et caritatis communitatem his in terris ut compaginem visibilem constituit et indesinenter sustentat, qua veritatem et gratiam ad omnes diffundit. Societas autem organis hierarchicis instructa et mysticum Christi Corpus, coetus adspectabilis et communitas spiritualis, Ecclesia terrestris et Ecclesia caelestibus bonis ditata, non ut duae res considerandae sunt, sed unam realitatem complexam efformant, quae humano e divino coalescit elemento. Ideo ob non mediocrem analogiam incarnati Verbi mysterio assimilatur. Sicut enim natura assumpta Verbo divino ut vivum organum salutis, Ei indissolubiliter unitum, inservit, non dissimili modo socialis compago Ecclesiae Spiritui Christi, eam vivificanti, ad augmentum corporis inservit (cf. Ep 4,16).
This is the one Church of Christ, which we profess in the Creed as one, holy, catholic, and apostolic, which our Savior, after His resurrection, handed over to Peter to be fed (Jn 21:17), and entrusted to him and the other Apostles to be spread and governed (cf. Mt 28:18 ff.), and erected forever as the pillar and bulwark of truth (1 Tim 3:15). This Church, constituted and organized in this world as a society, subsists in the Catholic Church, governed by the successor of Peter and the bishops in communion with him, although many elements of sanctification and truth are found outside her structure, which, as gifts proper to the Church of Christ, impel toward catholic unity.
¶65
Haec est unica Christi Ecclesia, quam in Symbolo unam, sanctam, catholicam et apostolicam profitemur, quam Salvator noster, post resurrectionem suam Petro pascendam tradidit (Jn 21,17), eique ac ceteris Apostolis diffundendam et regendam commisit (cf. Mt 28,18 ss.), et in perpetuum ut columnam et firmamentum veritatis erexit (1Tm 3,15). Haec Ecclesia, in hoc mundo ut societas constituta et ordinata, subsistit in Ecclesia catholica, a successore Petri et Episcopis in eius communione gubernata, licet extra eius compaginem elementa plura sanctificationis et veritatis inveniantur, quae ut dona Ecclesiae Christi propria, ad unitatem catholicam impellunt.
Just as Christ accomplished the work of redemption in poverty and persecution, so the Church is called to enter the same path, that she may communicate the fruits of salvation to men. Christ Jesus, "when He was in the form of God... emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant" (Phil 2:6-7), and "for our sakes became poor, whereas He was rich" (2 Cor 8:9): so the Church, although she needs human resources to carry out her mission, is not raised up to seek earthly glory, but to spread abroad humility and self-denial by her example also. Christ was sent by the Father "to preach the good news to the poor, to heal the contrite of heart" (Lk 4:18), "to seek and to save what was lost" (Lk 19:10): similarly the Church surrounds with love all afflicted by human infirmity, indeed she recognizes in the poor and the suffering the image of her poor and suffering Founder, strives to relieve their want, and intends to serve Christ in them. While Christ, holy, innocent, undefiled (Heb 7:26), "knew no sin" (2 Cor 5:21), but came only to propitiate the offenses of the people (cf. Heb 2:17), the Church, embracing sinners in her bosom, holy and always to be purified, continually pursues penance and renewal.
¶66
Sicut autem Christus opus redemptionis in paupertate et persecutione perfecit, ita Ecclesia ad eamdem viam ingrediendam vocatur, ut fructus salutis hominibus communicet. Christus Iesus, cum in forma Dei esset, ... semetipsum exinanivit, formam servi accipiens (Ph 2,6) et propter nos egenus factus est, cum esset dives (2Co 8,9): ita Ecclesia, licet ad missionem suam exsequendam humanis opibus indigeat, non ad gloriam terrestrem quaerendam erigitur, sed ad humilitatem et abnegationem etiam exemplo suo divulgandas. Christus a Patre missus est evangelizare pauperibus, ... sanare contritos corde (Lc 4,18), quaerere et salvum facere quod perierat (Lc 19,10): similiter Ecclesia omnes infirmitate humana afflictos amore circumdat, imo in pauperibus et patientibus imaginem Fundatoris sui pauperis et patientis agnoscit, eorum inopiam sublevare satagit, et Christo in eis inservire intendit. Dum vero Christus, sanctus, innocens, impollutus (He 7,26), peccatum non novit (2Co 5,21), sed sola delicta populi repropitiare venit (cf. He 2,17), Ecclesia in proprio sinu peccatores complectens, sancta simul et semper purificanda, poenitentiam et renovationem continuo prosequitur.
Pilgrimaging amid the persecutions of the world and the consolations of God, the Church runs forward, announcing the cross and death of the Lord until He comes (cf. 1 Cor 11:26). She is strengthened by the power of the risen Lord, that, by patience and charity, she may overcome her afflictions and difficulties, both internal and external, and faithfully reveal His mystery in the world, albeit under shadows, until in the end it will be manifested in full light.
¶67
Inter persecutiones mundi et consolationes Dei peregrinando procurrit Ecclesia, crucem et mortem Domini annuntians, donec veniat (cf. 1Co 11,26). Virtute autem Domini resuscitati roboratur, ut afflictiones et difficultates suas, internas pariter et extrinsecas, patientia et caritate devincat, et mysterium Eius, licet sub umbris, fideliter tamen in mundo revelet, donec in fine lumine pleno manifestabitur.
At all times and among every nation, anyone who fears God and works justice is acceptable to Him (cf. Acts 10:35). Yet it pleased God to sanctify and save men not individually, apart from any mutual connection, but to make them into a people who would acknowledge Him in truth and serve Him holy. He therefore chose the Israelite people for Himself as a people, with whom He established a covenant and whom He gradually instructed, manifesting Himself and the purpose of His will in their history and sanctifying it for Himself. Yet all these things happened as a preparation and a figure of that new and perfect covenant to be made in Christ, and of the fuller revelation to be handed down through the incarnate Word of God Himself. "Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah... I will put my law in their bowels, and in their heart I will write it, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people... For all shall know me, from the least to the greatest, says the Lord" (Jer 31:31-34). Christ instituted this new covenant, namely the new testament in His blood (cf. 1 Cor 11:25), calling a people from Jews and Gentiles, which would coalesce into unity not according to the flesh but in the Spirit, and would be the new People of God.
¶68
CAPUT II. DE POPULO DEI
CHAPTER II. ON THE PEOPLE OF GOD
In omni quidem tempore et in omni gente Deo acceptus est quicumque timet Eum et operatur iustitiam (cf. Ac 10,35). Placuit tamen Deo homines non singulatim, quavis mutua connexione seclusa, sanctificare et salvare, sed eos in populum constituere, qui in veritate Ipsum agnosceret Ipsique sancte serviret. Plebem igitur israeliticam Sibi in populum elegit, quocum foedus instituit et quem gradatim instruxit, Sese atque propositum voluntatis suae in eius historia manifestando eumque Sibi sanctificando. Haec tamen omnia in praeparationem et figuram contigerunt foederis illius novi et perfecti, in Christo feriendi, et plenioris revelationis per Ipsum Dei Verbum carnem factum tradendae. Ecce dies veniunt, dicit Dominus, et feriam domui Israel et domui Iuda foedus novum... Dabo legem meam in visceribus eorum, et in corde eorum scribam eam, et ero eis in Deum, et ipsi erunt Mihi in populum... Omnes enim cognoscent Me, a minimo usque ad maximum, ait Dominus (Ier. 31, 31-34). Quod foedus novum Christus instituit, novum scilicet testamentum in suo sanguine (cf. 1Co 11,25), ex Iudaeis ac gentibus plebem vocans, quae non secundum carnem sed in Spiritu ad unitatem coalesceret, essetque novus Populus Dei.
For believers in Christ, reborn not of corruptible seed but of incorruptible through the word of the living God (cf. 1 Pet 1:23), not of flesh but of water and the Holy Spirit (cf. Jn 3:5-6), are finally made "a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition... who once were not a people, but now are the people of God" (1 Pet 2:9-10).
¶69
Credentes enim in Christum, renati non ex semine corruptibili, sed incorruptibili per verbum Dei vivi (cf. 1P 1,23), non ex carne sed ex aqua et Spiritu Sancto (cf. Jn 3,5-6), constituuntur tandem a genus electum, regale sacerdotium, gens sancta, populus acquisitionis ... qui aliquando non populus, nunc autem populus Dei (1P 2,9-10).
That messianic people has for its head Christ, who was delivered up for our sins and rose again for our justification (Rom 4:25), and now having obtained the name which is above all names, gloriously reigns in heaven. It has for its condition the dignity and liberty of the sons of God, in whose hearts the Holy Spirit dwells as in a temple. It has for its law the new commandment of loving as Christ loved us (cf. Jn 13:34). It has finally for its end the Kingdom of God, which has been begun on earth by God Himself, to be further enlarged, until it be also consummated by Him at the end of the ages, when Christ shall appear, our life (cf. Col 3:4), and "creation itself will be delivered from the slavery of corruption into the freedom of the glory of the sons of God" (Rom 8:21). Thus that messianic people, although it does not actually include all men, and often appears as a little flock, is nevertheless the firmest seed of unity, hope, and salvation for the whole human race. Constituted by Christ into a communion of life, charity, and truth, it is also assumed by Him as the instrument of redemption for all, and is sent forth into the whole world as the light of the world and the salt of the earth (cf. Mt 5:13-16).
¶70
Populus ille messianicus habet pro capite Christum, qui traditus est propter delicta nostra et resurrexit propter iustificationem nostram (Rm 4,25), et nunc nomen quod est super omne nomen adeptus, gloriose regnat in caelis. Habet pro conditione dignitatem libertatemque filiorum Dei, in quorum cordibus Spiritus Sanctus sicut in templo inhabitat. Habet pro lege mandatum novum diligendi sicut ipse Christus dilexit nos (cf. Jn 13,34). Habet tandem pro fine Regnum Dei, ab ipso Deo in terris inchoatum, ulterius dilatandum, donec in fine saeculorum ab Ipso etiam consummetur, cum Christus apparuerit, vita nostra (cf. Col 3,4), et ipsa creatura liberabitur a servitute corruptionis in libertatem gloriae filiorum Dei (Rm 8,21). Itaque populus ille messianicus, quamvis universos homines actu non comprehendat, et non semel ut pusillus grex appareat, pro toto tamen genere humano firmissimum est germen unitatis, spei et salutis. A Christo in communionem vitae, caritatis et veritatis constitutus, ab Eo etiam ut instrumentum redemptionis omnium adsumitur, et tamquam lux mundi et sal terrae (cf. Mt 5,13-16), ad universum mundum emittitur.
Just as Israel according to the flesh, which pilgrimaged in the desert, was already called the Church of God (2 Esd. 13:1; cf. Num 20:4; Dt 23:1 ff.), so the new Israel, which goes forward in the present age seeking the future and abiding city (cf. Heb 13:14), is also called the Church of Christ (cf. Mt 16:18), for He acquired her by His blood (cf. Acts 20:28), filled her with His Spirit, and provided her with suitable means of visible and social union. God convoked and constituted the congregation of those who look believingly upon Jesus, the author of salvation and the principle of unity and peace, as the Church, that she might be for all and each the visible sacrament of this saving unity. To be extended to all regions, she enters into the history of men, yet at the same time transcends times and the boundaries of peoples. Proceeding through temptations and tribulations, the Church is strengthened by the power of God's grace promised to her by the Lord, that in the weakness of the flesh she may not falter from perfect fidelity, but remain a worthy spouse of her Lord, and under the action of the Holy Spirit may not cease to renew herself, until through the cross she may come to the light which knows no setting.
¶71
Sicut vero Israel secundum carnem, qui in deserto peregrinabatur, Dei Ecclesia iam appellatur (2 Esd. 13,1 cf. Nb 20,4 Dt 23,1 ss ), ita novus Israel qui in praesenti saeculo incedens, futuram eamque manentem civitatem inquirit (cf. He 13,14), etiam Ecclesia Christi nuncupatur (cf. Mt 16,18), quippe quam Ipse sanguine suo acquisivit (cf. Ac 20,28) suo Spiritu replevit, aptisque mediis unionis visibilis et socialis instruxit. Deus congregationem eorum qui in Iesum, salutis auctorem et unitatis pacisque principium, credentes aspiciunt, convocavit et constituit Ecclesiam, ut sit universis et singulis sacramentum visibile huius salutiferae unitatis. Ad universas regiones extendenda, in historiam hominum intrat, dum tamen simul tempora et fines populorum transcendit. Per tentationes vero et tribulationes procedens Ecclesia virtute gratiae Dei sibi a Domino promissae confortatur, ut in infirmitate carnis a perfecta fidelitate non deficiat, sed Domini sui digna sponsa remaneat, et sub actione Spiritus Sancti, seipsam renovare non desinat, donec per crucem perveniat ad lucem, quae nescit occasum.
Christ the Lord, the Pontiff taken from among men (cf. Heb 5:1-5), made the new people "a kingdom and priests to God and His Father" (Rev 1:6; cf. 5:9-10). For the baptized, by regeneration and the anointing of the Holy Spirit, are consecrated into a spiritual house and a holy priesthood, that through all the works of Christian men they may offer spiritual sacrifices and proclaim the virtues of Him who called them out of darkness into His admirable light (cf. 1 Pet 2:4-10). Therefore all the disciples of Christ, persevering in prayer and praising God (cf. Acts 2:42-47), should present themselves as a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God (cf. Rom 12:1), everywhere on earth bear witness to Christ, and give an answer to those who ask concerning the hope of eternal life that is in them (cf. 1 Pet 3:15).
¶72
Christus Dominus, Pontifex ex hominibus assumptus (cf. He 5,1-5) novum populum fecit regnum et sacerdotes Deo et Patri suo (Ap 1,6 cf. Ap 5,9-10). Baptizati enim, per regenerationem et Spiritus Sancti unctionem consecrantur in domum spiritualem et sacerdotium sanctum, ut per omnia opera hominis christiani spirituales offerant hostias, et virtutes annuntient Eius qui de tenebris eos vocavit in admirabile lumen suum (cf. 1P 2,4-10). Ideo universi discipuli Christi, in oratione perseverantes et collaudantes Deum (cf. Ac 2,42-47), seipsos hostiam viventem, sanctam, Deo placentem exhibeant (cf. Rm 12,1), ubique terrarum de Christo testimonium perhibeant, atque poscentibus rationem reddant de ea quae in eis est spe vitae aeternae (cf. 1P 3,15).
The common priesthood of the faithful and the ministerial or hierarchical priesthood, although they differ in essence and not only in degree, are nevertheless ordered to one another; for each in its own peculiar way participates in the one priesthood of Christ. For the ministerial priest, by the sacred power which he enjoys, forms and rules the priestly people, confects the eucharistic sacrifice in the person of Christ and offers it to God in the name of the whole people; the faithful, by virtue of their royal priesthood, concur in the offering of the Eucharist, and exercise it in the reception of the sacraments, in prayer and thanksgiving, in the testimony of a holy life, in self-denial and active charity.
¶73
Sacerdotium autem commune fidelium et sacerdotium ministeriale seu hierarchicum, licet essentia et non gradu tantum differant, ad invicem tamen ordinantur; unum enim et alterum suo peculiari modo de uno Christi sacerdotio participant. Sacerdos quidem ministerialis, potestate sacra qua gaudet, populum sacerdotalem efformat ac regit, sacrificium eucharisticum in persona Christi conficit illudque nomine totius populi Deo offert; fideles vero, vi regalis sui sacerdotii, in oblationem Eucharistiae concurrunt, illudque in sacramentis suscipiendis, in oratione et gratiarum actione, testimonio vitae sanctae, abnegatione et actuosa caritate exercent.
The sacred and organically constructed nature of the priestly community is brought into act both through the sacraments and through the virtues. By baptism the faithful are incorporated into the Church, are deputed by the character to the cult of the Christian religion, and, regenerated as sons of God, are bound to profess before men the faith which they have received from God through the Church. By the sacrament of confirmation they are more perfectly bound to the Church, are enriched with the special strength of the Holy Spirit, and are thus more strictly obliged to spread and defend the faith as true witnesses of Christ both by word and by deed. Partaking of the eucharistic sacrifice, the source and summit of the whole Christian life, they offer the divine Victim to God and themselves with it; thus both by the oblation and by the sacred communion, not promiscuously but each in his own way, all have their own part in the liturgical action. Moreover, restored by the body of Christ in the sacred synaxis, they concretely exhibit the unity of the People of God, which is fittingly signified and wonderfully brought about by this most august sacrament.
¶74
Indoles sacra et organice exstructa communitatis sacerdotalis et per sacramenta et per virtutes ad actum deducitur. Fideles per baptismum in Ecclesia incorporati, ad cultum religionis christianae charactere deputantur et, in filios Dei regenerati, fidem quam a Deo per Ecclesiam acceperunt coram hominibus profiteri tenentur. Sacramento confirmationis perfectius Ecclesiae vinculantur, speciali Spiritus Sancti robore ditantur, sicque ad fidem tamquam veri testes Christi verbo et opere simul diffundendam et defendendam arctius obligantur. Sacrificium eucharisticum, totius vitae christianae fontem et culmen, participantes, divinam Victimam Deo offerunt atque seipsos cum Ea; ita tum oblatione tum sacra communione, non promiscue sed alii aliter, omnes in liturgica actione partem propriam agunt. Porro corpore Christi in sacra synaxi refecti, unitatem Populi Dei, quae hoc augustissimo sacramento apte significatur et mirabiliter efficitur, modo concreto exhibent.
Those who approach the sacrament of penance obtain pardon from God's mercy for the offense committed against Him, and are at the same time reconciled with the Church, which they wounded by sinning, and which by charity, example, and prayer labors for their conversion. By the sacred anointing of the sick and the prayer of the presbyters, the whole Church commends the sick to the suffering and glorified Lord, that He may relieve and save them (cf. Jas 5:14-16), indeed she exhorts them to associate themselves freely with the passion and death of Christ (cf. Rom 8:17; Col 1:24; 2 Tim 2:11-12; 1 Pet 4:13), and to contribute to the good of the People of God. Again, those among the faithful who are marked with sacred Order are instituted in the name of Christ to feed the Church by the word and grace of God. Finally, Christian spouses, by the virtue of the sacrament of matrimony, by which the mystery of unity and fruitful love between Christ and the Church is signified and participated in (cf. Eph 5:32), help one another in conjugal life and in the reception and education of offspring to attain holiness, and so in their state and order of life they have their own gift in the People of God (cf. 1 Cor 7:7). For from this marriage proceeds the family, in which new citizens of human society are born, who by the grace of the Holy Spirit are made sons of God by baptism to perpetuate the People of God through the course of the ages. In this as it were domestic church, parents should be for their children the first heralds of the faith by word and example, and they must foster the vocation proper to each, especially the sacred vocation.
¶75
Qui vero ad sacramentum poenitentiae accedunt, veniam offensionis Deo illatae ab Eius misericordia obtinent et simul reconciliantur cum Ecclesia, quam peccando vulneraverunt, et quae eorum conversioni caritate, exemplo, precibus adlaborat. Sacra infirmorum unctione atque oratione presbyterorum Ecclesia tota aegrotantes Domino patienti et glorificato commendat, ut eos alleviet et salvet (cf. Jc 5,14-16), immo eos hortatur ut sese Christi passioni et morti libere sociantes (cf. Rm 8,17 Col 1,24 2Tm 2,11-12 1P 4,13), ad bonum Populi Dei conferant. Iterum, qui inter fideles sacro Ordine insigniuntur, ad Ecclesiam verbo et gratia Dei pascendam, Christi nomine instituuntur Tandem coniuges christiani, virtute matrimonii sacramenti, quo mysterium unitatis et fecundi amoris inter Christum et Ecclesiam significant atque participant (cf. Ep 5,32), se invicem in vita coniugali necnon prolis susceptione et educatione ad sanctitatem adiuvant, adeoque in suo vitae statu et ordine proprium suum in Populo Dei donum habent (cf. 1Co 7,7). Ex hoc enim connubio procedit familia, in qua nascuntur novi societatis humanae cives, qui per Spiritus Sancti gratiam, ad Populum Dei saeculorum decursu perpetuandum, baptismo in filios Dei constituuntur. In hac velut Ecclesia domestica parentes verbo et exemplo sint pro filiis suis primi fidei praecones, et vocationem unicuique propriam, sacram vero peculiari cura, foveant oportet.
Furnished with so many and so great salutary means, all Christ's faithful, of whatever condition and state, are called by the Lord, each in his own way, to the perfection of holiness by which the Father Himself is perfect.
¶76
Tot ac tantis salutaribus mediis muniti, christifideles omnes, cuiusvis conditionis ac status, ad perfectionem sanctitatis qua Pater ipse perfectus est, sua quisque via, a Domino vocantur.
The holy People of God also participates in the prophetic office of Christ, spreading abroad a living witness of Him especially through a life of faith and charity, and offering to God a sacrifice of praise, the fruit of lips confessing His name (cf. Heb 13:15). The whole body of the faithful, who have the anointing from the Holy One (cf. 1 Jn 2:20, 27), cannot err in believing, and manifests this peculiar property through the supernatural sense of faith of the whole people, when it exhibits universal consent concerning matters of faith and morals from the bishops down to the last of the lay faithful. For by that sense of faith, which is aroused and sustained by the Spirit of truth, the People of God, under the guidance of the sacred magisterium, which it faithfully obeys, receives not the word of men but truly the word of God (cf. 1 Th 2:13), adheres indefectibly to the faith once delivered to the saints (cf. Jude 3), penetrates it more deeply by right judgment, and more fully applies it in life.
¶77
Populus Dei sanctus de munere quoque prophetico Christi participat, vivum Eius testimonium maxime per vitam fidei ac caritatis diffundendo, et Deo hostiam laudis offerendo, fructum labiorum confitentium nomini Eius (cf. He 13,15). Universitas fidelium, qui unctionem habent a Sancto (cf. 1Jn 2,20 1Jn 2,27), in credendo falli nequit, atque hanc suam peculiarem proprietatem mediante supernaturali sensu fidei totius populi manifestat, cum ab Episcopis usque ad extremos laicos fideles universalem suum consensum de rebus fidei et morum exhibet. Illo enim sensu fidei, qui a Spiritu veritatis excitatur et sustentatur, Populus Dei sub ductu sacri magisterii, cui fideliter obsequens, iam non verbum hominum, sed vere accipit verbum Dei (cf. 1Th 2,13), semel traditae sanctis fidei (cf. Jud 1,3), indefectibiliter adhaeret, recto iudicio in eam profundius penetrat eamque in vita plenius applicat.
Moreover, the same Holy Spirit not only sanctifies and leads the people of God through the sacraments and ministries and adorns them with virtues, but, dividing His gifts to each as He wills (1 Cor 12:11), He distributes special graces also among the faithful of every order, by which He renders them apt and ready to undertake various works or offices for the renewal and greater building up of the Church, according to that: "To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good" (1 Cor 12:7). These charisms, whether the more remarkable or the simpler and more widely diffused, since they are exceedingly suited and useful to the needs of the Church, are to be received with thanksgiving and consolation. Extraordinary gifts, however, are not rashly to be sought after, nor are the fruits of apostolic works to be presumptuously expected from them; but the judgment concerning their genuineness and orderly exercise pertains to those who are in charge of the Church, to whom it belongs especially not to extinguish the Spirit, but to test all things and hold fast to what is good (cf. 1 Th 5:12, 19-21).
¶78
Idem praeterea Spiritus Sanctus non tantum per sacramenta et ministeria populum Dei sanctificat et ducit eumque virtutibus ornat, sed dona sua dividens singulis prout vult (1Co 12,11), inter omnis ordinis fideles distribuit gratias quoque speciales, quibus illos aptos et promptos reddit ad suscipienda varia opera vel officia, pro renovatione et ampliore aedificatione Ecclesiae proficua, secundum illud: Unicuique datur manifestatio Spiritus ad utilitatem (1Co 12,7). Quae charismata, sive clarissima, sive etiam simpliciora et latius diffusa, cum sint necessitatibus Ecclesiae apprime accommodata et utilia, cum gratiarum actione ac consolatione accipienda sunt. Dona autem extraordinaria non sunt temere expetenda, neque praesumptuose ab eis sperandi sunt fructus operarum apostolicarum; sed iudicium de eorum genuinitate et ordinato exercitio ad eos pertinet, qui in Ecclesia praesunt, et quibus speciatim competit, non Spiritum exstinguere, sed omnia probare et quod bonum est tenere (cf. 1Th 5,12 1Th 5,19-21).
All men are called to the new People of God. Therefore this people, remaining one and unique, is to be spread throughout the whole world and through all ages, that the purpose of God's will may be fulfilled, who created human nature in the beginning one, and decreed to gather together at last His sons who were scattered (cf. Jn 11:52). For to this end God sent His Son, whom He appointed heir of all things (cf. Heb 1:2), that He might be Teacher, King, and Priest of all, the Head of the new and universal people of the sons of God. Finally, to this end God sent the Spirit of His Son, the Lord and Giver of life, who is the principle of gathering and unity for the whole Church and for each and every believer in the doctrine of the Apostles and in communion, in the breaking of bread and in prayers (cf. Acts 2:42).
¶79
Ad novum Populum Dei cuncti vocantur homines. Quapropter hic populus, unus et unicus manens, ad universum mundum et per omnia saecula est dilatandus, ut propositum adimpleatur voluntatis Dei, qui naturam humanam in initio condidit unam, filiosque suos, qui erant dispersi, in unum tandem congregare statuit (cf. Jn 11,52). Ad hoc enim misit Deus Filium suum, quem constituit heredem universorum (cf. He 1,2), ut sit Magister, Rex et Sacerdos omnium, Caput novi et universalis populi filiorum Dei. Ad hoc tandem misit Deus Spiritum Filii sui, Dominum et Vivificantem, qui pro tota Ecclesia et singulis universisque credentibus principium est congregationis et unitatis in doctrina Apostolorum et communione, fractione panis et orationibus (cf. Ac 2,42, gr.) .
Therefore, among all the nations of the earth, there is one People of God, since it borrows its citizens from all nations, of a kingdom indeed not earthly but heavenly. For all the faithful scattered through the whole world communicate with one another in the Holy Spirit, and thus he who sits at Rome knows the Indian to be his member. Since, moreover, the Kingdom of Christ is not of this world (cf. Jn 18:36), therefore the Church, this People of God, bringing in this Kingdom, takes nothing away from the temporal good of any people, but on the contrary fosters and assumes the faculties, resources, and customs of peoples, insofar as they are good, and, in assuming them, purifies, strengthens, and elevates them. For she is mindful that she must gather with that King to whom the nations are given as an inheritance (cf. Ps 2:8), and into whose city they bring gifts and offerings (cf. Ps 71:10; Is 60:4-7; Rev 21:24). This character of universality which adorns the People of God is a gift of the Lord Himself, by which the Catholic Church effectively and perpetually tends toward recapitulating the whole of humanity with all its goods under Christ the Head, in the unity of His Spirit.
¶80
Omnibus itaque gentibus terrae inest unus Populus Dei, cum ex omnibus gentibus mutuetur suos cives, Regni quidem indolis non terrestris, sed caelestis. Cuncti enim per orbem sparsi fideles cum ceteris in Spiritu Sancto communicant, et sic qui Romae sedet, Indos scit membrum suum esse. Cum autem Regnum Christi de hoc mundo non sit (cf. Jn 18,36), ideo Ecclesia seu Populus Dei, hoc Regnum inducens, nihil bono temporali cuiusvis populi subtrahit, sed e contra facultates et copias moresque populorum, quantum bona sunt, fovet et assumit, assumendo vero purificat, roborat et elevat. Memor est enim se cum illo Rege colligere debere, Cui gentes in hereditatem datae sunt (cf. Ps 2,8), et in Cuius civitatem dona et munera adducunt (cf. Ps 71, 10; Is. 60, 4-7; Apoc. 21, 24). Hic universalitatis character, qui Populum Dei condecorat, ipsius Domini donum est, quo catholica Ecclesia efficaciter et perpetuo tendit ad recapitulandam totam humanitatem cum omnibus bonis eius, sub Capite Christo, in unitate Spiritus Eius.
By virtue of this catholicity, each part brings its own gifts to the other parts and to the whole Church, so that the whole and each part are increased by all who communicate with one another and conspire to fullness in unity. Hence it comes about that the People of God is not only gathered from diverse peoples, but also in itself is compounded of various orders. For there is diversity among its members, whether according to offices, while some exercise the sacred ministry for the good of their brothers, or according to condition and ordering of life, while many in the religious state, tending to holiness by a narrower path, stimulate their brothers by their example. Hence also in ecclesiastical communion there legitimately exist particular churches, enjoying their own traditions, the primacy of the Chair of Peter remaining intact, which presides over the whole assembly of charity, protects legitimate varieties, and at the same time watches lest particularities, far from harming unity, rather serve it. Hence, finally, between the various parts of the Church there are bonds of intimate communion with respect to spiritual riches, apostolic workers, and temporal resources. For the members of the People of God are called to communicate goods, and the words of the Apostle apply also to each of the churches: "As each has received a gift, ministering it to one another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God" (1 Pet 4:10).
¶81
Vi huius catholicitatis, singulae partes propria dona ceteris partibus et toti Ecclesiae afferunt, ita ut totum et singulae partes augeantur ex omnibus invicem communicantibus et ad plenitudinem in unitate conspirantibus. Inde fit ut Populus Dei non tantum ex diversis populis congregetur, sed etiam in seipso ex variis ordinibus confletur. Adest enim inter membra eius diversitas, sive secundum officia, dum aliqui sacro ministerio in bonum fratrum suorum funguntur, sive secundum condicionem et vitae ordinationem, dum plures in statu religioso, arctiore via ad sanctitatem tendentes, fratres exemplo suo stimulant. Inde etiam in ecclesiastica communione legitime adsunt Ecclesiae particulares, propriis traditionibus fruentes, integro manente primatu Petri Cathedrae, quae universo caritatis coetui praesidet, legitimas varietates tuetur et simul invigilat ut particularia, nedum unitati noceant, ei potius inserviant. Inde denique inter diversas Ecclesiae partes vincula intimae communionis quoad divitias spirituales, operarios apostolicos et temporalia subsidia. Ad communicandum enim bona vocantur membra Populi Dei, et de singulis etiam Ecclesiis valent verba Apostoli: Unusquisque, sicut accepit gratiam, in alterutrum illam administrantes, sicut boni dispensatores multiformis gratiae Dei (1P 4,10).
To this catholic unity of the People of God, which prefigures and promotes universal peace, all men are called, and to it in various ways pertain or are ordered, whether Catholic faithful, or other believers in Christ, or finally all men universally, called by the grace of God to salvation.
¶82
Ad hanc igitur catholicam Populi Dei unitatem, quae pacem universalem praesignat et promovet, omnes vocantur homines, ad eamque variis modis pertinent vel ordinantur sive fideles catholici, sive alii credentes in Christo, sive denique omnes universaliter homines, gratia Dei ad salutem vocati.
Therefore the holy Synod first turns its mind to the Catholic faithful. It teaches, based on Sacred Scripture and Tradition, that this pilgrimaging Church is necessary for salvation. For Christ alone is the Mediator and the way of salvation, who is made present to us in His body, which is the Church, and He Himself, by inculcating the necessity of faith and baptism in express words (cf. Mk 16:16; Jn 3:5), confirmed at the same time the necessity of the Church, into which men enter through baptism as through a door. Hence those men could not be saved who, not ignorant that the Catholic Church was established as necessary by God through Jesus Christ, nevertheless have been unwilling either to enter it or to persevere in it.
¶83
Ad fideles ergo catholicos imprimis Sancta Synodus animum vertit. Docet autem, Sacra Scriptura et Traditione innixa, Ecclesiam hanc peregrinantem necessariam esse ad salutem. Unus enim Christus est Mediator ac via salutis, qui in Corpore suo, quod est Ecclesia, praesens nobis fit, Ipse autem necessitatem fidei et baptismi expressis verbis inculcando (cf. Mc 16,16 Jn 3,5), necessitatem Ecclesiae, in quam homines per baptismum tamquam per ianuam intrant, simul confirmavit. Quare illi homines salvari non possent, qui Ecclesiam Catholicam a Deo per Iesum Christum ut necessariam esse conditam non ignorantes, tamen vel in eam intrare, vel in eadem perseverare noluerint.
Those are fully incorporated into the society of the Church who, having the Spirit of Christ, accept her entire order and all the means of salvation established in her, and are joined in the visible structure of the Church with Christ, who governs her through the Supreme Pontiff and the bishops, namely by the bonds of profession of faith, the sacraments, ecclesiastical governance, and communion. Yet he is not saved who, although incorporated into the Church, does not persevere in charity, remaining in the bosom of the Church indeed in body but not in heart. Let all children of the Church be mindful that their eminent condition is to be ascribed not to their own merits but to the special grace of Christ; to which if they do not correspond in thought, word, and deed, far from being saved, they will be more severely judged.
¶84
Illi plene Ecclesiae societati incorporantur, qui Spiritum Christi habentes, integram eius ordinationem omniaque media salutis in ea instituta accipiunt, et in eiusdem compage visibili cum Christo, eam per Summum Pontificem atque Episcopos regente, iunguntur, vinculis nempe professionis fidei, sacramentorum et ecclesiastici regiminis ac communionis. Non salvatur tamen, licet Ecclesiae incorporetur, qui in caritate non perseverans, in Ecclesiae sinu corpore quidem, sed non corde remanet. Memores autem sint omnes Ecclesiae filii condicionem suam eximiam non propriis meritis sed peculiari gratiae Christi esse adscribendam; cui si cogitatione, verbo et opere non respondent, nedum salventur, severius iudicabuntur.
Catechumens who, moved by the Holy Spirit, seek with explicit will to be incorporated into the Church, are by that very desire joined to her; Mother Church already embraces them as her own with love and care.
¶85
Catechumeni qui, Spiritu Sancto movente, explicita voluntate ut Ecclesiae incorporentur expetunt, hoc ipso voto cum ea coniunguntur; quos iam ut suos dilectione curaque complectitur Mater Ecclesia.
With those who, baptized, are adorned with the Christian name, yet do not profess the whole faith or do not preserve the unity of communion under the successor of Peter, the Church knows that she is joined for many reasons. For there are many who hold sacred Scripture in honor as the norm of believing and living, show a sincere religious zeal, lovingly believe in God the Father Almighty and in Christ, the Son of God the Savior, are marked by baptism, by which they are joined to Christ, indeed also recognize and receive other sacraments in their own churches or ecclesiastical communities. Many among them also enjoy the episcopate, celebrate the holy Eucharist, and foster piety toward the Virgin Mother of God. There follows a communion of prayers and other spiritual benefits; indeed, a certain true union in the Holy Spirit, who by His sanctifying power operates with gifts and graces also in them, and has strengthened some of them even to the shedding of blood. Thus the Spirit arouses in all Christ's disciples a desire and an action that all may, in the manner established by Christ, be peacefully united in one flock under one Shepherd. That this may be obtained, Mother Church does not cease to pray, hope, and work, and exhorts her sons to purification and renewal, that the sign of Christ may shine more clearly upon the face of the Church.
¶86
Cum illis qui, baptizati, christiano nomine decorantur, integram autem fidem non profitentur vel unitatem communionis sub Successore Petri non servant, Ecclesia semetipsam novit plures ob rationes coniunctam. Sunt enim multi, qui sacram Scripturam ut normam credendi et vivendi in honore habent sincerumque zelum religiosum ostendunt, amanter credunt in Deum Patrem omnipotentem et in Christum, Filium Dei Salvatorem, baptismo signantur, quo Christo coniunguntur, imo et alia sacramenta in propriis Ecclesiis vel communitatibus ecclesiasticis agnoscunt et recipiunt. Plures inter illos et episcopatu gaudent, Sacram Eucharistiam celebrant necnon pietatem erga Deiparam Virginem fovent. Accedit orationum aliorumque beneficiorum spiritualium communio; imo vera quaedam in Spiritu Sancto coniunctio, quippe qui donis et gratiis etiam in illis sua virtute sanctificante operatur, et quosdam illorum usque ad sanguinis effusionem roboravit. Ita Spiritus in cunctis Christi discipulis desiderium actionemque suscitat, ut omnes, modo a Christo statuto, in uno grege sub uno Pastore pacifice uniantur. Quod ut obtineat, Ecclesia Mater precari, sperare et agere non desinit, filiosque ad purificationem et renovationem exhortatur, ut signum Christi super faciem Ecclesiae clarius effulgeat.
Those who have not yet received the Gospel are related to the People of God in various ways. First, that people to whom the covenants and promises were given and from whom Christ was born according to the flesh (cf. Rom 9:4-5), a people most dear by election because of the fathers: for the gifts and calling of God are without repentance (cf. Rom 11:28-29). But the plan of salvation also embraces those who acknowledge the Creator, among whom first are the Muslims, who, professing to hold the faith of Abraham, adore together with us the one God, merciful, who will judge men on the last day. Nor is God Himself far from others who seek the unknown God in shadows and images, since He gives to all life and inspiration and all things (cf. Acts 17:25-28), and the Savior wills that all men be saved (cf. 1 Tim 2:4). For those who without fault are ignorant of the Gospel of Christ and His Church, yet seek God with a sincere heart, and try, under the influx of grace, to fulfill His will known through the dictate of conscience, can attain eternal salvation. Nor does divine Providence deny the aids necessary for salvation to those who, without fault, have not yet arrived at explicit knowledge of God and strive to attain a right life not without divine grace. For whatever good and truth is found among them is esteemed by the Church as a preparation for the Gospel, and as given by Him who illuminates every man, that at last he may have life. But often men, deceived by the Evil One, have become vain in their thoughts, and have exchanged the truth of God for a lie, serving the creature rather than the Creator (cf. Rom 1:21, 25), or living and dying without God in this world are exposed to final desperation. Therefore, to promote the glory of God and the salvation of all these, the Church, mindful of the Lord's command saying, "Preach the Gospel to every creature" (Mk 16:16), sedulously fosters missions.
¶87
Ii tandem qui Evangelium nondum acceperunt, ad Populum Dei diversis rationibus ordinantur. In primis quidem populus ille cui data fuerunt testamenta et promissa et ex quo Christus ortus est secundum carnem (cf. Rm 9,4-5), populus secundum electionem carissimus propter patres: sine poenitentia enim sunt dona et vocatio Dei (cf. Rm 11,28-29). Sed propositum salutis et eos amplectitur, qui Creatorem agnoscunt, inter quos imprimis Musulmanos, qui fidem Abrahae se tenere profitentes, nobiscum Deum adorant unicum, misericordem, homines die novissimo iudicaturum Neque ab aliis, qui in umbris et imaginibus Deum ignotum quaerunt, ab huiusmodi Deus ipse longe est, cum det omnibus vitam et inspirationem et omnia (cf. Ac 17,25-28), et Salvator velit omnes homines salvos fieri (cf. 1Tm 2,4). Qui enim Evangelium Christi Eiusque Ecclesiam sine culpa ignorantes, Deum tamen sincero corde quaerunt, Eiusque voluntatem per conscientiae dictamen agnitam, operibus adimplere, sub influxu gratiae, conantur, aeternam salutem consequi possunt. Nec divina Providentia auxilia ad salutem necessaria denegat his qui sine culpa ad expressam agnitionem Dei nondum pervenerunt et rectam vitam non sine divina gratia assequi nituntur. Quidquid enim boni et veri apud illos invenitur, ab Ecclesia tamquam praeparatio evangelica aestimatur et ab Illo datum qui illuminat omnem hominem, ut tandem vitam habeat. At saepius homines, a Maligno decepti, evanuerunt in cogitationibus suis, et commutaverunt veritatem Dei in mendacium, servientes creaturae magis quam Creatori (cf. Rm 1,21 Rm 1,25) vel sine Deo viventes ac morientes in hoc mundo, extremae desperationi exponuntur. Quapropter ad gloriam Dei et salutem istorum omnium promovendam, Ecclesia, memor mandati Domini dicentis: Praedicate Evangelium omni creaturae (Mc 16,16), missiones fovere sedulo curat.
For just as the Son was sent by the Father, so He also sent the Apostles (cf. Jn 20:21), saying: "Go therefore and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you. And behold I am with you all days even to the consummation of the world" (Mt 28:18-20). This solemn command of Christ to announce the saving truth the Church received from the Apostles to fulfill even to the ends of the earth (cf. Acts 1:8). Hence she makes her own the words of the Apostle: "Woe to me if I do not preach the Gospel!" (1 Cor 9:16), and therefore she continues unceasingly in sending heralds, until the new churches are fully constituted and they themselves continue the work of evangelizing. For she is compelled by the Holy Spirit to cooperate, that the purpose of God, who made Christ the principle of salvation for the whole world, may be accomplished by effect. By preaching the Gospel, the Church draws her hearers to faith and confession of faith, disposes them for baptism, snatches them from the slavery of error, and incorporates them into Christ, that through charity they may grow into Him to fullness.
¶88
Sicut enim Filius missus est a Patre, et Ipse Apostolos misit (cf. Jn 20,21), dicens: Euntes ergo docete omnes gentes, baptizantes eos in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti, docentes eos servare omnia quaecumque mandavi vobis. Et ecce Ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus usque ad consummationem saeculi (Mt 28,18-20). Quod solemne Christi mandatum annuntiandi veritatem salutarem Ecclesia ab Apostolis recepit adimplendum usque ad ultimum terrae (cf. Ac 1,8). Unde sua facit verba Apostoli: Vae... mihi est si non evangelizavero! (2Co 9,16), ideoque in mittendis praeconibus indesinenter pergit, usquedum novellae Ecclesiae plene constituantur atque opus evangelizandi et ipsae continuent. A Spiritu Sancto enim ad cooperandum compellitur, ut propositum Dei, qui Christum principium salutis pro universo mundo constituit, effectu compleatur. Praedicando Evangelium, Ecclesia audientes ad fidem confessionemque fidei allicit, ad baptismum disponit, a servitute erroris eripit, eosque Christo incorporat, ut per caritatem in Illum usque ad plenitudinem crescant.
Moreover, she does her work that whatever good is found sown in the heart and mind of men or in the proper rites and cultures of peoples may not only not perish but be healed, elevated, and consummated for the glory of God, the confusion of the demon, and the beatitude of man. Upon each disciple of Christ the burden of disseminating the faith falls according to his part. But although any believer can baptize, it is the priest's part to perfect the building of the Body by the eucharistic sacrifice, fulfilling the words of God through the prophet: "From the rising of the sun even to the going down, my name is great among the nations, and in every place a clean oblation is sacrificed and offered to my name" (Mal 1:11). Thus, at the same time, the Church prays and labors, that the fullness of the whole world may pass into the People of God, the Body of the Lord, and the Temple of the Holy Spirit, and that in Christ, the Head of all, all honor and glory be rendered to the Creator and Father of all.
¶89
Opera autem sua efficit ut quidquid boni in corde menteque hominum vel in propriis ritibus et culturis populorum seminatum invenitur, non tantum non pereat, sed sanetur, elevetur et consummetur ad gloriam Dei, confusionem daemonis et beatitudinem hominis. Cuilibet discipulo Christi onus fidei disseminandae pro parte sua incumbit. Sed si quilibet credentes baptizare potest, sacerdotis tamen est aedificationem Corporis sacrificio eucharistico perficere, adimplendo verba Dei per prophetam: Ab ortu solis usque ad occasum magnum est nomen meum in gentibus, et in omni loco sacrificatur et offertur nomini meo oblatio munda (Ml 1,11). Ita autem simul orat et laborat Ecclesia, ut in Populum Dei, Corpus Domini et Templum Spiritus Sancti, totius mundi transeat plenitudo, et in Christo, omnium Capite, reddatur universorum Creatori ac Patri omnis honor et gloria.
Christ the Lord, to feed the People of God and always increase it, instituted various ministries in His Church, which tend to the good of the whole Body. For the ministers, who are endowed with sacred power, serve their brothers, that all who belong to the People of God, and therefore enjoy true Christian dignity, may, conspiring freely and orderly to the same end, attain salvation.
¶90
CAPUT III. DE CONSTITUTIONE HIERARCHICA ECCLESIAE ET IN SPEICE DE EPISCOPATU
CHAPTER III. ON THE HIERARCHICAL CONSTITUTION OF THE CHURCH AND IN PARTICULAR ON THE EPISCOPATE
Christus Dominus, ad Populum Dei pascendum semperque augendum, in Ecclesia sua varia ministeria instituit, quae ad bonum totius Corporis tendunt. Ministri enim, qui sacra potestate pollent, fratribus suis inserviunt, ut omnes qui de Populo Dei sunt, ideoque vera dignitate christiana gaudent, ad eumdem finem libere et ordinatim conspirantes, ad salutem perveniant.
This sacred Synod, following in the footsteps of the First Vatican Council, teaches and declares with it that Jesus Christ the eternal Shepherd built the holy Church, sending the Apostles as He Himself was sent by the Father (cf. Jn 20:21); He willed that their successors, namely the bishops, should be pastors in His Church until the consummation of the world. So that the episcopate itself might be one and undivided, He placed blessed Peter before the other Apostles, and in Him established the perpetual and visible principle and foundation of unity of faith and communion. This doctrine concerning the institution, perpetuity, force, and nature of the sacred Primacy of the Roman Pontiff and concerning his infallible Magisterium, the sacred Synod again proposes to all the faithful to be firmly believed, and, proceeding in the same undertaking, decides to profess and declare before all the doctrine concerning bishops, the successors of the Apostles, who, together with the successor of Peter, the Vicar of Christ and the visible Head of the whole Church, govern the house of the living God.
¶91
Haec Sacrosancta Synodus, Concilii Vaticani primi vestigia premens, cum eo docet et declarat Iesum Christum Pastorem aeternum sanctam aedificasse Ecclesiam, missis Apostolis sicut Ipse missus erat a Patre (cf. Jn 20,21); quorum successores, videlicet Episcopos, in Ecclesia sua usque ad consummationem saeculi pastores esse voluit. Ut vero Episcopatus ipse unus et indivisus esset, beatum Petrum ceteris Apostolis praeposuit in ipsoque instituit perpetuum ac visibile unitatis fidei et communionis principium et fundamentum. Quam doctrinam de institutione, perpetuitate, vi ac ratione sacri Primatus Romani Pontificis deque eius infallibili Magisterio, Sacra Synodus cunctis fidelibus firmiter credendam rursus proponit, et in eodem incepto pergens, doctrinam de Episcopis, successoribus Apostolorum, qui cum successore Petri, Christi Vicario ac totius Ecclesiae visibili Capite, domum Dei viventis regunt, coram omnibus profiteri et declarare constituit.
The Lord Jesus, after praying to the Father, calling to Himself those whom He willed, appointed twelve to be with Him and to send them to preach the Kingdom of God (cf. Mk 3:13-19; Mt 10:1-42); whom He also named apostles (cf. Lk 6:13), He instituted in the manner of a college or stable assembly, over which He placed Peter, chosen from among them (cf. Jn 21:15-17). He sent them first to the children of Israel and then to all nations (cf. Rom 1:16), that, as sharers of His power, they might make all peoples disciples of Him, and sanctify and govern them (cf. Mt 28:16-20; Mk 16:15; Lk 24:45-48; Jn 20:21-23), and thus spread the Church, and, ministering under the guidance of the Lord, feed her all days until the consummation of the world (cf. Mt 28:20). In this mission they were fully confirmed on the day of Pentecost (cf. Acts 2:1-26) according to the Lord's promise: "You shall receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you shall be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the uttermost part of the earth" (Acts 1:8). The Apostles, preaching the Gospel everywhere (cf. Mk 16:20), received by the hearers through the working of the Holy Spirit, gather together the universal Church, which the Lord founded on the Apostles and built upon blessed Peter, their prince, with Christ Jesus Himself as the chief cornerstone (cf. Rev 21:14; Mt 16:18; Eph 2:20).
¶92
Dominus Iesus, precibus ad Patrem fusis, vocans ad Se quos voluit Ipse, duodecim constituit ut essent cum Illo et ut mitteret eos praedicare Regnum Dei (cf. Mc 3,13-19 Mt 10,1-42); quos Apostolos (cf. Lc 6,13) ad modum collegii seu coetus stabilis instituit, cui ex iisdem electum Petrum praefecit (cf. Jn 21,15-17). Eos ad filios Israel primum et ad omnes gentes misit (cf. Rm 1,16), ut suae participes potestatis, omnes populos discipulos Ipsius facerent, eosque sanctificarent et gubernarent (cf. Mt 28,16-20 Mc 16,15 Lc 24,45-48 Jn 20,21-23), sicque Ecclesiam propagarent, eamque sub ductu Domini ministrando pascerent, omnibus diebus usque ad consummationem saeculi (cf. Mt 28,20). In qua missione die Pentecostes plene confirmati sunt (cf. Ac 2,1-26) secundum promissum Domini: Accipietis virtutem supervenientis Spiritus Sancti in vos, et eritis Mihi testes in Ierusalem, et in omni Iudaea et Samaria, et usque ad ultimum terrae (Ac 1,8). Apostoli autem praedicando ubique Evangelium (cf. Mc 16,20), ab audientibus Spiritu Sancto operante acceptum, Ecclesiam congregant universalem, quam Dominus in Apostolis condidit et supra beatum Petrum, eorum principem, aedificavit, ipso summo angulari lapide Christo Iesu (cf. Ap 21,14 Mt 16,18 Ep 2,20).
That divine mission, entrusted by Christ to the Apostles, will last to the end of the world (cf. Mt 28:20), since the Gospel, to be handed down by them, is for all time the principle of the whole life of the Church. Therefore the Apostles, in this hierarchically ordered society, took care to appoint successors.
¶93
Missio illa divina, a Christo Apostolis concredita, ad finem saeculi erit duratura (cf. Mt 28,20), cum Evangelium, ab eis tradendum, sit in omne tempus pro Ecclesia totius vitae principium. Quapropter Apostoli, in hac societate hierarchice ordinata, de instituendis successoribus curam egerunt.
For they had not only various helpers in the ministry, but also, so that the mission entrusted to them might continue after their death, they handed down to their immediate cooperators, as if by a testament, the duty of perfecting and confirming the work begun by them, commending to them that they should attend to the whole flock, in which the Holy Spirit placed them to feed the Church of God (cf. Acts 20:28). They therefore appointed such men and thereafter gave the order that when they died, other approved men should take up their ministry. Among the various ministries which have been exercised in the Church from the first times, according to tradition, the principal place is held by the office of those who, being constituted in the episcopate, by a succession descending from the beginning, have the transmission of the apostolic seed. Thus, as St. Irenaeus testifies, through those who were constituted bishops by the Apostles and their successors down to us, the apostolic tradition is manifested and preserved throughout the whole world.
¶94
Non solum enim varios adiutores in ministerio habuerunt, sed ut missio ipsis concredita post eorum mortem continuaretur, cooperatoribus suis immediatis, quasi per modum testamenti, demandaverunt munus perficiendi et confirmandi opus ab ipsis inceptum, commendantes illis ut attenderent universo gregi, in quo Spiritus Sanctus eos posuit pascere Ecclesiam Dei (cf. Ac 20,28). Constituerunt itaque huius modi viros ac deinceps ordinationem dederunt, ut cum decessissent, ministerium eorum alii viri probati exciperent. Inter varia illa ministeria quae inde a primis temporibus in Ecclesia exercentur, teste traditione, praecipuum locum tenet munus illorum qui, in episcopatum constituti, per successionem ab initio decurrentem, apostolici seminis traduces habent. Ita, ut testatur S.Irenaeus, per eos qui ab Apostolis instituti sunt Episcopi et successores eorum usque ad nos, traditio apostolica in toto mundo manifestatur et custoditur.
Bishops therefore have taken up the ministry of the community, with presbyters and deacons as helpers, presiding in the place of God over the flock, whose shepherds they are, as teachers of doctrine, priests of sacred worship, ministers of governance. Just as the office given singularly by the Lord to Peter, the first of the Apostles, and to be transmitted to his successors, remains, so also the office of the Apostles of feeding the Church, to be continually exercised by the sacred order of bishops, remains. Hence the sacred Synod teaches that bishops, by divine institution, have succeeded to the place of the Apostles, as shepherds of the Church; he who hears them hears Christ, and he who despises them despises Christ and Him who sent Christ (cf. Lk 10:16).
¶95
Episcopi igitur communitatis ministerium cum adiutoribus presbyteris et diaconis susceperunt, loco Dei praesidentes gregi, cuius sunt pastores, ut doctrinae magistri, sacri cultus sacerdotes, gubernationis ministri. Sicut autem permanet munus a Domino singulariter Petro, primo Apostolorum, concessum et successoribus eius transmittendum, ita permanet munus Apostolorum pascendi Ecclesiam, ab ordine sacrato Episcoporum iugiter exercendum. Proinde docet Sacra Synodus Episcopos ex divina institutione in locum Apostolorum successisse, tamquam Ecclesiae pastores, quos qui audit, Christum audit, qui vero spernit, Christum spernit et Eum qui Christum misit (cf. Lc 10,16).
In the bishops, therefore, to whom the presbyters assist, the Lord Jesus Christ, the Supreme Pontiff, is present in the midst of believers. For sitting at the right hand of God the Father, He is not absent from the congregation of His pontiffs, but above all through their eminent service He preaches the word of God to all nations and continually administers the sacraments of faith to believers, by their fatherly office (cf. 1 Cor 4:15) He incorporates new members into His body by supernal regeneration, and finally by their wisdom and prudence He directs and orders the People of the New Testament in their pilgrimage to eternal beatitude. These pastors, chosen to feed the Lord's flock, are ministers of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God (cf. 1 Cor 4:1), to whom is entrusted the testimony of the Gospel of the grace of God (cf. Rom 15:16; Acts 20:24), and the ministry of the Spirit and of justice in glory (cf. 2 Cor 3:8-9).
¶96
In Episcopis igitur, quibus presbyteri assistunt, adest in medio credentium Dominus Iesus Christus, Pontifex Summus. Sedens enim ad dexteram Dei Patris, non deest a suorum congregatione pontificum, sed imprimis per eorum eximium servitium verbum Dei omnibus gentibus praedicat et credentibus sacramenta fidei continuo administrat, eorum paterno munere (cf. 1Co 4,15) nova membra Corpori suo regeneratione superna incorporat, eorum denique sapientia et prudentia Populum Novi Testamenti in sua ad aeternam beatitudinem peregrinatione dirigit et ordinat. Hi pastores ad pascendum dominicum gregem electi, ministri Christi sunt et dispensatores mysteriorum Dei (cf. 1Co 4,1), quibus concredita est testificatio Evangelii gratiae Dei (cf. Rm 15,16 Ac 20,24), atque ministratio Spiritus et iustitiae in gloria (cf. 2Co 3,8-9).
To fulfill so great functions, the Apostles were enriched by Christ with a special outpouring of the Holy Spirit coming upon them (cf. Acts 1:8; 2:4; Jn 20:22-23), and they themselves handed down the spiritual gift to their helpers by the imposition of hands (cf. 1 Tim 4:14; 2 Tim 1:6-7), which has been transmitted down to us in episcopal consecration. The holy Synod teaches that by episcopal consecration the fullness of the sacrament of Order is conferred, which indeed both by the liturgical custom of the Church and by the voice of the holy Fathers is called the supreme priesthood, the summit of the sacred ministry. Moreover, episcopal consecration, together with the office of sanctifying, also confers the offices of teaching and governing, which, however, by their very nature can only be exercised in hierarchical communion with the Head and the members of the College. For from tradition, which is especially declared by the liturgical rites and the usage of the Church both in the East and the West, it is clear that by the imposition of hands and the words of consecration, the grace of the Holy Spirit is so conferred and the sacred character so impressed, that bishops, in an eminent and visible manner, sustain the parts of Christ Himself, Teacher, Shepherd, and Pontiff, and act in His person. It pertains to bishops, through the sacrament of Order, to assume new elect into the episcopal body.
¶97
Ad tanta munera explenda, Apostoli speciali effusione supervenientis Spiritus Sancti a Christo ditati sunt (cf. Ac 1,8 Ac 2,4 Jn 20,22-23), et ipsi adiutoribus suis per impositionem manuum donum spirituale tradiderunt (cf. 1Tm 4,14 2Tm 1,6-7), quod usque ad nos in episcopali consecratione transmissum est. Docet autem Sancta Synodus episcopali consecratione plenitudinem conferri sacramenti Ordinis, quae nimirum et liturgica Ecclesiae consuetudine et voce Sanctorum Patrum summum sacerdotium, sacri ministerii summa nuncupatur. Episcopalis autem consecratio, cum munere sanctificandi, munera quoque confert docendi et regendi, quae tamen natura sua nonnisi in hierarchica communione cum Collegii Capite et membris exerceri possunt. Ex traditione enim, quae praesertim liturgicis ritibus et Ecclesiae tum Orientis tum Occidentis usu declaratur, perspicuum est manuum impositione et verbis consecrationis gratiam Spiritus Sancti ita conferri et sacrum characterem ita imprimi, ut Episcopi, eminenti ac adspectabili modo, ipsius Christi Magistri, Pastoris et Pontificis partes sustineant et in Eius persona agant. Episcoporum est per Sacramentum Ordinis novos electos in corpus episcopale assumere.
Just as, by the Lord's disposition, St. Peter and the other Apostles constitute one apostolic college, in like manner the Roman Pontiff, the successor of Peter, and the bishops, the successors of the Apostles, are joined together among themselves. Already the most ancient discipline, by which bishops constituted throughout the whole world communicated with one another and with the Roman bishop in the bond of unity, charity, and peace, and likewise the councils assembled, by which deeper matters were decided in common, the opinion of many having been weighed by counsel, signify the nature and collegial character of the episcopal order; which the ecumenical councils celebrated in the course of the centuries clearly prove. The same is also indicated by the very practice, introduced from ancient times, of summoning several bishops to take part in raising a newly elected to the ministry of the supreme priesthood. One is constituted a member of the episcopal body by virtue of sacramental consecration and hierarchical communion with the Head and the members of the College.
¶98
Sicut, statuente Domino, sanctus Petrus et ceteri Apostoli unum Collegium apostolicum constituunt, pari ratione Romanus Pontifex, successor Petri, et Episcopi, successores Apostolorum, inter se coniunguntur. Iam perantiqua disciplina, qua Episcopi in universo orbe constituti ad invicem et cum Romano Episcopo communicabant in vinculo unitatis, caritatis et pacis, itemque concilia coadunata, per quae et altiora quaeque in commune statuerentur, sententia multorum consilio ponderata, ordinis episcopalis indolem et rationem collegialem significant; quam manifeste comprobant Concilia oecumenica decursu saeculorum celebrata. Eandem vero iam innuit ipse usus, antiquitus inductus, plures advocandi Episcopos qui in novo electo ad summi sacerdotii ministerium elevando partem haberent. Membrum Corporis episcopalis aliquis constituitur vi sacramentalis consecrationis et hierarchica communione cum Collegii Capite atque membris.
But the college or body of bishops has no authority unless it is understood together with the Roman Pontiff, the successor of Peter, as its head, the power of primacy of the same remaining whole, which is over all, both pastors and faithful. For the Roman Pontiff has in the Church, by virtue of his office, namely as the Vicar of Christ and Pastor of the whole Church, full, supreme, and universal power, which he can always exercise freely. The order of bishops, which succeeds the college of apostles in pastoral teaching and governance, indeed in which the apostolic body continuously perseveres, together with its head the Roman Pontiff, and never without this head, also exists as the subject of supreme and full power over the universal Church, which power, however, can only be exercised with the consent of the Roman Pontiff. The Lord placed Simon alone as the rock and key-bearer of the Church (cf. Mt 16:18-19), and constituted him pastor of His whole flock (cf. Jn 21:15 ff.); but the office of binding and loosing, which was given to Peter (Mt 16:19), is known to have been given also to the college of apostles joined with its head (Mt 18:18; 28:16-20). This college, insofar as it is composed of many, expresses the variety and universality of the People of God, but insofar as it is gathered under one head, expresses the unity of the flock of Christ.
¶99
Collegium autem seu corpus Episcoporum auctoritatem non habet, nisi simul cum Pontifice Romano, successore Petri, ut capite eius intellegatur, huiusque integre manente potestate Primatus in omnes sive Pastores sive fideles Romanus enim Pontifex habet in Ecclesiam, vi muneris sui, Vicarii scilicet Christi et totius Ecclesiae Pastoris, plenam, supremam et universalem potestatem, quam semper libere exercere valet. Ordo autem Episcoporum, qui collegio Apostolorum in magisterio et regimine pastorali succedit, immo in quo corpus apostolicum continuo perseverat, una cum Capite suo Romano Pontifice, et numquam sine hoc Capite, subiectum quoque supremae ac plenae potestatis in universam Ecclesiam exsistit, quae quidem potestas nonnisi consentiente Romano Pontifice exerceri potest. Dominus unum Simonem ut petram et clavigerum Ecclesiae posuit (cf. Mt 16,18-19), eumque Pastorem totius sui gregis constituit (cf. Jn 21,15 ss.); illud autem ligandi ac solvendi munus, quod Petro datum est (Mt 16,19), collegio quoque Apostolorum, suo Capiti coniuncto, tributum esse constat (Mt 18,18 Mt 28,16-20). Collegium hoc quatenus ex multis compositum, varietatem et universalitatem Populi Dei, quatenus vero sub uno capite collectum, unitatem gregis Christi exprimit.
In it, the bishops, faithfully preserving the primacy and leadership of their head, exercise their own power for the good of their faithful, indeed of the whole Church, the Holy Spirit continually strengthening the organic structure and its concord. The supreme power over the universal Church, which this college possesses, is exercised in a solemn manner in an Ecumenical Council. An Ecumenical Council never takes place which is not confirmed as such or at least received by the successor of Peter; and it is the prerogative of the Roman Pontiff to convoke these councils, to preside over them, and to confirm them. The same collegial power can be exercised together with the pope by the bishops living throughout the world, provided that the Head of the College calls them to collegial action, or at least approves or freely accepts the united action of the dispersed bishops, so that it becomes a true collegial act.
¶100
In ipso, Episcopi, primatum et principatum Capitis sui fideliter servantes, propria potestate in bonum fidelium suorum, immo totius Ecclesiae funguntur, Spiritu Sancto organicam structuram eiusque concordiam continenter roborante Suprema in universam Ecclesiam potestas, qua istud Collegium pollet, sollemni modo in Concilio Oecumenico exercetur Concilium Oecumenicum numquam datur, quod a Successore Petri non sit ut tale confirmatum vel saltem receptum; et Romani Pontificis praerogativa est haec Concilia convocare, iisdem praesidere et eadem confirmare. Eadem potestas collegialis una cum Papa exerceri potest ab Episcopis in orbe terrarum degentibus, dummodo Caput Collegii eos ad actionem collegialem vocet, vel saltem Episcoporum dispersorum unitam actionem approbet vel libere recipiat, ita ut verus actus collegialis efficiatur.
The collegial union also appears in the mutual relations of individual bishops with particular churches and the universal church. The Roman Pontiff, as the successor of Peter, is the perpetual and visible principle and foundation of the unity both of the bishops and of the multitude of the faithful. Individual bishops, however, are the visible principle and foundation of unity in their own particular churches, formed after the image of the universal church, in which and from which the one and only catholic Church exists. For this reason, each individual bishop represents his own church, but all together with the pope represent the whole Church in the bond of peace, love, and unity.
¶101
Collegialis unio etiam in mutuis relationibus singulorum Episcoporum cum particularibus Ecclesiis Ecclesiaque universali apparet. Romanus Pontifex, ut successor Petri, est unitatis, tum Episcoporum tum fidelium multitudinis, perpetuum ac visibile principium et fundamentum. Episcopi autem singuli visibile principium et fundamentum sunt unitatis in suis Ecclesiis particularibus, ad imaginem Ecclesiae universalis formatis, in quibus et ex quibus una et unica Ecclesia catholica exsistit. Qua de causa singuli Episcopi suam Ecclesiam, omnes autem simul cum Papa totam Ecclesiam repraesentant in vinculo pacis, amoris et unitatis.
Individual bishops, who are placed over particular churches, exercise their pastoral governance over the portion of the People of God entrusted to them, not over other churches nor over the universal church. But as members of the episcopal college and legitimate successors of the apostles, each is bound by the institution and precept of Christ to that solicitude for the universal church which, even if not exercised through an act of jurisdiction, nevertheless greatly contributes to the good of the universal church. For all bishops ought to promote and protect the unity of faith and the discipline common to the whole Church, to instruct the faithful to love the whole mystical body of Christ, especially the poor, the suffering, and those who suffer persecution for justice's sake (cf. Mt 5:10), finally to promote every activity which is common to the whole Church, especially that faith may increase and the light of full truth arise for all men. Moreover, this is holy, that by well governing his own church as a portion of the universal church, he effectively contributes to the good of the whole mystical body, which is also the body of churches.
¶102
Singuli Episcopi, qui particularibus Ecclesiis praeficiuntur, regimen suum pastorale super portionem Populi Dei sibi commissam, non super alias Ecclesias neque super Ecclesiam universalem exercent. Sed qua membra Collegii episcopalis et legitimi Apostolorum successores singuli ea sollicitudine pro universa Ecclesia ex Christi institutione et praecepto tenentur, quae, etiamsi per actum iurisdictionis non exerceatur, summopere tamen confert ad Ecclesiae universalis emolumentum. Debent enim omnes Episcopi promovere et tueri unitatem fidei et disciplinam cunctae Ecclesiae communem, fideles edocere ad amorem totius Corporis mystici Christi, praesertim membrorum pauperum, dolentium et eorum qui persecutionem patiuntur propter iustitiam (cf. Mt 5,10), tandem promovere omnem actuositatem quae toti Ecclesiae communis est, praesertim ut fides incrementum capiat et lux plenae veritatis omnibus hominibus oriatur. Ceterum hoc sanctum est quod, bene regendo propriam Ecclesiam ut portionem Ecclesiae universalis, ipsi efficaciter conferunt ad bonum totius mystici Corporis, quod est etiam corpus Ecclesiarum.
The care to announce the Gospel everywhere on earth pertains to the body of pastors, to whom Christ gave the mandate in common, imposing a common office, as Pope Celestine already commended to the Fathers of the Council of Ephesus. Hence individual bishops, insofar as the performance of their own office permits, are bound to enter into a fellowship of labors among themselves and with the successor of Peter, to whom the great office of propagating the Christian name has been entrusted in a special manner. Therefore they ought to supply to missions both harvest workers and also spiritual and material aids, both directly by themselves, and by arousing the ardent cooperation of the faithful. Finally, bishops, in the universal fellowship of charity, should gladly offer fraternal assistance to other churches, especially to neighboring and more needy ones, according to the venerable example of antiquity.
¶103
Cura Evangelium ubique terrarum annuntiandi ad corpus Pastorum pertinet, quibus omnibus in commune Christus mandatum dedit imponendo commune officium, ut iam Papa Coelestinus Patribus Ephesini Concilii commendavit. Unde singuli Episcopi, quantum propria eorum perfunctio muneris sinit, in laborum societatem venire tenentur inter se et cum successore Petri, cui grande munus christiani nominis propagandi singulari modo demandatum est. Quare missionibus tum messis operarios, tum etiam auxilia spiritualia et materialia, tam per se directe, quam suscitando fidelium ardentem cooperationem, suppeditare omnibus viribus debent. Episcopi denique, in universali caritatis societate, fraternum adiutorium aliis Ecclesiis, praesertim finitimis et egentioribus, secundum venerandum antiquitatis exemplum, libenter praebeant.
By divine Providence it has come about that various churches established in various places by the Apostles and their successors have in the course of time coalesced into several groups, organically joined, which, preserving the unity of faith and the unique divine constitution of the universal church, enjoy their own discipline, their own liturgical usage, their own theological and spiritual patrimony. Among these, some, notably the ancient Patriarchal churches, as it were mother churches, gave birth to others as daughters, with which they are connected up to our times by a closer bond of charity in sacramental life and in mutual respect of rights and duties. This variety of local churches conspiring in unity more lucidly demonstrates the catholicity of the undivided Church. In like manner, episcopal conferences today can contribute manifold and fruitful aid, that the collegial effect may be brought to concrete application.
¶104
Divina autem Providentia factum est ut variae variis in locis ab Apostolis eorumque successoribus institutae Ecclesiae decursu temporum in plures coaluerint coetus, organice coniunctos, qui, salva fidei unitate et unica divina constitutione universalis Ecclesiae, gaudent propria disciplina, proprio liturgico usu, theologico spiritualique patrimonio. Inter quas aliquae, notatim antiquae Patriarchales Ecclesiae, veluti matrices fidei, alias pepererunt quasi filias, quibuscum arctiore vinculo caritatis in vita sacramentali atque in mutua iurium et officiorum reverentia ad nostra usque tempora connectuntur. Quae Ecclesiarum localium in unum conspirans varietas indivisae Ecclesiae catholicitatem luculentius demonstrat. Simili ratione Coetus Episcopales hodie multiplicem atque fecundam opem conferre possunt, ut collegialis effectus ad concretam applicationem perducatur.
Bishops, as successors of the Apostles, receive from the Lord, to whom all power is given in heaven and on earth, the mission to teach all nations and to preach the Gospel to every creature, that all men, through faith, baptism, and the fulfillment of the commandments, may attain salvation (cf. Mt 28:18; Mk 16:15-16; Acts 26:17 ff.). To fulfill this mission, Christ the Lord promised the Holy Spirit to the Apostles and sent Him from heaven on the day of Pentecost, by whose power they would be witnesses to Him to the ends of the earth, before nations and peoples and kings (cf. Acts 1:8; 2:1 ff.; 9:15). That office, however, which the Lord entrusted to the pastors of His people, is a true service which in sacred literature is significantly called diakonia or ministry (cf. Acts 1:17, 25; 21:19; Rom 11:13; 1 Tim 1:12).
¶105
Episcopi, utpote Apostolorum successores, a Domino, cui omnis potestas in caelo et in terra data est, missionem accipiunt docendi omnes gentes et praedicandi Evangelium omni creaturae, ut homines universi, per fidem, baptismum et adimpletionem mandatorum salutem consequantur (cf. Mt 28,18 Mc 16,15-16 Ac 26,17 s.). Ad hanc missionem implendam, Christus Dominus Spiritum Sanctum promisit Apostolis et die Pentecostes e caelo misit, cuius virtute testes Eidem essent usque ad ultimum terrae, coram gentibus et populis et regibus (cf. Ac 1,8 Ac 2,1 ss.; 9, 15). Munus autem illud, quod Dominus pastoribus populi sui commisit, verum est servitium quod in sacris Litteris diakonia seu ministerium significanter nuncupatur (cf. Ac 1,17 Ac 1,25 Ac 21,19 Rm 11,13 1Tm 1,12).
The canonical mission of bishops can be made through legitimate customs, not revoked by the supreme and universal power of the Church, or through laws enacted or recognized by the same authority, or directly through the successor of Peter Himself; the latter refusing or denying apostolic communion, bishops cannot be assumed into office.
¶106
Episcoporum autem missio canonica fieri potest per legitimas consuetudines, a suprema et universali potestate Ecclesiae non revocatas, vel per leges ab eadem auctoritate latas aut agnitas, vel directe per ipsum Successorem Petri; quo renuente seu communionem Apostolicam denegante, Episcopi in officium assumi nequeunt.
Among the principal offices of bishops, the preaching of the Gospel stands out. For bishops are heralds of the faith, who lead new disciples to Christ, and authentic teachers, that is, endowed with the authority of Christ, who preach to the people entrusted to them the faith to be believed and to be applied to morals, and illustrate it under the light of the Holy Spirit, bringing forth from the treasure of Revelation new and old (cf. Mt 13:52), make it fruitful, and vigilantly ward off errors threatening their flock (cf. 2 Tim 4:1-4). Bishops, teaching in communion with the Roman Pontiff, are to be venerated by all as witnesses of divine and catholic truth; the faithful, moreover, ought to concur in the sentence of their bishop concerning faith and morals pronounced in the name of Christ, and adhere to it with a religious homage of spirit. This religious homage of will and intellect is to be rendered in a special way to the authentic magisterium of the Roman Pontiff even when he is not speaking ex cathedra; namely, so that his supreme magisterium be reverently acknowledged, and the sentences pronounced by him be sincerely adhered to, according to his manifest mind and will, which shows itself especially either by the nature of the documents, or by the frequent proposition of the same doctrine, or by the manner of speaking.
¶107
Inter praecipua Episcoporum munera eminet praedicatio Evangelii. Episcopi enim sunt fidei praecones, qui novos discipulos ad Christum adducunt, et doctores authentici seu auctoritate Christi praediti, qui populo sibi commisso fidem credendam et moribus applicandam praedicant, et sub lumine Sancti Spiritus illustrant, ex thesauro Revelationis nova et vetera proferentes (cf. Mt 13,52), eam fructificare faciunt erroresque gregi suo impendentes vigilanter arcent (cf. 2Tm 4,1-4). Episcopi in communione cum Romano Pontifice docentes ab omnibus tamquam divinae et catholicae veritatis testes venerandi sunt; fideles autem in sui Episcopi sententiam de fide et moribus nomine Christi prolatam concurrere, eique religioso animi obsequio adhaerere debent. Hoc vero religiosum voluntatis et intellectus obsequium singulari ratione praestandum est Romani Pontificis authentico magisterio etiam cum non ex cathedra loquitur; ita nempe ut magisterium eius supremum reverenter agnoscatur, et sententiis ab eo prolatis sincere adhaereatur, iuxta mentem et voluntatem manifestatam ipsius, quae se prodit praecipue sive indole documentorum, sive ex frequenti propositione eiusdem doctrinae, sive ex dicendi ratione.
Although individual bishops do not enjoy the prerogative of infallibility, when they, even dispersed throughout the world, but preserving the bond of communion among themselves and with the successor of Peter, authentically teaching matters of faith and morals, agree in one sentence as to be held definitively, they infallibly enunciate the doctrine of Christ. This is still more clearly had when, assembled in an Ecumenical Council, they are teachers and judges of faith and morals for the universal Church, whose definitions in faith are to be adhered to with the homage of faith.
¶108
Licet singuli praesules infallibilitatis praerogativa non polleant, quando tamen, etiam per orbem dispersi, sed communionis nexum inter se et cum Successore Petri servantes, authentice res fidei et morum docentes in unam sententiam tamquam definitive tenendam conveniunt, doctrinam Christi infallibiliter enuntiant. Quod adhuc manifestius habetur quando, in Concilio Oecumenico coadunati, pro universa Ecclesia fidei et morum doctores et iudices sunt, quorum definitionibus fidei obsequio est adhaerendum.
This infallibility, however, with which the Divine Redeemer willed His Church to be endowed in defining doctrine concerning faith or morals, extends only as far as the deposit of divine Revelation extends, to be holy guarded and faithfully expounded. With this infallibility, the Roman Pontiff, the Head of the College of Bishops, enjoys by virtue of his office, when, as the supreme pastor and teacher of all Christ's faithful, who confirms his brothers in the faith (cf. Lk 22:32), he proclaims doctrine concerning faith or morals by a definitive act. Therefore his definitions, of themselves, and not from the consent of the Church, are justly said to be irreformable, since they were pronounced with the assistance of the Holy Spirit, promised to him in blessed Peter, and therefore need no approval of others, nor do they admit appeal to any other judgment. For then the Roman Pontiff pronounces sentence not as a private person, but as the supreme teacher of the universal Church, in whom the charism of infallibility of the Church itself resides individually, when he expounds or defends the doctrine of catholic faith. The infallibility promised to the Church also resides in the body of bishops when it exercises the supreme magisterium with the successor of Peter. To these definitions the assent of the Church can never be lacking, on account of the action of the same Holy Spirit, by which the whole flock of Christ is kept and progresses in the unity of faith.
¶109
Haec autem infallibilitas, qua Divinus Redemptor Ecclesiam suam in definienda doctrina de fide vel moribus instructam esse voluit, tantum patet quantum divinae Revelationis patet depositum, sancte custodiendum et fideliter exponendum. Qua quidem infallibilitate Romanus Pontifex, Collegii Episcoporum Caput, vi muneris sui gaudet, quando, ut supremus omnium christifidelium pastor et doctor, qui fratres suos in fide confirmat (cf. Lc 22,32), doctrinam de fide vel moribus definitivo actu proclamat. Quare definitiones eius ex sese, et non ex consensu Ecclesiae, irreformabiles merito dicuntur, quippe quae sub assistentia Spiritus Sancti, ipsi in beato Petro promissa, prolatae sint, ideoque nulla indigeant aliorum approbatione, nec ullam ad aliud iudicium appellationem patiantur. Tunc enim Romanus Pontifex non ut persona privata sententiam profert, sed ut universalis Ecclesiae magister supremus, in quo charisma infallibilitatis ipsius Ecclesiae singulariter inest, doctrinam fidei catholicae exponit vel tuetur. Infallibilitas Ecclesiae promissa in corpore Episcoporum quoque inest, quando supremum magisterium cum Petri Successore exercet. Istis autem definitionibus assensus Ecclesiae numquam deesse potest propter actionem eiusdem Spiritus Sancti, qua universus Christi grex in unitate fidei servatur et proficit.
When, however, either the Roman Pontiff or the body of bishops with him define a sentence, they pronounce it according to Revelation itself, to which all are bound to stand and be conformed, and which is integrally transmitted in writing or by tradition through the legitimate succession of bishops and especially through the care of the Roman Pontiff Himself, and under the light of the Spirit of truth is holy kept and faithfully expounded in the Church. To rightly investigate and aptly set forth this Revelation, the Roman Pontiff and bishops, according to their office and the gravity of the matter, sedulously work through suitable means; but they do not accept new public revelation as pertaining to the divine deposit of faith.
¶110
Cum autem sive Romanus Pontifex sive Corpus Episcoporum cum eo sententiam definiunt, eam proferunt secundum ipsam Revelationem, cui omnes stare et conformari tenentur et quae scripta vel tradita per legitimam Episcoporum successionem et imprimis ipsius Romani Pontificis cura integre transmittitur, atque praelucente Spiritu veritatis in Ecclesia sancte servatur et fideliter exponitur. Ad quam rite indagandam et apte enuntiandam, Romanus Pontifex et Episcopi, pro officio suo et rei gravitate, per media apta, sedulo operam navant; novam vero revelationem publicam tamquam ad divinum fidei depositum pertinentem non accipiunt.
The bishop, marked with the fullness of the sacrament of Order, is the steward of the grace of the supreme priesthood, especially in the Eucharist, which he himself offers or causes to be offered, and by which the Church continually lives and grows. This Church of Christ is truly present in all legitimate local congregations of the faithful, which, adhering to their shepherds, are themselves called churches in the New Testament. For these are in their own place the new people called by God, in the Holy Spirit and in much fullness (cf. 1 Th 1:5). In them, by the preaching of the Gospel of Christ, the faithful are gathered together and the mystery of the Lord's Supper is celebrated, that through the food and blood of the Lord the whole brotherhood of the body may be bound together. In any altar community, under the sacred ministry of the bishop, is exhibited the symbol of that charity and unity of the mystical body, without which there can be no salvation. In these communities, although often small and poor, or living in dispersion, Christ is present, by whose power the one, holy, catholic, and apostolic Church is united. For the participation in the body and blood of Christ does nothing other than to transform us into that which we receive.
¶111
Episcopus, plenitudine sacramenti Ordinis insignitus, est oeconomus gratiae supremi sacerdotii, praesertim in Eucharistia, quam ipse offert vel offerri curat, et qua continuo vivit et crescit Ecclesia. Haec Christi Ecclesia vere adest in omnibus legitimis fidelium congregationibus localibus, quae, pastoribus suis adhaerentes, et ipsae in Novo Testamento ecclesiae vocantur. Hae sunt enim loco suo Populus novus a Deo vocatus, in Spiritu Sancto et in plenitudine multa (cf. 1Th 1,5). In eis praedicatione Evangelii Christi congregantur fideles et celebratur mysterium Coenae Domini, ut per escam et sanguinem Domini corporis fraternitas cuncta copuletur. In quavis altaris communitate, sub Episcopi sacro ministerio, exhibetur symbolum illius caritatis et unitatis Corporis mystici, sine qua non potest esse salus. In his communitatibus, licet saepe exiguis et pauperibus, vel in dispersione degentibus, praesens est Christus, cuius virtute consociatur una, sancta, catholica et apostolica Ecclesia. Etenim non aliud agit participatio corporis et sanguinis Christi, quam ut in id quod sumimus transeamus.
Moreover, every legitimate celebration of the Eucharist is directed by the bishop, to whom the office has been committed of deferring the cult of the Christian religion to the Divine Majesty and of administering it according to the precepts of the Lord and the laws of the Church, further determined by his particular judgment for the diocese.
¶112
Omnis autem legitima Eucharistiae celebratio dirigitur ab Episcopo, cui officium commissum est cultum christianae religionis Divinae Maiestati deferendi atque administrandi secundum praecepta Domi ni et Ecclesiae leges, eius particulari iudicio ulterius pro dioecesi determinatas.
Thus bishops, praying and laboring for the people, from the fullness of the holiness of Christ pour forth multiformly and abundantly. Through the ministry of the word they communicate the power of God to believers for salvation (cf. Rom 1:16), and through the sacraments, the regular and fruitful distribution of which they order by their authority, they sanctify the faithful. They rule the conferral of baptism, by which participation in the royal priesthood of Christ is granted. They are the original ministers of confirmation, dispensers of sacred orders, and moderators of penitential discipline, and they solicitously exhort and instruct their people that they may fulfill their parts in the liturgy and especially in the holy sacrifice of the Mass with faith and reverence. Finally, they ought to profit those whom they preside over by the example of their conversation, tempering their morals from all evil and, as much as they can, the Lord helping, changing them to good, that they may come to eternal life, together with the flock entrusted to them.
¶113
Ita Episcopi, orando pro populo et laborando, de plenitudine sanctitatis Christi multiformiter et abundanter effundunt. Per ministerium verbi virtutem Dei credentibus in salutem communicant (cf. Rm 1,16), et per sacramenta, quorum regularem et fructuosam distributionem auctoritate sua ordinant, fideles sanctificant. Ipsi regunt collationem baptismi, quo regalis sacerdotii Christi participatio conceditur. Ipsi sunt ministri originarii confirmationis, dispensatores sacrorum ordinum et moderatores disciplinae poenitentialis, atque populos suos, ut in liturgia et praesertim in sacro Missae sacrificio partes suas fide et reverentia impleant, sollicite exhortantur et instruunt. Eis denique quibus praesunt exemplo conversationis suae proficere debent, mores suos ab omni malo temperantes et quantum poterint, Domino adiuvante, ad bonum commutando, ut ad vitam, una cum grege sibi credito, perveniant sempiternam.
Bishops govern the particular churches entrusted to them as vicars and legates of Christ, by counsels, persuasions, examples, but also by authority and sacred power, which indeed they use only for building up their flock in truth and holiness, mindful that he who is greater should become as the younger, and he who is the leader as the servant (cf. Lk 22:26-27). This power, which they exercise personally in the name of Christ, is proper, ordinary, and immediate, although the exercise of it is ultimately regulated by the supreme authority of the Church and, for the utility of the Church or of the faithful, can be circumscribed by certain limits. By virtue of this power, bishops have the sacred right and duty before the Lord to make laws for their subjects, to pass judgment, and to moderate all things which pertain to the order of worship and apostolate.
¶114
Episcopi Ecclesias particulares sibi commissas ut vicarii et legati Christi regunt, consiliis, suasionibus, exemplis, verum etiam auctoritate et sacra potestate, qua quidem nonnisi ad gregem suum in veritate et sanctitate aedificandum utuntur, memores quod qui maior est fiat sicut minor et qui praecessor est sicut ministrator (cf. Lc 22,26-27). Haec potestas qua nomine Christi personaliter funguntur, est propria, ordinaria et immediata, licet a suprema Ecclesiae auctoritate exercitium eiusdem ultimatim regatur et certis limitibus, intuitu utilitatis Ecclesiae vel fidelium, circumscribi possit. Vi huius potestatis Episcopi sacrum ius et coram Domino officium habent in suos subditos leges ferendi, iudicium faciendi, atque omnia, quae ad cultus apostolatusque ordinem pertinent, moderandi.
Their pastoral office, that is, the habitual and daily care of their sheep, is fully committed to them, nor are they to be considered vicars of the Roman Pontiffs, because they exercise a power proper to themselves and are truly called prelates of the people they govern. Their power therefore is not destroyed by the supreme and universal power, but is on the contrary asserted, strengthened, and vindicated, the Holy Spirit indefectibly preserving the form of governance established by Christ the Lord in His Church.
¶115
Ipsis munus pastorale seu habitualis et cotidiana cura ovium suarum plene committitur, neque vicarii Romanorum Pontificum putandi sunt, quia potestatem gerunt sibi propriam verissimeque populorum quos regunt, Antistites dicuntur. Eorum itaque potestas a suprema et universali potestate non eliditur, sed e contra asseritur, roboratur et vindicatur, Spiritu Sancto constitutam a Christo Domino in sua Ecclesia regiminis formam indefectibiliter servante.
The bishop, sent by the Father of the family to govern his family, should keep before his eyes the example of the Good Shepherd, who came not to be ministered unto but to minister (cf. Mt 20:28; Mk 10:45) and to lay down his life for the sheep (cf. Jn 10:11). Taken from among men and surrounded with weakness, he can sympathize with those who are ignorant and err (cf. Heb 5:1-2). Let him not refuse to hear his subjects, whom he fosters as true sons and exhorts to cooperate with him readily. He must have care, about to give an account for their souls to God (cf. Heb 13:17), both for them and also for those who are not yet of the one flock, whom he has commended to him in the Lord, through prayer, preaching, and all works of charity. Since, like Paul the Apostle, he is a debtor to all, let him be ready to preach the Gospel to all (cf. Rom 1:14-15), and to exhort his faithful to apostolic and missionary activity. The faithful, moreover, ought to adhere to their bishop as the Church does to Jesus Christ, and as Jesus Christ does to the Father, that all things may consent through unity and abound to the glory of God (cf. 2 Cor 4:15).
¶116
Episcopus, missus a Patrefamilias ad gubernandam familiam suam, ante oculos teneat exemplum Boni Pastoris, qui venit non ministrari sed ministrare (cf. Mt 20,28 Mc 10,45) et animam suam pro ovibus ponere (cf. Jn 10,11). Assumptus ex hominibus et circumdatus infirmitate, condolere potest iis qui ignorant et errant (cf. He 5,1-2). Subditos, quos ut veros filios suos fovet et ad alacriter secum cooperandum exhortatur, audire ne renuat. Pro animabus eorum rationem redditurus Deo (cf. He 13,17) oratione, praedicatione omnibusque operibus caritatis curam habeat tum eorundem, tum etiam illorum qui de uno grege nondum sunt, quos in Domino commendatos sibi habeat. Ipse, cum sicut Paulus Apostolus cunctis debitor sit, promptus sit omnibus evangelizare (cf. Rm 1,14-15), fidelesque suos ad operositatem apostolicam et missionalem exhortari. Fideles autem Episcopo adhaerere debent sicut Ecclesia Iesu Christo, et sicut Iesus Christus Patri, ut omnia per unitatem consentiant, et abundent in gloriam Dei (cf. 2Co 4,15).
Christ, whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world (Jn 10:36), made His apostles, and their successors, namely the bishops, sharers in His consecration and mission, who have legitimately handed down the office of their ministry, in various degrees, to various subjects in the Church. Thus the ecclesiastical ministry, divinely instituted, is exercised in different orders by those who from ancient times are called bishops, presbyters, deacons.
¶117
Christus, quem Pater sanctificavit et misit in mundum (Jn 10,36), consecrationis missionisque suae per Apostolos suos, eorum successores, videlicet Episcopos participes effecit, qui munus ministerii sui, vario gradu, variis subiectis in Ecclesia legitime tradiderunt. Sic ministerium ecclesiasticum divinitus institutum diversis ordinibus exercetur ab illis qui iam ab antiquo Episcopi, Presbyteri, Diaconi vocantur.
Presbyters, although they do not have the summit of the pontificate and depend on bishops in the exercise of their power, are nevertheless joined with them in priestly honor and, by virtue of the sacrament of Order, after the image of Christ, the supreme and eternal Priest (Heb 5:1-10; 7:24; 9:11-28), are consecrated to preach the Gospel, to feed the faithful, and to celebrate divine worship, as true priests of the New Testament. Participants in the office of the one Mediator Christ (1 Tim 2:5) in their degree of ministry, they announce the divine word to all. They exercise their sacred office especially in the eucharistic cult or synaxis, in which, acting in the person of Christ and proclaiming His mystery, they join the prayers of the faithful to the sacrifice of their Head, and represent and apply in the sacrifice of the Mass, until the coming of the Lord (cf. 1 Cor 11:26), the unique sacrifice of the New Testament, namely Christ offering Himself once as an immaculate victim to the Father (cf. Heb 9:11-28). For penitent or sick faithful they highly exercise the ministry of reconciliation and alleviation, and they bring the needs and prayers of the faithful to God the Father (cf. Heb 5:1-4). Exercising in their part of authority the office of Christ the Shepherd and Head, they gather and lead the family of God as a fraternity animated in one spirit, and through Christ in the Spirit they lead it to God the Father. They adore Him in spirit and truth (cf. Jn 4:24) in the midst of the flock. Finally, they labor in word and doctrine (cf. 1 Tim 5:17), believing what they have read meditating on the law of the Lord, teaching what they have believed, imitating what they have taught.
¶118
Presbyteri, quamvis pontificatus apicem non habeant et in exercenda sua potestate ab Episcopis pendeant, cum eis tamen sacerdotali honore coniuncti sunt et vi sacramenti Ordinis, ad imaginem Christi, summi atque aeterni Sacerdotis (He 5,1-10 He 7,24 He 9,11-28), ad Evangelium praedicandum fidelesque pascendos et ad divinum cultum celebrandum consecrantur, ut veri sacerdotes Novi Testamenti. Muneris unici Mediatoris Christi (1Tm 2,5) participes in suo gradu ministerii, omnibus verbum divinum annuntiant. Suum vero munus sacrum maxime exercent in eucharistico cultu vel synaxi, qua in persona Christi agentes Eiusque mysterium proclamantes, vota fidelium sacrificio Capitis ipsorum coniungunt, et unicum sacrificium Novi Testamenti, Christi scilicet Sese Patri immaculatam hostiam semel offerentis (cf. He 9,11-28), in sacrificio Missae usque ad adventum Domini (cf. 1Co 11,26) repraesentant et applicant. Pro fidelibus autem poenitentibus vel aegrotantibus ministerio reconciliationis et alleviationis summe funguntur, et necessitates ac preces fidelium ad Deum Patrem afferunt (cf. He 5,1-4). Munus Christi Pastoris et Capitis pro sua parte auctoritatis exercentes, familiam Dei, ut fraternitatem in unum animatam, colligunt et per Christum in Spiritu ad Deum Patrem adducunt. In medio gregis Eum in spiritu et veritate adorant (cf. Jn 4,24). In verbo demum et doctrina laborant (cf. 1Tm 5,17), credentes quod in lege Domini meditantes legerint, docentes quod crediderint, imitantes quod docuerint.
Presbyters, the provident cooperators of the episcopal order and its help and instrument, called to serve the People of God, constitute one presbytery with their bishop, dedicated to different offices. In each local congregation of the faithful they in a certain way make the bishop present, with whom they are joined with confident and great spirit, and take up his functions and solicitude in part and exercise daily care. Under the authority of the bishop they sanctify and govern the portion of the Lord's flock assigned to them, make the universal church visible in their place, and bring strong help to building up the whole body of Christ (cf. Eph 4:12). Always intent on the good of the sons of God, they should strive to contribute their work to the pastoral work of the whole diocese, indeed of the whole Church. On account of this participation in priesthood and mission, let presbyters truly acknowledge the bishop as father and reverently obey him. The bishop, however, should consider his presbyters as his cooperators, as sons and friends, just as Christ called His disciples no longer servants but friends (cf. Jn 15:15). Therefore all priests, both diocesan and religious, are incorporated into the body of bishops by reason of Order and ministry, and serve the good of the whole Church according to their vocation and grace.
¶119
Presbyteri, ordinis Episcopalis providi cooperatores eiusque adiutorium et organum, ad Populo Dei inserviendum vocati, unum presbyterium cum suo Episcopo constituunt, diversis quidem officiis mancipatum. In singulis localibus fidelium congregationibus Episcopum, quocum fidenti et magno animo consociantur, quodammodo praesentem reddunt eiusque munera et sollicitudinem pro parte suscipiunt et cura cotidiana exercent. Qui sub auctoritate Episcopi portionem gregis dominici sibi addictam sanctificant et regunt, Ecclesiam universalem in suo loco visibilem faciunt et in aedificando toto corpore Christi (cf. Ep 4,12) validam opem afferunt. Ad bonum autem filiorum Dei semper intenti operam suam ad opus pastorale totius dioeceseos, immo totius Ecclesiae conferre studeant. Propter hanc in sacerdotio et missione participationem Presbyteri Episcopum vere ut patrem suum agnoscant eique reverenter oboediant. Episcopus vero Sacerdotes cooperatores suos ut filios et amicos consideret, sicut Christus discipulos suos iam non servos, sed amicos vocat (cf. Jn 15,15). Corpori igitur Episcoporum, ratione Ordinis et ministerii, omnes sacerdotes, tum dioecesani tum religiosi coaptantur et bono totius Ecclesiae pro sua vocatione et gratia inserviunt.
By virtue of the common sacred ordination and mission, all presbyters are bound together in intimate fraternity, which should spontaneously and willingly manifest itself in mutual help, both spiritual and material, both pastoral and personal, in meetings and in communion of life, labor, and charity. Let them exercise the care of the faithful, whom they have spiritually begotten by baptism and doctrine (cf. 1 Cor 4:15; 1 Pet 1:23), as fathers in Christ. Made a form of the flock from the heart (1 Pet 5:3), let them so preside over and serve their local community that it may be able to be called worthily by that name by which the one and whole People of God is marked, namely the Church of God (cf. 1 Cor 1:2; 2 Cor 1:1). Let them be mindful that by their daily conversation and solicitude they ought to exhibit the face of a truly priestly and pastoral ministry to the faithful and the unfaithful, to catholics and non-catholics, and to bear witness to truth and life to all, and as good shepherds seek also those (cf. Lk 15:4-7) who, although baptized in the catholic Church, have fallen away from the practice of the sacraments, or indeed from the faith.
¶120
Vi communis sacrae ordinationis et missionis Presbyteri omnes inter se intima fraternitate nectuntur, quae sponte ac libenter sese manifestet in mutuo auxilio, tam spirituali quam materiali, tam pastorali quam personali, in conventibus et communione vitae, laboris et caritatis. Fidelium vero, quos spiritualiter baptismate et doctrina genuerunt (cf. 1Co 4,15 1P 1,23), curam tamquam patres in Christo agant. Forma facti gregis ex animo (l PT 5,3) suae communitati locali ita praesint et inserviant, ut ista digne vocari possit illo nomine, quo unus et totus Populus Dei insignitur, Ecclesiae scilicet Dei (cf. 1Co 1,2 2Co 1,1 cf.. Memores sint se sua cotidiana conversatione et sollicitudine fidelibus et infidelibus, catholicis et non catholicis, faciem ministerii vere sacerdotalis et pastoralis exhibere, omnibusque testimonium veritatis et vitae reddere debere, et ut boni pastores illos quoque quaerere (cf. Lc 15,4-7), qui baptizati quidem in Ecclesia catholica a praxi sacramentorum, vel imo a fide defecerunt.
Because the human race today is more and more coalescing into civil, economic, and social unity, the more it is necessary that priests, with united care and help under the guidance of the bishops and the Supreme Pontiff, remove every form of dispersion, that the whole human race may be led into the unity of the family of God.
¶121
Quia genus humanum hodie magis magisque in unitatem civilem, oeconomicam et socialem coalescit, eo magis oportet ut sacerdotes, coniuncta cura et ope sub ductu Episcoporum et Summi Pontificis, omnem rationem dispersionis elidant, ut in unitatem familiae Dei totum genus humanum adducatur.
At the lower grade of the hierarchy stand deacons, upon whom hands are imposed not for the priesthood but for the ministry. For, strengthened by sacramental grace, they serve the People of God in the diaconia of liturgy, word, and charity, in communion with the bishop and his presbytery. It is the deacon's office, as assigned to him by competent authority, to administer baptism solemnly, to reserve and distribute the Eucharist, to assist at and bless marriages in the name of the Church, to bring Viaticum to the dying, to read sacred Scripture to the faithful, to instruct and exhort the people, to preside over the cult and prayer of the faithful, to administer sacramentals, to be in charge of the funeral and burial rite. Dedicated to the offices of charity and administration, let deacons be mindful of the admonition of blessed Polycarp: "Merciful, diligent, walking according to the truth of the Lord, who was made the servant of all."
¶122
In gradu inferiori hierarchiae sistunt Diaconi, quibus non ad sacerdotium, sed ad ministerium manus imponuntur. Gratia etenim sacramentali roborati, in diaconia liturgiae, verbi et caritatis Populo Dei, in communione cum Episcopo eiusque presbyterio, inserviunt. Diaconi est, prout ei a competenti auctoritate assignatum fuerit, solemniter baptismum administrare, Eucharistiam servare et distribuere, matrimonio Ecclesiae nomine adsistere et benedicere, Viaticum moribundis deferre, fidelibus sacram legere Scripturam, populum instruere et exhortari, fidelium cultui et orationi praesidere, sacramentalia ministrare, ritui funeris ac sepulturae praeesse. Caritatis et administrationis officiis dediti, meminerint Diaconi moniti Beati Polycarpi: Misericordes, seduli, incedentes iuxta veritatem Domini, qui omnium minister factus est.
Since, however, these offices, most necessary for the life of the Church, can with difficulty be fulfilled in many regions according to the discipline of the Latin Church currently in force, the Diaconate can in the future be restored as a proper and permanent grade of the hierarchy. It pertains to the competent various territorial episcopal conferences, the Supreme Pontiff approving, to decide whether and where it is opportune to institute such deacons for the care of souls. With the consent of the Roman Pontiff, this diaconate can be conferred on men of more mature age, even those living in marriage, as well as on suitable young men, for whom, however, the law of celibacy must remain firm.
¶123
Cum vero haec munera, ad vitam Ecclesiae summopere necessaria, in disciplina Ecclesiae latinae hodie vigenti in pluribus regionibus adimpleri difficulter possint, Diaconatus in futurum tamquam proprius ac permanens gradus hierarchiae restitui poterit. Ad competentes autem varii generis territoriales Episcoporum coetus, approbante ipso Summo Pontifice, spectat decernere, utrum et ubinam pro cura animarum huiusmodi diaconos institui opportunum sit. De consensu Romani Pontificis hic diaconatus viris maturioris aetatis etiam in matrimonio viventibus conferri poterit, necnon iuvenibus idoneis, pro quibus tamen lex coelibatus firma remanere debet.
The holy Synod, having declared the offices of the Hierarchy, gladly turns its attention to the state of those Christ's faithful who are called laity. But if all things which have been said concerning the People of God are directed equally to laity, religious, and clerics, nevertheless certain things pertain particularly to laity, men and women, by reason of their condition and mission, the foundations of which, on account of the special circumstances of our times, must be more fully considered. For sacred pastors know well how much the laity contribute to the good of the whole Church. For pastors know that they were not instituted by Christ to assume upon themselves alone the whole saving mission of the Church toward the world, but that their excellent office is so to feed the faithful and to recognize their ministries and charisms that all may in their own way cooperate in the common work unanimously. For it is necessary that all, doing truth in charity, grow into Him in all things, who is the head Christ: from whom the whole body, compacted and knit together by every joint of supply, according to the operation in the measure of each member, makes increase of the body to the building up of itself in charity (Eph 4:15-16).
¶124
CAPUT IV. DE LAICIS
CHAPTER IV. ON THE LAITY
Sancta Synodus, muneribus Hierarchiae declaratis, libenter animum advertit statui illorum Christifidelium qui laici nuncupantur. Quodsi omnia quae de Populo Dei dicta sunt, ad laicos, religiosos et clericos aequaliter diriguntur, laicis tamen, viris et mulieribus, ratione condicionis et missionis, quaedam particulariter pertinent, quorum fundamenta ob specialia rerum adiuncta nostri temporis magis expendenda sunt. Pastores enim sacri probe norunt quantum laici ad bonum totius Ecclesiae conferant. Sciunt enim Pastores se a Christo non esse institutos, ut totam missionem salvificam Ecclesiae versus mundum in se solos suscipiant, sed praeclarum munus suum esse ita pascere fideles eorumque ministrationes et charismata ita recognoscere, ut cuncti suo modo ad commune opus unanimiter cooperentur. Oportet enim, ut omnes veritatem facientes in caritate, crescamus in Illo per omnia, qui est caput Christus: ex quo totum corpus compactum et connexum per omnem iuncturam subministrationis, secundum operationem in mensuram uniuscuiusque membri, augmentum corporis facit in aedificationem sui in caritate (Ep 4,15-16).
By the name of laity are here understood all Christ's faithful except members of the sacred order and the religious state sanctioned in the Church, namely Christ's faithful who, as being incorporated into Christ by baptism, constituted into the People of God, and made sharers in their own way in the priestly, prophetic, and regal office of Christ, exercise for their own part the mission of the whole Christian people in the Church and in the world.
¶125
Nomine laicorum hic intelleguntur omnes christifideles praeter membra ordinis sacri et status religiosi in Ecclesia sanciti, christifideles scilicet qui, utpote baptismate Christo concorporati, in Populum Dei constituti, et de munere Christi sacerdotali, prophetico et regali suo modo participes facti, pro parte sua missionem totius populi christiani in Ecclesia et in mundo exercent.
A secular character is proper and peculiar to laity. For members of the sacred order, although they can sometimes be engaged in secular matters, even by exercising a secular profession, are by reason of their particular vocation especially and professedly ordained to the sacred ministry, while religious by their state give a splendid and outstanding testimony that the world cannot be transfigured and offered to God without the spirit of the beatitudes. It is the laity's proper vocation to seek the kingdom of God by engaging in temporal affairs and ordering them according to God. They live in the world, that is, in all and each of the worldly duties and works and in the ordinary conditions of family and social life, from which their existence is as it were woven together. There they are called by God that, exercising their proper office, led by the spirit of the Gospel, like leaven they may contribute to the sanctification of the world from within, and thus especially by the testimony of their life, shining with faith, hope, and charity, they may manifest Christ to others. Therefore it specially pertains to them to so illuminate and order all temporal things, with which they are closely joined, that they may always come to be and grow and be to the praise of the Creator and Redeemer.
¶126
Laicis indoles saecularis propria et peculiaris est. Membra enim ordinis sacri, quamquam aliquando in saecularibus versari possunt, etiam saecularem professionem exercendo, ratione suae particularis vocationis praecipue et ex professo ad sacrum ministerium ordinantur, dum religiosi suo statu praeclarum et eximium testimonium reddunt, mundum transfigurari Deoque offerri non posse sine spiritu beatitudinum. Laicorum est, ex vocatione propria, res temporales gerendo et secundum Deum ordinando, regnum Dei quaerere. In saeculo vivunt, scilicet in omnibus et singulis mundi officiis et operibus et in ordinariis vitae familiaris et socialis condicionibus, quibus eorum exsistentia quasi contexitur. Ubi a Deo vocantur, ut suum proprium munus exercendo, spiritu evangelico ducti, fermenti instar ad mundi sanctificationem velut ab intra conferant, sicque praeprimis testimonio vitae suae, fide, spe et caritate fulgentes, Christum aliis manifestent. Ad illos ergo peculiari modo spectat res temporales omnes, quibus arcte coniunguntur, ita illuminare et ordinare, ut secundum Christum iugiter fiant et crescant et sint in laudem Creatoris et Redemptoris.
The holy Church, by divine institution, is ordered and governed with a wonderful variety. For just as in one body we have many members, but all the members have not the same function: so we, many, are one body in Christ, but each members one of another (Rom 12:4-5).
¶127
Ecclesia sancta, ex divina institutione, mira varietate ordinatur et regitur. Sicut enim in uno corpore multa membra habemus, omnia autem membra non eundem actum habent: ita multi unum corpus sumus in Christo singuli autem alter alterius membra (Rm 12,4-5).
There is therefore one chosen People of God: one Lord, one faith, one baptism (Eph 4:5); common dignity of the members from their regeneration in Christ, common grace of sons, common vocation to perfection, one salvation, one hope, and undivided charity. Therefore there is no inequality in Christ and in the Church, regard being had to race or nation, social condition or sex, for there is neither Jew nor Greek: neither servant nor free: neither male nor female. For you are all one in Christ Jesus (Gal 3:28; cf. Col 3:11).
¶128
Unus est ergo Populus Dei electus: unus Dominus, una fides, unum baptisma (Ep 4,5); communis dignitas membrorum ex eorum in Christo regeneratione, communis filiorum gratia, communis ad perfectionem vocatio, una salus, una spes indivisaque caritas. Nulla igitur in Christo et in Ecclesia inaequalitas, spectata stirpe vel natione, condicione sociali vel sexu, quia non est Iudaeus neque Graecus: non est servus neque liber: non est masculus neque femina. Omnes enim vos "unus" estis in Christo Iesu (Ga 3,28 gr.; cf. Col 3,11).
If therefore in the Church not all walk by the same path, yet all are called to holiness and have obtained co-equal faith in the justice of God (cf. 2 Pet 1:1). Although some by the will of Christ are constituted as teachers, stewards of the mysteries, and shepherds for others, yet true equality among all flourishes as to dignity and common action for building up the body of Christ. For the distinction which the Lord placed between sacred ministers and the rest of the People of God carries with it conjunction, since pastors and other faithful are bound together by a common mutual relation; the pastors of the Church, following the example of the Lord, minister to one another and to the other faithful, while these gladly offer their cooperative work to the pastors and teachers. Thus in variety all bear witness to the wonderful unity in the body of Christ: for the very diversity of graces, ministries, and works gathers the sons of God into one, because "all these things work one and the same Spirit" (1 Cor 12:11).
¶129
Si igitur in Ecclesia non omnes eadem via incedunt, omnes tamen ad sanctitatem vocantur et coaequalem sortiti sunt fidem in iustitia Dei (cf. 2P 1,1). Etsi quidam ex voluntate Christi ut doctores, mysteriorum dispensatores et pastores pro aliis constituuntur, vera tamen inter omnes viget aequalitas quoad dignitatem et actionem cunctis fidelibus communem circa aedificationem Corporis Christi. Distinctio enim quam Dominus posuit inter sacros ministros et reliquum Populum Dei, secumfert coniunctionem, cum Pastores et alii fideles inter se communi necessitudine devinciantur; Ecclesiae Pastores, exemplum Domini secuti, sibi invicem aliisque fidelibus ministrent, hi autem alacriter Pastoribus et doctoribus sociam operam praestent. Sic in varietate omnes testimonium perhibent de mirabili unitate in Corpore Christi: ipsa enim diversitas gratiarum, ministrationum et operationum filios Dei in unum colligit, quia haec omnia operatur unus atque idem Spiritus (1Co 12,11).
Therefore, just as the laity, by divine condescension, have Christ as a brother, who, although He is Lord of all, came not to be ministered unto but to minister (cf. Mt 20:28), so also they have as brothers those who, placed in the sacred ministry, by the authority of Christ so feed the family of God by teaching, sanctifying, and governing, that the new commandment of charity be fulfilled by all. Wherefore St. Augustine most beautifully says: "Where I am terrified by what I am to you, I am consoled by what I am with you. To you I am a bishop, with you I am a Christian. The former is the name of an office, the latter of grace; the former is peril, the latter salvation."
¶130
Laici igitur sicut ex divina dignatione fratrem habent Christum, qui cum sit Dominus omnium, venit tamen non ministrari sed ministrare (cf. Mt 20,28), ita etiam fratres habent eos, qui in sacro ministerio positi, auctoritate Christi docendo et sanctificando et regendo familiam Dei ita pascunt, ut mandatum novum caritatis ab omnibus impleatur. Quocirca pulcherrime dicit S. Augustinus: Ubi me terret quod vobis sum, ibi me consolatur quod vobiscum sum. Vobis enim sum episcopus, vobiscum sum christianus. Illud est nomen officii, hoc gratiae; illud periculi est, hoc salutis.
Laity, gathered into the People of God and constituted into one body of Christ under one head, whoever they are, are called, as living members, to contribute all their forces, received from the benefit of the Creator and the grace of the Redeemer, to the increase of the Church and her continual sanctification.
¶131
Laici in Populo Dei congregati et in uno Corpore Christi sub uno capite constituti, quicumque sunt, vocantur, ut tamquam viva membra ad Ecclesiae incrementum eiusque iugem sanctificationem vires suas omnes, beneficio Creatoris et gratia Redemptoris acceptas, conferant.
The apostolate of the laity is a participation in the saving mission of the Church itself, to which apostolate all are deputed by the Lord Himself through baptism and confirmation. By the sacraments, especially the holy Eucharist, that charity toward God and man, which is the soul of the whole apostolate, is communicated and nourished. Laity are especially called to make the Church present and active in those places and circumstances where she cannot become the salt of the earth except through them. Thus every layman, by the very gifts given to him, is both a witness and a living instrument of the mission of the Church itself according to the measure of the gift of Christ (Eph 4:7).
¶132
Apostolatus autem laicorum est participatio ipsius salvificae missionis Ecclesiae, ad quem apostolatum omnes ab ipso Domino per baptismum et confirmationem deputantur. Sacramentis autem, praesertim sacra Eucharistia, communicatur et alitur illa caritas erga Deum et homines, quae anima est totius apostolatus. Laici autem speciatim ad hoc vocantur, ut praesentem et actuosam reddant Ecclesiam in eis locis et rerum adiunctis, ubi ipsa nonnisi per eos sal terrae evadere potest. Sic omnis laicus, ex ipsis donis sibi collatis, testis simul et vivum instrumentum missionis ipsius Ecclesiae exsistit secundum mensuram donationis Christi (Ep 4,7).
Besides this apostolate, which pertains to absolutely all Christ's faithful, laity can also be called in various ways to more immediate cooperation with the apostolate of the Hierarchy, after the manner of those men and women who helped Paul the apostle in the Gospel, laboring much in the Lord (cf. Phil 4:3; Rom 16:3 ff.). Moreover, they enjoy aptitude to be assumed by the Hierarchy into certain ecclesiastical offices, to be exercised for a spiritual purpose.
¶133
Praeter hunc apostolatum, qui ad omnes omnino christifideles spectat, laici insuper diversis modis ad cooperationem magis immediatam cum apostolatu Hierarchiae vocari possunt, ad modum illorum virorum ac mulierum, qui Paulum apostolum in Evangelio adiuvabant, multum in Domino laborantes (cf. Ph 4,3 Rm 16,3 ss.). Praeterea aptitudine gaudent, ut ad quaedam munera ecclesiastica, ad finem spiritualem exercenda, ab Hierarchia adsumantur.
Therefore all laity have the splendid duty of working that the divine plan of salvation may reach all men of all times and everywhere on earth more and more. Hence let the way be open to them everywhere, that according to their strength and the necessities of the times they may vigorously participate in the saving work of the Church.
¶134
Omnibus igitur laicis onus praeclarum incumbit adlaborandi, ut divinum salutis propositum ad universos homines omnium temporum et ubique terrarum magis magisque pertingat. Via proinde eisdem undequaque pateat, ut pro suis viribus temporumque necessitatibus opus salutare Ecclesiae naviter et ipsi participent.
The supreme and eternal Priest, Christ Jesus, since He wishes to continue His testimony and service also through the laity, vivifies them with His Spirit and unceasingly impels them to every good and perfect work.
¶135
Supremus et aeternus Sacerdos Christus Iesus, cum etiam per laicos suum testimonium suumque servitium continuare velit, eos suo Spiritu vivificat indesinenterque impellit ad omne opus bonum et perfectum.
For to those whom He intimately joins to His life and mission, He also gives a share in His own priestly office, for the exercise of a spiritual cult, that God may be glorified and men saved. For this reason laity, as dedicated to Christ and anointed by the Holy Spirit, are wonderfully called and instructed that ever more abundant fruits of the Spirit may be produced in them. For all their works, prayers, and apostolic undertakings, conjugal and family life, daily work, relaxation of mind and body, if they are carried out in the Spirit, indeed even the hardships of life if patiently borne, become spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God through Jesus Christ (cf. 1 Pet 2:5), which are most piously offered to the Father in the celebration of the Eucharist, together with the offering of the Lord's body. Thus laity, as holy adorers everywhere acting, consecrate the world itself to God.
¶136
Illis enim, quos vitae et missioni suae intime coniungit, etiam sui muneris sacerdotalis partem tribuit ad cultum spiritualem exercendum, ut glorificetur Deus et salventur homines. Qua de causa laici, utpote Christo dicati et Spiritu Sancto uncti, mirabiliter vocantur et instruuntur, ut uberiores semper fructus Spiritus in ipsis producantur. Omnia enim eorum opera, preces et incepta apostolica, conversatio coniugalis et familiaris, labor quotidianus, animi corporisque relaxatio, si in Spiritu peragantur, immo molestiae vitae si patienter sustineantur, fiunt spirituales hostiae, acceptabiles Deo per Iesum Christum (cf. 1P 2,5), quae in Eucharistiae celebratione, cum dominici Corporis oblatione, Patri piissime offeruntur. Sic et laici, qua adoratores ubique sancte agentes, ipsum mundum Deo consecrant.
Christ, the great Prophet, who proclaimed the Kingdom of the Father both by the testimony of life and the power of the word, fulfills His prophetic office until the full manifestation of His glory, not only through the Hierarchy, which teaches in His name and power, but also through laity, whom therefore He both constitutes witnesses and supplies with the sense of faith and the grace of the word (cf. Acts 2:17-18; Rev 19:10), that the power of the Gospel may shine in daily, family, and social life. They show themselves sons of the promise if, strong in faith and hope, they redeem the present moment (cf. Eph 5:16; Col 4:5) and by patience await future glory (cf. Rom 8:25). Let them not hide this hope in the interior of their mind, but express it by continual conversion and struggle against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against the spiritual forces of wickedness (Eph 6:12), even through the structures of secular life.
¶137
Christus, Propheta magnus, qui et testimonio vitae et verbi virtute Regnum proclamavit Patris, usque ad plenam manifestationem gloriae suum munus propheticum adimplet, non solum per Hierarchiam, quae nomine et potestate Eius docet, sed etiam per laicos, quos ideo et testes constituit et sensu fidei et gratia verbi instruit (cf. Ac 2,17-18 Ap 19,10), ut virtus Evangelii in vita quotidiana, familiari et sociali eluceat. Ipsi se praebent ut filios repromissionis, si fortes in fide et spe praesens momentum redimunt (cf. Ep 5,16 Col 4,5) et futuram gloriam per patientiam exspectant (cf. Rm 8,25). Hanc autem spem non in animi interioritate abscondant, sed conversione continua et colluctatione adversus mundi rectores tenebrarum harum, contra spiritualia nequitiae (Ep 6,12) etiam per vitae saecularis structuras exprimant.
Just as the sacraments of the New Law, by which the life and apostolate of the faithful are nourished, prefigure a new heaven and a new earth (cf. Rev 21:1), so laity become powerful heralds of the faith of things to be hoped for (cf. Heb 11:1), if they unhesitatingly join the profession of faith to the life of faith. This evangelization, namely the message of Christ proclaimed both by testimony of life and by word, acquires a certain specific and peculiar efficacy from the fact that it is accomplished in the common conditions of the world.
¶138
Sicut sacramenta Novae Legis, quibus vita et apostolatus fidelium alitur, caelum novum et terram novam (cf. Ap 21,1) praefigurant, ita laici evadunt validi praecones fidei sperandarum rerum (cf. He 11,1), si cum vita ex fide professionem fidei inhaesitanter coniungunt. Haec evangelizatio, nuntium Christi scilicet et testimonio vitae et verbo prolatum, notam quamdam specificam et peculiarem efficacitatem acquirit ex hoc, quod in communibus condicionibus saeculi completur.
In this office, that state of life which is sanctified by a special sacrament, namely marital and family life, appears of great value. There the exercise and excellent school of the apostolate of laity is had, where Christian religion pervades the whole institution of life and transforms it more from day to day. There spouses have their own vocation, to be witnesses to each other and to their children of the faith and love of Christ. The Christian family then loudly proclaims both the present virtues of the Kingdom of God and the hope of blessed life. Thus by example and testimony it convicts the world of sin and illuminates those who seek the truth.
¶139
Quo in munere magni pretii apparet ille status vitae, qui speciali sacramento sanctificatur, scilicet vita matrimonialis et familiaris. Ibi exercitium et schola praeclara apostolatus laicorum habetur, ubi religio christiana totam vitae institutionem pervadit et in dies magis transformat. Ibi coniuges propriam habent vocationem, ut sibi invicem et filiis sint testes fidei et amoris Christi. Familia christiana tum praesentes virtutes Regni Dei tum spem vitae beatae alta voce proclamat. Ita exemplo et testimonio suo arguit mundum de peccato et eos qui veritatem quaerunt illuminat.
Therefore laity, even when occupied with temporal cares, can and ought to exercise a precious action for evangelizing the world. And if some of them, in the absence of sacred ministers or when the same are impeded in governance under persecution, supply certain sacred offices according to their ability; and if many indeed expend all their strength in apostolic work: nevertheless it is necessary that all cooperate in the spread and increase of the Kingdom of Christ in the world. Therefore laity should diligently apply themselves to a deeper knowledge of revealed truth, and urgently ask of God the gift of wisdom.
¶140
Proinde laici. etiam quando curis temporalibus occupantur, pretiosam actionem ad evangelizandum mundum exercere possunt et debent. Quodsi quidam eorum, deficientibus sacris ministris, vel iisdem in regimine persecutionis impeditis, quaedam officia sacra pro facultate supplent; et si plures quidem ex eis totas vires suas in opere apostolico impendunt: universos tamen oportet ad dilatationem et incrementum Regni Christi in mundo cooperari. Quapropter laici sollerter in profundiorem cognitionem veritatis revelatae incumbant, et instanter a Deo sapientiae donum impetrent.
Christ, made obedient unto death and on this account exalted by the Father (cf. Phil 2:8-9), entered into the glory of His kingdom. To Him all things are made subject, until He subjects Himself and all created things to the Father, that God may be all in all (cf. 1 Cor 15:27-28). He communicated this power to His disciples, that they also may be constituted in royal liberty and, by self-denial and a holy life, conquer the kingdom of sin in themselves (cf. Rom 6:12), indeed, that serving Christ also in others, by humility and patience they may lead their brothers to the King, to whom to serve is to reign. For the Lord wishes to extend His kingdom also through lay faithful, a kingdom namely of truth and life, a kingdom of holiness and grace, a kingdom of justice, love, and peace; in which kingdom "creation itself will be delivered from the slavery of corruption into the freedom of the glory of the sons of God" (Rom 8:21). Truly a great promise, and a great commandment, is given to disciples: "For all things are yours, and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's" (1 Cor 3:23).
¶141
Christus, factus oboediens usque ad mortem et propter hoc a Patre exaltatus (cf. Ph 2,8-9), in gloriam regni sui intravit. Cui omnia subiiciuntur, donec Ipse se cunctaque creata Patri subiiciat, ut sit Deus omnia in omnibus (cf. 1Co 15,27-28). Quam potestatem discipulis communicavit, ut et illi in regali libertate constituantur et sui abnegatione vitaque sancta regnum peccati in seipsis devincant (cf. Rm 6,12), immo ut Christo etiam in aliis servientes, fratres suos ad Regem, cui servire regnare est, humilitate et patientia perducant. Dominus enim regnum suum etiam per laicos fideles dilatare cupit, regnum scilicet veritatis et vitae, regnum sanctitatis et gratiae, regnum iustitiae, amoris et pacis; in quo regno ipsa creatura liberabitur a servitute corruptionis in libertatem; gloriae filiorum Dei (cf. Rm 8,21). Magna sane promissio, magnumque mandatum discipulis datur: Omnia enim vestra sunt, vos autem Christi, Christus autem Dei (1Co 3,23).
Therefore the faithful ought to recognize the intimate nature, value, and ordering of the whole of creation for the praise of God, and by works even secular to help one another to a holier life, that the world may be imbued with the spirit of Christ and more effectively attain its end in justice, charity, and peace. In this universally fulfilling office laity hold the principal place. Therefore let them, with competence in profane disciplines and with activity, inwardly elevated by the grace of Christ, vigorously contribute that goods created according to the ordering of the Creator and the illumination of His Word be cultivated by human labor, technical art, and civil culture for the utility of absolutely all men, and be more fittingly distributed among them, and in their own way lead to universal progress in human and Christian liberty. Thus Christ through the members of His Church will more and more illuminate the whole human society with His salutary light.
¶142
Fideles igitur totius creaturae intimam naturam, valorem et ordinationem in laudem Dei agnoscere, et per opera etiam saecularia se invicem ad sanctiorem vitam adiuvare debent, ita ut mundus spiritu Christi imbuatur atque in iustitia, caritate et pace finem suum efficacius attingat. In quo officio universaliter adimplendo laici praecipuum locum obtinent. Sua igitur in profanis disciplinis competentia suaque activitate, gratia Christi intrinsecus elevata, valide conferant operam, ut bona creata secundum Creatoris ordinationem Eiusque Verbi illuminationem humano labore, arte technica, civilique cultura ad utilitatem omnium prorsus hominum excolantur, aptiusque inter illos distribuantur, et suo modo ad universalem progressum in humana et christiana libertate conducant. Ita Christus per Ecclesiae membra totam societatem humanam suo salutari lumine magis magisque illuminabit.
Laity moreover, with their forces also joined, should so heal institutions and conditions of the world, if any incite to sin, that all these may be conformed to the norms of justice and rather favor than hinder the exercise of virtues. Thus acting, they will imbue culture and human works with moral value. Thus at the same time the field of the world is better prepared for the seed of the divine word, and the doors of the Church are more widely opened, through which the herald of peace may enter the world.
¶143
Laici praeterea, collatis quoque viribus, instituta et condiciones mundi, si qua mores ad peccatum incitant, ita sanent, ut haec omnia ad iustitiae normas conformentur et virtutum exercitio potius faveant quam obsint. Ita agendo culturam operaque humana valore morali imbuent. Hoc modo simul ager mundi melius pro semine verbi divini paratur, et Ecclesiae latius patent portae, quibus praeconium pacis in mundum introeat.
By the very economy of salvation, the faithful should learn sedulously to distinguish between the rights and duties which are incumbent on them insofar as they are gathered into the Church, and those which pertain to them as members of human society. They will strive to harmonize the two harmoniously, mindful that in every temporal matter they must be guided by a Christian conscience, since no human activity, even in temporal matters, can be withdrawn from God's rule. But in our time it is especially necessary that this distinction and harmony should shine as clearly as possible in the manner of acting of the faithful, that the mission of the Church may be able to respond more fully to the particular conditions of the modern world. For just as it must be acknowledged that the earthly city, rightly dedicated to secular cares, is ruled by its own principles, so the unhappy doctrine which strives to build society without any regard for religion, and impugns and destroys the religious liberty of citizens, is justly rejected.
¶144
Propter ipsam oeconomiam salutis, fideles discant sedulo distinguere inter iura et officia quae eis incumbunt, quatenus Ecclesiae aggregantur, et ea quae eis competunt, ut sunt humanae societatis membra. Utraque inter se harmonice consociare satagent, memores se, in quavis re temporali, christiana conscientia duci debere, cum nulla humana activitas, ne in rebus temporalibus quidem, Dei imperio subtrahi possit. Nostro autem tempore maxime oportet ut distinctio haec simul et harmonia quam clarissime in modo agendi fidelium elucescant, ut missio Ecclesiae particularibus mundi hodierni condicionibus plenius respondere valeat. Sicut enim agnoscendum est terrenam civitatem, saecularibus curis iure addictam propriis regi principiis, ita infausta doctrina, quae societatem, nulla habita religionis ratione, exstruere contendit et libertatem religiosam civium impugnat et eruit, merito reiicitur.
Laity, like all Christ's faithful, have the right to receive abundantly from the spiritual goods of the Church, especially the aids of the word of God and the sacraments, from the sacred pastors, and to make known to them their needs and desires with that freedom and confidence which befits the sons of God and brothers in Christ. According to the knowledge, competence, and excellence which they possess, they have the faculty, indeed sometimes the duty, of declaring their opinion concerning those things which pertain to the good of the Church. Let this be done, if the case requires, through the institutions established by the Church for this purpose, and always in truth, fortitude, and prudence, with reverence and charity toward those who, by reason of their sacred office, hold the person of Christ.
¶145
Laici, sicut omnes christifideles, ius habent ex spiritualibus Ecclesiae bonis, verbi Dei praesertim et sacramentorum adiumenta a sacris Pastoribus abundanter accipiendi, hisque necessitates et optata sua ea libertate et fiducia, quae filios Dei et fratres in Christo decet, patefaciant. Pro scientia, competentia et praestantia quibus pollent, facultatem, immo aliquando et omnium habent suam sententiam de iis quae bonum Ecclesiae respiciunt declarandi. Hoc fiat, si casus ferat, per instituta ad hoc ab Ecclesia stabilita, et semper in veracitate, fortitudine et prudentia, cum reverentia et caritate erga illos, qui ratione sacri sui muneris personam Christi gerunt.
Laity, like all Christ's faithful, should promptly embrace with Christian obedience those things which the sacred pastors, as representing Christ, establish as teachers and rulers in the Church, following the example of Christ, who by His obedience unto death opened to all men the blessed way of the freedom of the sons of God. Nor should they omit to commend their rulers to God by their prayers, who keep watch as about to render an account for our souls, that they may do this with joy and not with grief (cf. Heb 13:17).
¶146
Laici, sicut omnes christifideles, illa quae sacri Pastores, utpote Christum repraesentantes, tamquam magistri et rectores in Ecclesia statuunt, christiana oboedientia prompte amplectantur, Christi exemplum secuti, qui, sua oboedientia usque ad mortem, beatam libertatis filiorum Dei viam omnibus hominibus aperuit. Neque omittant precibus suis Praepositos suos Deo commendare, quippe qui pervigilant quasi rationem pro animabus nostris reddituri, ut cum gaudio hoc faciant et non gementes (cf. He 13,17).
Sacred pastors, however, should recognize and promote the dignity and responsibility of laity in the Church; let them freely use their prudent counsel, confidently entrust to them all things for the service of the Church, and leave them freedom and space to act, indeed add spirit to them, that they may also undertake works on their own initiative. With fatherly love let them attentively consider in Christ the undertakings, vows, and desires proposed by laity. Moreover, they will reverently acknowledge the just freedom which belongs to all in the earthly city.
¶147
Sacri vero Pastores laicorum dignitatem et responsabilitatem in Ecclesia agnoscant et promoveant; libenter eorum prudenti consilio utantur, cum confidentia eis in servitium Ecclesiae omnia committant et eis agendi libertatem et spatium relinquant, immo animum eis addant, ut etiam sua sponte opera aggrediantur. Paterno cum amore coepta, vota et desideria a laicis proposita attente in Christo considerent. Iustam autem libertatem, quae omnibus in civitate terrestri competit, Pastores observanter agnoscent.
From this familiar intercourse between laity and pastors many good things for the Church are to be expected: for thus in laity the sense of their own responsibility is strengthened, alacrity is fostered, and the forces of laity are more easily associated with the work of the pastors. These, helped by the experience of laity, can judge more distinctly and more aptly both in spiritual and in temporal matters, so that the whole Church, strengthened by all its members, may more effectively fulfill its mission for the life of the world.
¶148
Ex hoc familiari commercio inter Laicos et Pastores permulta bona Ecclesiae exspectanda sunt: ita enim in laicis roboratur propriae responsabilitatis sensus, fovetur alacritas, et facilius laicorum vires Pastorum operi associantur. Hi vero, laicorum experientia adiuti, tam in rebus spiritualibus quam in temporalibus, distinctius et aptius iudicare valent, ita ut tota Ecclesia, ab omnibus membris suis roborata, suam pro mundi vita missionem efficacius compleat.
Every layman should be before the world a witness of the resurrection and life of the Lord Jesus, and a sign of the living God. All together and each according to his part should nourish the world with spiritual fruits (cf. Gal 5:22), and diffuse in it the spirit with which are animated those poor, meek, and peaceful, whom the Lord proclaimed blessed in the Gospel (cf. Mt 5:3-9). In a word, what the soul is in the body, let Christians be in the world.
¶149
Unusquisque laicus debet esse coram saeculo testis resurrectionis et vitae Domini Iesu atque signum Dei vivi. Omnes insimul et unusquisque pro sua parte mundum fructibus spiritualibus alere debent (cf. Ga 5,22), in eumque spiritum diffundere, quo animantur illi pauperes, mites et pacifici, quos Dominus in Evangelio beatos proclamavit (cf. Mt 5,3-9). Uno verbo, quod anima est in corpore, hoc sint in mundo christiani.
The Church, whose mystery is set forth by the sacred Synod, is believed to be indefectibly holy. For Christ, the Son of God, who with the Father and the Spirit alone is celebrated as holy, loved the Church as His spouse, delivering Himself up for her, that He might sanctify her (cf. Eph 5:25-26), and joined her to Himself as His body and enriched her with the gift of the Holy Spirit, to the glory of God. Therefore in the Church all, whether they belong to the Hierarchy or are fed by it, are called to holiness, according to that of the Apostle: "For this is the will of God, your sanctification" (1 Th 4:3; cf. Eph 1:4). This holiness of the Church is constantly manifested and ought to be manifested in the fruits of grace which the Spirit produces in the faithful; it is multiformly expressed among individuals, who in their own order of life tend to the perfection of charity, building up others; it appears in a proper way in the practice of the counsels, which are commonly called evangelical. The practice of these counsels, undertaken by many Christians, whether privately or in a condition or state sanctioned in the Church, bears and ought to bear a splendid testimony and example of the same holiness in the world.
¶150
CAPUT V. DE UNIVERSALI VOCATIONE AD SANCTITATEM IN ECCLESIA
CHAPTER V. ON THE UNIVERSAL VOCATION TO HOLINESS IN THE CHURCH
Ecclesia, cuius mysterium a Sacra Synodo proponitur, indefectibiliter sancta creditur. Christus enim, Dei Filius, qui cum Patre et Spiritu solus Sanctus celebratur, Ecclesiam tamquam sponsam suam dilexit, Seipsum tradens pro ea, ut illam sanctificaret (cf. Ep 5,25-26), eamque Sibi ut corpus suum coniunxit atque Spiritus Sancti dono cumulavit, ad gloriam Dei. Ideo in Ecclesia omnes, sive ad Hierarchiam pertinent sive ab ea pascuntur, ad sanctitatem vocantur, iuxta illud Apostoli: Haec est enim voluntas Dei, sanctificatio vestra (1Th 4,3 cf. Ep 1,4). Haec autem Ecclesiae sanctitas in gratiae fructibus quos Spiritus in fidelibus producit, incessanter manifestatur et manifestari debet; multiformiter exprimitur apud singulos, qui in suo vitae ordine ad perfectionem caritatis, aedificantes alios, tendunt; proprio quodam modo apparet in praxi consiliorum, quae evangelica appellari consueverunt. Quae consiliorum praxis, Spiritu Sancto impellente, a multis christianis assumpta, sive privatim sive in conditione vel statu in Ecclesia sancitis, praeclarum in mundo fert, et ferre oportet, eiusdem sanctitatis testimonium et exemplum.
The divine Master and Exemplar of all perfection, the Lord Jesus, preached holiness of life, of which He is both author and consummator, to all and each of His disciples of whatever condition: "Be therefore perfect, as also your heavenly Father is perfect" (Mt 5:48). For He sent the Holy Spirit into all, who moves them from within, that they may love God from their whole heart, from their whole soul, from their whole mind, and from their whole strength (cf. Mk 12:30), and that they may love one another as Christ loved them (cf. Jn 13:34; 15:12). The followers of Christ, called by God not according to their works but according to His purpose and grace, and justified in the Lord Jesus, have been truly made sons of God and partakers of the divine nature in the baptism of faith, and therefore are really made holy. Hence they must hold and perfect, God giving, the sanctification they have received. They are admonished by the Apostle to live as becomes saints (Eph 5:3), and to put on as the elect of God, holy and beloved, the bowels of mercy, benignity, humility, modesty, patience (Col 3:12), and to have the fruits of the Spirit unto sanctification (cf. Gal 5:22; Rom 6:22). Since, however, in many things we all offend (cf. Jas 3:2), we constantly need the mercy of God and ought to pray daily: "Forgive us our debts" (Mt 6:12).
¶151
Omnis perfectionis divinus Magister et Exemplar, Dominus Iesus, sanctitatem vitae, cuius Ipse et auctor et consummator exstat, omnibus et singulis discipulis suis cuiuscumque conditionis praedicavit: Estote ergo vos perfecti, sicut et Pater vester coelestis perfectus est (Mt 5,48). In omnes enim Spiritum Sanctum misit, qui eos intus moveat, ut Deum diligant ex toto corde, ex tota anima, ex tota mente et ex tota virtute sua (cf. Mc 12,30), et ut invicem se diligant sicut Christus eos dilexit (cf. Jn 13,34 Jn 15,12). Christi asseclae a Deo non secundum opera sua, sed secundum propositum et gratiam Eius vocati atque in Iesu Domino iustificati, in fidei baptismate vere filii Dei et consortes divinae naturae, ideoque reapse sancti effecti sunt. Eos proinde oportet sanctificationem quam acceperunt, Deo dante, vivendo tenere atque perficere. Ab Apostolo monentur, ut vivant sicut decet sanctos (Ep 5,3), et induant sicut electi Dei, sancti et dilecti, viscera misericordiae, benignitatem, humilitatem, modestiam, patientiam (Col 3,12), fructusque Spiritus habeant in sanctificationem (cf. Ga 5,22 Rm 6,22). Cum vero in multis offendimus omnes (cf. Jc 3,2), misericordiae Dei iugiter egemus atque orare quotidie debemus: Et dimitte nobis debita nostra (Mt 6,12).
Hence it is clear to all that all Christ's faithful of whatever state or order are called to the fullness of Christian life and the perfection of charity, by which holiness a more human manner of living is also promoted in earthly society. To attain this perfection, let the faithful use the forces received according to the measure of the gift of Christ, that, following His footprints and made conformed to His image, obeying the will of the Father in all things, they may devote themselves with whole spirit to the glory of God and the service of neighbor. Thus the holiness of the People of God will grow into abundant fruits, as is lucidly shown in the history of the Church through the life of so many saints.
¶152
Cunctis proinde perspicuum est, omnes christifideles cuiuscumque status vel ordinis ad vitae christianae plenitudinem et caritatis perfectionem vocari, qua sanctitate, in societate quoque terrena, humanior vivendi modus promovetur. Ad quam perfectionem adipiscendam fideles vires secundum mensuram donationis Christi acceptas adhibeant, ut Eius vestigia sequentes Eiusque imagini conformes effecti, voluntatem Patris in omnibus obsequentes, gloriae Dei et servitio proximi toto animo sese devoveant. Ita sanctitas Populi Dei in abundantes fructus excrescet, sicut in Ecclesiae historia per tot Sanctorum vitam luculenter commonstratur.
In various types of life and offices, one holiness is cultivated by all who are moved by the Spirit of God, and, obeying the voice of the Father and adoring God the Father in spirit and truth, follow the poor, humble, and cross-bearing Christ, that they may deserve to be partakers of His glory. But each must walk unhesitatingly according to his own gifts and offices by the way of living faith, which excites hope and works through charity.
¶153
In variis vitae generibus et officiis una sanctitas excolitur ab omnibus, qui a Spiritu Dei aguntur, atque voci Patris oboedientes Deumque Patrem in spiritu et veritate adorantes, Christum pauperem, humilem, et crucem baiulantem sequuntur, ut gloriae Eius mereantur esse consortes. Unusquisque vero secundum propria dona et munera per viam fidei vivae, quae spem excitat et per caritatem operatur, incunctanter incedere debet.
The pastors of the flock of Christ, first of all, ought to exercise their ministry holily and alacritously, humbly and strongly, after the image of the supreme and eternal Priest, the Shepherd and Bishop of our souls, which ministry, thus fulfilled, will be for them also a most exalted means of sanctification. Chosen to the fullness of the priesthood, they are endowed with sacramental grace, that by praying, sacrificing, and preaching, through every form of episcopal care and service, they may exercise the perfect office of pastoral charity, not fear to lay down their lives for the sheep, and, made a form of the flock (cf. 1 Pet 5:3), may promote the Church to greater holiness day by day also by their example.
¶154
Gregis Christi Pastores imprimis oportet, ut ad imaginem summi et aeterni Sacerdotis, Pastoris et Episcopi animarum nostrarum, sancte et alacriter, humiliter et fortiter exsequantur ministerium suum, quod ita adimpletum, etiam pro eis erit praecelsum sanctificationis medium. Ad plenitudinem sacerdotii electi, sacramentali gratia donantur, ut orando, sacrificando et praedicando, per omnem formam episcopalis curae et servitii, perfectum pastoralis caritatis munus exerceant, animam suam pro ovibus ponere ne timeant et forma facti gregis (cf. 1P 5,3), Ecclesiam etiam exemplo suo ad maiorem in dies sanctitatem promoveant.
Presbyters, in the likeness of the episcopal order, whose spiritual crown they form, by the grace of their office participating through Christ, the eternal and unique Mediator, grow in the love of God and neighbor by the daily exercise of their office, preserve the bond of priestly communion, abound in all spiritual good, and give a living testimony of God to all, emulating those priests who in the course of the ages, often in humble and hidden service, have left a splendid specimen of holiness. Their praise is in the Church of God. Praying and offering sacrifice for their people and the whole People of God by office, recognizing what they do and imitating what they handle, far from being hindered by apostolic cares, perils, and hardships, they ascend by them to higher holiness, nourishing and fostering their action from the abundance of contemplation, for the delight of the whole Church of God. All presbyters, and especially those who by the special title of their ordination are called diocesan priests, should be mindful how much faithful union and generous cooperation with their bishop contributes to their sanctification.
¶155
Presbyteri in similitudinem ordinis Episcoporum, quorum spiritualem coronam efformant, de eorum muneris gratia participantes per Christum, aeternum et unicum Mediatorem, quotidiano officii sui exercitio in Dei proximique amore crescant, communionis sacerdotalis vinculum servent, in omni bono spirituali abundent atque vivum Dei testimonium omnibus praestent, aemuli eorum sacerdotum, qui saeculorum decursu, in humili saepe et abscondito servitio praeclarum sanctitatis specimen reliquerunt. Quorum laus est in Ecclesia Dei. Pro plebe sua et toto Populo Dei ex officio precantes et sacrificium offerentes, agnoscendo quod agunt et imitando quod tractant, nedum apostolicis curis, periculis et aerumnis impediantur, per ea potius ad altiorem sanctitatem ascendant, ex abundantia contemplationis actionem suam nutriendo et fovendo, in oblectamentum totius Ecclesiae Dei. Omnes presbyteri et speciatim illi qui peculiari suae ordinationis titulo sacerdotes dioecesani vocantur, meminerint quantum ad suam sanctificationem conferant cum suo Episcopo fidelis coniunctio atque generosa cooperatio.
Of the ministry and grace of the supreme Priest, those of the lower order of ministers, especially deacons, are sharers in a particular way, who, serving the mysteries of Christ and the Church, ought to keep themselves pure from every vice, and to please God and provide for all good before men (cf. 1 Tim 3:8-10, 12-13). Clerics, who are called by the Lord and set apart for His part, under the vigilance of pastors prepare themselves for the offices of ministers, are bound to conform their minds and hearts to so excellent an election, assiduous in prayer, fervent in love, thinking on whatsoever things are true, just, and of good fame, doing all things for the glory and honor of God. To these are added those laity chosen by God, who, that they may fully devote themselves to apostolic works, are called by the bishop and labor with much fruit in the Lord's field.
¶156
Missionis autem et gratiae supremi Sacerdotis peculiari modo participes sunt inferioris quoque ordinis ministri, imprimis Diaconi, qui mysteriis Christi et Ecclesiae servientes, ab omni vitio puros se custodire atque Deo placere et omne bonum coram hominibus providere debent (cf. 1Tm 3,8-10 1Tm 3,12-13). Clerici, qui a Domino vocati et in partem Eius sepositi, sub vigilantia Pastorum ad officia ministrorum se praeparant, mentes et corda sua tam praeclarae electioni conformare tenentur, in oratione assidui, amore ferventes, quaecumque sunt vera, iusta et bonae famae cogitantes, omnia in gloriam et honorem Dei perficientes. Quibus accedunt illi a Deo electi laici, qui, ut plene se dedant apostolicis operibus, ab Episcopo vocantur et in agro Domini cum multo fructu laborant.
Christian spouses and parents ought, following their own way, to support one another in grace with faithful love throughout the whole course of life, and imbue their lovingly accepted offspring from God with Christian doctrines and evangelical virtues. For thus they give to all an example of indefatigable and generous love, build up the brotherhood of charity, and are witnesses and cooperators of the fecundity of Mother Church, in sign and participation of that love with which Christ loved His Spouse and delivered Himself up for her. A similar example is given in another way by widows and the unmarried, who themselves also can contribute not a little to holiness and activity in the Church. Those, who are engaged in often hard labors, ought to perfect themselves by human works, to help their fellow citizens, to promote the whole society and creation to a better state, and also to imitate Christ, whose hands were exercised with work, and who always works with the Father for the salvation of all, in active charity, rejoicing in hope, bearing one another's burdens, and by their daily labor itself to ascend to higher, even apostolic, holiness.
¶157
Coniuges autem parentesque christiani oportet ut propriam viam sequentes, amore fideli, totius vitae decursu se invicem in gratia sustineant, et prolem amanter a Deo acceptam christianis doctrinis et evangelicis virtutibus imbuant. Ita enim exemplum indefessi et generosi amoris omnibus praebent, fraternitatem caritatis aedificant, et foecunditatis Matris Ecclesiae testes et cooperatores exsistunt, in signum et participationem illius dilectionis, qua Christus Sponsam suam dilexit Seque pro ea tradidit. Simile exemplum alio modo praebetur a viduis et innuptis, qui ad sanctitatem et operositatem in Ecclesia, et ipsi haud parum conferre possunt. Ii vero, qui laboribus saepe duris incumbunt, oportet ut humanis operibus seipsos perficiant, concives adiuvent, totamque societatem et creationem ad meliorem statum promoveant, verum etiam ut Christum, cuius manus fabrilibus se exercuerunt et qui semper cum Patre ad salutem omnium operatur, in actuosa caritate imitentur, spe gaudentes, alter alterius onera portantes, atque ipso suo quotidiano labore ad altiorem ascendant sanctitatem etiam apostolicam.
Those who are oppressed by poverty, infirmity, sickness, and various hardships, or who suffer persecution for justice's sake, should know that they are specially united to Christ suffering for the salvation of the world, whom the Lord proclaimed blessed in the Gospel, and whom God, the God of all grace, who called us into His eternal glory in Christ Jesus, after having suffered a little, will perfect, confirm, and establish (cf. 1 Pet 5:10).
¶158
Specialiter etiam Christo pro salute mundi patienti se uniri sciant ii, qui paupertate, infirmitate, morbo, variisque aerumnis opprimuntur, vel persecutionem propter iustitiam patiuntur, quos Dominus in Evangelio beatos praedicavit et quos Deus... omnis gratiae, qui vocavit nos in aeternam suam gloriam in Christo Iesu, modicum passos, ipse perficiet, confirmabit solidabitque (1P 5,10).
Therefore all Christ's faithful, in their conditions, offices, or circumstances of life, and through all these, will be more and more sanctified day by day, if they receive all things from the hand of the heavenly Father with faith and cooperate with the divine will, manifesting to all in temporal service the charity with which God loved the world.
¶159
Omnes igitur christifideles in vitae suae conditionibus, officiis vel circumstantiis, et per illa omnia, in dies magis sanctificabuntur, si cuncta e manu Patris coelestis cum fide suscipiunt et voluntati divinae cooperantur, caritatem qua Deus dilexit mundum in ipso temporali servitio omnibus manifestando.
God is charity, and he who abides in charity abides in God, and God in him (1 Jn 4:16). But God has diffused His charity in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to us (cf. Rom 5:5); therefore the first and most necessary gift is charity, by which we love God above all things and our neighbor for His sake. So that charity, as a good seed, may grow and fructify in the soul, every faithful must willingly hear the word of God and, with His grace helping, do His will in work, frequently participate in the sacraments, especially the Eucharist, and sacred actions, and constantly apply himself to prayer, self-denial, active fraternal service, and the exercise of all virtues. For charity, as the bond of perfection and the fullness of the law (cf. Col 3:14; Rom 13:10), guides all means of sanctification, informs them, and leads them to the end. Hence the true disciple of Christ is marked by charity both toward God and toward neighbor.
¶160
Deus caritas est, et qui manet in caritate, in Deo manet, et Deus in eo (1Jn 4,16). Deus autem caritatem suam in cordibus nostris diffudit per Spiritum Sanctum qui datus est nobis (cf. Rm 5,5); ideoque donum primum et maxime necessarium est caritas, qua Deum super omnia et proximum propter Illum diligimus. Ut vero caritas tamquam bonum semen in anima increscat et fructificet, unusquisque fidelis debet verbum Dei libenter audire Eiusque voluntatem, opitulante Eius gratia, opere complere, sacramentis, praesertim Eucharistiae, et sacris actionibus frequenter participare, seseque orationi, sui ipsius abnegationi, fraterno actuoso servitio et omnium virtutum exercitationi constanter applicare. Caritas enim, ut vinculum perfectionis et plenitudo legis (cf. Col 3,14 Rm 13,10), omnia sanctificationis media regit, informat ad finemque perducit. Unde caritate tum in Deum tum in proximum signatur verus Christi discipulus.
Since Jesus, the Son of God, manifested His charity by laying down His soul for us, no one has greater love than he who lays down his soul for Him and for his brothers (cf. 1 Jn 3:16; Jn 15:13). Therefore, to give this greatest testimony of love before all, especially persecutors, some Christians have been called from the first times and will always be called. Martyrdom, therefore, by which the disciple is assimilated to the Master, who freely accepts death for the salvation of the world, and is conformed to Him in the shedding of blood, is esteemed by the Church as an excellent gift and the supreme proof of charity. Although it is given to few, all however must be prepared to confess Christ before men and to follow Him on the way of the cross in the persecutions which never cease from the Church.
¶161
Cum Iesus, Dei Filius, caritatem suam manifestaverit, animam suam pro nobis ponendo, nemo maiorem habet dilectionem, quam qui animam suam pro Eo et fratribus suis ponit (cf. Io. Jn 3,16 Jn 15,13). Ad hoc ergo maximum amoris testimonium reddendum coram omnibus, praesertim persecutoribus, aliqui christiani iam a primo tempore vocati sunt et semper vocabuntur. Martyrium igitur, quo discipulus Magistro pro mundi salute mortem libere accipienti assimilatur, Eique in effusione sanguinis conformatur, ab Ecclesia eximium donum supremaque probatio caritatis aestimatur. Quod si paucis datur, omnes tamen parati sint oportet, Christum coram hominibus confiteri, Eumque inter persecutiones, quae Ecclesiae numquam desunt, in via crucis subsequi.
The holiness of the Church is also specially fostered by the multiple counsels which the Lord proposes to His disciples to be observed in the Gospel. Among these stands out the precious gift of divine grace, which is given to some by the Father (cf. Mt 19:11; 1 Cor 7:7), to devote themselves more easily with undivided heart (cf. 1 Cor 7:32-34) to God alone in virginity or celibacy. This perfect continence for the sake of the kingdom of heaven has always been held in special honor by the Church, as a sign and stimulus of charity, and as a peculiar source of spiritual fecundity in the world.
¶162
Sanctitas Ecclesiae item speciali modo fovetur multiplicibus consiliis, quae Dominus in Evangelio discipulis suis observanda proponit. Inter quae eminet pretiosum gratiae divinae donum, quod a Patre quibusdam datur (cf. Mt 19,11 1Co 7,7), ut in virginitate vel coelibatu facilius indiviso corde (cf. 1Co 7,32-34) Deo soli se devoveant. Haec perfecta propter Regnum coelorum continentia semper in honore praecipuo ab Ecclesia habita est, tamquam signum et stimulus caritatis, ac quidam peculiaris fons spiritualis foecunditatis in mundo.
The Church also considers the Apostle's admonition, who, provoking the faithful to charity, exhorts them to feel in themselves that which was also in Christ Jesus, who emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant... becoming obedient unto death (Phil 2:7-8), and became poor for our sakes, whereas He was rich (2 Cor 8:9). Since imitation and testimony of this charity and humility of Christ must always be given by disciples, Mother Church rejoices that many men and women are found in her bosom who follow more closely and show more clearly the self-emptying of the Savior, receiving poverty in the freedom of the sons of God and renouncing their own wills: namely those who subject themselves to man for the sake of God in the matter of perfection beyond the measure of the precept, that they may more fully conform themselves to the obedient Christ.
¶163
Ecclesia etiam Apostoli monitionem recogitat, qui fideles ad caritatem provocans, eos exhortatur, ut hoc in se sentiant quod et in Christo Iesu, qui semetipsum exinanivit formam servi accipiens, ... factus oboediens usque ad mortem (Ph 2,7-8) et propter nos egenus factus est, cum esset dives (2Co 8,9). Huius caritatis et humilitatis Christi imitationem et testimonium cum a discipulis semper praeberi necesse sit, gaudet Mater Ecclesia plures in sinu suo inveniri viros ac mulieres, qui exinanitionem Salvatoris pressius sequuntur et clarius demonstrant, paupertatem in filiorum Dei libertate suscipientes et propriis voluntatibus abrenuntiantes: illi scilicet sese homini propter Deum in re perfectionis ultra mensuram praecepti subiiciunt, ut Christo oboedienti sese plenius conforment.
All Christ's faithful, therefore, are invited and bound to pursue holiness and the perfection of their own state. Let all therefore attend that they direct their affections rightly, lest by the use of worldly things and attachment to riches they be hindered from pursuing perfect charity against the spirit of evangelical poverty, the Apostle admonishing: "Those who use this world, as if they did not use it: for the figure of this world passes away" (cf. 1 Cor 7:31).
¶164
Omnes igitur christifideles ad sanctitatem et proprii status perfectionem prosequendam invitantur et tenentur. Attendant igitur omnes, ut affectus suos recte dirigant, ne usu rerum mundanarum et adhaesione ad divitias contra spiritum paupertatis evangelicae a caritate perfecta prosequenda impediantur, monente Apostolo: Qui utuntur hoc mundo, in eo ne sistant: praeterit enim figura huius mundi (cf. 1Co 7,31 gr.) .
The evangelical counsels of chastity dedicated to God, poverty, and obedience, as founded on the words and examples of the Lord and commended by the Apostles and the Fathers and doctors and pastors of the Church, are a divine gift, which the Church has received from her Lord and ever preserves by His grace. The authority of the Church, guided by the Holy Spirit, has taken care to interpret them, to moderate their practice, and also to constitute stable forms of living from them. Hence it has come about that, as if in a tree miraculously and multiply branching in the Lord's field from a divinely given germ, various forms of solitary or common life, and various families have grown, which increase resources both for the progress of their members and for the good of the whole body of Christ. For those families confer on their members aids of stability in a firmer manner of living, of approved doctrine for pursuing perfection, of fraternal communion in the militia of Christ, of liberty strengthened by obedience, that they may be able to fulfill and faithfully keep their religious profession securely, and progressing in the way of charity with rejoicing spirit.
¶165
CAPUT VI. DE RELIGIOSIS
CHAPTER VI. ON RELIGIOUS
Consilia evangelica castitatis Deo dicatae, paupertatis et oboedientiae, utpote in verbis et exemplis Domini fundata et ab Apostolis et Patribus Ecclesiaeque doctoribus et pastoribus commendata, sunt donum divinum, quod Ecclesia a Domino suo accepit et gratia Eius semper conservat. Ipsa autem auctoritas Ecclesiae, duce Spiritu Sancto, ea interpretari, eorum praxim moderari et etiam stabiles inde vivendi formas constituere curavit. Quo factum est ut, quasi in arbore ex germine divinitus dato mirabiliter et multipliciter in agro Domini ramificata, variae formae vitae solitariae vel communis, variaeque familiae creverint, quae tum ad profectum sodalium, tum ad bonum totius Corporis Christi opes augent. Illae enim familiae sodalibus suis adminicula conferunt stabilitatis in modo vivendi firmioris, doctrinae ad perfectionem prosequendam probatae, communionis in militia Christi fraternae, libertatis per oboedientiam roboratae, ita ut suam religiosam professionem secure implere et fideliter custodire valeant, atque in caritatis via spiritu gaudentes progrediantur.
This state, regard being had to the divine and hierarchical constitution of the Church, is not intermediate between the clerical and lay condition, but from both parts certain Christ's faithful are called by God to enjoy a special gift in the life of the Church and, each in his own way, to profit the same saving mission.
¶166
Status huiusmodi, ratione habita divinae et hierarchicae Ecclesiae constitutionis, non est intermedius inter clericalem et laicalem conditionem, sed ex utraque parte quidam christifideles a Deo vocantur, ut in vita Ecclesiae peculiari dono fruantur et, suo quisque modo, eiusdem missioni salvificae prosint.
Through vows or other sacred bonds, assimilated to vows by their own nature, by which the Christian faithful obliges himself to the three aforesaid evangelical counsels, he is totally handed over to God most beloved, so that he is referred to the service of God and His honor by a new and special title. For by baptism he is dead to sin and consecrated to God; but that he may be able to receive a richer fruit of baptismal grace, by the profession of the evangelical counsels in the Church he intends to be freed from impediments which could draw him back from the fervor of charity and the perfection of divine worship, and is more intimately consecrated to divine service. But his consecration will be the more perfect, by how much through firmer and more stable bonds Christ is more represented joined with the Spouse Church by an indissoluble bond.
¶167
Per vota autem alia sacra ligamina, votis propria sua ratione assimiIata, quibus christifidelis ad tria praedicta consilia evangelica se obligat, Deo summe dilecto totaliter mancipatur, ita ut ipse ad Dei servitium Eiusque honorem novo et peculiari titulo referatur. Per baptismum quidem mortuus est peccato, et Deo sacratus; ut autem gratiae baptismalis uberiorem fructum percipere queat, consiliorum evangelicorum professione in Ecclesia liberari intendit ab impedimentis, quae ipsum a caritatis fervore et divini cultus perfectione retrahere possent, et divino obsequio intimius consecratur. Tanto autem perfectior erit consecratio, quo per firmiora et stabiliora vincula magis repraesentatur Christus cum sponsa Ecclesia indissolubili vinculo coniunctus.
Since, however, the evangelical counsels join their followers to the Church and its mystery in a special way through the charity to which they lead, the spiritual life of these should be devoted also to the good of the whole Church. Hence arises the duty, according to their strength and according to the form of their own vocation, of laboring, whether by prayer or by active work, to root and strengthen the kingdom of Christ in souls and to spread it to all regions. Hence also the Church protects and fosters the proper character of the various religious institutes.
¶168
Cum vero evangelica consilia suos asseclas, per caritatem ad quam ducunt, Ecclesiae eiusque mysterio speciali modo coniungant, spiritualis horum vita bono quoque totius Ecclesiae devoveatur oportet. Inde oritur officium pro viribus et secundum formam propriae vocationis, sive oratione, sive actuosa quoque opera, laborandi ad Regnum Christi in animis radicandum et roborandum, illudque ad omnes plagas dilatandum. Unde et Ecclesia propriam indolem variorum Institutorum religiosorum tuetur et fovet.
The profession of the evangelical counsels therefore appears as a sign which can and ought effectively to attract all the members of the Church to the duties of the Christian vocation without shirking. For since the People of God here have no abiding city but seek the one to come, the religious state, which more frees its followers from earthly cares, also more manifests to all believers both heavenly goods already present in this age, and testifies to the new and eternal life acquired by the redemption of Christ, and foretells the future resurrection and the glory of the heavenly kingdom. The same state also more closely imitates and perpetually represents in the Church that form of life which the Son of God accepted, entering the world to do the will of the Father, which He proposed to disciples following Him. Finally, it in a special way lays open the elevation of the kingdom of God above all earthly things and its highest necessities; it also shows to all men the surpassing greatness of the power of Christ the King and the infinite power of the Holy Spirit wonderfully working in the Church.
¶169
Evangelicorum proinde consiliorum professio tamquam signum apparet, quod omnia Ecclesiae membra ad officia vocationis christianae impigre adimplenda efficaciter attrahere potest ac debet. Cum enim Populus Dei hic manentem civitatem non habeat, sed futuram inquirat, status religiosus, qui suos asseclas a curis terrenis magis liberat, magis etiam tum bona caelestia iam in hoc saeculo praesentia omnibus credentibus manifestat, tum vitam novam et aeternam redemptione Christi acquisitam testificat, tum resurrectionem futuram et gloriam Regni caelestis praenuntiat. Formam quoque vitae, quam Filius Dei accepit, mundum ingressus ut faceret voluntatem Patris, quamque discipulis Ipsum sequentibus proposuit, idem status pressius imitatur atque in Ecclesia perpetuo repraesentat. Regni Dei denique super omnia terrestria elevationem eiusque summas necessitudines peculiari modo patefacit; supereminentem quoque magnitudinem virtutis Christi regnantis atque infinitam Spiritus Sancti potentiam, in Ecclesia mirabiliter operantem, cunctis hominibus demonstrat.
The state, therefore, which is constituted by the profession of the evangelical counsels, although it does not pertain to the hierarchical structure of the Church, nevertheless pertains unshakenly to its life and holiness.
¶170
Status ergo, qui professione consiliorum evangelicorum constituitur, licet ad Ecclesiae structuram hierarchicam non spectet, ad eius tamen vitam et sanctitatem inconcusse pertinet.
Since the office of the ecclesiastical Hierarchy is to feed the People of God and to lead them to most abundant pastures (cf. Ez 34:14), it pertains to it to wisely moderate by its laws the practice of the evangelical counsels, by which the perfection of charity toward God and neighbor is singularly fostered. It also, docilely following the impulses of the Holy Spirit, receives and authentically approves the rules proposed by illustrious men and women, and further arranged, and also, by its watchful and protecting authority, is present to institutes everywhere erected for the building up of the body of Christ, that they may grow and flourish according to the spirit of the founders.
¶171
Cum ecclesiasticae Hierarchiae munus sit Populum Dei pascere et ad pascua uberrima ducere (cf. Ez 34,14), ad ipsam spectat evangelicorum consiliorum praxim, quibus perfectio caritatis erga Deum et proximum singulariter fovetur, legibus suis sapienter moderari. Ipsa etiam, Spiritus Sancti impulsus dociliter sequens, regulas a praeclaris viris et mulieribus propositas recipit et ulterius ordinatas authentice adprobat, necnon Institutis ad aedificationem Corporis Christi passim erectis, ut secundum spiritus fundatorum crescant atque floreant, auctoritate sua invigilante et protegente adest.
So that the needs of the whole Lord's flock may be better provided for, any institute of perfection and its individual members can be exempted from the jurisdiction of the ordinaries of the place by the Supreme Pontiff, by reason of his primacy over the universal Church, in view of the common utility, and subjected to him alone. Similarly, they can be left or committed to their own patriarchal authorities. The members themselves, in fulfilling their duty toward the Church from the special form of their life, ought to show reverence and obedience to bishops according to the canonical laws, on account of their pastoral authority in the particular churches and on account of the necessary unity and concord in apostolic work.
¶172
Quo autem melius necessitatibus totius dominici gregis provideatur, quodcumque perfectionis Institutum ac sodales singuli a Summo Pontifice, ratione ipsius in universam Ecclesiam primatus, intuitu utilitatis communis, ab Ordinariorum loci iurisdictione eximi et ei soli subiici possunt. Similiter possunt propriis auctoritatibus patriarchalibus relinqui aut committi. Ipsi sodales, in officio erga Ecclesiam ex peculiari suae vitae forma adimplendo, reverentiam et oboedientiam secundum canonicas leges praestare debent Episcopis, ob eorum in Ecclesiis particularibus auctoritatem pastoralem et ob necessariam in labore apostolico unitatem et concordiam.
The Church, moreover, not only raises religious profession to the dignity of a canonical state by its sanction, but also exhibits it by its liturgical action as a state consecrated to God. For the Church itself, by the authority committed to it by God, receives the vows of the professing, by its public prayer implores for them help and grace from God, commends them to God and imparts to them a spiritual blessing, associating their offering with the eucharistic sacrifice.
¶173
Ecclesia autem professionem religiosam non tantum sua sanctione ad status canonici dignitatem erigit, sed eam ut statum Deo consecratum etiam actione sua liturgica exhibet. Ipsa enim Ecclesia, auctoritate sibi a Deo commissa, profitentium vota suscipit, prece sua publica eis auxilia et gratiam a Deo impetrat, eos Deo commendat eisque spiritualem benedictionem impertitur, oblationem eorum sacrificio eucharistico adsocians.
Religious should carefully attend that through them the Church may truly better show Christ day by day, both to the faithful and to the infidels, whether on the mountain contemplating, or to the crowds announcing the kingdom of God, or healing the sick and wounded and converting sinners to good fruit, or blessing children, and doing good to all, always obedient to the will of the Father who sent Him.
¶174
Sollicite attendant religiosi, ut per ipsos Ecclesia revera Christum in dies, sive fidelibus sive infidelibus, melius commonstret, vel in monte contemplantem, vel turbis Regnum Dei annuntiantem, vel aegrotos et saucios sanantem ac peccatores ad bonam frugem convertentem, vel pueris benedicentem, et omnibus benefacientem, semper autem voluntati Patris qui Eum misit oboedientem.
Let all at last have clearly in view that the profession of the evangelical counsels, although it carries with it a renunciation of goods which are undoubtedly to be much esteemed, nevertheless does not stand against the true progress of the human person, but by its nature greatly profits it. For the counsels, voluntarily taken according to anyone's personal vocation, contribute not a little to the purification of the heart and spiritual liberty, constantly excite the fervor of charity, and especially are able more to conform the Christian man to that kind of virginal and poor life which Christ the Lord chose for Himself, and which His Virgin Mother embraced, as is proved by the example of so many holy founders. Nor let anyone think that religious by their consecration are made alien from men or useless in the earthly city. For although they sometimes do not directly assist their contemporaries, yet in a deeper way they have them present in the bowels of Christ and spiritually cooperate with them, that the building up of the earthly city may always be founded in the Lord and directed to Him, lest perhaps those who build it have labored in vain.
¶175
Omnes tandem perspectum habeant, consiliorum evangelicorum professionem, quamvis renuntiationem secumferat bonorum quae indubie magni aestimanda veniunt, tamen personae humanae vero profectui non obstare, sed natura sua ei summopere prodesse. Consilia enim, secundum cuiusquam personalem vocationem voluntarie suscepta, ad cordis purificationem et spiritualem libertatem non parum conferunt, fervorem caritatis iugiter excitant et praesertim ad genus vitae virginalis ac pauperis, quod sibi elegit Christus Dominus, quodque Mater Eius Virgo amplexa est, hominem christianum magis conformare valent, ut exemplo tot sanctorum fundatorum comprobatur. Nec quisquam aestimet religiosos consecratione sua aut ab hominibus alienos aut inutiles in civitate terrestri fieri. Nam etsi quandoque coaetaneis suis non directe adsistunt, profundiore tamen modo eos in visceribus Christi praesentes habent atque cum eis spiritualiter cooperantur, ut aedificatio terrenae civitatis semper in Domino fundetur ad Ipsumque dirigatur, ne forte in vanum laboraverint qui aedificant eam.
Therefore, finally, the sacred Synod confirms and praises men and women, Brothers and Sisters, who in monasteries, or in schools and hospitals, or in missions, by constant and humble fidelity in the aforesaid consecration adorn the Spouse of Christ, and render generous and most diverse services to all men.
¶176
Idcirco denique Sacra Synodus confirmat et laudat viros ac mulieres, Fratres ac Sorores, qui in monasteriis, vel in scholis et nosocomiis, vel in missionibus, constanti et humili fidelitate in praedicta consecratione Sponsam Christi condecorant, omnibusque hominibus generosa ac diversissima servitia praestant.
Each one, however, who is called to the profession of the counsels should sedulously take care that in the vocation to which he is called by God, he remain and more excel, to the richer holiness of the Church, to the greater glory of the one and undivided Trinity, which in Christ and through Christ is the source and origin of all holiness.
¶177
Unusquisque autem ad professionem consiliorum vocatus sedulo curet, ut in quam vocationem a Deo vocatus est, in ea permaneat atque magis excellat, ad uberiorem Ecclesiae sanctitatem, ad maiorem gloriam unius et indivisae Trinitatis, quae in Christo et per Christum est omnis sanctitatis fons et origo.
The Church, to which we are all called in Christ Jesus and in which through the grace of God we acquire holiness, will be consummated only in heavenly glory, when the time of the restoration of all things shall come (Acts 3:21), and with the human race the whole world also, which is intimately joined with man and attains its end through him, will be perfectly restored in Christ (cf. Eph 1:10; Col 1:20; 2 Pet 3:10-13).
¶178
CAPUT VII. DE INDOLE ESCHATOLOGICA ECCLESIAE PEREGRINANTIS EIUSQUE UNIONE CUM ECCLESIA COELESTI
CHAPTER VII. ON THE ESCHATOLOGICAL CHARACTER OF THE PILGRIM CHURCH AND ITS UNION WITH THE HEAVENLY CHURCH
Ecclesia, ad quam in Christo Iesu vocamur omnes et in qua per gratiam Dei sanctitatem acquirimus, nonnisi in gloria caelesti consummabitur, quando adveniet tempus restitutionis omnium (Ac 3,21) atque cum genere humano universus quoque mundus, qui intime cum homine coniungitur et per eum ad finem suum accedit, perfecte in Christo instaurabitur (cf. Ep 1,10 Col 1,20 2P 3,10-13).
Christ indeed, lifted up from the earth, has drawn all to Himself (cf. Jn 12:32); rising from the dead (cf. Rom 6:9) He sent His life-giving Spirit into His disciples and through Him constituted His body which is the Church as the universal sacrament of salvation; sitting at the right hand of the Father, He continuously works in the world that He may lead men to the Church and more closely join them to Himself through it, and that, nourishing them with His own body and blood, He may make them partakers of His glorious life. Therefore the restoration, which we await promised, has already begun in Christ, is advanced in the mission of the Holy Spirit, and through Him continues in the Church, in which by faith we are also taught about the sense of our temporal life, while we carry out the work entrusted to us in the world by the Father with the hope of future goods and work out our salvation (cf. Phil 2:12).
¶179
Christus quidem exaltatus a terra omnes traxit ad seipsum (cf. Jn 12,32 gr.); resurgens ex mortibus (cf. Rm 6,9) Spiritum suum vivificantem in discipulos immisit et per eum Corpus suum quod est Ecclesia ut universale salutis sacramentum constituit; sedens ad dexteram Patris continuo operatur in mundo ut homines ad Ecclesiam perducat arctiusque per eam sibi coniungat ac proprio Corpore et Sanguine illos nutriendo gloriosae vitae suae faciat esse participes. Restitutio ergo quam promissam exspectamus, iam incepit in Christo, provehitur in missione Spiritus Sancti et per Eum pergit in Ecclesia in qua per fidem de sensu quoque vitae nostrae temporalis edocemur, dum opus a Patre nobis in mundo commissum cum spe futurorum bonorum ad finem perducimus et salutem nostram operamur (cf. Ph 2,12).
Already therefore the ends of the ages have come upon us (cf. 1 Cor 10:11) and the renewal of the world is irrevocably established and is in a certain real way anticipated in this age: for the Church already on earth is marked with true though imperfect holiness. Until, however, there be new heavens and a new earth, in which justice dwells (cf. 2 Pet 3:13), the pilgrim Church, in her sacraments and institutions, which pertain to this age, bears the figure of this world which passes away, and she herself dwells among creatures which groan and are in labor until now and await the revelation of the sons of God (cf. Rom 8:19-22).
¶180
Iam ergo fines saeculorum ad nos pervenerunt (cf. 1Co 10,11) et renovatio mundi irrevocabiliter est constituta atque in hoc saeculo reali quodam modo anticipatur: etenim Ecclesia iam in terris vera sanctitate licet imperfecta insignitur. Donec tamen fuerint novi caeli et nova terra, in quibus iustitia habitat (cf. 2P 3,13), Ecclesia peregrinans, in suis sacramentis et institutionibus, quae ad hoc aevum pertinent, portat figuram huius saeculi quae praeterit et ipsa inter creaturas degit quae ingemiscunt et parturiunt usque adhuc et exspectant revelationem filiorum Dei (cf. Rm 8,19-22).
Joined therefore to Christ in the Church and sealed by the Holy Spirit who is the pledge of our inheritance (Eph 1:14), we are truly called and are sons of God (cf. 1 Jn 3:1), but not yet have appeared with Christ in glory (cf. Col 3:4), in which we shall be like to God, because we shall see Him as He is (cf. 1 Jn 3:2). Therefore while we are in the body, we are away from the Lord (2 Cor 5:6), and having the first-fruits of the Spirit, we groan within ourselves (cf. Rom 8:23) and desire to be with Christ (cf. Phil 1:23). The same charity urges us that we may live more for Him who died for us and rose again (cf. 2 Cor 5:15). We strive therefore to please the Lord in all things (cf. 2 Cor 5:9), and put on the armor of God, that we may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil and resist in the evil day (cf. Eph 6:11-13). Since, however, we do not know the day nor the hour, the Lord admonishing, we must constantly be watchful, that, having completed the one course of our earthly life (cf. Heb 9:27), we may deserve to enter with Him into the wedding and to be numbered among the blessed (cf. Mt 25:31-46), nor be commanded as evil and lazy servants (cf. Mt 25:26) to depart into eternal fire (cf. Mt 25:41), into the outer darkness where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth (Mt 22:13; cf. Mt 25:30). For before we reign with glorious Christ, we shall all be manifested before the tribunal of Christ, that each may receive according to what he has done in the body, whether good or evil (2 Cor 5:10), and at the end of the world those who have done good shall come forth to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment (Jn 5:29; cf. Mt 25:46). Thinking therefore that the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared to the future glory which shall be revealed in us (Rom 8:18; cf. 2 Tim 2:11-12), strong in faith we await the blessed hope and the advent of the glory of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ (Tit 2:13), who will reform the body of our lowliness configured to the body of His glory (Phil 3:21), and who will come to be glorified in His saints and to be admired in all who have believed (2 Th 1:10).
¶181
Coniuncti ergo Christo in Ecclesia et signati Spiritu Sancto qui est pignus hereditatis nostrae (Ep 1,14), vere filii Dei nominamur et sumus (cf. 1Jn 3,1), sed nondum apparuimus cum Christo in gloria (cf. Col 3,4), in qua similes Deo erimus, quoniam videbimus Eum sicuti est (cf. 1Jn 3,2). Itaque dum sumus in corpore, peregrinamur a Domino (2Co 5,6) et primitias Spiritus habentes intra nos gemimus (cf. Rm 8,23) et cupimus esse cum Christo (cf. Ph 1,23). Eadem autem caritate urgemur ut magis vivamus Ei, qui pro nobis mortuus est et resurrexit (cf. 2Co 5,15). Contendimus ergo in omnibus placere Domino (cf. 2Co 5,9) et induimus armaturam Dei, ut possimus stare adversus insidias diaboli et resistere in die malo (cf. Ep 6,11-13). Cum vero nesciamus diem neque horam, monente Domino, constanter vigilemus oportet ut, expleto unico terrestris nostrae vitae cursu (cf. He 9,27), cum Ipso ad nuptias intrare et cum benedictis connumerari mereamur (cf. Mt 25,31-46), neque sicut servi mali et pigri (cf. Mt 25,26) iubeamur discedere in ignem aeternum (cf. Mt 25,41), in tenebras exteriores ubi erit fletus et stridor dentium (Mt 22,13 cf. Mt 25,30). Etenim, antequam cum Christo glorioso regnemus, omnes nos manifestabimur ante tribunal Christi, ut referat unusquisque propria corporis, prout gessit sive bonum sive maIum (2Co 5,10) et in fine mundi procedent qui bona fecerunt in resurrectionem vitae, qui vero mala egerunt, in resurrectionem iudicii (Jn 5,29 cf. Mt 25,46). Existimantes proinde quod non sunt condignae passiones huius temporis ad futuram gloriam quae revelabitur in nobis (Rm 8,18 cf. 2Tm 2,11-12), fortes in fide exspectamus beatam spem et adventum gloriae magni Dei et Salvatoris nostri Iesu Christi (Tt 2,13), qui reformabit corpus humilitatis nostrae configuratum corporis claritatis suae (Ph 3,21) et qui veniet glorificari in sanctis suis, et admirabilis fieri in omnibus qui crediderunt (2Th 1,10).
Until, therefore, the Lord comes in His majesty and all the angels with Him (cf. Mt 25:31), and death being destroyed, all things are made subject to Him (cf. 1 Cor 15:26-27), some of His disciples pilgrimage on earth, others, this life having been finished, are purified, others are glorified, contemplating clearly God Himself three and one, as He is; all, however, in different degree and mode, communicate in the same charity of God and neighbor, and sing the same hymn of glory to our God. For all who are of Christ, having His Spirit, coalesce into one Church and cohere together in Him (cf. Eph 4:16). Therefore the union of the wayfarers with the brothers who have fallen asleep in the peace of Christ is by no means interrupted, but is, according to the perennial faith of the Church, strengthened by the communication of spiritual goods. For because the heavenly ones are more intimately united with Christ, they more firmly consolidate the whole Church in holiness, and ennoble the cult which she here on earth offers to God, and contribute multiply to its greater building up (cf. 1 Cor 12:12-27). For received into the heavenly homeland and present to the Lord (cf. 2 Cor 5:8), through Him, with Him, and in Him they do not cease to intercede for us before the Father, exhibiting the merits which they have acquired on earth through the one Mediator of God and men, Christ Jesus (cf. 1 Tim 2:5), serving the Lord in all things and fulfilling those things which are lacking of the passions of Christ in their flesh for His body, which is the Church (cf. Col 1:24). Hence our weakness is greatly helped by their fraternal solicitude.
¶182
Donec ergo Dominus venerit in maiestate sua et omnes Angeli cum eo (cf. Mt 25,31) et, destructa morte, Illi subiecta fuerint omnia (cf. 1Co 15,26-27), alii e discipulis Eius in terris peregrinantur, alii hac vita functi purificantur, alii vero glorificantur intuentes clare ipsum Deum trinum et unum, sicuti est; omnes tamen, gradu quidem modoque diverso, in eadem Dei et proximi caritate communicamus et eundem hymnum gloriae Deo nostro canimus. Universi enim qui Christi sunt, Spiritum Eius habentes, in unam Ecclesiam coalescunt et invicem cohaerent in Ipso (cf. Ep 4,16). Viatorum igitur unio cum fratribus qui in pace Christi dormierunt, minime intermittitur, immo secundum perennem Ecclesiae fidem, spiritualium bonorum communicatione roboratur. Ex eo enim quod caelites intimius cum Christo uniuntur, totam Ecclesiam in sanctitatem firmius consolidant, cultum, quem ipsa hic ih terris Deo exhibet, nobilitant ac multipliciter ad ampliorem eius aedificationem contribuunt (cf. 1Co 12,12-27). Nam in patriam recepti et praesentes ad Dominum (cf. 2Co 5,8), per Ipsum, cum Ipso et in Ipso non desinunt apud Patrem pro nobis intercedere, exhibentes merita quae per unum Mediatorem Dei et hominum, Christum Iesum (cf. 1Tm 2,5) in terris sunt adepti, Domino in omnibus servientes et adimplentes ea quae desunt passionum Christi in carne sua pro Corpore Eius quod est Ecclesia (cf. Col 1,24). Eorum proinde fraterna sollicitudine infirmitas nostra plurimum iuvatur.
Acknowledging this communion of the whole mystical body of Jesus Christ, the pilgrim Church from the first times of the Christian religion has cultivated the memory of the dead with great piety, and because it is a holy and wholesome thought to pray for the dead that they may be loosed from their sins (2 Macc 12:46), she has also offered suffrages for them. Moreover, the Church has always believed that the Apostles and martyrs of Christ, who had given the supreme testimony of faith and charity by the shedding of their blood, are more closely joined to us in Christ, has venerated them with the blessed Virgin Mary and the holy angels with special affection, and has piously implored the help of their intercession. To these were soon added others also who more closely imitated the virginity and poverty of Christ, and finally others whom the excellent practice of Christian virtues and divine charisms commended to the pious devotion and imitation of the faithful.
¶183
Hanc communionem totius Iesu Christi Mystici Corporis apprime agnoscens, Ecclesia viatorum inde a primaevis christianae religionis temporibus, defunctorum memoriam magna cum pietate excoluit et, quia sancta et salubris est cogitatio pro defunctis exorare ut a peccatis solvantur (Mach.Mach. 12, 46), etiam suffragia pro illis obtulit. Apostolos autem et martyres Christi, qui sui sanguinis effusione supremum fidei et caritatis testimonium dederant, in Christo arctius nobis coniunctos esse Ecclesia semper credidit, eos simul cum Beata Virgine Maria et sanctis Angelis peculiari affectu venerata est, eorumque intercessionis auxilium pie imploravit. Quibus mox adnumerati sunt alii quoque qui Christi virginitatem et paupertatem pressius erant imitati et tandem ceteri quos praeclarum virtutum christianarum exercitium ac divina charismata piae fidelium devotioni et imitationi commendabant.
For while we look upon the life of those who have faithfully followed Christ, we are incited in a new way to seek the future City (cf. Heb 13:14; cf. Heb 11:10), and at the same time we are taught the safest way by which, amid the variations of the world, according to the state and condition proper to each, we may be able to attain to perfect union with Christ, that is holiness. In the life of those who, sharing our humanity, are more perfectly transformed into the image of Christ (cf. 2 Cor 3:18), God vividly manifests His presence and face to men. In them He Himself speaks to us, and gives us a sign of His kingdom, to which we are powerfully attracted, having so great a cloud of witnesses placed around us (cf. Heb 12:1) and such a testimony of the truth of the Gospel.
¶184
Dum enim illorum conspicimus vitam qui Christum fideliter sunt secuti, nova ratione ad futuram Civitatem inquirendam (cf. He 13,14 cf. He 11,10) incitamur simulque tutissimam edocemur viam qua inter mundanas varietates, secundum statum ac condicionem unicuique propriam, ad perfectam cum Christo unionem seu sanctitatem pervenire poterimus. In vita eorum qui, humanitatis nostrae consortes, ad imaginem tamen Christi perfectius transformantur (cf. 2Co 3,18), Deus praesentiam vultumque suum hominibus vivide manifestat. In eis Ipse nos alloquitur, signumque nobis praebet Regni sui, ad quod tantam habentes impositam nubem testium (cf. He 12,1), talemque contestationem veritatis Evangelii, potenter attrahimur.
Nor, however, do we cultivate the memory of the heavenly ones for the title of example only, but rather still more that the union of the whole Church in the Spirit may be strengthened through the exercise of fraternal charity (cf. Eph 4:1-6). For just as Christian communion among the wayfarers brings us nearer to Christ, so the fellowship with the saints joins us to Christ, from whom as from the Fountain and Head all grace and the life of the People of God itself proceeds. Therefore it is eminently fitting that we love these friends of Jesus Christ and co-heirs, also our brothers and excellent benefactors, give due thanks to God for them, suppliantly invoke them, and flee to their prayers, help, and assistance for obtaining benefits from God through His Son Jesus Christ our Lord, who alone is our Redeemer and Savior. For every genuine testimony of love shown by us to the heavenly ones, by its very nature tends and terminates to Christ, who is the crown of all saints, and through Him to God, who is wonderful in His saints and is magnified in them.
¶185
Nec tamen solius exempli titulo caelitum memoriam colimus, sed magis adhuc ut totius Ecclesiae unio in Spiritu roboretur per fraternae caritatis exercitium (cf. Ep 4,1-6). Nam sicut christiana inter viatores communio propinquius nos ad Christum adducit, ita consortium cum Sanctis nos Christo coniungit, a quo tamquam a Fonte et Capite omnis gratia et ipsius Populi Dei vita promanat. Summopere ergo decet ut hos Iesu Christi amicos et coheredes, fratres quoque nostros et benefactores eximios diligamus, debitas pro ipsis Deo rependamus gratias, suppliciter eos invocemus et ob beneficia impetranda a Deo per Filium eius Iesum Christum, Dominum nostrum, qui solus noster Redemptor et Salvator est, ad eorum orationes, opem auxiliumque confugiamus. Omne enim genuinum amoris testimonium caelitibus a nobis exhibitum, suapte natura tendit ac terrninatur ad Christum qui est corona Sanctorum omnium et per Ipsum ad Deum qui est mirabilis in Sanctis suis et in ipsis magnificatur.
Our union with the heavenly Church is in the noblest way actualized when, especially in the sacred Liturgy, in which the power of the Holy Spirit acts upon us through sacramental signs, we celebrate the praise of the divine majesty with social exultation, and when, all redeemed in the blood of Christ from every tribe and tongue and people and nation (cf. Rev 5:9) and gathered into one Church, we magnify God one and three with one song of praise. Therefore, celebrating the eucharistic sacrifice, we are most especially joined to the cult of the heavenly Church, communicating and venerating the memory first of the glorious ever-Virgin Mary, but also of blessed Joseph and the blessed Apostles and Martyrs and of all the saints.
¶186
Nobilissima vero ratione unio nostra cum Ecclesia caelesti actuatur, cum, praesertim in sacra Liturgia, in qua virtus Spiritus Sancti per signa sacramentalia super nos agit, divinae maiestatis laudem socia exsultatione concelebramus, et universi, in sanguine Christi ex omni tribu et lingua et populo et natione redempti (cf. Ap 5,9) atque in unam Ecclesiam congregati, uno cantico laudis Deum unum et trinum magnificamus. Eucharisticum ergo sacrificium celebrantes cultui Ecclesiae caelestis vel maxime iungimur communicantes et memoriam venerantes in primis gloriosae semper Virginis Mariae, sed et beati Ioseph et beatorum Apostolorum et Martyrum et omnium Sanctorum.
This venerable faith of our fathers concerning the vital fellowship with the brothers who are in heavenly glory or are still purified after death, the sacred Synod receives with great piety, and again proposes the decrees of the sacred councils of Nicaea II, Florence, and Trent. At the same time, however, for its pastoral solicitude, it exhorts all to whom it pertains, that if any abuses, excesses, or defects have crept in here or there, they strive to ward or correct them, and restore all things to the fuller praise of Christ and God. Let them therefore teach the faithful that the authentic cult of the saints does not consist so much in the multiplicity of external acts as rather in the intensity of our active love, by which, for our greater good and that of the Church, we seek the example of the saints in their conversation, and fellowship in communion, and help in intercession. On the other hand, let them instruct the faithful that our conversation with the heavenly ones, provided it be conceived in the fuller light of faith, by no means lessens the cult of latria rendered to God the Father through Christ in the Spirit, but rather enriches it more abundantly.
¶187
Quam venerabilem maiorum nostrorum fidem circa vitale consortium cum fratribus qui in gloria caelesti sunt vel adhuc post mortem purificantur, magna cum pietate haec Sacrosancta Synodus recipit et decreta Sacrorum Conciliorum Nicaeni II, Florentini et Tridentini rursus proponit. Simul autem pro pastorali sua sollicitudine omnes ad quos spectat hortatur, ut si qui abusus, excessus vel defectus hic illicve irrepserint, eos arcere aut corrigere satagant ac omnia ad pleniorem Christi et Dei laudem instaurent. Doceant ergo fideles authenticum Sanctorum cultum non tam in actuum exteriorum multiplicitate quam potius in intensitate amoris nostri actuosi consistere, quo, ad maius nostrum et Ecclesiae bonum, Sanctorum quaerimus et conversatione exemplum et communione consortium, et intercessione subsidium. Ex altera vero parte instruant fideles nostram cum caelitibus conversationem, dummodo haec in pleniore fidei luce concipiatur, nequaquam extenuare latreuticum cultum, Deo Patri per Christum in Spiritu tributum, sed illum e contra impensius ditare.
For all who are sons of God and constitute one family in Christ (cf. Heb 3:6), while we communicate with one another in mutual charity and in the one praise of the most holy Trinity, we correspond to the intimate vocation of the Church and participate by foretaste in the liturgy of consummated glory. For when Christ shall appear and the glorious resurrection of the dead shall be, the glory of God will illuminate the heavenly City and the Lamb will be its lamp (cf. Rev 21:24). Then the whole Church of the saints in the highest beatitude of charity will adore God and the Lamb who was slain (Rev 5:12), proclaiming with one voice: "To Him who sits upon the throne, and to the Lamb, benediction and honor and glory and power forever and ever" (Rev 5:13-14).
¶188
Nam omnes qui filii Dei sumus et unam familiam in Christo constituimus (cf. He 3,6), dum in mutua caritate et una sanctissimae Trinitatis laude invicem communicamus, intimae Ecclesiae vocationi correspondemus et consummatae gloriae liturgiam praegustando participamus. Quando enim Christus apparebit et gloriosa mortuorum resurrectio erit, claritas Dei illuminabit caelestem Civitatem et eius lucerna erit Agnus (cf. Ap 21,24). Tunc tota Ecclesia sanctorum in summa caritatis beatitudine adorabit Deum et Agnum qui occisus est (Ap 5,12), una voce proclamans: Sedenti in throno, et Agno: benedictio, et honor, et gloria, et potestas in saecula saeculorum (Ap 5,13-14).
The most benign and wise God, wishing to accomplish the redemption of the world, when the fullness of time came, sent His Son, made of a woman... that we might receive the adoption of sons (Gal 4:4-5). "Who for us men and for our salvation came down from heaven, and was incarnate by the Holy Spirit of the Virgin Mary." This divine mystery of salvation is revealed to us and continued in the Church, which the Lord constituted as His body, and in which the faithful adhering to Christ the Head and communicating with all His saints, ought also to venerate the memory first of the glorious ever-Virgin Mary, the Mother of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ.
¶189
CAPUT VIII. DE BEATA MARIA VIRGINE DEIPARA IN MYSTERIO CHRISTI ET ECCLESIAE
CHAPTER VIII. ON THE BLESSED VIRGIN MARY, THE MOTHER OF GOD, IN THE MYSTERY OF CHRIST AND THE CHURCH
I. PROOEMIUM
Benignissimus et sapientissimus Deus, mundi redemptionem complere volens, ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Filium suum, factum ex muliere, ... ut adoptionem filiorum reciperemus (Ga 4,4-5). Qui propter nos homines et propter nostram salutem descendit de caelis, et incarnatus est de Spiritu Sancto ex Maria Virgine. Quod salutis divinum mysterium nobis revelatur et continuatur in Ecclesia, quam Dominus ut corpus suum constituit, et in qua fideles Christo Capiti adhaerentes atque cum omnibus sanctis Eius communicantes, memoriam etiam venerentur oportet in primis gloriosae semper Virginis Mariae, Genetricis Dei et Domini nostri Iesu Christi.
For the Virgin Mary, who, the angel announcing, received the word of God in heart and body and brought forth life to the world, is acknowledged and honored as true Mother of God and of the Redeemer. Redeemed in a more sublime way on account of the merits of her Son, and united to Him by a close and indissoluble bond, she is enriched with this highest office and dignity, that she is the Mother of the Son of God, and therefore the beloved daughter of the Father and also the sanctuary of the Holy Spirit, by which gift of excellent grace she far excels all other creatures, heavenly and earthly. At the same time, however, she is found joined with all men to be saved in the stock of Adam, indeed plainly the mother of the members of Christ, because she cooperated by charity that the faithful might be born in the Church, who are members of that Head. Wherefore she is also hailed as the supereminent and altogether singular member of the Church and as its type and most excellent exemplar in faith and charity, and the Catholic Church, taught by the Holy Spirit, follows her as a most beloved mother with filial affection of piety.
¶190
Virgo enim Maria, quae Angelo nuntiante Verbum Dei corde et corpore suscepit et Vitam mundo protulit, ut vera Mater Dei ac Redemptoris agnoscitur et honoratur. Intuitu meritorum Filii sui sublimiore modo redempta Eique arcto et indissolubili vinculo unita, hoc summo munere ac dignitate ditatur ut sit Genetrix Dei Filii, ideoque praedilecta filia Patris necnon sacrarium Spiritus Sancti, quo eximiae gratiae dono omnibus aliis creaturis, caelestibus et terrestribus, longe antecellit. Simul autem cum omnibus hominibus salvandis in stirpe Adam invenitur coniuncta, immo plane mater membrorum (Christi), ... quia cooperata est caritate ut fideles in Ecclesia nascerentur, quae illius Capitis membra sunt. Quapropter etiam ut supereminens prorsusque singulare membrum Ecclesiae necnon eius in fide et caritate typus et exemplar spectatissimum salutatur eamque Catholica Ecclesia, a Spiritu Sancto edocta, filialis pietatis affectu tamquam matrem amantissimam prosequitur.
Therefore the sacred Synod, setting forth the doctrine concerning the Church, in which the Divine Redeemer works salvation, intends sedulously to illustrate both the office of the Blessed Virgin in the mystery of the Incarnate Word and the Mystical Body, and the duties of redeemed men toward the Mother of God, mother of Christ and mother of men, especially the faithful, not having in mind, however, to set forth a complete doctrine concerning Mary, nor to decide questions not yet fully brought to light by the labor of theologians. Therefore, those opinions which are freely proposed in Catholic schools concerning Her, who holds the place after Christ highest and next to us, are preserved in their own right.
¶191
Ideo Sacrosancta Synodus, doctrinam de Ecclesia, in qua divinus Redemptor salutem operatur, exponens, illustrare sedulo intendit tum munus Beatae Virginis in mysterio Incarnati Verbi et Corporis Mystici, tum hominum redemptorum officia erga Deiparam, matrem Christi et matrem hominum, maxime fidelium, quin tamen in animo habeat completam de Maria proponere doctrinam, neque quaestiones labore theologorum nondum ad plenam lucem perductas dirimere. Servantur itaque in suo iure sententiae quae in scholis catholicis libere proponuntur de Illa, quae in Sancta Ecclesia locum occupat post Christum altissimum nobisque maxime propinquum.
The sacred Scriptures of the Old and New Testament and the venerable Tradition show the office of the Mother of the Savior in the economy of salvation in a more and more clear light and set it forth as to be seen. The books of the Old Testament describe the history of salvation, by which the coming of Christ into the world is gradually prepared. These early documents, as they are read in the Church and understood under the light of further and fuller revelation, step by step bring more clearly into light the figure of the woman, the Mother of the Redeemer. Under this light, she is already prophetically adumbrated in the promise given to our first parents, fallen into sin, concerning victory over the serpent (cf. Gen 3:15). Similarly, she is the Virgin who shall conceive and bear a Son, whose name shall be called Emmanuel (cf. Is 7:14; cf. Mic 5:2-3; Mt 1:22-23). She excels among the humble and poor of the Lord, who hope for and receive salvation from Him with confidence. With her at length, the exalted Daughter of Zion, after the long expectation of the promise, the times are fulfilled and the new Economy is established, when the Son of God assumed human nature from her, that by the mysteries of His flesh He might free man from sin.
