Terra nova et caelum novum
New earth and new heaven
Terrae ac humanitatis consummandae tempus ignoramus, nec universi transformandi modum novimus. Transit quidem figura huius mundi per peccatum deformata, sed docemur Deum novam habitationem novamque terram parare in qua iustitia habitat, et cuius beatitudo omnia pacis desideria, quae in cordibus hominum ascendunt, implebit ac superabit. Tunc, morte devicta, filii Dei in Christo resuscitabuntur, et id quod seminatum fuit in infirmitate ac corruptione, incorruptionem induet; et, manente caritate eiusque opere, a servitute vanitatis liberabitur tota creatura illa, quam Deus propter hominem creavit. Monemur sane nihil prodesse homini, si universum mundum lucretur, seipsum autem perdat. Exspectatio tamen novae terrae extenuare non debet, sed potius excitare, sollicitudinem hanc terram excolendi, ubi Corpus illud novae familiae humanae crescit quod aliqualem novi saeculi adumbrationem iam praebere valet. Ideo, licet progressus terrenus a Regni Christi augmento sedulo distinguendus sit, inquantum tamen ad societatem humanam melius ordinandam conferre potest, Regni Dei magnopere interest. Bona enim humanae dignitatis, communionis fraternae et libertatis, hos omnes scilicet bonos naturae ac industriae nostrae fructus, postquam in Spiritu Domini et iuxta Eius mandatum in terris propagaverimus, postea denuo inveniemus, mundata tamen ab omni sorde, illuminata ac transfigurata, cum Christus Patri reddet regnum aeternum et universale: regnum veritatis et vitae, regnum sanctitatis et gratiae, regnum iustitiae, amoris et pacis. His in terris Regnum iam in mysterio adest; adveniente autem Domino consummabitur.
40 All that we have said concerning the dignity of the human person, concerning the community of men, concerning the deep meaning of human activity, constitutes the foundation of the relation between the Church and the world and likewise the basis of their mutual dialogue. Therefore, in this chapter, all having been presupposed that has already been set forth by this Council concerning the mystery of the Church, the same Church now comes to be considered as she exists in this world, lives and acts with it. Proceeding from the love of the eternal Father, founded in time by Christ the Redeemer, gathered together in the Holy Spirit, the Church has a saving and eschatological end, which can be fully attained only in the future age. She herself, however, is already present here on earth, composed of men, namely of the members of the earthly city who are called to form, already in the history of the human race, the family of the sons of God, always to be increased until the coming of the Lord. Indeed, united on account of heavenly goods and enriched by them, this family was constituted and ordered by Christ in this world as a society, and furnished with suitable means of visible and social union. Thus the Church, at once a visible assembly and a spiritual community, goes forward together with the whole of humanity and experiences the same earthly lot with the world, and exists as a leaven and as a kind of soul of human society to be renewed in Christ and to be transformed into the family of God. This interpenetration of the earthly and heavenly city can only be perceived by faith, indeed it remains a mystery of human history, which is disturbed by sin until the full revelation of the clarity of the sons of God. The Church, indeed, pursuing her own saving end, not only communicates divine life with man, but also in a certain way sheds the repercussed light of that life over the whole world, especially by this that she heals and elevates the dignity of the human person, strengthens the structure of human society, and imbues daily human activity with a deeper meaning and significance. Thus the Church believes that through each of her members and her whole community she can contribute many things to making the human family and its history more humane. Moreover, the Catholic Church gladly esteems that to which other Christian churches or ecclesial communities have contributed and contribute by cooperative work to the same office. At the same time, she firmly persuades herself that she can be helped much and in various ways by the world, both by individual men and by human society, by their gifts and activity, in the preparation of the Gospel. The principles of promoting this mutual commerce and help, in those things which are in a certain way common to the Church and the world, are set forth.

CAPUT IV. DE MUNERA ECCLESIAE IN MUNDO HUIUS TEMPORIS
CHAPTER IV. ON THE OFFICE OF THE CHURCH IN THE MODERN WORLD
De Ecclesiae et mundi mutua relatione
On the mutual relation of Church and world
Omnia quae a nobis dicta sunt de dignitate personae humanae, de hominum communitate, de profundo sensu navitatis humanae, fundamentum relationis Ecclesiam inter et mundum necnon basim eorum mutui dialogi constituunt. Ideo in hoc capite, omnibus praesuppositis ab hoc Concilio de mysterio Ecclesiae iam edictis, eadem Ecclesia nunc consideranda venit prout ipsa, in hoc mundo exsistit et cum eo vivit atque agit. Procedens ex amore Patris aeterni, in tempore fundata a Christo Redemptore, coadunata in Spiritu Sancto, Ecclesia finem salutarem et eschatologicum habet, qui nonnisi in futuro saeculo plene attingi potest. Ipsa autem iam hic in terris adest, ex hominibus collecta, terrestris nempe civitatis membris quae ad hoc vocantur ut iam in generis humani historia familiam filiorum Dei, usque ad adventum Domini semper augendam, efforment. Unita quidem propter bona caelestia iisque ditata, haec familia a Christo in hoc mundo ut societas constituta et ordinata est, atque aptis mediis unionis visibilis et socialis instructa. Ita Ecclesia, insimul coetus adspectabilis et communitas spiritualis, una cum tota humanitate incedit eamdemque cum mundo sortem terrenam experitur, ac tamquam fermentum et veluti anima societatis humanae in Christo renovandae et in familiam Dei transformandae exsistit. Haec quidem terrestris et caelestis civitatis compenetratio nonnisi fide percipi potest, immo mysterium manet historiae humanae, quae usque ad plenam revelationem claritatis filiorum Dei peccato perturbatur. Ecclesia quidem, proprium suum finem salutarem persequens, non solum vitam divinam cum homine communicat, sed etiam lumen eius repercussum quodammodo super universum mundum fundit, potissimum per hoc quod personae humanae dignitatem sanat et elevat, humanae societatis compaginem firmat, atque cotidianam hominum navitatem profundiori sensu et significatione imbuit. Ita Ecclesia per singula sua membra et totam suam communitatem multa se conferre posse credit ad hominum familiam eiusque historiam humaniorem reddendam. Libenter insuper Ecclesia Catholica ea magni aestimat quae ad idem munus adimplendum aliae Ecclesiae christianae vel communitates ecclesiasticae socia opera contulerunt ac conferunt. Simul sibi firmiter persuasum habet se multum varioque modo a mundo, sive a singulis hominibus sive ab humana societate, eorum dotibus ac navitate, in praeparatione Evangelii iuvari posse. Mutui huius commercii atque adiutorii, in illis quae Ecclesiae et mundo quodammodo sunt communia, rite promovendi, principia quaedam generalia exponuntur.
41 Modern man is on the way to more fully developing his personality and daily more discovering and affirming his rights. Since, however, the Church is entrusted with manifesting the mystery of God, who is the ultimate end of man, she at the same time opens to man the meaning of his own existence, namely the intimate truth concerning man. The Church truly knows that only God, whom she serves, responds to the deepest desires of the human heart, which is never fully satisfied by earthly nourishment. She knows, moreover, that man, incessantly incited by the Spirit of God, will never be wholly indifferent concerning the problem of religion, as not only the experience of past ages, but also the manifold testimony of our times proves. For man will always desire to know, at least confusedly, what is the meaning of his life, his activity, his death. The very presence of the Church recalls these problems to his mind. But only God, who created man to His own image and redeemed him from sin, gives the fullest answer to these questions, and that through revelation in His Son who was made man. Whoever follows Christ, the perfect Man, he too becomes more man. From this faith the Church can subtract the dignity of human nature from all changes of opinion, which, for example, either too much depress or immoderately extol the human body. By no human law can the personal dignity and freedom of man be so aptly placed in safety as by the Gospel entrusted to the Church. For this Gospel announces and proclaims the freedom of the sons of God, rejects all servitude ultimately flowing from sin, sacredly respects the dignity of conscience and its free decision, unceasingly admonishes to reduplicate all human talents to the service of God and the good of men, finally commends all to the charity of all. Which corresponds to the fundamental law of the Christian economy. For although the same God is the Savior who is the Creator, the same also is the Lord of human history and of the history of salvation, yet in this same divine order the just autonomy of the creature, and especially of man, far from being taken away, is rather restored to its dignity and firmly established in it.

De adiutorio quod Ecclesia singulis hominibus praestare satagit
On the help which the Church strives to give to individual men
Homo hodiernus in via est ad personalitatem suam plenius evolvendam iuraque sua in dies magis detegenda et affirmanda. Cum autem Ecclesiae concreditum sit manifestare mysterium Dei, qui est ultimus finis hominis, ipsa homini simul aperit sensum propriae eius exsistentiae, intimam scilicet de homine veritatem. Vere novit Ecclesia solum Deum, cui ipsa inservit, profundissimis respondere desideriis humani cordis, quod nutrimentis terrestribus numquam plene satiatur. Novit praeterea hominem, incessanter a Spiritu Dei incitatum, numquam circa problema religionis prorsus indifferentem fore, sicut non solum experientia saeculorum anteactorum, sed multiplici etiam testimonio nostrorum temporum comprobatur. Semper enim homo scire desiderabit, saltem confuse, quae sit significatio suae vitae, suae navitatis ac suae mortis. Ipsa praesentia Ecclesiae haec problemata in eius mentem revocat. Solus autem Deus, qui hominem ad imaginem suam creavit atque a peccato redemit, his quaestionibus plenissimum responsum praebet, idque per revelationem in Filio suo qui homo factus est. Quicumque Christum sequitur, Hominem perfectum, et ipse magis homo fit. Ex hac fide Ecclesia dignitatem naturae humanae omnibus opinionum mutationibus subtrahere potest, quae, exempli gratia, corpus humanum aut nimis deprimunt aut immoderate extollunt. Nulla lege humana personalis dignitas atque libertas hominis tam apte in tuto collocari possunt quam Evangelio Christi Ecclesiae concredito. Hoc enim Evangelium libertatem filiorum Dei annuntiat et proclamat, omnem servitutem ex peccato ultimatim fluentem respuit, dignitatem conscientiae eiusque liberam decisionem sancte veretur, omnia talenta humana in Dei servitium hominumque bonum reduplicare indesinenter monet, omnes denique omnium commendans caritati. Quod legi fundamentali oeconomiae christianae correspondet. Etsi enim idem Deus sit Salvator qui et Creator, idem quoque Dominus et historiae humanae et historiae salutis, tamen in hoc ipso ordine divino iusta creaturae autonomia et praesertim hominis nedum auferatur, potius in suam dignitatem restituitur atque in ipsa firmatur.
The Church, therefore, by virtue of the Gospel entrusted to her, proclaims the rights of man and acknowledges and greatly esteems the dynamism of our time, by which these rights are promoted everywhere. This movement, however, must be imbued with the spirit of the Gospel and guarded against every form of false autonomy. For we are subject to the temptation to judge that our personal rights are then only fully preserved when we are freed from every norm of the divine law. By this way, however, the dignity of the human person, far from being saved, rather perishes.
¶433
Ecclesia ergo, vi Evangelii sibi concrediti, iura hominum proclamat et hodierni temporis dynamismum, quo haec iura undique promoventur, agnoscit et magni aestimat. Qui motus tamen spiritu Evangelii imbuendus et adversus omnem speciem falsae autonomiae tutandus est. Tentationi enim subiicimur, iudicandi nostra iura personalia tunc tantum plene servari, cum ab omni norma Legis divinae solvimur. Hac autem via, personae humanae dignitas, nedum salvetur, potius perit.
42 The union of the human family is greatly strengthened and completed by the unity of the family of the sons of God founded in Christ. The proper mission, indeed, which Christ entrusted to His Church, is not of political, economic, or social order: for the end which He set for it is of religious order. Yet from this very religious mission, light and forces flow which can serve to constitute and strengthen the community of men according to the divine law. Likewise, where it is necessary, according to circumstances of times and places, she can arouse, indeed ought, works destined for the service of all, especially the needy, such as works of mercy or others of this kind. The Church moreover acknowledges whatever good is found in the current social dynamism: especially the evolution toward unity, the process of sound socialization and civil and economic association. For the promotion of unity is consistent with the intimate mission of the Church, since she is in Christ as a sacrament, that is, a sign and instrument of intimate union with God and of the unity of the whole human race. Thus she shows to the world that true external social union flows from the union of minds and hearts, namely from that faith and charity by which her unity is indissolubly founded in the Holy Spirit. For the power which the Church can inject into modern human society consists in that faith and charity, brought into effect in life, not however in any external dominion exercised by mere human means. Since, moreover, by virtue of her mission and nature, she is bound to no particular form of human culture or political, economic, or social system, the Church can from her universality exist as a most close bond between the various human communities and nations, provided only that they trust her and truly acknowledge her true freedom to fulfill this mission. Wherefore the Church admonishes her children, but also all men, that in this family spirit of the sons of God, they overcome all dissensions between nations and races and give internal firmness to just human associations. Whatever, therefore, true, good, and just is found in the most diverse institutions which the human race has made for itself and unceasingly makes, the Council considers with great reverence. It declares, moreover, that the Church wishes to help and promote all such institutions, insofar as this depends on her and can be connected with her mission. She desires nothing more ardently than that, serving the good of all, she be able to develop freely under any regime which acknowledges the fundamental rights of the person and the family and the necessities of the common good.

De adiutorio quod Ecclesia societati humanae afferre satagit
On the help which the Church strives to give to human society
Unio familiae humanae unitate familiae filiorum Dei in Christo fundata multum roboratur et completur. Missio quidem propria, quam Christus Ecclesiae suae concredidit, non est ordinis politici, oeconomici vel socialis: finis enim quem ei praefixit ordinis religiosi est. At sane ex hac ipsa missione religiosa munus, lux et vires fluunt quae communitati hominum secundum Legem divinam constituendae et firmandae inservire possunt. Item, ubi opus fuerit, secundum temporum et locorum circumstantias, et ipsa suscitare potest, immo et debet, opera in servitium omnium, praesertim vero egentium destinata, uti opera misericordiae vel alia huiusmodi. Ecclesia insuper agnoscit quidquid boni in dynamismo sociali hodierno invenitur: praesertim evolutionem versus unitatem, processum sanae socializationis et consociationis civilis et oeconomicae. Promotio enim unitatis cum intima Ecclesiae missione cohaeret, cum ipsa sit in Christo veluti sacramentum seu signum et instrumentum intimae cum Deo unionis totiusque generis humani unitatis. Ita ipsa mundo ostendit veram unionem socialem externam ex unione mentium et cordium fluere, ex illa scilicet fide et caritate, quibus in Spiritu Sancto eius unitas indissolubiliter condita est. Vis enim, quam Ecclesia hodiernae hominum societati iniicere valet, in illa fide et caritate, ad effectum vitae adductis, consistit, non autem in dominio aliquo externo mediis mere humanis exercendo. Cum insuper vi suae missionis et naturae ad nullam alligetur particularem culturae humanae formam aut systema politicum, oeconomicum vel sociale, Ecclesia ex hac sua universalitate ligamen arctissimum inter diversas hominum communitates et nationes exsistere potest, dummodo ipsae ei fidant eiusque veram libertatem ad hanc suam missionem adimplendam reapse agnoscant. Qua de causa Ecclesia filios suos, sed etiam omnes homines monet, ut in hoc familiali spiritu filiorum Dei, omnes dissensiones inter nationes et stirpes superent et iustis associationibus humanis internam firmitatem praebeant. Quaecumque igitur vera, bona, iustaque inveniuntur in diversissimis institutionibus, quae genus humanum sibi condidit incessanterque condit, eadem Concilium magna cum reverentia considerat. Declarat insuper Ecclesiam omnes tales institutiones adiuvare et promovere velle, quatenus hoc ab ea dependet et cum eius missione coniungi potest. Ipsa nihil ardentius desiderat quam ut omnium bono inserviens, se libere sub quovis regimine evolvere possit, quod iura fundamentalia personae ac familiae et boni communis necessitates agnoscat.
43 The Council exhorts Christians, citizens of both cities, that they strive faithfully to fulfill their earthly duties, led by the spirit of the Gospel. They depart from truth who, knowing that we have here no abiding city but seek the one to come, think that they can therefore neglect their earthly duties, not attending that by faith itself they are more bound to fulfill them, according to the vocation by which each is called. But they err no less who, on the contrary, think that they can so immerse themselves in earthly affairs as if these are altogether alien from religious life, which they suppose to consist only in the acts of worship and certain moral duties. That discord between the faith which they profess and their daily life of many is to be counted among the more grave errors of our time. This scandal the Prophets already vehemently rebuked in the Old Testament, and much more in the New Testament Jesus Christ Himself threatened with grave penalties. Lest, therefore, professional and social activities be wrongly opposed to religious life on the one hand, let the Christian, neglecting his temporal duties, neglect his duties toward his neighbor, indeed toward God himself, and bring his eternal salvation into peril. Let Christians rather rejoice, following the example of Christ, who exercised a craft, to be able to exercise all their earthly activities, to gather human, domestic, professional, scientific, or technical efforts into one vital synthesis with religious goods, under the highest ordering of which all things are coordinated to the glory of God. Secular offices and activities properly, although not exclusively, pertain to laity. Therefore, when they act as citizens of the world, whether individually or associated, they will not only keep the laws proper to each discipline, but also strive to acquire true skill in those fields. They will willingly cooperate with men pursuing the same ends. Recognizing the exigencies of faith and endowed with its virtue, they will unhesitatingly, where necessary, devise and bring to effect new undertakings. It pertains to their conscience, already aptly formed, that the divine law be inscribed in the life of the earthly city. From priests, however, laity expect light and spiritual strength. Yet they should not think that their pastors are always so skilled that they can have a concrete solution ready for every emerging question, even a grave one, or that they are sent for this: they themselves rather, illuminated by Christian wisdom and observantly attending to the doctrine of the Magisterium, should assume their own parts. Often the Christian vision of things will itself incline them to a certain determinate solution in certain circumstances. Others, however, not less sincere, as often and indeed legitimately happens, will judge differently concerning the same matter. But if the solutions proposed here and there, even beyond the intention of the parties, are easily connected by many with the Gospel message, they must be mindful that it is lawful for no one in the aforesaid cases to claim the authority of the Church exclusively for his own opinion. But always in sincere dialogue they should strive to enlighten one another, keeping mutual charity and especially solicitous for the common good. Laity, however, who have to play active parts in the whole life of the Church, are held not only to imbue the world with the Christian spirit, but also are called to be witnesses of Christ in all things, in the midst of human society. Bishops, to whom the office of governing the Church of God is committed, with their priests should so preach the message of Christ that all the earthly activities of the faithful may be imbued with the light of the Gospel. Moreover, all pastors should be mindful that by their daily conversation and solicitude they show the face of the Church to the world, from which men judge the power and truth of the Christian message. By life and word, together with religious and their faithful, let them show that the Church by her very presence, with all the gifts she contains, is an inexhaustible source of those virtues which the modern world most needs. By assiduous studies let them so render themselves apt that they may be able to play their part in instituting dialogue with the world and with men of any opinion. Above all, let them have in their heart the words of this Council: "Because the human race today is more and more coalescing into civil, economic, and social unity, the more it is necessary that priests, with united care and help under the guidance of the bishops and the Supreme Pontiff, remove every form of dispersion, that the whole human race may be led into the unity of the family of God." Although the Church, by the power of the Holy Spirit, has remained the faithful spouse of her Lord and never ceases to be the sign of salvation in the world, she yet is not unaware that among her members, both clergy and laity, through the course of many centuries, there have been those who have been unfaithful to the Spirit of God. Even in our age, the Church is not unaware how much the message proclaimed by her and the human weakness of those to whom the Gospel is entrusted differ from each other. Whatever history may judge of these defects, we ought to be conscious of them and strenuously fight against them, lest they bring detriment to the diffusion of the Gospel. Likewise the Church knows how much she must continually mature from the experience of ages in developing her relation with the world. Led by the Holy Spirit, Mother Church unceasingly exhorts her sons to purification and renewal, that the sign of Christ may shine more clearly upon the face of the Church.

De adiutorio quod Ecclesia per christianos navitati humanae conferre satagit
On the help which the Church strives to give to human activity through Christians
Concilium christianos, cives utriusque civitatis, adhortatur ut sua terrestria officia fideliter implere studeant, idque spiritu Evangelii ducti. A veritate discedunt qui, scientes nos non habere hic manentem civitatem sed futuram inquirere, putent se proinde officia sua terrestria negligere posse, non attendentes se per ipsam fidem ad eadem implenda magis teneri, secundum vocationem qua quisque vocatus est. At non minus errant qui, e contrario, opinentur se ita seipsos negotiis terrestribus immergere posse, quasi ista omnino aliena sint a vita religiosa, quippe quia ipsam in solius cultus actibus et officiis quibusdam moralibus implendis consistere arbitrentur. Discidium illud inter fidem quam profitentur et vitam quotidianam multorum, inter graviores nostri temporis errores recensendum est. Scandalum hoc iam in Vetere Testamento Prophetae vehementer redarguebant et multo magis in Novo Testamento ipse Iesus Christus gravibus poenis minabatur. Ne igitur perperam inter se opponantur activitates professionales et sociales ex una parte, vita religiosa ex altera. Christianus, officia sua temporalia negligens, officia sua erga proximum, immo et ipsum Deum negligit suamque aeternam salutem in discrimen adducit. Gaudeant potius christiani, exemplum Christi secuti, qui fabrilem artem exercuit, se omnes suas navitates terrestres exercere posse, conatus humanos, domesticos, professionales, scientificos vel technicos in unam synthesin vitalem cum bonis religiosis colligendo, sub quorum altissima ordinatione omnia in Dei gloriam coordinantur. Laicis proprie, etsi non exclusive, saecularia officia et navitates competunt. Cum igitur, sive singuli sive consociati, ut cives mundi agunt, non solum leges proprias uniuscuiusque disciplinae servabunt, sed veram peritiam in illis campis sibi comparare studebunt. Libenter cum hominibus eosdem fines prosequentibus cooperabuntur. Agnoscentes exigentias fidei eiusque virtute praediti, incunctanter, ubi oportet, nova incepta excogitent atque ad effectum deducant. Ad ipsorum conscientiam iam apte formatam spectat, ut lex divina in civitatis terrenae vita inscribatur. A sacerdotibus vero laici lucem ac vim spiritualem exspectent. Neque tamen ipsi censeant pastores suos semper adeo peritos esse ut, in omni quaestione exsurgente, etiam gravi, solutionem concretam in promptu habere queant, aut illos ad hoc missos esse: ipsi potius, sapientia christiana illustrati et ad doctrinam Magisterii observanter attendentes, partes suas proprias assumant. Pluries ipsa visio christiana rerum eos ad aliquam determinatam solutionem in quibusdam rerum adiunctis inclinabit. Alii tamen fideles, non minore sinceritate ducti, ut saepius et quidem legitime accidit, aliter de eadem re iudicabunt. Quodsi solutiones hinc inde propositae, etiam praeter partium intentionem, a multis facile connectantur cum nuntio evangelico, meminerint oportet nemini licere in praefatis casibus pro sua sententia auctoritatem Ecclesiae sibi exclusive vindicare. Semper autem colloquio sincero se invicem illuminare satagant, mutuam caritatem servantes et boni communis imprimis solliciti. Laici vero, qui in tota vita Ecclesiae actuosas partes gerendas habent, non solum mundum spiritu christiano imbuere tenentur, sed etiam ad hoc vocantur ut in omnibus, in media quidem humana consortione, Christi sint testes. Episcopi vero, quibus munus moderandi Ecclesiam Dei commissum est, cum presbyteris suis nuntium Christi ita praedicent, ut omnes fidelium terrestres activitates Evangelii luce perfundantur. Insuper pastores omnes memores sint se sua cotidiana conversatione et sollicitudine mundo faciem Ecclesiae exhibere, ex qua homines vim et veritatem nuntii christiani iudicant. Vita et verbo, una cum religiosis atque suis fidelibus, demonstrent Ecclesiam sola sua praesentia, cum omnibus quae continet donis, inexhaustum fontem esse illarum virtutum, quibus mundus hodiernus maxime indiget. Studiis assiduis se ita aptos reddant, ut in dialogo cum mundo et hominibus cuiuscumque opinionis instituendo partes suas agere possint. Imprimis vero in corde verba huius Concilii habeant: "Quia genus humanum hodie magis magisque in unitatem civilem, oeconomicam et socialem coalescit, eo magis oportet ut Sacerdotes, coniuncta cura et ope sub ductu Episcoporum et Summi Pontificis, omnem rationem dispersionis elidant, ut in unitatem familiae Dei totum genus humanum adducatur." Quamvis Ecclesia ex virtute Spiritus Sancti fidelis sponsa Domini sui manserit et numquam cessaverit esse signum salutis in mundo, ipsa tamen minime ignorat inter membra sua, sive clericos sive laicos, decurrente multorum saeculorum serie, non defuisse qui Spiritui Dei infideles exstiterint. Etiam hac nostra aetate Ecclesiam non fugit, quantum inter se distent nuntius a se prolatus et humana debilitas eorum quibus Evangelium concreditur. Quidquid de istis defectibus historia iudicet, eorum conscii esse debemus eosdemque strenue impugnare, ne Evangelio diffundendo detrimentum afferant. Pariter novit Ecclesia quantopere ipsa, in sua cum mundo relatione excolenda, ex saeculorum experientia iugiter maturescere debeat. A Spiritu Sancto ducta, Ecclesia Mater indesinenter filios suos ad purificationem et renovationem exhortatur, ut signum Christi super faciem Ecclesiae clarius effulgeat.
44 Just as it is in the interest of the world to acknowledge the Church as a social reality of history and its leaven, so the Church herself does not ignore how much she has received from the history and development of the human race. The experience of past ages, the progress of the sciences, the treasures hidden in the various forms of human culture, by which the very nature of man is more fully manifested and new ways to truth are opened, also profit the Church. For she, from the beginning of her history, has learned to express the message of Christ with the concepts and language of various peoples, and has striven to illustrate it with the wisdom of philosophers as well: to this end namely, that the Gospel might be accommodated to the capacity of all and the exigencies of the wise, as far as was fitting. This accommodated preaching of the revealed word ought to remain the law of all evangelization. For thus the faculty of expressing the message of Christ in its own way is aroused in every nation, and at the same time a living commerce between the Church and the various cultures of peoples is promoted. To increase such commerce, the Church especially in our times, in which things change very quickly and ways of thinking vary greatly, especially needs the help of those who, living in the world, know various institutions and disciplines and understand their intimate mind, whether of believers or of non-believers. It is the part of the whole People of God, especially of pastors and theologians, with the help of the Holy Spirit, to listen to, discern, and interpret the various languages of our time, and to judge them under the light of the divine word, that revealed Truth may always be more deeply perceived, better understood, and more aptly set forth. The Church, since she has a visible social structure, indeed a sign of her unity in Christ, can also be enriched and is enriched by the development of human social life, not as if something were lacking in the constitution given to her by Christ, but to understand it more deeply, to express it better, and to accommodate it more happily to our times. She gratefully perceives that she, in her community no less than in her individual sons, receives varied help from men of any grade or condition. For whoever promote the human community in the order of family, culture, economic and social life, and also national and international politics, according to the purpose of God bring no small help to the ecclesial community as well, insofar as it depends on external things. Indeed, the Church confesses that she has profited much and can profit from the very opposition of those who oppose or persecute her.
¶436
De adiutorio quod Ecclesia a mundo hodierno accipit
On the help which the Church receives from the modern world
Sicut autem mundi interest Ecclesiam ut socialem realitatem historiae eiusque fermentum agnoscere, ita ipsa Ecclesia non ignorat, quantum ex humani generis historia et evolutione acceperit. Praeteritorum saeculorum experientia, scientiarum profectus, thesauri in variis culturae humanae formis absconditi, quibus ipsius hominis natura plenius manifestatur novaeque viae ad veritatem aperiuntur, Ecclesiae quoque prosunt. Ipsa enim, inde ab initio suae historiae, nuntium Christi, ope conceptuum et linguarum diversorum populorum exprimere didicit, eumdemque sapientia insuper philosophorum illustrare conata est: in hunc finem nempe ut Evangelium tum omnium captui tum sapientium exigentiis, in quantum par erat, aptaret. Quae quidem verbi revelati accommodata praedicatio lex omnis evangelizationis permanere debet. Ita enim in omni natione facultas nuntium Christi suo modo exprimendi excitatur simulque vivum commercium inter Ecclesiam et diversas populorum culturas promovetur. Ad tale commercium augendum Ecclesia, imprimis nostris temporibus, in quibus res celerrime mutantur et cogitandi modi valde variantur, peculiariter eorum auxilio indiget qui, viventes in mundo, varias institutiones et disciplinas callent earumque intimam mentem intelligunt, sive de credentibus sive de non credentibus agitur. Totius Populi Dei est, praesertim pastorum et theologorum, adiuvante Spiritu Sancto, varias loquelas nostri temporis auscultare, discernere et interpretari easque sub lumine verbi divini diiudicare, ut revelata Veritas semper penitius percipi, melius intelligi aptiusque proponi possit. Ecclesia, cum visibilem structuram socialem habeat, signum quidem suae unitatis in Christo, etiam evolutione vitae socialis humanae ditari potest et ditatur, non quasi aliquid in constitutione a Christo sibi data deesset, sed ad eamdem profundius cognoscendam, melius exprimendam atque temporibus nostris felicius accommodandam. Ipsa grato animo percipit se, in sua communitate non minus quam in singulis suis filiis, varium adiutorium ab hominibus cuiusvis gradus vel condicionis accipere. Quicumque enim communitatem humanam in ordine familiae, culturae, vitae oeconomicae et socialis, necnon politicae tam nationalis quam internationalis, promovent, secundum consilium Dei communitati quoque ecclesiali, in quantum haec ab externis dependet, adiutorium non parvum afferunt. Immo Ecclesia, ex ipsa oppositione eorum qui ei adversantur vel eam persequuntur, se multum profecisse et proficere posse fatetur.
45 While the Church herself helps the world and receives many things from it, she tends to this one end, that the kingdom of God may come and the salvation of the whole human race be restored. For every good which the people of God can provide to the human family during the time of its earthly pilgrimage flows from this, that the Church is the universal sacrament of salvation, manifesting and at the same time operating the mystery of God's love for man. For the word of God, through whom all things were made, Himself made flesh, so that, perfect Man, He might save all and recapitulate all things. The Lord is the end of human history, the point upon which the desires of history and civilization turn, the center of the human race, the joy of all hearts and the fullness of their desires. He is whom the Father raised from the dead, exalted and placed at His right hand, constituting Him the judge of the living and the dead. In His Spirit, being vivified and gathered together, we pilgrimage toward the consummation of human history, which fully corresponds to the purpose of His love: "To restore all things in Christ, which are in heaven and which are on earth" (Eph 1:10). The Lord Himself says: "Behold, I come quickly, and my reward is with me, to render to each according to his works. I am Alpha and Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End" (Rev 22:12-13).
¶437
De Christo, alpha et omega
On Christ, Alpha and Omega
Ecclesia, dum ipsa mundum adiuvat et ab eo multa accipit, ad hoc unum tendit ut Regnum Dei adveniat et totius humani generis salus instauretur. Omne vero bonum, quod Populus Dei in suae peregrinationis terrestris tempore hominum familiae praebere potest, ex hoc profluit quod Ecclesia est universale salutis sacramentum, mysterium amoris Dei erga hominem manifestans simul et operans. Verbum enim Dei, per quod omnia facta sunt, Ipsum caro factum est, ita ut, perfectus Homo, omnes salvaret et universa recapitularet. Dominus finis est humanae historiae, punctum in quod historiae et civilizationis desideria vergunt, humani generis centrum, omnium cordium gaudium eorumque appetitionum plenitudo. Ille est quem Pater a mortuis suscitavit, exaltavit et a dextris suis collocavit, Eum vivorum atque mortuorum iudicem constituens. In Eius Spiritu vivificati et coadunati, versus historiae humanae peregrinamur consummationem, quae cum consilio Eius dilectionis plene congruit: Instaurare omnia in Christo, quae in caelis et quae in terra sunt (Ep 1,10). Dicit Ipse Dominus: Ecce venio cito, et merces mea mecum est, reddere unicuique secundum opera sua. Ego sum alpha et omega, primus et novissimus, principium et finis (Ap 22,12-13).
46 The Council, after having set forth of what dignity the human person is and to what office, both individual and social, in the whole world it is called to be fulfilled, under the light of the Gospel and human experience, now turns the minds of all to certain more urgent necessities of this time, which most affect the human race. Among many things which today excite the solicitude of all, it is especially pleasant to recall: matrimony and the family, human culture, economic-social life, political life, the bond of the family of peoples and peace. Concerning each of these, let the principles and lights flowing from Christ shine forth, by which Christ's faithful may be led and all men illuminated in seeking the solution of so many complex problems.

Pars secunda
DE QUIBUSDAM PROBLEMATIBUS URGENTIORIBUS
Part Two
ON SOME MORE URGENT PROBLEMS
Prooemium
Concilium, postquam exposuit cuiusnam dignitatis sit persona hominis necnon ad quodnam munus, sive individuale sive sociale, in universo mundo adimplendum sit vocata, sub luce Evangelii et humanae experientiae omnium nunc animos ad quasdam urgentiores huius temporis necessitates convertit, quae maxime genus humanum afficiunt. Inter multa quae hodie sollicitudinem omnium excitant haec praesertim recolere iuvat: matrimonium et familiam, culturam humanam, vitam oeconomicam-socialem ac politicam, coniunctionem familiae populorum et pacem. Circa haec singula clarescant principia et lumina a Christo manantia, quibus christifideles ducantur omnesque homines illuminentur in tot implicatorum problematum solutione quaerenda.
47 The salvation of the person and of human and Christian society is closely connected with the favorable condition of the conjugal and family community. Therefore Christians, together with all who hold the same community in high esteem, sincerely rejoice in the various aids by which men today progress in fostering this community of love and in cultivating life, and spouses and parents are helped in their excellent office; they also expect better benefits from it and strive to promote them.
¶439
CAPUT I. DE DIGNITATE MATRIMONII ET FAMILIAE FOVENDA
CHAPTER I. ON FOSTERING THE DIGNITY OF MATRIMONY AND THE FAMILY
De matrimonio et familia in mundo hodierno
On matrimony and the family in the modern world
Salus personae et societatis humanae ac christianae arcte cum fausta condicione communitatis coniugalis et familiaris connectitur. Ideo christiani, una cum omnibus qui eandem communitatem magni aestimant, sincere gaudent de variis subsidiis quibus homines, in hac communitate amoris fovenda et in vita colenda, hodie progrediuntur, et coniuges atque parentes in praecellenti suo munere adiuvantur; meliora insuper exinde beneficia exspectant atque promovere student.
However, not everywhere does the dignity of this institution shine with the same clarity, since it is obscured by polygamy, the plague of divorce, so-called free love, and other deformations; moreover, conjugal love is often profaned by selfishness, hedonism, and illicit practices against generation. Moreover, modern economic, socio-psychological, and civil conditions bring not light disturbances into the family. Finally, in certain parts of the world, problems arising from demographic increase are observed not without solicitude. By all these, consciences are anguished. Yet the force and strength of the marital and family institution also appear from the fact that the profound changes of modern society, notwithstanding the difficulties arising therefrom, often in various ways manifest the true nature of the same institution. Wherefore the Council, by putting certain heads of the Church's doctrine into clearer light, intends to illuminate and strengthen Christians and all men, who strive to protect and promote the native dignity of the marital state and its excellent sacred value.
¶440
Non ubique vero huius institutionis dignitas eadem claritate illucescit, siquidem polygamia, divortii lue, amore sic dicto libero, aliisve deformationibus obscuratur; insuper amor nuptialis saepius egoismo, hedonismo et illicitis usibus contra generationem profanatur. Praeterea hodiernae condiciones oeconomicae, socio-psychologicae et civiles non leves in familiam perturbationes inducunt. In certis denique orbis partibus non absque sollicitudine problemata ex incremento demographico exorta observantur. Quibus omnibus conscientiae anguntur. Verumtamen matrimonialis familiarisque instituti vis et robur ex eo quoque apparent, quod profundae immutationes societatis hodiernae, non obstantibus difficultatibus inde prorumpentibus, saepe saepius veram eiusdem instituti indolem vario modo manifestant. Quapropter Concilium, quaedam doctrinae Ecclesiae capita in clariorem lucem ponendo, christianos hominesque universos illuminare et confortare intendit, qui nativam status matrimonialis dignitatem eiusque eximium valorem sacrum tueri et promovere conantur.
48 The intimate community of life and conjugal love, established by the Creator and furnished with its own laws, is established by the marriage covenant, that is, by the irrevocable personal consent. Thus, by the human act by which spouses mutually give and receive each other, an institution firm by divine order arises, also before society; this sacred bond does not depend on human arbitrariness for the good of spouses, offspring, and society. God himself is the author of matrimony, endowed with various goods and ends; all of which are of the greatest importance for the continuation of the human race, for the personal progress and eternal lot of each member of the family, for the dignity, stability, peace, and prosperity of the family itself and of the whole human society.
¶441
De sanctitate matrimonii et familiae
On the holiness of matrimony and the family
Intima communitas vitae et amoris coniugalis, a Creatore condita suisque legibus instructa, foedere coniugii seu irrevocabili consensu personali instauratur. Ita actu humano, quo coniuges sese mutuo tradunt atque accipiunt, institutum ordinatione divina firmum oritur, etiam coram societate; hoc vinculum sacrum intuitu boni, tum coniugum et prolis tum societatis, non ex humano arbitrio pendet. Ipse vero Deus est auctor matrimonii, variis bonis ac finibus praediti; quae omnia pro generis humani continuatione, pro singulorum familiae membrorum profectu personali ac sorte aeterna, pro dignitate, stabilitate, pace et prosperitate ipsius familiae totiusque humanae societatis maximi sunt momenti.
By its own natural nature, the very institution of matrimony and conjugal love are ordered to the procreation and education of offspring and are crowned by them as by their summit. Thus man and woman, who by the marriage covenant are no longer two but one flesh (Mt 19:6), by the intimate conjunction of persons and works render mutual help and service to each other, and experience and more and more fully attain the sense of their unity. This intimate union, as the mutual gift of two persons, like the good of children, demands full fidelity of spouses and urges their indissoluble unity.
¶442
Indole autem sua naturali, ipsum institutum matrimonii amorque coniugalis ad procreationem et educationem prolis ordinantur iisque veluti suo fastigio coronantur. Vir itaque et mulier, qui foedere coniugali iam non sunt duo, sed una caro (Mt 19,6), intima personarum atque operum coniunctione mutuum sibi adiutorium et servitium praestant, sensumque suae unitatis experiuntur et plenius in dies adipiscuntur. Quae intima unio, utpote mutua duarum personarum donatio, sicut et bonum liberorum, plenam coniugum fidem exigunt atque indissolubilem eorum unitatem urgent.
Christ the Lord abundantly blessed this manifold love, arising from the divine source of charity and established to the pattern of His union with the Church. For just as God once met His people in a covenant of love and fidelity, so now the Savior of men and Spouse of the Church, through the sacrament of matrimony, comes to meet Christian spouses. He remains with them, that, just as He loved the Church and delivered Himself up for her, so spouses, by mutual surrender, may love one another with perpetual fidelity. Genuine conjugal love is assumed into divine love and is ruled and enriched by the redemptive power of Christ and the saving action of the Church, that spouses may be effectively led to God and be helped and strengthened in the sublime office of father and mother. Wherefore Christian spouses, strengthened and as it were consecrated by a special sacrament for the duties and dignity of their state, fulfilling their conjugal and family office imbued with the spirit of Christ, by which their whole life is pervaded with faith, hope, and charity, more and more approach to their own perfection and mutual sanctification, and thus together to the glorification of God.
¶443
Christus Dominus huic multiformi dilectioni, e divino caritatis fonte exortae et ad exemplar suae cum Ecclesia unionis constitutae, abundanter benedixit. Sicut enim Deus olim foedere dilectionis et fidelitatis populo suo occurrit, ita nunc hominum Salvator Ecclesiaeque Sponsus, per sacramentum matrimonii christifidelibus coniugibus obviam venit. Manet porro cum eis, ut quemadmodum Ipse dilexit Ecclesiam et Semetipsum pro ea tradidit, ita et coniuges, mutua deditione, se invicem perpetua fidelitate diligant. Germanus amor coniugalis in divinum amorem assumitur atque virtute redemptiva Christi et salvifica actione Ecclesiae regitur ac ditatur, ut coniuges efficaciter ad Deum ducantur atque in sublimi munere patris et matris adiuventur et confortentur. Quapropter coniuges christiani ad sui status officia et dignitatem peculiari sacramento roborantur et veluti consecrantur; cuius virtute munus suum coniugale et familiare explentes, spiritu Christi imbuti, quo tota eorum vita, fide, spe et caritate pervaditur, magis ac magis ad propriam suam perfectionem mutuamque sanctificationem, ideoque communiter ad Dei glorificationem accedunt.
Hence, the parents themselves leading the way by example and family prayer, children, indeed all dwelling in the family circle, will more easily find the way of humanity, salvation, and holiness. Spouses, however, adorned with the dignity and office of fatherhood and motherhood, will diligently fulfill the duty of education, especially religious, which pertains especially to them. Children, as living members of the family, contribute to the sanctification of parents in their own way. For they will respond to the benefits of parents with grateful affection, piety, and trust, and assist them in adversities and in the solitude of old age in the manner of children. Widowhood, strongly assumed in the continuity of the conjugal vocation, will be honored by all. The family will generously communicate its spiritual riches also with other families. Hence the Christian family, since it arises from matrimony, which is the image and participation of the covenant of love of Christ and the Church, will lay open to all the living presence of the Savior in the world and the genuine nature of the Church, both by the love of spouses, generous fecundity, unity, and fidelity, and by the loving cooperation of all members.
¶444
Unde, ipsis parentibus exemplo et oratione familiari praegredientibus, filii, immo et omnes in familiae convictu degentes, humanitatis, salutis atque sanctitatis viam facilius invenient. Coniuges autem, dignitate ac munere paternitatis et maternitatis ornati, officium educationis praesertim religiosae, quod ad ipsos imprimis spectat, diligenter adimplebunt. Liberi, ut viva familiae membra, ad sanctificationem parentum suo modo conferunt. Gratae enim mentis affectu, pietate atque fiducia beneficiis parentum respondebunt ipsisque in rebus adversis necnon in senectutis solitudine filiorum more assistent. Viduitas, in continuitate vocationis coniugalis forti animo assumpta, ab omnibus honorabitur. Familia suas divitias spirituales cum aliis quoque familiis generose communicabit. Proinde familia christiana, cum e matrimonio, quod est imago et participatio foederis dilectionis Christi et Ecclesiae, exoriatur, vivam Salvatoris in mundo praesentiam atque germanam Ecclesiae naturam omnibus patefaciet, tum coniugum amore, generosa fecunditate, unitate atque fidelitate, tum amabili omnium membrorum cooperatione.
49 The spouses and couples are often invited by the divine word to nourish their betrothal with chaste love and marriage with undivided love. Many men of our age also hold true love between husband and wife in high esteem, manifested in various ways according to the honest customs of peoples and times. This love, however, as eminently human, since it is directed by the will's affection from person to person, embraces the good of the whole person and therefore can enrich the expressions of body and spirit with special dignity and ennoble them as elements and special signs of conjugal friendship. The Lord deigned to heal, perfect, and elevate this love with a special gift of grace and charity. Such love, uniting human and divine, leads spouses to a free and mutual gift of self, proved by tender affection and work, and pervades their whole life; indeed, it is perfected and grows by its generous activity. It therefore far surpasses mere erotic inclination, which, egotistically cultivated, quickly and miserably vanishes. This love is singularly expressed and perfected by the proper work of matrimony. Hence, the acts by which spouses intimately and chastely unite with each other are honest and worthy, and, exercised in a truly human way, signify and foster mutual gift, by which they enrich each other with joyful and grateful spirit. That love, ratified by mutual fidelity, and especially sanctioned by the sacrament of Christ, is indissolubly faithful in body and mind through prosperity and adversity, and therefore remains alien from all adultery and divorce. The equality also of personal dignity of woman and man being acknowledged, the unity of matrimony confirmed by the Lord clearly appears in mutual and full love. For the duties of this Christian vocation to be constantly fulfilled, outstanding virtue is required: wherefore spouses, strengthened by grace for a holy life, will constantly cultivate the firmness of love, greatness of spirit, and the spirit of sacrifice, and obtain them by prayer. Genuine conjugal love will be more highly esteemed and sound public opinion concerning it will be formed, if Christian spouses excel by testimony of fidelity and harmony in the same love and by solicitude in educating children, and play their part in the necessary cultural, psychological, and social renewal in favor of matrimony and the family. Young people are to be suitably and timely instructed concerning the dignity, office, and work of conjugal love, especially in the bosom of the family itself, that, instructed in the cultivation of chastity, they may be able to pass from honest betrothal to marriage at a suitable age.
¶445
De amore coniugali
On conjugal love
Pluries verbo divino sponsi atque coniuges invitantur, ut casto amore sponsalia et indivisa dilectione coniugium nutriant atque foveant. Plures quoque nostrae aetatis homines verum amorem inter maritum et uxorem variis rationibus secundum honestos populorum et temporum mores manifestatum magni faciunt. Ille autem amor, utpote eminenter humanus, cum a persona in personam voluntatis affectu dirigatur, totius personae bonum complectitur ideoque corporis animique expressiones peculiari dignitate ditare easque tamquam elementa ac signa specialia coniugalis amicitiae nobilitare valet. Hunc amorem Dominus, speciali gratiae et caritatis dono, sanare, perficere et elevare dignatus est. Talis amor, humana simul et divina consocians, coniuges ad liberum et mutuum sui ipsius donum, tenero affectu et opere probatum, conducit totamque vitam eorum pervadit; immo ipse generosa sua operositate perficitur et crescit. Longe igitur exsuperat meram eroticam inclinationem, quae, egoistice exculta, citius et misere evanescit. Haec dilectio proprio matrimonii opere singulariter exprimitur et perficitur. Actus proinde, quibus coniuges intime et caste inter se uniuntur, honesti ac digni sunt et, modo vere humano exerciti, donationem mutuam significant et fovent, qua sese invicem laeto gratoque animo locupletant. Amor ille mutua fide ratus, et potissimum sacramento Christi sancitus, inter prospera et adversa corpore ac mente indissolubiliter fidelis est, et proinde ab omni adulterio et divortio alienus remanet. Aequali etiam dignitate personali cum mulieris tum viri agnoscenda in mutua atque plena dilectione, unitas matrimonii a Domino confirmata luculenter apparet. Ad officia autem huius vocationis christianae constanter exsequenda virtus insignis requiritur: quapropter coniuges, gratia ad vitam sanctam roborati, firmitatem amoris, magnitudinem animi et spiritum sacrificii assidue colent et oratione impetrabunt. Germanus autem amor coniugalis altius aestimabitur atque sana circa eum opinio publica efformabitur, si coniuges christiani testimonio fidelitatis et harmoniae in eodem amore necnon sollicitudine in filiis educandis, eminent atque in necessaria renovatione culturali, psychologica et sociali in favorem matrimonii et familiae partes suas agunt. Iuvenes de amoris coniugalis dignitate, munere et opere, potissimum in sinu ipsius familiae, apte et tempestive instruendi sunt, ut, castitatis cultu instituti, convenienti aetate ab honestis sponsalibus ad nuptias transire possint.
50 Matrimony and conjugal love are by their nature ordered to the procreation and education of offspring. Children indeed are the most excellent gift of matrimony and greatly contribute to the good of the parents themselves. God himself, who said: "It is not good for man to be alone" (Gen 2:18) and who "made man from the beginning male and female" (Mt 19:4), wishing to communicate to man a special participation in His own creative work, blessed man and woman saying: "Increase and multiply" (Gen 1:28). Hence the true cultivation of conjugal love and the whole manner of family life arising therefrom, the other ends of matrimony not being set aside, tend to this, that spouses be disposed with strong spirit to cooperate with the love of the Creator and Savior, who through them daily enlarges and enriches His family.
¶446
De matrimonii fecunditate
On the fecundity of matrimony
Matrimonium et amor coniugalis indole sua ad prolem procreandam et educandam ordinantur. Filii sane sunt praestantissimum matrimonii donum et ad ipsorum parentum bonum maxime conferunt. Ipse Deus qui dixit: non est bonum esse hominem solum (Gn 2,18) et qui hominem ab initio masculum et feminam... fecit (Mt 19,4), volens ei participationem specialem quamdam in Suiipsius opere creativo communicare, viro et mulieri benedixit dicens: crescite et multiplicamini (Gn 1,28). Unde verus amoris coniugalis cultus totaque vitae familiaris ratio inde oriens, non posthabitis ceteris matrimonii finibus, eo tendunt ut coniuges forti animo dispositi sint ad cooperandum cum amore Creatoris atque Salvatoris, qui per eos Suam familiam in dies dilatat et ditat.
In the duty of transmitting and educating human life, which is to be considered as their proper mission, spouses know that they are cooperators of the love of God the Creator and as it were its interpreters. Therefore they will fulfill their office with human and Christian responsibility, and with docile reverence toward God, by common counsel and effort, will form for themselves a right judgment, attending both to their own good and the good of children, whether already born or to be foreseen, discerning the conditions of times and state of life both material and spiritual, and finally keeping regard for the good of the family community, temporal society, and the Church itself. This judgment they themselves ultimately, spouses before God, must make. In their manner of acting, however, Christian spouses should be conscious that they cannot proceed at their own arbitrariness, but must always be ruled by conscience itself conformed to the divine law, docile to the Magisterium of the Church, which authentically interprets that law under the light of the Gospel. That divine law shows the full meaning of conjugal love, protects it, and impels it to its truly human perfection. Thus Christian spouses, trusting in divine Providence and cultivating the spirit of sacrifice, glorify the Creator and tend to perfection in Christ, when they exercise the office of procreation with generous, human, and Christian responsibility. Among spouses who in such manner satisfy the office entrusted to them by God, especially to be remembered are those who, by prudent and common counsel, with great spirit undertake to receive a larger number of children to be suitably educated.

In officio humanam vitam transmittendi atque educandi, quod tamquam propria eorum missio considerandum est, coniuges sciunt se cooperatores esse amoris Dei Creatoris eiusque veluti interpretes. Ideo humana et christiana responsabilitate suum munus adimplebunt ac docili erga Deum reverentia, communi consilio atque conatu, rectum iudicium sibi efformabunt, attendentes tum ad suum ipsorum bonum tum ad bonum liberorum, sive iam nati sint sive futuri praevideantur, dignoscentes temporum et status vitae condiciones tum materiales tum spirituales, ac denique rationem servantes boni communitatis familiaris, societatis temporalis ipsiusque Ecclesiae. Hoc iudicium ipsi ultimatim coniuges coram Deo ferre debent. In sua vero agendi ratione coniuges christiani conscii sint se non ad arbitrium suum procedere posse, sed semper regi debere conscientia ipsi legi divinae conformanda, dociles erga Ecclesiae Magisterium, quod illam sub luce Evangelii authentice interpretatur. Lex illa divina plenam amoris coniugalis significationem ostendit, illum protegit et ad eiusdem vere humanam perfectionem impellit. Ita coniuges christiani, divinae Providentiae confidentes et spiritum sacrificii excolentes, Creatorem glorificant atque ad perfectionem in Christo contendunt, cum procreandi munere generosa, humana atque christiana responsabilitate funguntur. Inter coniuges qui tali modo muneri sibi a Deo commisso satisfaciunt, peculiariter memorandi sunt illi qui, prudenti communique consilio, magno animo prolem congruenter educandam etiam numerosiorem suscipiunt.
Matrimony, however, is not instituted only for procreation; but the very nature of the covenant between persons, indissoluble, and the good of children demand that mutual love of spouses also be exhibited in right order, progress, and mature. Therefore, even if offspring, often so much desired, are lacking, matrimony remains as a whole-life community and communion, and retains its value and indissolubility.

Matrimonium vero, non est tantum ad procreationem institutum; sed ipsa indoles foederis inter personas indissolubilis atque bonum prolis exigunt, ut mutuus etiam coniugum amor recto ordine exhibeatur, proficiat et maturescat. Ideo etsi proles, saepius tam optata, deficit, matrimonium ut totius vitae consuetudo et communio perseverat, suumque valorem atque indissolubilitatem servat.
51 The Council knows that spouses, in harmoniously ordering conjugal life, are often hindered by certain modern conditions of life, and may be placed in circumstances in which the number of children cannot be increased, at least for a time, and the cultivation of faithful love and full community of life are not preserved without difficulty. But when intimate conjugal life is broken, the good of faith can often be called into peril and the good of children can be harmed: for then the education of children and the strong spirit to receive further children are endangered. There are those who presume to bring dishonest solutions to these problems, indeed do not shrink from killing; but the Church calls to mind that there can be no true contradiction between the divine laws of transmitting life and fostering genuine conjugal love. God, indeed, the Lord of life, entrusted to men the excellent ministry of preserving life, to be fulfilled in a manner worthy of man. Life, therefore, from conception is to be guarded with the greatest care; abortion and infanticide are nefarious crimes. The sexual nature of man and the human faculty of generation wonderfully surpass those which are had in lower grades of life; therefore the acts proper to conjugal life, ordered according to genuine human dignity, are to be revered with great respect. The moral nature, therefore, of the manner of acting, when it concerns reconciling conjugal love with responsible transmission of life, does not depend solely on sincere intention and estimation of motives, but must be determined by objective criteria, taken from the nature of the person and of his acts, which observe the full sense of mutual gift and human procreation in the context of true love; which cannot be done unless the virtue of conjugal chastity is cultivated with sincere spirit. The sons of the Church, based on these principles, may not enter into ways in regulating procreation which the Magisterium, in explaining the divine law, disapproves. Let all, however, know that the life of men and the office of transmitting it are not restricted to this age only nor can be measured and understood only by it, but always look to the eternal destiny of men.

De amore coniugali componendo cum observantia vitae humanae
On reconciling conjugal love with respect for human life
Concilium novit coniuges, in vita coniugali harmonice ordinanda, saepe quibusdam hodiernis vitae condicionibus praepediri atque in circumstantiis versari posse in quibus numerus prolis, saltem ad tempus, augeri nequit, et fidelis amoris cultus atque plena vitae consuetudo non sine difficultate conservantur. Ubi autem intima vita coniugalis abrumpitur, bonum fidei non raro in discrimen vocari et bonum prolis pessumdari possunt: tunc enim educatio liberorum necnon fortis animus ad prolem ulteriorem suscipiendam periclitantur. Sunt qui his problematibus solutiones inhonestas afferre praesumunt, immo ab occisione non abhorrent; at Ecclesia in memoriam revocat veram contradictionem inter divinas leges vitae transmittendae et germani amoris coniugalis fovendi adesse non posse. Deus enim, Dominus vitae, praecellens servandi vitam ministerium hominibus commisit, modo homine digno adimplendum. Vita igitur inde a conceptione, maxima cura tuenda est; abortus necnon infanticidium nefanda sunt crimina. Indoles vero sexualis hominis necnon humana generandi facultas mirabiliter exsuperant ea quae in inferioribus vitae gradibus habentur; proinde ipsi actus vitae coniugali proprii, secundum germanam dignitatem humanam ordinati, magna observantia reverendi sunt. Moralis igitur indoles rationis agendi, ubi de componendo amore coniugali cum responsabili vitae transmissione agitur, non a sola sincera intentione et aestimatione motivorum pendet, sed obiectivis criteriis, ex personae eiusdemque actuum natura desumptis, determinari debet, quae integrum sensum mutuae donationis ac humanae procreationis in contextu veri amoris observant; quod fieri nequit nisi virtus castitatis coniugalis sincero animo colatur. Filiis Ecclesiae, his principiis innixis, in procreatione regulanda, vias inire non licet, quae a Magisterio, in lege divina explicanda, improbantur. Omnibus vero compertum sit vitam hominum et munus eam transmittendi non ad hoc saeculum tantum restringi neque eo tantum commensurari et intelligi posse, sed ad aeternam hominum destinationem semper respicere.
52 The family is a school of richer humanity. But that it may be able to attain the fullness of its life and mission, kindly communication of spirit and common counsel of spouses, and sedulous cooperation of parents in the education of children, are required. The active presence of the father greatly profits their formation, but the domestic care of the mother, which the younger children especially need, is to be placed in safety, without the legitimate social promotion of woman being set aside. Children are to be instructed by education that, having grown to adult age, with full sense of responsibility they may be able to follow their vocation, even sacred, and choose a state of life, in which, if they join in matrimony, they may be able to found their own family, in moral, social, and economic conditions favorable to it. It is the part of parents or guardians to be guides to the young, in founding a family, by prudent counsel, willingly heard by them, taking care not to force them by direct or indirect coercion to enter marriage or to choose a partner. Thus the family, in which diverse generations meet and help one another to acquire fuller wisdom and to compose the rights of persons with other exigencies of social life, constitutes the foundation of society. Therefore all who exercise influence on communities and social groups ought effectively to contribute to the promotion of matrimony and the family. Civil power should acknowledge, protect, and promote their true nature, protect public morality, and favor domestic prosperity, as its sacred office. The right of parents to beget and educate children in the bosom of the family is to be protected. By provident legislation and various undertakings, even those who unhappily lack the good of the family are to be protected and helped with suitable aid. Christ's faithful, redeeming the present time and distinguishing eternal from changeable forms, diligently promote the goods of matrimony and the family, both by the testimony of their own life and by concordant action with men of good will, and thus, obstacles intercepted, will provide for the necessities and conveniences of the family, which agree with new times. To obtain this end, the Christian sense of the faithful, right moral conscience of men, and the wisdom and skill of those who are versed in sacred disciplines, will be of great help. Those who are skilled in sciences, especially biological, medical, social, and psychological, can contribute much to the good of matrimony and the family, and to the peace of consciences, if by joined studies they strive to elucidate more deeply the various conditions favoring the honest ordering of human procreation. It is the part of priests, with due knowledge concerning the family, to foster the vocation of spouses by various pastoral means, by preaching the word of God, by liturgical worship, and by other spiritual aids, in their conjugal and family life, and to strengthen them humanely and patiently in difficulties and to comfort them in charity, that truly radiant families may be formed. Various works, especially family associations, will strive to strengthen young people and spouses, especially those recently married, by doctrine and action, and to form them for family, social, and apostolic life. Finally, the spouses themselves, made to the image of the living God and constituted in the true order of persons, are to be united with equal affection, similar mind, and mutual holiness, that, following Christ the principle of life, in the joys and sacrifices of their vocation, through their faithful love, they may become witnesses of that mystery of love which the Lord revealed to the world by His death and resurrection.

De matrimonii et familiae promotione ab omnibus curanda
On the promotion of matrimony and the family to be cared for by all
Familia schola quaedam uberioris humanitatis est. Ut autem vitae ac missionis suae plenitudinem attingere valeat, benevola animi communicatio communeque coniugum consilium necnon sedula parentum cooperatio in filiorum educatione requiruntur. Praesentia actuosa patris eorumdem efformationi magnopere prodest, sed et cura domestica matris, qua liberi praesertim iuniores indigent, in tuto ponenda est, quin legitima mulieris promotio socialis posthabeatur. Liberi ita educatione instruantur ut ad aetatem adultam provecti pleno responsabilitatis sensu vocationem etiam sacram sequi ac vitae statum eligere queant, in quo, si nuptiis iungantur, familiam propriam, in condicionibus moralibus, socialibus et oeconomicis eidem propitiis, condere possint. Parentum vel tutorum est se iunioribus, in fundanda familia, prudenti consilio, ab eis libenter audiendo, duces praebere, caventes tamen ne eos coactione directa vel indirecta ad matrimonium ineundum aut ad electionem compartis adigant. Ita familia, in qua diversae generationes conveniunt ac sese mutuo adiuvant ad pleniorem sapientiam acquirendam atque iura personarum cum aliis vitae socialis exigentiis componenda, fundamentum societatis constituit. Ideoque omnes qui influxum in communitates et coetus sociales exercent, ad promotionem matrimonii et familiae efficaciter conferre debent. Potestas civilis veram eorumdem indolem agnoscere, protegere et provehere, moralitatem publicam tueri atque prosperitati domesticae favere, ut sacrum suum munus consideret. Ius parentum prolem procreandi et in sinu familiae educandi tutandum est. Provida legislatione variisque inceptis etiam illi protegantur aptoque adiumento subleventur qui bono familiae infeliciter carent. Christifideles, praesens tempus redimentes atque aeterna a mutabilibus formis discernentes, bona matrimonii et familiae, tum propriae vitae testimonio tum concordi actione cum hominibus bonae voluntatis, diligenter promoveant, et sic, interceptis difficultatibus, providebunt familiae necessitatibus et commodis, quae novis temporibus conveniunt. Ad quem finem obtinendum sensus christianus fidelium, recta hominum conscientia moralis necnon sapientia ac peritia eorum qui in sacris disciplinis versati sunt, magno auxilio erunt. Qui scientiis, praecipue biologicis, medicis, socialibus et psychologicis eruditi sunt, multum bono matrimonii et familiae, pacique conscientiarum inservire possunt, si collatis studiis diversas condiciones honestae ordinationi procreationis humanae faventes, penitius elucidare conentur. Sacerdotum est, debita de re familiari eruditione accepta, vocationem coniugum diversis mediis pastoralibus, verbi Dei praedicatione, cultu liturgico aliisve adiumentis spiritualibus in vita eorum coniugali et familiari fovere, eosque humaniter et patienter in difficultatibus roborare atque in caritate confortare ut vere radiosae familiae efformentur. Varia opera, praesertim familiarum consociationes, iuvenes ipsosque coniuges, praecipue nuper iunctos, doctrina et actione confirmare eosque ad vitam familiarem, socialem et apostolicam formare satagent. Ipsi denique coniuges, ad imaginem Dei vivi facti et in vero ordine personarum constituti, affectu compari, mente consimili et mutua sanctitate adunati sint, ut Christum, vitae principium secuti, in gaudiis et sacrificiis vocationis suae, per suum fidelum amorem, illius testes fiant mysterii dilectionis, quod Dominus morte et resurrectione sua mundo revelavit.
53 It pertains to the person of man that he only approach true and full humanity through culture, that is, by cultivating the goods and values of nature. Therefore wherever human life is treated, nature and culture are most intimately connected. By the word culture in a general sense are indicated all those things by which man develops and perfects the manifold gifts of mind and body; strives to subject the orb of the earth to his power by knowledge and labor; renders social life, both in the family and in the whole civil association, more humane by the progress of morals and institutions; finally expresses, communicates, and conserves in his works the great spiritual experiences and aspirations throughout the course of time, that they may serve for the progress of many, indeed of the whole human race. Hence it follows that human culture necessarily carries with it a historical and social aspect, and the word culture often assumes a sociological and ethnological sense. In this sense, the plurality of cultures is spoken of. For from the diverse manner of using things, of performing labor and expressing oneself, of cultivating religion and forming morals, of establishing laws and juridical institutions, of advancing sciences and arts and cultivating beauty, diverse common conditions of living and diverse forms of composing the goods of life arise. Thus from handed-down institutions a patrimony proper to each human community is brought about. Thus also a defined and historical environment is constituted, into which man of each people or age is inserted, and from which he draws goods for promoting human and civil culture.

CAPUT II. DE CULTURAE PROGRESSU RITE PROMOVENDO
CHAPTER II. ON THE RIGHT PROMOTION OF THE PROGRESS OF CULTURE
Introductio
Ad ipsam personam hominis pertinet ut nonnisi per culturam, hoc est bona naturae valoresque colendo, ad veram plenamque humanitatem accedat. Ubicumque ergo de vita humana agitur, natura et cultura quam intime connectuntur. Voce cultura sensu generali indicantur omnia quibus homo multifarias dotes animi corporisque perpolit atque explicat; ipsum orbem terrarum cognitione et labore in suam potestatem redigere studet; vitam socialem, tam in familia quam in tota consortione civili, progressu morum institutorumque humaniorem reddit; denique magnas experientias spirituales atque appetitiones decursu temporum in operibus suis exprimit, communicat atque conservat, ut ad profectum multorum, quinimmo totius generis humani, inserviant. Inde sequitur culturam humanam aspectum historicum atque socialem necessario prae se ferre, atque vocem cultura saepe sensum sociologicum necnon ethnologicum assumere. Hoc autem sensu de culturarum pluralitate sermo fit. Ex diverso enim modo utendi rebus, laborem praestandi et sese exprimendi, religionem colendi moresque formandi, statuendi leges et iuridica instituta, augendi scientias et artes atque colendi pulchrum, diversae oriuntur communes vivendi condiciones et diversae formae bona vitae componendi. Ita ex traditis institutis efficitur patrimonium cuique humanae communitati proprium. Ita etiam constituitur ambitus definitus et historicus, in quem homo cuiusque gentis vel aetatis inseritur, et ex quo bona ad humanum civilemque cultum promovendum haurit.
54 The conditions of life of modern man under the social and cultural respect have profoundly changed, so that it is permitted to speak of a new age of human history. Hence new ways open for perfecting and more widely spreading culture. These have been prepared by the immense increase of natural and human sciences, also social, by the increase of technical arts, and by the progress in cultivating and rightly disposing the instruments by which men communicate with one another. Hence modern culture is marked by particular notes: the sciences which are called exact greatly develop critical judgment; recent studies of psychology more profoundly explain human activity; historical disciplines greatly contribute to the viewing of things under the aspect of their mutability and evolution; customs and manners become more and more uniform; industrialization, urbanization, and other causes which promote community life, create new forms of culture (mass-culture), from which new ways of feeling, acting, and using leisure are born; increased commerce at the same time among various peoples and social groups more widely opens the treasures of the various forms of culture to all and each, and thus a more universal form of human culture is gradually prepared, which, the more it promotes and expresses the unity of the human race, the more it observes the particularities of different cultures.

Sectio I: DE CULTURAE CONDICIONIBUS IN MUNDO HODIERNO
Sectio I: ON THE CONDITIONS OF CULTURE IN THE MODERN WORLD
De novis vivendi formis
On new forms of living
Condiciones vitae hominis moderni sub respectu sociali et culturali profunde immutatae sunt, ita ut de nova historiae humanae aetate loqui liceat. Exinde ad culturam perficiendam ampliusque spargendam novae patent viae. Quas paraverunt ingens augmentum scientiarum naturalium et humanarum, etiam socialium, incrementum technicarum artium, necnon progressus in excolendis et recte disponendis instrumentis quibus homines inter se communicant. Hinc cultura hodierna particularibus signatur notis: scientiae, quae exactae nuncupantur, iudicium criticum maxime excolunt; recentiora psychologiae studia humanam activitatem profundius explicant; disciplinae historicae valde conferunt ut res sub specie suae mutabilitatis atque evolutionis adspiciantur; vitae consuetudines et mores in dies magis uniformes efficiuntur; industrializatio, urbanizatio aliaeque causae quae vitam communitariam promovent, novas culturae formas creant (mass-culture), ex quibus novi modi sentiendi, agendi otioque utendi nascuntur; aucta simul inter varias gentes societatisque coetus commercia thesauros diversarum culturae formarum omnibus et singulis latius aperiunt, et sic paulatim universalior paratur culturae humanae forma, quae eo magis humani generis unitatem promovet ac exprimit, quo melius diversarum culturarum particularitates observat.
55 The number of men and women of every group or nation who are conscious of being themselves the artificers and authors of their community's culture increases day by day. In the whole world, the sense of autonomy and at the same time of responsibility grows more and more, which is of the greatest importance for the spiritual and moral maturity of the human race. This appears more clearly if we place the unification of the world before our eyes and the office imposed on us, that we build a better world in truth and justice. Thus we are witnesses that a new humanism is being born, in which man is defined especially by his responsibility toward his brothers and toward history.
¶453
Man, author of culture
Homo auctor culturae
Maior in dies fit numerus virorum ac mulierum cuiusvis coetus vel nationis, qui conscii sunt suae communitatis culturae artifices se esse atque auctores. In universo mundo magis magisque crescit autonomiae simulque responsabilitatis sensus, quod pro spirituali ac morali maturitate generis humani maximi est momenti. Illud clarius apparet, si ante oculos unificationem mundi ponimus atque munus nobis impositum, ut in veritate et iustitia meliorem aedificemus mundum. Tali ergo modo testes sumus novum humanismum nasci, in quo homo imprimis sua responsabilitate erga suos fratres historiamque definitur.
56 In these conditions, it is not to be wondered that man, who feels his responsibility for the progress of culture, nourishes higher hope, but also looks with anxious mind at the multiple existing antinomies, which he himself must resolve: What must be done, that the more frequent commerce of cultures, which ought to lead to true and fruitful dialogue between various groups and nations, not disturb the life of communities, nor overturn the wisdom of the fathers, nor call into peril the proper character of peoples? How is the dynamism and expansion of new culture to be favored, without the living fidelity toward the heritage of traditions perishing? Which presses in a particular way when culture, which arises from the immense progress of sciences and technical arts, must be composed with that cultivation of mind which is nourished by studies according to various classical traditions. How can so swift and progressive dispersion of particular disciplines be conformed with the necessity of forming their synthesis, and of preserving in men the faculty of contemplation and admiration, which leads to wisdom? What is to be done, that all men may become participants of cultural goods in the world, while at the same time human culture of the more skilled always becomes more sublime and complex? How, finally, as the autonomy which culture claims for itself is to be acknowledged as legitimate, is one to avoid falling into a merely terrestrial humanism, indeed even opposed to religion itself? In the midst of those antinomies, human culture must therefore be so developed today that it may cultivate the whole human person in right order and help men in the offices, to which all, especially Christ's faithful, in one human family fraternally united, are called.

Difficultates et munera
Difficulties and duties
Quibus in condicionibus, non est mirandum, hominem, qui responsabilitatem suam sentit pro culturae progressu, altiorem spem nutrire, sed etiam anxio animo adspicere multiplices antinomias exsistentes, quas ipse resolvere debet: Quid faciendum est, ne frequentiora culturarum commercia, quae inter diversos coetus et nationes ad verum et fructuosum dialogum adducere deberent, vitam communitatum perturbent, neve sapientiam maiorum evertant, neve propriam populorum indolem in discrimen adducant? Quomodo dynamismo atque expansioni novae culturae est favendum, quin fidelitas viva erga traditionum haereditatem pereat? Quod particulari modo urget ubi cultura, quae ex ingenti scientiarum artiumque technicarum progressu oritur, componenda est cum eo ingenii cultu qui studiis secundum varias traditiones classicis alitur. Quomodo tam velox atque progrediens disciplinarum particularium dispersio conformari potest cum necessitate formandi earum synthesim, necnon servandi apud homines facultates contemplationis ac admirationis, quae ad sapientiam adducunt? Quid faciendum est, ut universi homines bonorum culturalium participes fiant in mundo, cum insimul cultus humanus peritiorum semper sublimior atque complexior evadat? Quomodo denique, prout legitima agnoscenda est autonomia, quam cultura sibi vindicat, quin ad humanismum mere terrestrem, immo ipsi religioni adversantem deveniatur? In medio quidem illarum antinomiarum cultura humana ita hodie evolvatur oportet, ut integram personam humanam aequo ordine excolat atque homines iuvet in muneribus, ad quae adimplenda omnes, praecipue autem christifideles, in una familia humana fraterne uniti, vocantur.
57 Christ's faithful, pilgrimaging to the heavenly city, ought to seek and savor the things that are above; by which, however, far from being diminished, the importance of their office together with all men to build a more humane world increases. And indeed the mystery of the Christian faith provides excellent incentives and aids to more earnestly fulfill that office, and especially to discover the full meaning of such work, by which human culture obtains its excellent place in the whole vocation of man. For when man cultivates the earth by the work of his hands or by means of technical arts, that it may bear fruit and become a worthy dwelling for the whole human family, and when he consciously assumes his part in the life of social groups, he executes the plan of God, laid open from the beginning of times, of subduing the earth and perfecting creation, and at the same time cultivates himself; likewise he keeps the great command of Christ, spending himself in the service of brothers. Moreover, when man applies himself to the various disciplines of philosophy, history, and mathematical and natural science, and practices the arts, he can greatly contribute that the human family be raised to higher reasons of truth, goodness, and beauty and to the judgment of universal value, and thus be more clearly illuminated by the wonderful Wisdom, which from eternity was with God, setting all things with Him, playing in the world, delighting to be with the sons of men. By that very fact, the mind of man, more freed from the servitude of things, can be more readily raised to the very worship and contemplation of the Creator. Indeed, under the impulse of grace, it is disposed to acknowledge the Word of God, who, before He was made flesh to save all things and recapitulate them in Himself, was already in the world, as the true light which enlightens every man (Jn 1:9). Indeed, the modern progress of sciences and technical arts, which by the force of their method cannot penetrate to the intimate reasons of things, can favor a certain phenomenalism and agnosticism, when the method of investigation, which those disciplines use, is undeservedly held as the supreme rule of all truth to be found. Indeed, the peril is that man, too trusting in modern discoveries, may think he suffices for himself and no longer seek higher things. However, these unhappy things do not necessarily follow from modern culture, nor should they lead us into temptation that we do not acknowledge its positive values. Among which are numbered: the study of sciences and exact fidelity toward truth in scientific investigations, the necessity of working together with others in technical groups, the sense of international solidarity, the ever more vivid consciousness of the responsibility of experts toward helping and indeed protecting men, the will to make the conditions of life more favorable to all, especially to those who labor under privation of responsibility or poverty of culture. All these can provide a certain preparation for receiving the message of the Gospel, which can be informed by divine charity from Him who came to save the world.

Sectio II: DE QUIBUSDAM PRINCIPIIS AD CULTURAM RITE PROMOVENDAM
Sectio II: ON CERTAIN PRINCIPLES FOR THE RIGHT PROMOTION OF CULTURE
Fides et cultura
Faith and culture
Christifideles, ad civitatem caelestem peregrinantes, ea quae sursum sunt quaerere et sapere debent; quo tamen nedum minuatur, potius crescit momentum muneris eorum una cum omnibus hominibus adlaborandi ad aedificationem mundi humanius exstruendi. Et revera fidei christianae mysterium praestantia incitamenta et adiumenta eis praebet ad munus illud impensius adimplendum et praesertim ad plenum huiusmodi operis sensum detegendum, quo cultus humanus in integra hominis vocatione suum eximium obtineat locum. Cum enim homo opere manuum suarum vel ope technicarum artium terram excolit, ut fructum afferat et dignum universae familiae humanae habitaculum fiat, et cum conscie partes assumit in socialium coetuum vita, ipse exsequitur consilium Dei, initio temporum patefactum, terrae subiiciendae creationisque perficiendae, atque seipsum excolit; insimul magnum Christi mandatum servat, sese in servitium fratrum impendendi. Insuper cum homo in varias philosophiae, historiae atque scientiae mathematicae et naturalis disciplinas incumbit et artibus versatur, maxime conferre potest, ut familia humana ad sublimiores veri, boni et pulchri rationes atque ad iudicium universi valoris elevetur et sic clarius illuminetur mirabili Sapientia, quae ab aeterno cum Deo erat, cuncta cum Eo componens, ludens in orbe terrarum, esse cum filiis hominum in deliciis habens. Eo ipso animus hominis, a rerum servitute magis solutus, expeditius ad ipsum Creatoris cultum et contemplationem evehi potest. Immo impulsu gratiae ad agnoscendum Dei Verbum disponitur, quod, antequam caro fieret ad omnia salvanda et in Se recapitulanda, iam in mundo erat, tamquam lux vera quae illuminat omnem hominem (Jn 1,9). Sane hodiernus progressus scientiarum artiumque technicarum, quae vi methodi suae usque ad intimas rerum rationes penetrare nequeunt, cuidam phaenomenismo et agnosticismo favere potest, quando methodus investigandi, qua disciplinae istae utuntur, immerito pro suprema totius veritatis inveniendae regula habetur. Immo periculum adest, ne homo, inventis hodiernis nimis fidens, se sibi sufficere aestimet et altiora amplius iam non quaerat. Haec tamen infausta non necessario ex hodierna cultura sequuntur, nec nos in tentationem inducere debent, ne eius valores positivos non agnoscamus. Inter quos adnumerantur: scientiarum studium atque exacta fidelitas erga veritatem in inquisitionibus scientificis, necessitas laborandi una cum aliis in coetibus technicis, sensus solidarietatis internationalis, conscientia in dies vividior responsabilitatis peritorum erga homines adiuvandos immo et protegendos, voluntas faustiores reddendi vitae condiciones omnibus, praesertim illis qui vel responsabilitatis privatione vel culturae paupertate laborant. Haec omnia aliquam praeparationem ad nuntium Evangelii accipiendum afferre valent, quae informari potest caritate divina ab Eo qui venit ut mundum salvaret.
58 Many connections are found between the message of salvation and human culture. For God, revealing Himself to His people up to the full manifestation of Himself in the incarnate Son, spoke according to the culture proper to various ages. Likewise the Church, living in various conditions through the course of times, has used the discoveries of diverse cultures to spread and explain the message of Christ to all nations, to investigate and more deeply understand it, to better express it in liturgical celebration and in the life of the manifold community of the faithful. But at the same time, sent to all peoples of any age and region, the Church is not exclusively and indissolubly bound to any race or nation, any particular manner of morals, any ancient or new custom. Adhering to her own tradition and at the same time conscious of her universal mission, she can enter into communion with the various forms of culture, by which both the Church herself and the various cultures are enriched. The good news of Christ continually renews the life and culture of fallen man, and impugns and removes errors and evils flowing from the ever threatening seduction of sin. It unceasingly purifies and elevates the morals of peoples. It fertilizes from within the ornaments and gifts of the mind of any people or age with heavenly riches, strengthens, completes, and restores them in Christ. Thus the Church, while fulfilling her own office, by that very fact already impels and contributes to human and civil culture, and by her action, even liturgical, educates man to interior freedom.
¶456
De multiplici inter bonum nuntium Christi et culturam humanam connexione
On the manifold connection between the good news of Christ and human culture
Inter nuntium salutis et culturam humanam multiplices nexus inveniuntur. Nam Deus, populo suo sese revelans usque ad plenam sui manifestationem in Filio incarnato, locutus est secundum culturam diversis aetatibus propriam. Pariter Ecclesia, decursu temporum variis in condicionibus vivens, diversarum culturarum inventa adhibuit, ut nuntium Christi in sua praedicatione ad omnes gentes diffundat et explicet, illud investiget et altius intelligat, in celebratione liturgica atque in vita multiformis communitatis fidelium melius exprimat. At simul, ad omnes populos cuiusvis aetatis et regionis missa, Ecclesia nulli stirpi aut nationi, nulli particulari morum rationi, nulli antiquae aut novae consuetudini exclusive et indissolubiliter nectitur. Propriae traditioni inhaerens et insimul missionis suae universalis conscia, communionem cum diversis culturae formis inire valet, qua tum ipsa Ecclesia tum variae culturae ditescunt. Bonum Christi nuntium hominis lapsi vitam et cultum continenter renovat, et errores ac mala, ex semper minaci peccati seductione manantia, impugnat et removet. Mores populorum indesinenter purificat et elevat. Animi ornamenta dotesque cuiuscumque populi vel aetatis supernis divitiis velut ab intra fecundat, communit, complet atque in Christo restaurat. Sic Ecclesia, proprium implendo munus, iam eo ipso ad humanum civilemque cultum impellit atque confert, et actione sua, etiam liturgica, hominem ad interiorem libertatem educat.
59 For the reasons stated above, the Church calls to the mind of all that culture is to be referred to the whole perfection of the human person, to the good of the community, and of the whole human society. Therefore the mind ought to be so cultivated that the faculty of admiration, of reading into things, of contemplating, and of forming personal judgment and of cultivating the religious, moral, and social sense may be promoted. For culture, since it flows immediately from the rational and social nature of man, constantly needs just freedom to develop itself and a legitimate, according to its own principles, autonomous faculty of action. It therefore rightly demands reverence and enjoys a certain inviolability, the rights of the person and community, both particular and universal, being preserved within the bounds of the common good. The sacred Synod, recalling what the First Vatican Council taught, declares that there is a double order of knowledge distinct, namely faith and reason, nor does the Church forbid the cultures of human arts and disciplines to use their own principles and proper method in their own sphere; therefore, acknowledging this just freedom, she affirms the legitimate autonomy of human culture and especially of the sciences. All these things also demand that man, the moral order and common utility being preserved, be free to seek truth and to declare and spread his opinion, and to cultivate any art; that finally he be informed of public events according to truth. To public authority, however, it pertains not to determine the proper character of forms of culture, but to foster the conditions and aids for promoting cultural life among all, even within the minorities of a nation. Therefore it is especially to be insisted, that culture be not forced to serve political or economic powers, turned away from its own end.
¶457
De diversis rationibus in cultus humani formis rite componendis
On the various ways to be rightly composed in the forms of human culture
Supradictis rationibus, Ecclesia in mentem omnium revocat culturam ad integram personae humanae perfectionem, ad bonum communitatis et totius humanae societatis esse referendam. Quare oportet animum ita excolere, ut promoveatur facultas admirandi, intus legendi, contemplandi atque efformandi iudicium personale et excolendi sensum religiosum, moralem ac socialem. Cultura enim, cum ex hominis indole rationali et sociali immediate fluat, indesinenter indiget iusta libertate ad sese explicandam atque legitima, secundum propria principia, sui iuris agendi facultate. Iure merito ergo postulat reverentiam et quadam gaudet inviolabilitate, servatis utique iuribus personae et communitatis, sive particularis sive universalis, intra fines boni communis. Sacra Synodus, recolens ea quae Concilium Vaticanum Primum docuit, declarat duplicem esse ordinem cognitionis distinctum, nempe fidei et rationis, nec sane Ecclesiam vetare ne humanarum artium et disciplinarum culturae ... in suo quaeque ambitu propriis utantur principiis et propria methodo; quare iustam hanc libertatem agnoscens, cultus humani et praesertim scientiarum legitimam autonomiam affirmat. Haec omnia postulant quoque ut homo, morali ordine communique utilitate servatis, libere possit verum inquirere et opinionem suam declarare ac vulgare, atque artem qualemcumque colere; ut denique secundum veritatem de publicis eventibus certior fiat. Ad publicam vero auctoritatem pertinet, non proprium cultus humani formarum indolem determinare, sed condiciones et subsidia ad vitam culturalem inter omnes promovendam fovere, etiam intra minoritates alicuius nationis. Ideo praeprimis instandum est, ne cultura, a proprio fine aversa, potestatibus politicis vel oeconomicis servire cogatur.
60 Since the opportunity is now given to free very many men from the misery of ignorance, it is the duty most consonant with our age, especially for Christians, to work strenuously that, both in economic and political matters, both in the national and international field, those fundamental judgments be made by which the right of all to human and civil culture, congruent with the dignity of the person, without discrimination of race, sex, nation, religion, or social condition, may be acknowledged and realized everywhere. Therefore a sufficient supply of cultural goods is to be provided for all, especially those which constitute so-called fundamental culture, lest very many be prevented from truly human cooperation for the common good by illiteracy and lack of responsible activity. It is to be striven, therefore, that men whose mental powers permit be able to ascend to higher orders of studies; in such a way that they may, as far as possible, emerge in human society to offices, duties, and services according to their talent and the skill they have acquired. Thus every man and social group of any people will be able to attain to the full development of their cultural life, congruent with their gifts and traditions. Moreover, it is to be strenuously labored that all become conscious both of the right to culture and of the duty by which they are bound to cultivate themselves and to help others. For conditions of life and work sometimes exist which impede the cultural endeavors of men and destroy the study of culture in them. This holds especially for farmers and workers, to whom such conditions of performing their work must be provided which do not impede but promote their human culture. Women already work in almost all fields of life; it is fitting, however, that they be able to fully assume their parts according to their proper character. It will be the part of all that the proper and necessary participation of women in cultural life be acknowledged and promoted.

Sectio III: DE QUIBUSDAM URGENTIORIBUS CHRISTIANORUM MUNERIBUS CIRCA CULTURAM
Sectio III: ON CERTAIN MORE URGENT DUTIES OF CHRISTIANS CONCERNING CULTURE
Ius ed culturae beneficia omnibus agnoscatur et in rem deducatur
The right to culture and the benefits of culture to be acknowledged and realized for all
Cum nunc facultas praebeatur plurimos homines ab ignorantiae miseria liberandi, officium nostrae aetati maxime consentaneum est, praesertim pro christianis, strenue adlaborandi ut, tam in re oeconomica quam in re politica, tam in campo nationali quam internationali, ferantur iudicia fundamentalia, quibus ius omnium ad humanum civilemque cultum, personae dignitati congruum, sine discrimine stirpis, sexus, nationis, religionis aut socialis condicionis, ubique terrarum agnoscatur et ad rem deducatur. Ideo sufficiens bonorum culturalium copia omnibus providenda est, praesertim eorum quae constituunt culturam sic dictam fundamentalem, ne plurimi litterarum ignorantia responsabilisque actuositatis privatione a cooperatione vere humana ad bonum commune impediantur. Contendendum est itaque ut homines, quorum ingenii vires id ferant, ad altiores studiorum ordines ascendere queant; ita quidem ut iidem, quoad fieri possit, in humana societate ad munera, officia et servitia emergant, tum suo ingenio, tum peritiae quam acquisierint consentanea. Sic quilibet homo et coetus sociales cuiuscumque populi ad plenam vitae suae culturalis explicationem, suis dotibus atque traditionibus congruam, pertingere valebunt. Enixe insuper adlaborandum est ut omnes conscii fiant tum iuris ad culturam, tum officii quo astringuntur sese excolendi aliosque adiuvandi. Condiciones enim vitae et laboris quandoque exstant, quae impediunt contentiones culturales hominum et in ipsis studium culturae destruunt. Res speciali ratione pro ruricolis et opificibus valet, quibus praebeantur oportet tales condiciones operam suam praestandi, quae humanam eorum culturam non praepediant sed promoveant. Mulieres in fere omnibus vitae campis iam adlaborant; convenit autem ut partes suas secundum propriam indolem plene assumere valeant. Omnium erit, ut mulierum propria necessariaque participatio vitae culturalis agnoscatur atque promoveatur.
61 There is today a greater difficulty than once in reducing the various disciplines of knowledge and arts into synthesis. For while the mass and diversity of the elements which constitute culture grow, at the same time the faculty for individuals to perceive them and organically compose them is diminished, so that the universal image of man more and more vanishes. Yet the office of keeping the reason of the whole human person, in which the values of intelligence, will, conscience, and fraternity excel, all of which are founded in God the Creator and wonderfully healed and elevated in Christ, remains for each man.

De educatione ad hominis integrum cultum
On education for the whole culture of man
Maior hodie adest difficultas quam olim varias cognitionis disciplinas et artes in synthesim redigendi. Dum enim crescunt moles et diversitas elementorum, quae culturam constituunt, insimul minuitur facultas pro singulis hominibus eadem percipiendi et organice componendi, ita ut imago hominis universalis magis ac magis evanescat. Attamen unicuique homini remanet officium retinendi rationem totius personae humanae, in qua eminent intelligentiae, voluntatis, conscientiae et fraternitatis valores, qui omnes in Deo Creatore fundantur et in Christo mirabiliter sanati et elevati sunt.
The family is first of all as mother and nurse of this education, in which children, fostered by love, more easily learn the right order of things, while approved forms of human culture are as it were naturally transfused into the progressing spirit of adolescence. For the same education in modern societies there are opportunities, especially from the increased diffusion of books and the new instruments of cultural and social communication, which can favor universal culture. For, everywhere, the reduced time of labor increasingly increases the advantages for many men. Leisure is to be rightly expended for relaxation of mind and health of body and spirit, through free industries and studies, travel to other regions (tourism), by which the genius of man is cultivated, but also men are enriched by mutual knowledge, through sporting exercises and manifestations, which contribute to preserving the equilibrium of mind, also in community, and to establishing fraternal relations among men of all conditions, nations, or diverse race. Christ's faithful, therefore, should cooperate that the manifestations of culture and collective actions, which are proper to our age, be imbued with the human and Christian spirit. Yet all these advantages of education cannot perfect the education of man to his whole culture, if at the same time the deep questioning concerning the meaning of culture and science for the human person is neglected.
¶460
Quasi mater et nutrix huius educationis est imprimis familia, in qua liberi, amore foti, rectum rerum ordinem facilius condiscunt, dum probatae cultus humani formae quasi naturaliter in progredientis adolescentiae animum transfunduntur. Pro eadem educatione in societatibus hodiernis exstant opportunitates, praesertim ex aucta librorum diffusione atque novis instrumentis communicationis culturalis et socialis, quae universali culturae favere possunt. Imminuto enim passim laboris spatio in dies augescunt pro pluribus hominibus commoda. Otia ad animum relaxandum et mentis ac corporis sanitatem roborandam rite insumantur, per liberas industrias et studia, versus alias regiones itinera (turismus), quibus ingenium hominis expolitur, sed et homines mutua cognitione locupletantur, per exercitationes quoque et manifestationes sportivas, quae ad animi aequilibrium, etiam in communitate, servandum necnon ad fraternas relationes inter homines omnium condicionum, nationum vel diversae stirpis statuendas, adiumentum praebent. Christifideles ergo cooperentur ut culturae manifestationes actionesque collectivae, quae nostrae aetatis sunt propriae, spiritu humano et christiano imbuantur. Haec autem omnia commoda educationem hominis ad integrum sui cultum perficere non valent, si insimul profunda interrogatio de sensu culturae et scientiae pro persona humana negligitur.
62 Although the Church has contributed much to the progress of culture, yet experience shows that the composition of culture with Christian instruction from contingent causes has not always proceeded without difficulties. These difficulties do not necessarily bring damage to the life of faith, indeed they can excite the mind to a more accurate and deeper understanding of faith. For the more recent studies and discoveries of sciences, also of history and philosophy, raise new questions, which carry consequences also for life, and also demand new investigations from theologians. Moreover, theologians, the proper methods and exigencies of theological science being preserved, are invited always to seek a more apt manner of communicating doctrine with the men of their time, because it is one thing that the deposit of Faith itself, namely the truths, is another the manner in which they are enunciated, preserving the same sense and the same meaning. In pastoral care, not only theological principles, but also the discoveries of profane sciences, especially of psychology and sociology, are to be sufficiently acknowledged and used, that even the faithful be led to a purer and more mature life of faith. Litterature and the arts also are of great importance in their own way for the life of the Church. For they strive to learn the proper character of man, his problems and his experience in the effort to know and perfect himself and the world; to discover his situation in history and in the whole world and to illustrate the miseries and joys, the necessities and forces of men, and to better adumbrate the lot of man. Thus they can elevate human life, expressed in manifold forms according to times and regions. Therefore it is to be labored that the cultivators of those arts feel themselves recognized by the Church in their activity, and, enjoying ordered freedom, may establish easier commerce with the Christian community. New forms of art also, which are adapted to our contemporaries according to the character of various nations and regions, are to be recognized by the Church. Let them be received in the sanctuary, when, in an accommodated manner of speech and conformed to the exigencies of the Liturgy, they raise the mind to God. Thus the knowledge of God is better manifested and the preaching of the Gospel becomes more perspicuous in the understanding of men and appears as it were inborn to their conditions.

De humano civilique cultu cum christiana institutione rite componendo
On rightly composing human and civil culture with Christian instruction
Quamvis Ecclesia ad culturae progressum multum contulerit, experientia tamen constat compositionem culturae cum christiana institutione ex causis contingentibus non semper sine difficultatibus procedere. Istae difficultates non necessario vitae fidei damnum afferunt, immo ad accuratiorem et altiorem intelligentiam fidei mentem excitare possunt. Etenim scientiarum, necnon historiae ac philosophiae recentiora studia et inventa novas suscitant quaestiones, quae sequelas pro vita quoque secumferunt et etiam a theologis novas investigationes postulant. Praeterea theologi, servatis propriis scientiae theologicae methodis et exigentiis, invitantur ut aptiorem modum doctrinam cum hominibus sui temporis communicandi semper inquirant, quia aliud est ipsum depositum Fidei seu veritates, aliud modus secundum quem enuntiantur, eodem tamen sensu eademque sententia. In cura pastorali non tantum principia theologica, sed etiam inventa scientiarum profanarum, imprimis psychologiae et sociologiae, satis agnoscantur et adhibeantur, ita ut etiam fideles ad puriorem et maturiorem fidei vitam ducantur. Suo quoque modo litterae et artes pro vita Ecclesiae magni sunt momenti. Indolem enim propriam hominis, eius problemata eiusque experientiam in conatu ad seipsum mundumque cognoscendum et perficiendum ediscere contendunt; situationem eius in historia et in universo mundo detegere necnon miserias et gaudia, necessitates et vires hominum illustrare atque sortem hominis meliorem adumbrare satagunt. Ita vitam humanam, multiplicibus formis secundum tempora et regiones expressam, elevare valent. Exinde adlaborandum est ut artium illarum cultores se ab Ecclesia in sua navitate agnitos sentiant et, ordinata libertate fruentes, faciliora commercia cum communitate christiana instituant. Novae quoque formae artis, quae coaevis nostris aptantur iuxta variarum nationum et regionum indolem, ab Ecclesia agnoscantur. In sanctuario autem recipiantur, cum, modo dicendi accommodato et liturgiae exigentiis conformi, mentem ad Deum erigunt. Sic notitia Dei melius manifestatur ac praedicatio evangelica in intellectu hominum magis perspicua fit et eorum condicionibus quasi insita apparet.
Therefore let the faithful live most closely with the other men of their time, and perfectly strive to perceive their ways of thinking and feeling, which are expressed by the cultivation of the mind. Let them join the knowledge of new sciences and doctrines and of the newest discoveries with Christian morals and instruction of Christian doctrine, that the cultivation of religion and probity of spirit may proceed in equal step with the knowledge of sciences and the daily progressing technical arts, and therefore they may be able to approve and interpret all things with a whole Christian sense. Those who apply themselves to theological disciplines in seminaries and universities of studies should strive to cooperate with joined forces and counsels with those who excel in other sciences. Theological investigation should at the same time pursue a deeper knowledge of revealed truth and not neglect connection with its own time, that it may be able to help men skilled in various disciplines to a fuller knowledge of faith. This joint work will greatly profit the instruction of sacred ministers who will be able to explain the doctrine of the Church concerning God, man, and the world more aptly to our contemporaries, that that word may also be more willingly received by them. Indeed, it is to be desired that many laity acquire sufficient instruction in sacred disciplines, and that not a few among them cultivate and more deeply produce these studies, given the work. So that they may be able to exercise their office, there be acknowledged for the faithful, whether clerics or laity, a just freedom of investigation, of thinking, and of opening their mind in humility and fortitude in those things in which they enjoy skill.
¶462
Fideles ergo coniunctissime cum aliis suae aetatis hominibus vivant, et perfecte eorum cogitandi atque sentiendi modos, qui per ingenii cultum exprimuntur, percipere studeant. Novarum scientiarum et doctrinarum necnon novissimorum inventorum notitias cum christianis moribus christianaeque doctrinae institutione coniungant, ut religionis cultus animique probitas apud ipsos pari gressu procedant cum scientiarum cognitione et cotidie progredientibus technicorum artibus, et ideo ipsi valeant res omnes integro christiano sensu probare atque interpretari. Qui theologicis disciplinis in Seminariis et Studiorum Universitatibus incumbunt, cum hominibus qui in aliis scientiis excellunt, collatis viribus atque consiliis, cooperari studeant. Theologica inquisitio insimul profundam veritatis revelatae cognitionem prosequatur et coniunctionem cum proprio tempore ne negligat, ut homines variis disciplinis excultos ad pleniorem fidei scientiam iuvare possit. Haec socia opera plurimum proderit institutioni sacrorum ministrorum qui Ecclesiae doctrinam de Deo, de homine et de mundo aptius coaevis nostris explanare poterunt, ita ut verbum illud etiam libentius ab eis suscipiatur. Immo optandum ut plures laici congruam in disciplinis sacris institutionem adipiscantur, nec pauci inter eos haec studia, data opera, colant et altius producant. Ut vero munus suum exercere valeant, agnoscatur fidelibus, sive clericis sive laicis, iusta libertas inquirendi, cogitandi necnon mentem suam in humilitate et fortitudine aperiendi in iis in quibus peritia gaudent.
63 Even in economic-social life, the dignity of the human person and his whole vocation, and the good of the whole society, are to be honored and promoted. For man is the author, center, and end of all economic-social life. Modern economy, no less than other provinces of social life, is marked by a growing domination of man over nature, by denser and more intense relations and mutual dependence among citizens, groups, and peoples, and by more frequent intervention of political power. At the same time, progress in the ways of producing and exchanging goods and services has made economy a suitable instrument by which the increased needs of the human family can be better provided for. Yet there are not lacking reasons of disquiet. Not a few men, especially in economically progressed regions, seem to be ruled by the economy, so that almost their whole personal and social life is imbued with a certain economistic spirit, both in nations which favor collectivistic economy and in others. At the same time as the increase of economic life, provided it be reasonably and humanely directed and coordinated, could mitigate social inequalities, it often turns into their exasperation, or even, somewhere, into a regression of the social condition of the weak and contempt of the poor. While a huge multitude still lacks things altogether necessary, some, even in less progressed regions, live opulently or waste goods. Luxury and misery exist together. While the few enjoy the greatest power of decision, many lack almost every possibility of acting with their own initiative and responsibility, often even in conditions of life and labor unworthy of the human person. Similar defects of economic and social balance are also observed between agriculture, industry, and services, as well as between various regions of one and the same nation. A graver opposition day by day becomes between economically more progressed nations and other nations, which can call into peril the very peace of the world. Our contemporaries perceive these disparities with an ever more vivid consciousness, since they are thoroughly persuaded that the greater technical and economic capacities which the modern world enjoys can and ought to correct this unhappy state of things. Hence many reforms in economic-social life and a conversion of mind and attitude are required of all. To this, the Church has, in the course of the ages, drawn up and especially in these last times set forth the principles of justice and equity, demanded by right reason, for individual and social life, and for international life, under the light of the Gospel. The sacred Council intends to strengthen these principles according to the circumstances of this age and to set forth certain orientations, especially regarding the exigencies of economic development.

CAPUT III. DE VITA OECONOMICA - SOCIALI
CHAPTER III. ON ECONOMIC-SOCIAL LIFE
De quibusdam vitae oeconomicae aspectibus
On certain aspects of economic life
Etiam in vita oeconomica-sociali personae humanae dignitas eiusque integra vocatio, totiusque societatis bonum, honoranda atque promovenda sunt. Homo enim totius vitae oeconomicae-socialis auctor, centrum et finis est. Oeconomia hodierna, non secus atque aliae vitae socialis provinciae, crescenti super naturam hominis dominatione notatur, densioribus impensioribusque relationibus atque mutua dependentia, inter cives, coetus et populos, necnon frequentiori politicae potestatis interventione. Insimul progressus in rationibus producendi atque in bonis ac servitiis commutandis, oeconomiam aptum effecerunt instrumentum, quo adauctis familiae humanae necessitatibus melius provideri possit. Non desunt tamen rationes inquietudinis. Homines non pauci, praesertim in regionibus oeconomice progressis, re oeconomica quasi regi videntur, ita ut fere tota eorum vita personalis ac socialis spiritu quodam oeconomistico imbuatur, tam in nationibus quae oeconomiae collectivisticae favent quam in aliis. Eo temporis momento quo vitae oeconomicae incrementum, dummodo rationabiliter humaniterque dirigatur atque coordinetur, inaequalitates sociales mitigare posset, saepe saepius in earum exasperationem, vel etiam alicubi in regressum condicionis socialis debilium et in despectum pauperum vertitur. Dum rebus omnino necessariis ingens multitudo adhuc caret, aliqui, etiam in regionibus minus progressis, opulenter vivunt vel bona dissipant. Luxus et miseria simul exsistunt. Dum pauci maxima deliberandi potestate fruuntur, multi omni paene possibilitate carent propria initiativa ac responsabilitate agendi, saepe etiam in condicionibus vitae et laboris persona humana indignis versantes. Similes aequilibrii oeconomici socialisque defectus inter agriculturam, industriam ac servitia, sicut etiam inter diversas regiones unius eiusdemque nationis advertuntur. Inter nationes oeconomice magis progressas aliasque nationes gravior in dies oppositio fit, quae ipsam pacem mundi in discrimen vocare potest. Has disparitates coaevi nostri conscientia in dies vividiore persentiunt, cum eis persuasissimum sit, ampliores technicas et oeconomicas facultates, quibus mundus hodiernus gaudet, hunc infaustum statum rerum corrigere posse atque debere. Inde autem reformationes multae in vita oeconomica-sociali atque mentis et habitudinis conversio ab omnibus requiruntur. Ad hoc Ecclesia iustitiae et aequitatis principia, tam pro vita individuali et sociali, quam pro vita internationali, a recta ratione postulata, in decursu saeculorum sub luce Evangelii exaravit atque his praesertim ultimis temporibus protulit. Sacrum Concilium haec principia secundum adiuncta huius aetatis roborare orientationesque quasdam proferre intendit, exigentias progressionis oeconomicae imprimis respiciens.
64 Today, more than before, to provide for the increase of population and to satisfy the growing desires of the human race, the increase of production of agricultural and industrial goods and the provision of services is rightly intended. Therefore technical progress, the spirit of innovation, the study of creating and expanding undertakings, methods of production adapted, and the strenuous efforts of all who exercise production are to be favored: namely all those elements which serve this progression. The fundamental finality of this production, however, is not mere increase of products, nor profit or domination, but the service of man, of the whole man, regard being had for the order of his material necessities and the exigencies of his intellectual, moral, spiritual, and religious life, of man, we say, of every man, and of every group of men, of every race or region of the world. Therefore economic activity, according to its own methods and laws, is to be exercised within the bounds of the moral order, that the purpose of God concerning man may be fulfilled.

Sectio I: DE PROGRESSIONE OECONOMICA
Sectio I: ON ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
De progressione oeconomica in hominis servitium
On economic development in the service of man
Hodie, magis quam antea, ut augmento populi consulatur et crescentibus generis humani optatis satisfiat, incrementum productionis bonorum agriculturae et industriae necnon praestationis servitiorum iure intenditur. Ideo favendum est progressui technico, spiritui innovationis, studio inceptuum creandorum atque ampliandorum, methodis productionis aptandis, ac strenuis quorumcumque productionem exercentium conatibus: omnibus nempe elementis quae huic progressioni inserviunt. Huius autem productionis finalitas fundamentalis non est merum productorum incrementum, neque lucrum vel dominatus, sed hominis servitium, hominis quidem integri, attento ordine materialium eius necessitatum atque eius vitae intellectualis, moralis, spiritualis ac religiosae exigentiarum, hominis, dicimus, cuiuscumque, et cuiuscumque hominum coetus, cuiusvis stirpis vel mundi regionis. Itaque navitas oeconomica, secundum methodos et leges proprias, intra fines ordinis moralis exercenda est ita ut Dei de homine consilium adimpleatur.
65 Economic development must remain under the counsel of man; nor is it to be left to the sole arbitrariness of a few men or groups wielding too great economic power, nor to that of the political community alone, nor of certain more powerful nations. It ought, on the contrary, that at any level, as many men as possible, and when it concerns international relations, all nations, play active parts in directing it. Likewise, it is necessary that the spontaneous work of individual men and free groups be coordinated with the efforts of public authorities, and be aptly and coherently composed. Increase is to be left neither to the quasi-mechanical course of the economic activity of individuals nor to the sole power of public authority. Wherefore those doctrines are to be faulted which, under the appearance of false freedom, oppose necessary reforms, as well as those which postpone the fundamental rights of individual persons and groups to the organization of collective production. Let citizens, moreover, be mindful that it is their right and duty, also to be acknowledged by civil power, to contribute to the true progress of their own community according to their ability. Especially in economically less progressed regions, where all resources are urgently to be used, the common good is gravely called into peril by those who keep their resources unfruitful or, saving the right of personal migration, deprive their community of aids, whether material or spiritual, which it needs.

De progressione oeconomica sub consilio hominis
On economic development under the counsel of man
Progressio oeconomica sub hominis consilio manere debet; nec soli arbitrio paucorum hominum aut coetuum nimia potentia oeconomica pollentium, nec solius communitatis politicae, nec quarundam potentiorum nationum remittenda est. Oportet e contra ut, in quocumque gradu, homines quam plurimi atque, ubi de relationibus internationalibus agitur, omnes nationes in ea dirigenda activas partes habeant. Pariter opus est ut opera spontanea singulorum hominum liberorumque coetuum cum auctoritatum publicarum conatibus coordinentur atque apte et cohaerenter componantur. Incrementum nec soli cursui quasi mechanico activitatis oeconomicae singulorum nec soli potestati auctoritatis publicae relinquendum est. Quare erroris arguendae sunt, tam doctrinae quae specie falsae libertatis reformationibus necessariis obstant, quam illae quae iura fundamentalia personarum singularum et coetuum organizationi productionis collectivae postponunt. Meminerint ceterum cives suum esse ius et officium, etiam a potestate civili agnoscendum, ad verum propriae communitatis progressum pro sua possibilitate conferre. Praesertim in regionibus oeconomice minus progressis, ubi omnes opes urgenter adhibendae sunt, bonum commune graviter in discrimen vocant qui opes suas infructuosas retinent vel - salvo iure personali migrationis - communitatem suam subsidiis sive materialibus sive spiritualibus privant quibus illa eget.
66 That the exigencies of justice and equity be satisfied, it is to be strenuously striven that, the rights of persons and the proper character of each people being preserved, the great economic inequalities which now exist and often grow, together with individual and social discrimination, be removed as soon as possible. Likewise, in many regions, the peculiar difficulties of agriculture having been attended to, both in producing and in selling goods, farmers are to be helped, both to increase and sell production, and to introduce necessary developments and innovations, and to obtain a just income, lest, as often happens, they remain in the condition of citizens of inferior order. Farmers themselves, especially the young, should skillfully apply themselves to perfecting their professional skill, without which the development of agriculture cannot take place. Justice and equity likewise require that mobility, which is necessary in progressing economic affairs, be so ordered that the life of individual men and their families not become uncertain and precarious. Towards workers, however, who, coming from another nation or region, contribute by their labor to the economic progress of a people or province, any discrimination concerning conditions of remuneration or work is to be sedulously avoided. Moreover, all, especially public powers, ought to regard them not simply as mere instruments of production but as persons, and help them that they may be able to bring their family to themselves and to provide a decent habitation for themselves, and to favor their insertion into the social life of the receiving people or region. Yet, as far as possible, sources of work are to be created in their own regions. In economic affairs today subject to change, as in new forms of industrial society in which, for example, automation progresses, care is to be taken that suitable work, convenient for each, be provided, together with the possibility of fitting technical and professional formation, and that the livelihood and human dignity of those especially who suffer graver difficulties from sickness or age be placed in safety.

De ingentibus differentiis oeconomicis-socialibus removendis
On the removal of great economic-social differences
Ut exigentiis iustitiae et aequitatis satisfiat, strenue enitendum est ut, servatis iuribus personarum atque propria indole cuiusque populi, ingentes quae nunc sunt et saepe crescunt inaequalitates oeconomicae cum discriminatione individuali et sociali coniunctae, quam citius removeantur. Pariter, in pluribus regionibus, attentis peculiaribus agriculturae difficultatibus sive in gignendis sive in vendendis bonis, adiuvandi sunt ruricolae cum ad productionem augendam et vendendam, tum ad necessarias evolutiones ac innovationes inducendas, tum ad aequum redditum consequendum, ne, ut saepius accidit, in condicione civium inferioris ordinis maneant. Ipsi autem agricolae, praesertim iuvenes, sese sollerter applicent ad suam peritiam professionalem perficiendam, sine qua progressio agriculturae dari nequit. Iustitia et aequitas item requirunt ut mobilitas, quae in progredientibus rebus oeconomicis necessaria est, ita ordinetur, ne vita hominum singulorum eorumque familiarum incerta et praecaria fiat. Erga opifices vero, qui ex alia natione vel regione oriundi, ad promotionem oeconomicam populi vel provinciae labore suo conferunt, quaevis discriminatio quoad condiciones remunerationis vel laboris sedulo vitanda est. Insuper universi, imprimis publicae potestates, illos non simpliciter veluti mera productionis instrumenta sed ut personas habere debent, eosque adiuvare ut familiam suam apud se arcessere et decentem habitationem sibi providere possint, atque eorum insertioni in vitam socialem populi vel regionis recipientis favere. Attamen, quantum fieri potest, in propriis regionibus fontes laboris creentur. In rebus oeconomicis hodie mutationi obnoxiis uti in novis societatis industrialis formis in quibus e. g. automatio progreditur, curandum est ut labor sufficiens et unicuique conveniens simul ac possibilitas congruae formationis technicae et professionalis praebeatur, et in tuto collocentur victus atque dignitas humana eorum praesertim qui ob morbum vel aetatem gravioribus laborant difficultatibus.
67 Human labor, which is exercised in producing and exchanging goods or in supplying economic services, excels the other elements of economic life, which are only as instruments. For this labor, whether undertaken on one's own or hired from another, proceeds immediately from the person, who signs things of nature as it were with his own seal and subjects them to his will. By his labor, man ordinarily sustains his own and his family's life, joins with his brothers and serves them, can exercise genuine charity, and offers cooperative work to the perfection of divine creation. Indeed, by labor offered to God, we hold that man is consociated with the work of redemption of Jesus Christ, who conferred an eminent dignity on labor, when He worked with His own hands in Nazareth. Hence arises for each the duty of working faithfully and also the right to work; it is the part of society, according to the conditions prevailing in it, to help citizens for its part that they may find sufficient occasion of work. Finally, labor is to be so remunerated that it give to man the faculties to cultivate worthily his own and his family's material, social, cultural, and spiritual life, regard being had to the office and productiveness of each, as well as to the conditions of the workshop and the common good.

Sectio II: DE QUIBUSDAM PRINCIPIIS TOTAM VITAM OECONOMICAM-SOCIALEM REGENTIBUS
Sectio II: ON CERTAIN PRINCIPLES RULING THE WHOLE ECONOMIC-SOCIAL LIFE
De labore eiusque condicionibus atque de otio
On labor, its conditions, and on leisure
Labor humanus, qui in bonis gignendis ac commutandis vel in servitiis oeconomicis suppeditandis exercetur, ceteris elementis vitae oeconomicae praestat, quippe quae tantum rationem instrumentorum habeant. Hic enim labor, sive proprio marte assumatur sive ab alio conducatur, a persona immediate procedit, quae res naturae quasi suo sigillo signat easque suae voluntati submittit. Labore suo, homo ordinarie suam suorumque vitam sustentat, cum fratribus suis coniungitur eisque inservit, germanam caritatem exercere potest atque creationi divinae perficiendae sociam operam praebere. Immo, per laborem Deo oblatum, tenemus hominem ipsi redemptionis operi Iesu Christi consociari, qui praecellentem labori detulit dignitatem, cum in Nazareth propriis manibus operaretur. Exinde oritur pro unoquoque officium fideliter laborandi atque etiam ius ad laborem; societatis vero est, iuxta adiuncta in ea vigentia, pro sua parte cives adiuvare ut sufficientis laboris occasionem invenire possint. Denique ita remunerandus est labor ut homini facultates praebeantur suam suorumque vitam materialem, socialem, culturalem spiritualemque digne excolendi, spectatis uniuscuiusque munere et productivitate necnon officinae condicionibus et bono communi.
Since economic activity is generally done by the associated labor of men, it is unjust and inhuman to so compose and order it that it be to the detriment of any of the laborers. Often, however, it happens even in our days that those who work are in a certain way reduced to the servitude of their own work. Which is by no means justified by the so-called laws of economics. The whole process of fruitful work, therefore, must be accommodated to the needs of the person and his living conditions; especially his domestic life, regard always being had for sex and age. Moreover, the workers be given the faculty of developing their own qualities and their own person in the very exercise of labor. Applying their time and strength to this exercise with due responsibility, yet let all enjoy sufficient rest and leisure to cultivate family, cultural, social, and religious life. Indeed, let them have the opportunity freely to develop the forces and powers which perhaps they can little cultivate in professional labor.
¶468
Cum navitas oeconomica plerumque consociato hominum labore fiat, iniquum et inhumanum est illam ita componere et ordinare ut quibusvis laborantibus detrimento sit. Saepius autem accidit, etiam nostris diebus, ut opus facientes quodammodo in servitutem proprii operis redigantur. Quod sic dictis legibus oeconomicis nequaquam iustificatur. Universus igitur fructuosi laboris processus necessitatibus personae eiusque vitae vivendi rationibus accommodandus est; imprimis eius vitae domesticae, praesertim quod ad matres familiae spectat, respectu semper habito et sexus et aetatis. Laborantibus praebeatur insuper facultas proprias qualitates personamque suam in ipso laboris exercitio explicandi. Huic exercitio tempus viresque suas debita cum responsabilitate applicantes, tamen ad vitam familiarem, culturalem, socialem et religiosam colendam etiam sufficienti quiete et otio omnes gaudeant. Immo opportunitatem habeant vires ac potentias libere evolvendi, quas fortasse in labore professionali parum excolere valent.
68 In economic enterprises, persons are associated, namely free and independent men, created to the image of God. Therefore, the offices of each having been attended to, whether of owners, or of those who hire the work, or of directors, or of workers, and the necessary unity of the direction of the work being saved, the active participation of all is to be promoted, in the management of enterprises, in ways aptly determined. Since, however, it is often not in the enterprise itself, but higher by institutions of superior order, that decisions concerning economic and social conditions are made, on which the future lot of workers and their children depends, they should also have a part in establishing these, and indeed through themselves or through freely elected delegates. Among the fundamental rights of the human person is to be numbered the right of workers to freely form associations which can truly represent them and contribute to the right ordering of economic life, and also the right to freely participate in their activity without peril of retaliation. Through such ordered participation, together with progressing economic and social formation, the consciousness of their own office and burden will day by day increase among all, whereby they are led that, according to their capacities and aptitudes proper to them, they feel themselves partners in the whole work of economic and social development and of the procuring of the whole common good. But when economic-social conflicts arise, it is to be striven to come to a peaceful solution of them. Although, however, one must always first have recourse to sincere dialogue between the parties, a strike, also in present circumstances, can remain a necessary, if ultimate, aid for defending one's own rights and for fulfilling the just demands of workers. As soon as possible, however, let the ways to resume negotiation and conciliation dialogue be sought.

De participatione in inceptis et in universa rei oeconomicae dispositione, et de conflictibus in labore
On participation in enterprises and in the whole disposition of economic affairs, and on conflicts in labor
In inceptis oeconomicis personae consociantur, homines scilicet liberi et sui iuris, ad imaginem Dei creati. Ideo, attentis muneribus uniuscuiusque, sive proprietariorum, sive conductorum operis, sive dirigentium, sive operariorum, atque salva necessaria directionis operis unitate, promoveatur, modis apte determinandis, omnium actuosa participatio in inceptorum curatione. Cum autem saepius non iam in ipso incepto, sed altius a superioris ordinis institutis de oeconomicis et socialibus condicionibus decernatur, e quibus sors futura laborantium eorumque liberorum pendet, etiam in his statuendis partem habeant, et quidem per seipsos vel per delegatos libere electos. Inter fundamentalia personae humanae iura adnumerandum est ius pro laborantibus consociationes libere condendi, quae eos vere repraesentare et ad vitam oeconomicam recto ordine disponendam conferre possint, necnon ius earum navitatem sine ultionis periculo libere participandi. Per huiusmodi ordinatam participationem, cum progrediente formatione oeconomica et sociali iunctam, in dies augebitur apud omnes proprii muneris onerisque conscientia, qua ipsi eo adducantur ut, secundum capacitates aptitudinesque sibi proprias, totius operis progressionis oeconomicae et socialis necnon universi boni communis procurandi socios se sentiant. Cum vero conflictus oeconomici-sociales oriuntur, ut ad pacificam eorum solutionem deveniatur enitendum est. Licet autem semper praeprimis ad sincerum inter partes colloquium sit recurrendum, operistitium tamen, et in hodiernis adiunctis, ad propria iura defendenda et ad iusta laborantium quaesita implenda, adiumentum necessarium, etsi ultimum, manere potest. Quamprimum vero viae ad negotiationem et conciliationis colloquium resumendum quaerantur.
69 God destined the earth with all that is in it for the use of all men and peoples, so that created goods should flow to all in equal measure, justice leading, charity accompanying. Whatever forms of property there may be, accommodated to the legitimate institutions of peoples, according to diverse and changeable circumstances, regard must always be had to this universal destination of goods. Wherefore man, using those goods, ought to hold external things which he legitimately possesses not only as his own, but also as common, in the sense that they be able to profit not only himself but also others. Moreover, the right to have a part of goods sufficient for himself and his family belongs to all. So the Fathers and Doctors of the Church have felt, teaching that men are bound to relieve the poor, and indeed, not only from the superfluous. But he who is in extreme need has the right to procure the necessities for himself from the riches of others. Since there are so many in the world oppressed by famine, the sacred Council urges all, both individuals and authorities, mindful of that saying of the Fathers: "Feed the dying of hunger, because if you have not fed him, you have killed him," that they truly communicate and spend their goods according to the ability of each, especially supplying those aids, whether individuals or peoples, by which they themselves may be able to help and develop themselves.

De bonorum terrestrium ad universos homines destinatione
On the universal destination of earthly goods to all men
Deus terram cum omnibus quae in ea continentur in usum universorum hominum et populorum destinavit, ita ut bona creata aequa ratione ad omnes affluere debeant, iustitia duce, caritate comite. Quaecumque formae proprietatis sint, legitimis institutis populorum accommodatae, secundum diversa atque mutabilia adiuncta, ad hanc bonorum universalem destinationem semper attendendum est. Quapropter homo, illis bonis utens, res exteriores quas legitime possidet non tantum tamquam sibi proprias, sed etiam tamquam communes habere debet, eo sensu ut non sibi tantum sed etiam aliis prodesse queant. Ceterum, ius habendi partem bonorum sibi suisque familiis sufficientem omnibus competit. Ita Patres Doctoresque Ecclesiae senserunt, docentes ad pauperes sublevandos homines obligari, et quidem, non tantum ex superfluis. Qui autem in extrema necessitate degit, ius habet ut ex aliorum divitiis necessaria sibi procuret. Cum tot sint in mundo fame oppressi, Sacrum Concilium omnes sive singulos sive auctoritates urget ut, illius sententiae Patrum memores: Pasce fame morientem, quia si non pavisti occidisti, pro uniuscuiusque facultate, bona sua revera communicent et impendant, praesertim illos, sive singulos sive populos, auxiliis muniendo, quibus ipsi sese adiuvare atque evolvere possint.
In economically less progressed societies, the common destination of goods is not seldom sufficiently satisfied by customs and traditions proper to the community, by which absolutely necessary goods are provided to each member. Yet it is to be avoided that certain customs be held as altogether immovable, if they no longer respond to the new exigencies of this time; on the other hand, that one not act imprudently against honest customs, which, as long as they be aptly accommodated to present circumstances, do not cease to be very useful. Similarly, in economically highly progressed nations, a certain body of social institutions, looking to caution and security, can for its part bring the common destination of goods into act. Family and social services are to be further promoted, especially those which provide for cultivation of mind and for education. In instituting all these, it must be watched, however, that citizens not be induced to a certain inertness toward society nor reject the burden of the office undertaken and repudiate service.
¶471
In societatibus oeconomice minus progressis non raro destinationi communi bonorum ex parte per consuetudines et traditiones communitati proprias satis fit, quibus unicuique membro bona prorsus necessaria praebentur. Vitandum tamen est ne consuetudines quaedam ut omnino immutabiles habeantur, si novis exigentiis huius temporis non iam respondeant; altera vero parte, ne contra honestas consuetudines imprudenter agatur quae, dummodo hodiernis adiunctis apte accommodentur, perutiles esse non desinunt. Similiter in nationibus oeconomice valde progressis, corpus quoddam socialium institutionum, ad cautionem et securitatem spectantium, communem bonorum destinationem pro sua parte ad actum adducere potest. Ulterius promovenda sunt servitia familiaria et socialia, praesertim quae animi cultui atque educationi consulunt. In his omnibus instituendis, invigilandum tamen est ne cives ad quamdam erga societatem inertiam inducantur neve suscepti officii onus reiiciant et servitium repudient.
70 Investments, for their part, ought to tend to procure occasions of work and sufficient income both for the present people and for the future. Whoever decides concerning these investments and the ordering of economic life—whether individuals, groups, or public authorities—are bound to keep these ends before their eyes, and to acknowledge their grave obligation on the one hand to watch that the necessities for a decent life, both of individuals and of the whole community, be provided for, on the other hand to foresee the future and to constitute a just balance between the necessities of present consumption, both individual and collective, and the exigencies of investing goods for the future generation. Always also the urgent necessities of economically less progressed nations or regions must be kept before their eyes. In monetary matters, however, care is to be taken lest the good of one's own nation nor that of other nations be injured. Let it be provided, moreover, that the economically weak not suffer unjustly from changes in the value of money.
¶472
De bonorum collocationibus et de re nummaria
On investments and on money matters
Bonorum collocationes, ex sua parte, tendere debent ad occasiones laboris redditusque sufficientes tam populo hodierno quam futuro procurandos. Quicumque de his collocationibus et vitae oeconomicae ordinatione decernunt - sive singuli, sive coetus, sive auctoritates publicae -, hos fines prae oculis habere tenentur, atque gravem suam obligationem agnoscere ex una parte invigilandi, ut necessariis ad vitam decentem requisitis, sive singulorum sive totius communitatis, provideatur, ex altera parte futura praevidendi et iustum aequilibrium constituendi inter necessitates hodiernae consumptionis, sive individualis sive collectivae, et exigentias collocandi bona pro generatione ventura. Semper etiam prae oculis habeantur urgentes nationum vel regionum oeconomice minus progressarum necessitates. In re autem monetaria caveatur ne propriae nationis necnon aliarum nationum bono offendatur. Provideatur insuper ne oeconomice debiles ex valoris pecuniae immutatione iniuste detrimentum patiantur.
71 Since property and other forms of private dominion over external goods contribute to the expression of the person, and since they also give him the occasion to exercise his office in society and economy, it is very important that accession to some external dominion over goods, whether of individuals or of communities, be fostered. Private property or a certain dominion over external goods confers a quite necessary space for personal and family autonomy to each, and is to be considered as a kind of extension of human freedom. Finally, because it adds incentives to exercise office and burden, it constitutes a condition of civil liberties. The forms of such dominion or property are today various and day by day still more diversified. All, however, not with standing social funds, rights, and services provided by society, remain a cause of not-small-importance for security. Which is to be said not only of material properties, but also of immaterial goods, such as professional capacities.

De accessione ad proprietatem et dominium privatum bonorum; et de latifundiis
On accession to property and private dominion of goods; and on large estates
Cum proprietas ac aliae in bona exteriora dominii privati formae ad expressionem personae conferant, cum insuper occasionem ei praebeant suum munus in societate et oeconomia exercendi, valde interest ut, sive singolorum sive communitatum, ad quoddam bonorum exteriorum dominium accessio foveatur. Proprietas privata aut dominium quoddam in bona exteriora spatium plane necessarium ad personalem et familiarem autonomiam unicuique conferunt, et velut extensio libertatis humanae considerari debent. Demum, quia ad munus onusque exercendum stimulos addunt, condicionem quamdam libertatum civilium constituunt. Formae talis dominii vel proprietatis sunt hodie diversae et in dies adhuc magis diversificantur. Omnes autem, non obstantibus fundis socialibus, iuribus et ministeriis a societate procuratis, causa non parvipendenda securitatis manent. Quod non tantum de proprietatibus materialibus dicendum est, sed etiam de immaterialibus bonis, uti sunt capacitates professionales.
The right of private dominion, however, does not stand against that right which inheres in various forms of public property. The transfer of goods to public property, indeed, can be done only by competent authority, according to the exigencies of the common good and within its limits, just compensation having been offered. Moreover, it pertains to public authority to watch that no one abuse private property against the common good. Private property, moreover, also has a social character by its very nature, which is founded on the law of the common destination of goods. With this social character neglected, property often becomes an occasion of cupidities and grave disturbances, so that a pretext is given to impugners to call the very right into peril. In many economically less progressed regions, large or even very wide rural estates exist, moderately cultivated or remaining without any culture for profit, while the greater part of the people either lacks lands or enjoys only the smallest fields, and, on the other hand, the increase of fructification of fields clearly urgent appears. Not seldom those who are hired for labor by the lords, or who cultivate a part of their land under a lease, receive only wages or income unworthy of man, are deprived of decent habitation, and are despoiled by mediators. Lacking all security, they live under such personal servitude that almost all faculty of acting spontaneously and with responsibility is taken from them, and all promotion in human culture and all part in social and political life is forbidden to them. For various cases, therefore, reforms are necessary: that incomes increase, conditions of work be amended, security in hiring increase, and stimulus to work spontaneously be given; indeed, that fields not sufficiently cultivated be distributed to those who can render the same lands fruitful. In which case, things and means necessary are to be supplied, especially aids of education and faculties of just cooperative ordering. As often, however, as the common good has required the removal of property, compensation is to be estimated from equity, all circumstances having been weighed.
¶474
Ius autem privati dominii illi iuri non obstat quod variis formis proprietatum publicarum inest. Translatio quidem bonorum in publicam proprietatem non nisi a competenti auctoritate, iuxta boni communis exigentias et intra eius limites, aequa compensatione oblata, fieri potest. Praeterea, ad publicam auctoritatem pertinet praecavere ne quis privata proprietate contra bonum commune abutatur. Ipsa autem proprietas privata et indolem socialem natura sua habet, quae in communis destinationis bonorum lege fundatur. Qua sociali indole neglecta, proprietas multoties occasio cupiditatum et gravium perturbationum fieri accidit, ita ut ad ipsum ius in discrimen vocandum impugnatoribus praetextus detur. In pluribus regionibus oeconomice minus progressis, magni vel etiam latissimi rustici fundi existunt, mediocriter exculti vel lucri causa sine ulla cultura manentes, dum maior pars populi vel terris caret vel minimis tantum agris gaudet, atque, ex altera parte, incrementum fructificationis agrorum evidenter urgens apparet. Non raro ii qui a dominis ad laborem conducuntur, vel qui partem illorum titulo locationis colunt, nonnisi stipendium vel proventum homine indignum recipiunt, decenti habitatione privantur, necnon a mediatoribus exspoliantur. Omni securitate carentes, sub tali personali famulatu vivunt, ut fere omnis facultas sponte et cum responsabilitate agendi eis tollatur, omnisque promotio in cultu humano et omnis pars in vita sociali et politica illis prohibeantur. Pro variis igitur casibus reformationes necessariae sunt: ut crescant reditus, emendentur condiciones laboris, augeatur securitas in conductione, deturque incitamentum ad sponte operandum; immo ut distribuantur fundi non satis exculti iis qui easdem terras fructuosas reddere valeant. Quo in casu suppeditanda sunt res et media necessaria, praesertim educationis subsidia et iustae ordinationis cooperativae facultates. Quoties autem proprietatis ademptionem bonum commune exegerit, compensatio ex aequitate, omnibus adiunctis perpensis, aestimanda est.
72 Christians who play active parts in modern economic-social development and fight for justice and charity, should be persuaded that they can contribute much to the prosperity of humanity and the peace of the world. In these activities, whether individually or associated, let them shine by example. Having acquired the skill and experience altogether necessary, let them keep the right order among earthly activities, in fidelity to Christ and His Gospel, that their whole life, both individual and social, may be imbued with the spirit of the Beatitudes, notably poverty. He who, obeying Christ, first seeks the kingdom of God, from it receives a stronger and purer love, to help all his brothers and to perfect the work of justice, inspired by charity.

De navitate oeconomica-sociali et de Regno Christi
On economic-social activity and the kingdom of Christ
Christiani qui activas partes in hodierna progressione oeconomica-sociali agunt et iustitiam caritatemque propugnant, persuasum sibi habeant se multum ad humanitatis prosperitatem et mundi pacem conferre posse. In his activitatibus sive singuli sive consociati exemplo fulgeant. Acquisitis quidem peritia experientiaque omnino necessariis, inter terrestres navitates rectum ordinem servent, in fidelitate erga Christum Eiusque Evangelium, ita ut integra eorum vita, tam individualis quam socialis, spiritu Beatitudinum, notabiliter paupertatis, imbuatur. Quicumque Christo obediens, primum quaerit Regnum Dei, inde validiorem ac puriorem amorem suscipit, ad omnes fratres suos adiuvandos et ad opus iustitiae, inspirante caritate, perficiendum.
73 In our times, profound transformations are also observed in the structure and institutions of peoples, which follow their cultural, economic, and social development; which transformations exert great influence on the life of the political community, especially as concerns the rights and duties of all in the exercise of civil liberty and in attaining the common good, and in ordering the relations of citizens among themselves and with public authority. From the more vivid consciousness of human dignity arises, in various regions of the world, a zeal for establishing a political-juridical order in which the rights of the person in public life may be better protected, such as the right freely to assemble, to associate, to express one's own opinions, and to profess religion privately and publicly. For the protection of the rights of the person is a necessary condition that citizens, whether individually or associated, may be able to participate actively in the life and governance of the republic. Together with cultural, economic, and social progress, among many the desire is strengthened to assume a greater part in ordering the life of the political community. In the conscience of many, the zeal increases that the rights of minorities of any part of a nation be preserved, their duties toward the political community not being neglected; moreover, reverence day by day increases toward men professing another opinion or another religion; at the same time a wider cooperation is instituted, that all citizens, and not only a few endowed with privileges, may truly enjoy personal rights. However, whatever forms of politics in some regions are reproved, which impede civil or religious freedom, multiply the victims of cupidities and political crimes, and turn the exercise of authority from the common good to the advantage of a certain faction or of the rulers themselves. To restore truly human political life, nothing is better than to foster an interior sense of justice and benevolence and of service of the common good, and to strengthen fundamental persuasions concerning the true nature of the political community and also concerning the end, right exercise, and limits of public authority.

CAPUT IV. DE VITA COMMUNITATIS POLITICAE
CHAPTER IV. ON THE LIFE OF THE POLITICAL COMMUNITY
De hodierna vita publica
On modern public life
Nostris temporibus profundae advertuntur transformationes etiam in compage et institutionibus populorum, quae ipsorum evolutionem culturalem, oeconomicam ac socialem consequuntur; quae transformationes magnum influxum in communitatis politicae vitam exercent, praesertim quod attinet ad omnium iura et officia in libertatis civilis exercitio ac in bono communi attingendo et ad civium relationes inter se et cum publica auctoritate ordinandas. Ex vividiore humanae dignitatis conscientia exoritur, in variis mundi regionibus, studium ordinem politicum-iuridicum instaurandi, in quo personae iura in vita publica melius protegantur, ut sunt iura libere sese coadunandi, consociandi, proprias opiniones exprimendi ac religionem privatim publiceque profitendi. Tutamen enim personae iurium condicio necessaria est ut cives, sive singuli sive consociati, in rei publicae vita et moderamine actuose participare possint. Una cum progressu culturali, oeconomico ac sociali apud plures roboratur desiderium assumendi maiorem partem in communitatis politicae vita ordinanda. In multorum conscientia studium augetur ut iura minorum alicuius nationis partium serventur, earum officiis erga communitatem politicam non neglectis; insuper reverentia in dies increscit erga homines aliam opinionem vel aliam religionem profitentes; simul latior instituitur cooperatio, ut omnes cives, et non tantum quidam privilegiis ornati, iuribus personalibus reapse frui possint. Reprobantur autem quaecumque formae politicae, in aliquibus regionibus vigentes, quae libertatem civilem vel religiosam praepediunt, victimas cupiditatum et criminum politicorum multiplicant ac exercitium auctoritatis a bono communi ad commodum cuiusdam factionis vel ipsorum moderatorum detorquent. Ad vitam politicam vere humanam instaurandam nihil melius est quam interiorem iustitiae et benevolentiae ac servitii boni communis sensum fovere atque persuasiones fundamentales circa veram indolem communitatis politicae necnon circa finem, rectum exercitium et limites publicae auctoritatis corroborare.
74 Men, families, and various groups, which constitute civil society, are conscious of their own insufficiency to order a fully human life and perceive the necessity of a wider community, in which all, to ever better procure the common good, daily contribute their own strengths. Hence they constitute the political community according to various forms. The political community, therefore, exists for that common good, in which it obtains its full justification and sense, and from which it draws its own primary and proper right. The common good, however, embraces the sum of those conditions of social life by which men, families, and associations can more fully and conveniently attain their own perfection. Many and diverse, however, are the men who gather in the political community and can legitimately turn to diverse counsels. Lest, therefore, each going off into his own opinion, the political community be distracted, authority is required, which directs the strengths of all citizens to the common good, not mechanically nor despotically, but especially as a moral force, which rests on freedom and the consciousness of duty and burden undertaken. It is clear, therefore, that the political community and public authority are founded in human nature and therefore pertain to the order preestablished by God; although the determination of government and the designation of rulers are left to the free will of citizens. It follows likewise that the exercise of political authority, whether in the community as such, or in the institutions representing the republic, must always be brought to effect within the bounds of the moral order, to procure the common good—and indeed dynamically conceived—according to a juridical order legitimately established or to be established. Then citizens are bound to obey from conscience. From this, however, appears the responsibility, dignity, and importance of those who govern. But where, by public authority exceeding its competence, citizens are pressed, they themselves should not refuse what is objectively required by the common good; but it is lawful for them to defend their own and their fellow citizens' rights against abuse of this authority, preserving those limits which the natural and evangelical law delineates. The concrete ways, however, by which the political community orders its own structure and the tempering of public authority, can be various according to the different character of peoples and the progress of history; but they must always serve to form the cultivated, peaceful, and beneficent man, for the benefit of the whole human family.

De communitatis politicae natura et fine
On the nature and end of the political community
Homines, familiae et varii coetus, qui communitatem civilem constituunt, propriae insufficientiae ad vitam plene humanam instituendam conscii sunt et necessitatem amplioris communitatis percipiunt, in qua omnes, ad commune bonum semper melius procurandum, cotidie proprias vires conferant. Quapropter communitatem politicam secundum varias formas constituunt. Communitas ergo politica propter illud commune bonum exsistit, in quo suam plenam iustificationem et sensum obtinet, et ex quo ius suum primigenum et proprium depromit. Bonum vero commune summam complectitur earum vitae socialis condicionum, quibus homines, familiae et consociationes, suam ipsorum perfectionem plenius atque expeditius consequi possint. Multi autem et diversi sunt homines, qui in communitatem politicam conveniunt et legitime in diversa consilia declinare possunt. Ne igitur, unoquoque in suam sententiam abeunte, communitas politica distrahatur, auctoritas requiritur, quae omnium civium vires in bonum commune dirigat, non mechanice nec despotice, sed imprimis ut vis moralis, quae libertate et suscepti officii onerisque conscientia nititur. Patet ergo communitatem politicam et auctoritatem publicam in natura humana fundari ideoque ad ordinem a Deo praefinitum pertinere; etsi regiminis determinatio et moderatorum designatio liberae civium voluntati relinquantur. Sequitur item auctoritatis politicae exercitium sive in communitate ut tali, sive in institutis rem publicam repraesentantibus, semper intra fines ordinis moralis ad effectum deducendum esse, ad commune bonum - et quidem dynamice conceptum - procurandum, secundum ordinem iuridicum legitime statutum vel statuendum. Tunc cives ad oboedientiam praestandam ex conscientia obligantur. Exinde vero patet responsabilitas, dignitas et momentum eorum, qui praesunt. Ubi autem a publica auctoritate, suam competentiam excedente, cives premuntur, ipsi, quae a bono communi obiective postulantur, ne recusent; fas vero sit eis contra abusum huius auctoritatis sua conciviumque suorum iura defendere, illis servatis limitibus, quos lex naturalis et evangelica delineat. Modi vero concreti, quibus communitas politica propriam compagem et publicae auctoritatis temperationem ordinat, varii esse possunt secundum diversam populorum indolem et historiae progressum; semper autem ad hominem excultum, pacificum et erga omnes beneficum efformandum inservire debent, ad totius familiae humanae emolumentum.
75 It fully accords with human nature that political-juridical structures be found which give to all citizens always better and without any discrimination the effective possibility of freely and actively participating both in establishing the juridical foundations of the political community, and in the governance of the republic and in determining the fields and limits of various institutions, and in the election of rulers. Therefore let all citizens be mindful of the right and at the same time of the duty of using their free vote to promote the common good. The Church holds the work of those to be worthy of praise and consideration who, in the service of men, devote themselves to the good of the republic and undertake the burdens of this office. That the cooperation of citizens, joined with the consciousness of duty, may happily attain its effect in the daily life of the public affairs, a positive order of law is required, in which a convenient division of offices and institutions of public authority and at the same time an effective protection of rights, subject to no one, be established. The rights of all persons, families, and groups, and their exercise, are to be acknowledged, preserved, and promoted, together with the duties by which all citizens are bound. Among which duties it is necessary to be mindful of providing material and personal services of the republic, which are required for the common good. Let rulers take care that family, social, or cultural groups, bodies, or intermediate institutions not be impeded, nor be deprived of their legitimate and effective action, but rather be willing and orderly to promote them. Citizens, however, whether individually or associated, should take care not to attribute too great power to public authority, nor to inopportunely demand too many advantages and utilities from it, thus diminishing the duty and burden of persons and social groups. From the more complex circumstances of our age, public authority is often forced to intervene in social, economic, and cultural matters to introduce more apt conditions by which citizens and groups may be more effectively helped freely to pursue the whole human good. According, however, to various regions and the development of peoples, the relations between socialization and personal autonomy and progress can be understood in different ways. But where the exercise of rights is temporarily restricted for the common good, freedom, the circumstances having changed, is to be restored as soon as possible. But it is inhuman that political authority fall into totalitarian forms or into dictatorial forms which injure the rights of the person or of social groups. Citizens should cultivate piety toward the fatherland magnanimously and faithfully, yet without narrowness of mind, namely so that at the same time they always intend the good of the whole human family, which is joined by various bonds among races, peoples, and nations. All Christ's faithful should feel a special and proper vocation in the political community, by which they ought to shine by example insofar as they are bound by the consciousness of duty and serve to cultivate the common good, that they may show by deeds also how authority with freedom, personal initiative with the union and necessity of the whole social body, opportune unity with profitable diversity, are composed. Concerning ordering temporal matters, let them acknowledge legitimate, yet differing, opinions, and respect citizens, even associated, who honestly defend them. Political parties, however, should promote those things which in their judgment are required for the common good; they may never, however, prefer their own utility to the common good. Civil and political education, today very necessary both for the people and especially for the young, is to be sedulously taken care of, that all citizens may be able to play their part in the life of the political community. Those who are suitable or can become suitable for the art of politics, difficult and at the same time most noble, should prepare themselves and strive to exercise it, unmindful of their own advantage and venal benefit. Against injury and oppression, the arbitrary domination and intolerance of one man or political party, let them act with integrity of morals and prudence; but with sincerity and equity, indeed with charity and political fortitude, let them devote themselves to the good of all.

De omnium in vita publica cooperatione
On the cooperation of all in public life
Cum humana natura plene congruit ut structurae politicae-iuridicae inveniantur, quae omnibus civibus semper melius ac sine ulla discriminatione possibilitatem effectivam praebeant libere et actuose participandi tum in fundamentis iuridicis communitatis politicae statuendis, tum in rei publicae moderamine et variorum institutorum campis et finibus determinandis, tum in moderatorum electione. Memores ergo omnes cives sint iuris simul et officii suo libero suffragio utendi ad bonum commune promovendum. Ecclesia laude et consideratione dignum opus illorum habet, qui in hominum servitium rei publicae bono se devovent et huius officii onera suscipiunt. Ut civium cooperatio, cum officii conscientia coniuncta, in cotidiana publicae rei vita effectum suum felicem attingat, requiritur positivus ordo iuris, in quo conveniens divisio munerum et institutorum auctoritatis publicae atque simul efficax tuitio iurium, neminique obnoxia, instaurentur. Omnium personarum, familiarum ac coetuum iura eorumque exercitium agnoscantur, serventur et promoveantur, simul cum officiis, quibus cuncti cives obstringuntur. Inter quae officia meminisse oportet rei publicae materialia et personalia servitia praestandi, quae ad bonum commune requiruntur. Caveant moderatores ne coetus familiares, sociales aut culturales, corpora aut instituta intermedia, impediant, neve ea sua legitima et efficaci actione privent, quam potius libenter et ordinatim promovere satagant. Cives vero, sive singuli sive consociati, caveant ne nimiam potestatem publicae auctoritati tribuant, neve nimia commoda et utilitates ab ipsa inopportune postulent, ita ut personarum, familiarum necnon coetuum socialium officii onus imminuant. Ab implicatioribus nostrae aetatis adiunctis publica auctoritas saepius in res sociales et oeconomicas atque culturales se interponere cogitur ad aptiores inducendas condiciones, quibus cives ac coetus ad integrum humanum bonum libere prosequendum efficacius iuventur. Secundum autem diversas regiones et populorum evolutionem diverso modo intellegi possunt relationes inter socializationem et personae autonomiam ac progressum. Sed ubi exercitium iurium propter bonum commune ad tempus restringitur, libertas, circumstantiis mutatis, quam primum restituatur. At inhumanum est quod auctoritas politica incidat in formas totalitarias vel in formas dictatorias quae iura personae vel socialium coetuum laedant. Cives pietatem erga patriam magnanimiter et fideliter excolant, sine tamen mentis angustia, ita scilicet ut ad bonum totius familiae humanae, quae variis nexibus inter stirpes, gentes ac nationes coniungitur, semper simul animum intendant. Christifideles omnes in communitate politica specialem et propriam vocationem sentiant, qua exemplo praefulgere debent quatenus officii conscientia sunt adstricti et communi bono excolendo inserviunt, ita ut factis quoque commonstrent quomodo auctoritas cum libertate, inceptio personalis cum totius corporis socialis coniunctione ac necessitudine, unitas opportuna cum proficua diversitate componantur. Circa rem temporalem ordinandam legitimas, at inter se discrepantes, opiniones agnoscant, civesque, etiam consociatos, qui eas honeste defendunt, revereantur. Partes autem politicae ea promovere debent, quae earum iudicio ad bonum commune requiruntur; numquam vero propriam utilitatem communi bono praeponere licet. Educatio vero civilis et politica, hodie tum populo tum praesertim iuvenibus maxime necessaria, sedulo curanda est, ut omnes cives in communitatis politicae vita partes suas agere valeant. Qui idonei sunt aut fieri possunt ad artem politicam, difficilem simul et nobilissimam, sese praeparent et eam, proprii commodi et venalis beneficii immemores, exercere satagant. Contra iniuriam et oppressionem, unius hominis vel partis politicae arbitrarium dominatum et intolerantiam, integritate morum ac prudentia agant; sinceritate autem et aequitate, immo caritate et fortitudine politica, bono omnium se devoveant.
76 It is of great importance, especially where a pluralistic society flourishes, that a right respect of the relation between the political community and the Church be had, and that between those things which Christ's faithful, whether individually or associated, in their own name as citizens, led by Christian conscience, do, and those things which they do in the name of the Church together with their pastors, be clearly distinguished. The Church, which by reason of her office and competence is by no means confused with the political community, nor bound to any political system, is at once the sign and protection of the transcendence of the human person. The political community and the Church are independent and autonomous in their own field. Both, however, albeit by a different title, serve the personal and social vocation of the same men. Which service they will exercise the more effectively for the good of all, the better both cultivate sound cooperation among themselves, the circumstances of places and times being also attended to. For man is not confined to the temporal order alone, but living in human history keeps his eternal vocation wholly. The Church, however, founded in the love of the Redeemer, contributes to this, that within the boundaries of the nation and between nations justice and charity may flourish more widely. By preaching the gospel truth and illuminating all provinces of human activity through her doctrine and the testimony of Christ's faithful, she also reverences and promotes the political freedom and responsibility of citizens. The Apostles themselves and their successors and their cooperators, since they are sent to announce to men Christ the Savior of the world, in exercising their apostolate rely on the power of God, who often manifests the power of the Gospel in the weakness of witnesses. For whoever devote themselves to the ministry of the word of God ought to use the ways and means proper to the Gospel, which differ in many things from the aids of the earthly city. Earthly things indeed and those which exceed the condition of men this world are closely joined, and the Church herself uses temporal things as much as her own mission demands. She does not, however, place her hope in privileges offered by civil authority; indeed, she will renounce the exercise of certain legitimately acquired rights, when it is established that their use may call the sincerity of her testimony into doubt or that new conditions of life require another ordering. Always and everywhere, however, it be lawful for her with true freedom to preach the faith, to teach her social doctrine, to exercise her office among men expeditiously, and to make moral judgment, even concerning those things which respect the political order, when the fundamental rights of the person or the salvation of souls demand it, using all and only those aids which are congruous with the Gospel and the good of all according to the diversity of times and conditions. Faithfully adhering to the Gospel and exercising her mission in the world, the Church, whose office it is to foster and elevate whatever true, good, and beautiful is found in the human community, strengthens peace among men to the glory of God.

Communitas politica et Ecclesia
The political community and the Church
Magni momenti est, praesertim ubi societas pluralistica viget, ut rectus respectus relationis inter communitatem politicam et Ecclesiam habeatur, utque inter ea, quae christifideles, sive singuli sive consociati, suo nomine tamquam cives, christiana conscientia ducti, et ea, quae nomine Ecclesiae una cum pastoribus suis agunt, clare distinguatur. Ecclesia, quae, ratione sui muneris et competentiae, nullo modo cum communitate politica confunditur, neque ad ullum systema politicum alligatur, simul signum est et tutamentum transcendentiae humanae personae. Communitas politica et Ecclesia in proprio campo ab invicem sunt independentes et autonomae. Ambae autem, licet diverso titulo, eorumdem hominum vocationi personali et sociali inserviunt. Quod servitium eo efficacius in omnium bonum exercebunt, quo ambae melius sanam cooperationem inter se colunt, attentis quoque locorum temporumque adiunctis. Homo enim ad solum ordinem temporalem non coarctatur, sed in historia humana vivens aeternam suam vocationem integre servat. Ecclesia vero, in Redemptoris amore fundata, ad hoc confert ut intra nationis terminos et inter nationes iustitia et caritas latius vigeant. Evangelicam veritatem praedicando atque omnes navitatis humanae provincias, per suam doctrinam et testimonium a christifidelibus exhibitum, illuminando, etiam politicam civium libertatem et responsabilitatem reveretur atque promovet. Apostoli ipsorumque successores et horum cooperatores, cum mittantur ut hominibus Christum mundi Salvatorem annuntient, in suo apostolatu exercendo Dei potentia innituntur, qui persaepe in testium infirmitate virtutem Evangelii manifestat. Quicumque enim Dei verbi ministerio se devovent, utantur oportet viis et subsidiis Evangelio propriis, quae in pluribus a terrenae civitatis subsidiis differunt. Res quidem terrenae et ea, quae in hominum condicione hunc mundum exsuperant, arcte inter se iunguntur, et ipsa Ecclesia rebus temporalibus utitur quantum propria eius missio id postulat. Spem vero suam in privilegiis ab auctoritate civili oblatis non reponit; immo quorundam iurium legitime acquisitorum exercitio renuntiabit, ubi constiterit eorum usu sinceritatem sui testimonii vocari in dubium aut novas vitae condiciones aliam exigere ordinationem. Semper autem et ubique ei fas sit cum vera libertate fidem praedicare, socialem suam doctrinam docere, munus suum inter homines expedite exercere necnon iudicium morale ferre, etiam de rebus quae ordinem politicum respiciunt, quando personae iura fundamentalia aut animarum salus id exigant, omnia et sola subsidia adhibendo, quae Evangelio et omnium bono secundum temporum et condicionum diversitatem congruant. Fideliter Evangelio adhaerens et suam missionem in mundo exercens, Ecclesia, cuius est, quidquid verum, bonum et pulchrum in communitate humana invenitur, fovere ac elevare, pacem inter homines ad Dei gloriam roborat.
77 In these our years, in which the miseries and anguishes flowing from rampant or impending war still persist most grievously among men, the whole human family has come to a moment of highest crisis in its process of maturity. Gradually gathered into one and now more conscious of its unity everywhere, it can not accomplish the work incumbent on it, namely to build a truly more humane world for all men everywhere, unless all turn to the truth of peace with renewed spirit. Hence it is that the gospel message, being congruent with the deeper studies and desires of the human race, shines with new clarity in our times, since it proclaims the peacemakers blessed, because they shall be called the children of God (Mt 5:9). Therefore the Council, illustrating the true and most noble reason of peace, and condemning the immanity of war, intends fervently to call upon Christians that, with the help of Christ the author of peace, they cooperate with all men to establish peace among them in justice and love and to prepare the instruments of peace.

CAPUT V. DE PACE FOVENDA ET DE COMMUNITATE GENTIUM PROMOVENDA
CHAPTER V. ON FOSTERING PEACE AND PROMOTING THE COMMUNITY OF NATIONS
Introductio
Hisce nostris annis, quibus aerumnae et angustiae ex grassante vel impendente bello profluentes adhuc gravissimae inter homines perdurant, universa familia humana ad horam summi discriminis in suae maturitatis processu pervenit. In unum paulatim congregata atque ubivis suae unitatis melius iam conscia, opus quod ei incumbit, mundum scilicet pro omnibus hominibus ubique terrarum vere humaniorem aedificandi, peragere nequit nisi cuncti ad veritatem pacis renovato animo convertantur. Hinc fit ut evangelicum nuntium, cum altioribus generis humani studiis atque optatis congruens, nostris temporibus nova claritate elucescat dum pacis artifices beatos proclamat, quoniam filii Dei vocabuntur (Mt 5,9). Ideo Concilium veram et nobilissimam pacis rationem illustrans, belli immanitate damnata, christianos ferventer evocare intendit ut, auxiliante Christo auctore pacis, cum omnibus hominibus ad pacem in iustitia et amore inter eos firmandam et ad instrumenta pacis apparanda cooperentur.
78 Peace is not a mere absence of war, nor is it reduced to the stable equilibrium of opposing forces, nor does it arise from imperious domination, but it is rightly and properly said to be a work of justice (Is 32:17). It is the fruit of the order of human society insufflated by its divine Founder and brought into act by men always thirsting for more perfect justice. For since the common good of the human race is ruled in its primary notion by the eternal law, but, as to what it concretely demands, is subject to unceasing changes as time progresses, peace is never acquired once for all, but is perpetually to be built. Since, moreover, the human will is slippery and wounded by sin, the procuring of peace demands constant domination of passions and vigilance of legitimate authority. This, however, does not suffice. This peace on earth cannot be obtained unless the good of persons be placed in safety and men spontaneously communicate the riches of their soul and genius to one another with confidence. A firm will to reverence other men and peoples and their dignity, and a zealous exercise of fraternity, are altogether necessary to build peace. Thus peace is also the fruit of love, which goes beyond what justice can do. Earthly peace, however, which arises from love of neighbor, is the figure and effect of the peace of Christ, proceeding from God the Father. For He, the incarnate Son, the prince of peace, reconciled all men to God by His cross, and, restoring the unity of all in one People and one Body, destroyed hatred in His own flesh, and, exalted by resurrection, poured the Spirit of love into the hearts of men. Wherefore all Christians are earnestly called to, doing truth in charity (Eph 4:15), unite themselves with truly peaceful men to implore and restore peace. Moved by the same spirit, we cannot but praise those who, in vindicating rights, renouncing violent action, have recourse to means of defense which are otherwise available to even the weaker, provided this can be done without injury to the rights and obligations of others or of the community. Insofar as men are sinners, the peril of war threatens them, and will threaten until the coming of Christ; insofar, however, as they, joined in charity, overcome sin, they overcome violence as well, until the word be fulfilled: "And they shall beat their swords into ploughshares, and their spears into pruning hooks. Nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they be exercised any more to war" (Is 2:4).

De natura pacis
On the nature of peace
Pax non est mera absentia belli, neque ad solum adversarum virium aequilibrium stabiliendum reducitur neque ex imperioso dominatu oritur, sed recte proprieque dicitur opus iustitiae (Is 32,7). Fructus exsistit ordinis humanae societati a divino suo Fundatore insiti et ab hominibus perfectiorem semper iustitiam sitientibus in actum deducendi. Cum enim generis humani bonum commune primaria quidem sua ratione lege aeterna regatur, sed, quoad id quod concrete exigit, progrediente tempore incessantibus mutationibus subiciatur, numquam pax pro semper acquisita est, sed perpetuo aedificanda. Cum insuper labilis sit humana voluntas necnon peccato sauciata, procuratio pacis constantem uniuscuiusque exposcit passionum dominationem et legitimae auctoritatis invigilantiam. Hoc tamen non sufficit. Pax haec in terris obtineri non potest nisi bonum personarum in tuto collocetur et homines cum fiducia divitias sui animi atque ingenii inter se sponte communicent. Firma voluntas alios homines et populos eorumque dignitatem reverendi studiosumque fraternitatis exercitium ad pacem construendam omnino necessaria sunt. Ita pax fructus etiam amoris exsistit, qui ultra ea progreditur quae iustitia praestare valet. Pax autem terrena, quae ex dilectione proximi oritur, figura et effectus est pacis Christi, a Deo Patre promanantis. Ipse enim Filius incarnatus, princeps pacis, per crucem suam omnes homines Deo reconciliavit ac, restituens omnium unitatem in uno Populo et uno Corpore, in propria sua carne occidit odium, et, resurrectione exaltatus, Spiritum caritatis in corda hominum diffudit. Quapropter omnes christiani enixe evocantur ut, veritatem facientes in caritate (Ep 4,15), cum hominibus vere pacificis sese uniant ad pacem implorandam et instaurandam. Eodem spiritu moti, non possumus non laudare eos, qui in iuribus vindicandis actioni violentae renuntiantes, ad media defensionis recurrunt quae ceteroquin etiam debilioribus praesto sunt, dummodo hoc sine laesione iurium et obligationum aliorum vel communitatis fieri possit. Quatenus homines peccatores sunt, eis imminet periculum belli, et usque ad adventum Christi imminebit; quatenus autem, caritate coniuncti, peccatum superant, superantur et violentiae, donec impleatur verbum: Conflabunt gladios suos in vomeres et lanceas suas in falces. Non levabit gens contra gentem gladium, nec exercebuntur ultra ad praelium (Is 2,4).
79 Although recent wars have inflicted most grievous damages, both material and moral, on our world, war still pursues its devastations daily in some part of the world. Indeed, while scientific weapons of every kind are used in war, its cruel character threatens to lead combatants to barbarism which far surpasses that of past times. Moreover, the complexity of the present condition and the intrication of relations between nations permit that, by new methods, insidious and subversive, disguised wars be prolonged. In many circumstances, the use of terrorism methods is held as a new manner of waging war. Having before his eyes this dejected state of humanity, the Council intends first of all to call to mind the permanent force of the natural law of nations and its universal principles. The very conscience of the human race more and more firmly proclaims these principles. Actions, therefore, which deliberately oppose them, and commands which prescribe such actions, are criminal, nor can blind obedience excuse those who obey them. Among these actions, those are first to be counted by which, by a certain reason and method, a whole people, nation, or ethnic minority are exterminated: which as horrendous crimes are to be vehemently condemned. But most to be approved is the spirit of those who are not afraid openly to resist those commanding such things. There exist various international conventions concerning wars to which many nations have subscribed, to make military actions and their consequences less inhuman: such are the conventions which concern the lot of wounded or captive soldiers, and various stipulations of this kind. These pacts are to be kept; indeed, all, especially public authorities and those skilled in these matters, are bound to strive as much as they can that they be perfected and thus better and more effectively contribute to restraining the immanity of wars. Moreover, it seems equitable that laws humanely provide for the case of those who, for reason of conscience, refuse to use arms, while nevertheless accepting another form of service to the community of men. War, of course, has not been eradicated from human things. But as long as the peril of war shall remain, and the competent international authority be lacking, so long, all peaceful means of negotiation having indeed been exhausted, the right of legitimate defense cannot be denied to governments. It therefore pertains to the rulers of states and to others who share the responsibility of the republic, gravely conducting such grave matters, to protect the safety of the peoples entrusted to them. But it is one thing to wage military affairs to defend a people justly, another to wish to subjugate other nations. Nor does military power make all its military or political use legitimate. Nor, war having already unfortunately broken out, by that very fact all things become lawful between the adverse parties.

Sectio I: DE BELLO VITANDO
Sectio I: ON AVOIDING WAR
De bellorum immanitate refrenanda
On restraining the immanity of wars
Quamvis recentia bella nostro mundo gravissima damna tum materialia tum moralia intulerint, adhuc cotidie in aliqua terrarum parte bellum suas vastationes persequitur. Immo, dum arma scientifica cuiuslibet generis in bello adhibentur, saeva eius indoles proeliantes ad barbariem adducere minatur quae illam anteactorum temporum longe superet. Porro condicionis hodiernae complexitas ac relationum inter nationes intricatio permittunt ut novis methodis, iisque insidiosis et subversivis, bella larvata protrahantur. In pluribus adiunctis usus methodorum terrorismi tamquam nova ratio bellandi habetur. Deiectum istum humanitatis statum prae oculis habens, Concilium ante omnia in memoriam revocare intendit permanentem vim iuris naturalis gentium eiusque principiorum universalium. Ipsa generis humani conscientia haec principia firmiter magis magisque proclamat. Actiones ergo quae iisdem deliberate adversantur necnon iussa quibus tales actiones praescribuntur scelesta sunt, nec caeca oboedientia illos qui iis parent excusare valet. Inter has actiones illae ante omnia recensendae sunt quibus, ratione quadam et methodo, universa gens, natio aut minoritas ethnica exterminantur: quae tamquam crimina horrenda vehementer condemnandae sunt. Maxime vero probandus est animus illorum qui talia praecipientibus aperte resistere non timescunt. Exstant de rebus bellicis variae conventiones internationales quibus sat multae nationes subscripserunt, ut minus inhumanae efficiantur actiones militares earumque sequelae: huiusmodi sunt conventiones quae pertinent ad militum vulneratorum aut captivorum sortem, variaeque huius generis stipulationes. Quae pactiones servandae sunt; immo tenentur omnes, praesertim auctoritates publicae et de his rebus periti, quantum possunt conari ut illae perficiantur sicque melius et efficacius ad bellorum immanitatem refrenandam conducant. Insuper aequum videtur ut leges humaniter provideant pro casu illorum qui ex motivo conscientiae arma adhibere recusant, dum tamen aliam formam communitati hominum serviendi acceptant. Utique bellum non est e rebus humanis eradicatum. Quamdiu autem periculum belli aderit, auctoritasque internationalis competens congruisque viribus munita defuerit, tamdiu, exhaustis quidem omnibus pacificae tractationis subsidiis, ius legitimae defensionis guberniis denegari non poterit. Civitatum rectoribus aliisque qui rei publicae responsabilitatem participant, incumbit igitur officium ut populorum sibi commissorum salutem tueantur, res tam graves graviter gerentes. At aliud est res militares gerere ut populi iuste defendantur, aliud alias nationes subiugare velle. Nec potentia bellica omnem eiusdem militarem vel politicum usum legitimum facit. Nec bello infeliciter iam exorto, eo ipso omnia inter partes adversas licita fiunt.
Those who, devoted to the service of the fatherland, are in the army, should also hold themselves as ministers of the security and liberty of peoples, and, while they rightly exercise this office, truly contribute to establishing peace. But the horror and perversity of war are immensely increased by the growth of scientific arms. For military actions, using these arms, can cause immense indiscriminate destructions which therefore far exceed the limits of legitimate defense. Indeed, if these means, such as are already found in the arsenals of great nations, should be thoroughly used, from this the almost full and entirely reciprocal slaughter of each side would be had, to say nothing of the many devastations arising in the world and the deadly effects following from the use of such arms. All which force us to institute an examination of war with an altogether new mind. Let the men of this age know that they will render a grave account concerning their military actions. For from their present counsels the course of future times will much depend. These things having been attended to, this sacrosanct Synod, making its own the condemnations of total war already enunciated by recent Roman Pontiffs, declares: Every military action which tends to the indiscriminate destruction of whole cities or large regions with their inhabitants is a crime against God and against man himself, which is to be firmly and unhesitatingly condemned. The particular peril of modern war consists in this, that it offers an occasion to those who possess more recent scientific arms to perpetrate such crimes, and, by a certain inexorable connection, can impel human wills to most atrocious counsels. Lest this ever happen in the future, the bishops of the whole world gathered together, beseech all, especially the rulers of nations and those who are in charge of military affairs, that they unceasingly consider so great a responsibility before God and before the whole of humanity.

Qui vero, patriae servitio addicti, in exercitu versantur, et ipsi tamquam securitatis libertatisque populorum ministros sese habeant, et, dum hoc munere recte funguntur, vere ad pacem stabiliendam conferunt. Horror pravitasque belli scientificorum armorum incremento in immensum augentur. Bellicae enim actiones, his armis adhibitis, ingentes indiscriminatasque inferre possunt destructiones quae proinde limites legitimae defensionis longe excedunt. Immo, si haec media, qualia iam in magnarum nationum armamentariis inveniuntur, penitus adhiberentur, ex eo internecio fere plena et omnino reciproca uniuscuiusque partis a parte adversa haberetur praetermissis multis vastationibus in mundo oboriundis et exitialibus effectibus ex usu huiusmodi armorum consequentibus. Quae omnia cogunt nos ut de bello examen mente omnino nova instituamus. Sciant huius aetatis homines se de suis bellicis actionibus gravem rationem esse reddituros. Ab eorum enim hodiernis consiliis temporum futurorum decursus multum pendebit. His attentis, haec Sacrosancta Synodus, suas faciens condemnationes belli totalis iam a recentibus Summis Pontificibus enuntiatas, declarat: Omnis actio bellica quae in urbium integrarum vel amplarum regionum cum earum incolis destructionem indiscriminatim tendit, est crimen contra Deum et ipsum hominem, quod firmiter et incunctanter damnandum est. Singulare belli hodierni periculum in hoc consistit quod illis qui recentiora arma scientifica possident quasi occasionem praebet talia scelera perpetrandi et, connexione quadam inexorabili, hominum voluntates ad atrocissima consilia impellere potest. Ne vero hoc in futurum unquam eveniat, Episcopi totius orbis terrarum in unum congregati, omnes, nationum moderatores praesertim, necnon eos qui rei militari praesunt, obsecrant, ut tantam responsabilitatem coram Deo et coram universa humanitate incessanter perpendant.
81 Scientific arms are indeed accumulated not only to be used in time of war. For since the firmness of the defense of each side is thought to depend on the fulminant capacity to strike back at the adversary, this accumulation of arms, which grows worse every year, serves in an unusual way to deter possible insurgent adversaries. Which is held by many as the most effective of all means by which a certain peace among nations can now be placed in safety. Whatever may be of that dissuasive manner, let men be persuaded that the race to armaments, to which many nations have recourse, is not a secure way to keep peace firmly, nor is the so-called equilibrium resulting from that a certain and true peace. The causes of war from it, far from being eliminated, rather threaten to gradually become more severe. While abundant riches are spent on ever new armaments, a sufficient remedy for so many miseries of the whole world cannot be provided. Rather than truly and radically healing dissensions between nations, other parts of the world are infected by them. New ways, beginning from a reformed spirit, will have to be chosen to remove this scandal, and, the world being freed from the anxiety by which it is oppressed, true peace can be restored. Wherefore it must again be declared: the race to armaments is a most grave plague of humanity, and intolerably injures the poor. But it is greatly to be feared that, if it continues, it will at some time bring forth deadly calamities, the means of which it already prepares. Admonished by the calamities which the human race has made possible, let us use the respite granted to us from above, that, more conscious of our own responsibility, we may find ways by which we may be able to compose our controversies in a manner more worthy of man. Divine Providence urgently requires us to free ourselves from the ancient servitude of war. But if we refuse to make such an effort, we know not whither we are led on this evil way which we have entered.

De cursu ad arma apparanda
On the race to armaments
Arma quidem scientifica non ad hoc unice accumulantur ut tempore belli adhibeantur. Cum enim firmitas uniuscuiusque partis defensionis a capacitate fulminea adversarium repercutiendi dependere aestimetur, haec armorum accumulatio, quae quotannis ingravescit, inconsueto quidem modo ad deterrendos adversarios forte insurgentes inservit. Quod a multis habetur tamquam omnium mediorum efficacissimum quibus nunc pax quaedam inter nationes in tuto poni possit. Quidquid sit de illo dissuasioni modo, persuasum habeant homines cursum ad arma apparanda, ad quem sat multae nationes confugiunt, non securam esse viam ad pacem firmiter servandam, neque sic dictum aequilibrium ex illo manans certam ac veram esse pacem. Belli exinde causae quin eliminentur, potius paulatim aggravari minantur. Dum in arma semper nova apparanda perabundantes divitiae erogantur, tot miseriis hodiernis mundi universi remedium sufficiens praeberi non potest. Potius quam dissensiones inter nationes vere ac funditus sanentur, iisdem aliae mundi partes inficiuntur. Novae viae, ex reformato animo initium sumentes, eligendae erunt ut hoc scandalum removeatur et, mundo ab anxietate qua opprimitur liberato, vera pax restitui possit. Quapropter denuo declarandum est: cursum ad arma apparanda gravissimam plagam humanitatis esse, ac pauperes intolerabiliter laedere. Valde autem timendum est ne, si perduret, aliquando omnes exitiales clades pariat, quarum media iam praeparat. Calamitatibus commonefacti quas humanum genus possibiles effecit, moram, nobis desuper concessam, qua gaudemus, adhibeamus ut propriae responsabilitatis magis conscii, vias inveniamus quibus controversias nostras modo homine digniore componere possimus. Providentia divina a nobis instanter requirit ut nosmetipsos ab antiqua belli servitute liberemus. Quod si huiusmodi conamen facere renuerimus, quo ducamur in hac via mala quam ingressi sumus, nescimus.
82 It is clear, therefore, that we must strive with all our strength to prepare times in which, with the consent of nations, any war whatsoever can be altogether forbidden. Which indeed requires that some universal public authority, acknowledged by all, be instituted, which may have effective power to place safety, observance of justice, and reverence for rights in safety for all. But before such a desirable authority can be instituted, it is necessary that the present supreme international bodies ardently devote themselves to the studies of means more apt for procuring common security. Since peace must arise from mutual trust of nations rather than be imposed on nations by the terror of arms, all must strive that the race to armaments finally have an end; that the diminution of arms really begin, not unilaterally indeed, but proceeding step by step from agreement, furnished with true and effective cautions. In the meantime, the attempts which have already been made and are still being made to remove the peril of war are not to be made little of. Rather, the good will of very many is to be helped, who, burdened with the greatest cares of their highest offices, moved by the most grave duty by which they are bound, strive to eliminate the war they abhor, even if they cannot prescind from the complexity of things as they are. But we must earnestly pray to God that He give them the strength to persevere in undertaking and to fortify in perfecting this work of highest love of men, by which peace is manfully built. Which today most certainly requires that they extend their mind and spirit beyond the boundaries of their own nation, lay down national selfishness and ambition to dominate other nations, and nourish a deep reverence for the whole of humanity, which is already so laboriously hastening to its greater unity. Concerning the problems of peace and disarmament, investigations already strenuously and unceasingly protracted, and international congresses which have dealt with this matter, are to be considered as first steps to solving so grave questions, and are to be more urgently promoted in the future to obtain practical effects. Nevertheless, let men take care not to commit themselves only to the efforts of some, without taking care of their own minds. For the rulers of peoples, who are sponsors of the common good of their own nation and at the same time promoters of the good of the whole world, depend most greatly on the opinions and feelings of the multitudes. Nothing profits them to strive to build peace, as long as feelings of hostility, contempt, and distrust, racial hatreds, and obstinate ideologies divide men and oppose them to one another. Hence the greatest necessity urgently demands a renewed education of minds and a new inspiration in public opinion. Those who devote themselves to the work of education, especially of the young, or to form public opinion, should regard as a most grave office the care of instructing the minds of all to new peaceful senses. We all must indeed change our hearts, looking to the whole world and to those offices which we, together, can do to make our race advance to better things. Nor let false hope deceive us. Unless, enmities and hatreds having been laid aside, firm and honest pacts of universal peace be concluded in the future, humanity, which is already in grave crisis, although endowed with wonderful science, may perhaps be led fatally to that hour in which it may experience no other peace than the horrendous peace of death. Nevertheless, while uttering this, the Church of Christ, placed in the midst of the anxiety of this time, does not cease most firmly to hope. To our age she intends to propose, opportunely and importunely, the apostolic message: "Behold, now is the acceptable time" for hearts to be changed, "behold, now is the day of salvation."

De bello omnino interdicendo et actione internationali ad bellum vitandum
On war to be altogether forbidden and on international action to avoid war
Patet ergo nobis enitendum esse ut viribus omnibus tempora praeparemus quibus, consentientibus nationibus, bellum quodlibet omnino interdici possit. Quod sane requirit ut quaedam publica auctoritas universalis, ab omnibus agnita, instituatur, quae efficaci potestate polleat ut pro omnibus tum securitas, tum iustitiae observantia, tum iurium reverentia in tuto ponantur. Antequam vero haec optanda auctoritas institui possit, opus est ut hodierna suprema gremia internationalia studiis mediorum ad securitatem communem procurandam aptiorum acriter se dedicent. Cum pax e mutua gentium fiducia nasci oporteat potius quam armorum terrore nationibus imponi, omnibus adlaborandum est ut cursus ad arma apparanda finem tandem habeat; ut diminutio armorum re incipiat, non unilateraliter quidem sed pari passu ex condicto progrediatur, veris efficacibusque cautionibus munita. Interea non parvipendenda sunt conamina quae iam facta sunt et adhuc fiunt ut periculum belli amoveatur. Potius adiuvanda est bona voluntas permultorum qui, ingentibus suorum supremorum munerum curis onerati, gravissimo autem quo obstringuntur officio moti, bellum quod abhorrent eliminare satagunt, etiamsi a complexitate rerum quales sunt, praescindere non possunt. Deum autem enixe rogare oportet ut illis vim det perseveranter aggrediendi ac fortiter perficiendi hoc summi amoris hominum opus quo pax viriliter aedificatur. Quod hodie certissime ab eis exigit ut mentem et spiritum ultra fines propriae nationis extendant, egoismum nationalem atque ambitionem aliis nationibus dominandi deponant, profundamque reverentiam erga totam humanitatem nutriant, quae iam ad maiorem sui unitatem tam laboriose properat. Circa pacis et armorum depositionis problemata perscrutationes iam strenue et indesinenter protractae, internationalesque congressus, qui hac de re egerunt, tamquam primi passus ad solvendas tantopere graves quaestiones consideranda sunt atque urgentiori modo ad obtinendos effectus practicos in futurum promovenda. Nihilominus caveant homines ne solummodo conatibus quorumdam se committant, quin de propriis mentibus curent. Nam populorum moderatores, qui boni communis propriae gentis sponsores sunt et simul boni universi orbis promotores, ex multitudinum opinionibus et animi sensibus quam maxime pendent. Nihil eis prodest ut paci aedificandae instent, quamdiu hostilitatis, contemptus et diffidentiae sensus, racialia odia necnon obstinatae ideologiae, homines dividunt atque inter se opponunt. Hinc maxima necessitas urget renovatae mentium educationis novaeque in publica opinione inspirationis. Qui operi educationis se devovent, praesertim iuvenum, aut opinionem publicam efformant, tamquam gravissimum officium reputent curam mentium omnium ad novos pacificos sensus instituendi. Nos omnes quidem commutare corda nostra oportet, universum orbem et illa munera prospicientes quae nos, una simul, agere possumus ut genus nostrum ad meliora proficiat. Nec falsa spes nos decipiat. Nisi enim, dimissis inimicitiis et odiis, firma honestaque pacta de pace universali in futuro concludantur, humanitas quae iam in gravi discrimine versatur, quamvis scientia mirabili praedita, forsan funeste ad illam horam adducetur in qua non aliam pacem quam horrendam mortis pacem experiatur. Attamen, dum haec profert, Ecclesia Christi, in media anxietate huius temporis constituta, firmissime sperare non cessat. Aetati nostrae iterum iterumque, opportune importune, nuntium apostolicum proponere intendit: ecce nunc tempus acceptabile ut mutentur corda, ecce nunc dies salutis.
83 To build peace, first of all the causes of discords between men, by which wars are nourished, especially injustices, are to be eradicated. Not a few of them arise from excessive economic inequalities, and also from retarded necessary remedies. Others, however, arise from the spirit of domination and contempt of persons, and, if we inquire into deeper causes, from human envy, distrust, pride, and other selfish passions. Since man cannot bear so many defects of order, from this it follows that, even war not raging, the world is unceasingly infected with contentions and violences between men. Since, moreover, the same evils are also found in relations between nations themselves, it is altogether necessary that, to overcome or prevent them, and to restrain unbridled violences, international institutions cooperate and coordinate better and more firmly, and the creation of organisms to promote peace be unceasingly stimulated.

Sectio II: DE COMMUNITATE INTERNATIONALI AEDIFICANDA
Sectio II: ON BUILDING THE INTERNATIONAL COMMUNITY
De causis discordiarum earumque remediis
On the causes of discords and their remedies
Ad pacem aedificandam ante omnia requiritur ut eradicentur causae discordiarum inter homines, quibus bella aluntur, praesertim iniustitiae. Non paucae earum ex nimiis inaequalitatibus oeconomicis proveniunt, necnon ex necessariis remediis retardatis. Aliae vero ex spiritu dominationis et personarum contemptu oriuntur et, si in causas profundiores inquirimus, ex humana invidia, diffidentia, superbia aliisque egoisticis passionibus. Cum tot ordinis defectus homo ferre non possit, ex iis consequitur ut, etiam bello non saeviente, mundus indesinenter contentionibus inter homines et violentiis inficiatur. Cum insuper eadem mala in relationibus inter ipsas nationes inveniantur, necessarium omnino est ut, ad illa vincenda vel praevenienda, et ad effrenatas violentias coercendas, melius et firmius cooperentur et coordinentur institutiones internationales necnon indefesse stimuletur creatio organismorum qui pacem promoveant.
84 So that, the close bonds of mutual necessity increasing in this time between all citizens and all peoples of the world, the universal common good may be aptly sought and more effectively obtained, it is already necessary that the community of nations constitute for itself an order which is congruous with present offices, especially relating to those many regions which still suffer intolerable poverty. To achieve these ends, the institutions of the international community must for their part provide for various needs of men, both in the fields of social life to which pertain livelihood, health, education, labor, and in certain particular conditions which can arise elsewhere, such as the necessity of favoring the general development of progressing nations, of meeting the miseries of refugees scattered through the whole world, or of helping migrants and their families. International institutions, universal or regional, which already exist, certainly well deserve of the human race. They appear as first attempts to lay international foundations of the whole human community to solve the most grave questions of our times, and indeed to promote progress everywhere and to prevent wars in any form. In all these fields, the Church rejoices in the spirit of true fraternity flourishing between Christians and non-Christians which strives that efforts always be intensified to relieve immense misery.

De communitate gentium et de institutionibus internationalibus
On the community of nations and on international institutions
Ut, crescentibus hoc tempore arctis mutuae necessitudinis nexibus inter omnes cives et omnes populos orbis terrarum, bonum commune universale apte quaeratur et efficacius obtineatur, iam necesse est communitatem gentium sibi constituere ordinem qui cum hodiernis muneribus congruat, praesertim relate ad illas numerosas regiones quae intolerabilem egestatem adhuc patiuntur. Ad hos fines assequendos, institutiones communitatis internationalis variis hominum necessitatibus pro sua parte providere debent, tam in vitae socialis campis ad quos pertinent victus, sanitas, educatio, labor, quam in nonnullis condicionibus particularibus quae alicubi oriri possunt, ut sunt necessitas generali nationum progredientium incremento fovendi, aerumnis profugorum per universum mundum dispersorum occurrendi, vel etiam migrantes eorumque familias adiuvandi. Institutiones internationales, universales vel regionales, quae iam exsistunt certe de genere humano bene merentur. Eaedem tamquam primi conatus apparent internationalia fundamenta totius communitatis humanae ponendi ut gravissimae nostrorum temporum quaestiones solvantur, et quidem ad progressum ubique terrarum promovendum et ad bella in quacumque forma praecavenda. In omnibus istis campis gaudet Ecclesia de spiritu verae fraternitatis inter christianos et non christianos florentis qui enititur ut conamina semper intensiora fiant ad ingentem miseriam sublevandam.
85 The present conjunction of the human race also demands the restoration of a greater international cooperation in the economic field. For, although almost all peoples have become independent, they are far, however, from being already free from excessive inequalities and from all form of undue dependence, nor do they altogether avoid the peril of grave internal difficulties. The development of any nation depends on human and pecuniary aids. The citizens of each nation are to be prepared by education and professional formation for assuming various offices of economic and social life. To this, however, the aid of foreign experts is required, who, while they give help, should not conduct themselves as masters but as helpers and cooperators. Material aid to progressing nations will not be procured unless the customs of present commerce in the world be profoundly changed. Other aids besides are to be provided by progressed nations in the form of gifts or loans or investments of money; which are to be provided with generosity and without cupidity on the one hand, received with all honesty on the other. To restore a true universal economic order, excessive desires of profit, national ambitions, the appetite for political domination, military calculations, and machinations to propagate and impose ideologies are to be abolished. Many economic and social systems are proposed; it is to be desired that in these the skilled find common foundations of sound world commerce; which will happen more easily if each rejects his own prejudices and makes himself ready to sincerely pursue dialogue.

De cooperatione internationali in campo oeconomico
On international cooperation in the economic field
Hodierna generis humani coniunctio etiam instaurationem maioris cooperationis internationalis in campo oeconomico expostulat. Etenim, etsi omnes fere populi sui iuris facti sunt, longe tamen abest ut a nimiis inaequalitatibus et ab omni indebitae dependentiae forma iam liberi sint omneque gravium difficultatum internarum periculum effugiant. Incrementum alicuius nationis ex adiumentis humanis et pecuniariis pendet. Cives uniuscuiusque nationis per educationem et formationem professionalem ad varia munera vitae oeconomicae et socialis obeunda praeparandi sunt. Ad hoc autem requiritur auxilium peritorum alienigenarum qui, dum opem ferunt, non ut dominatores se gerant sed ut adiutores et cooperatores. Auxilium materiale nationibus progredientibus non procurabitur, nisi consuetudines hodierni commercii in mundo profunde mutentur. Alia insuper auxilia a nationibus progressis praestanda sunt sub forma sive donorum sive mutuorum sive pecuniarum collocationum; quae praestentur cum generositate et sine cupiditate ex una parte, accipiantur cum omni honestate ex altera. Ad verum ordinem oeconomicum universalem instaurandum abolenda sunt nimia lucrorum studia, ambitiones nationales, appetitus dominationis politicae, calculi ordinis militaristici necnon machinationes ad ideologias propagandas et imponendas. Plura oeconomica et socialia systemata proponuntur; optandum est ut in his periti communia fundamenta sani commercii mundialis inveniant; quod facilius continget si singuli propria praeiudicia abnuant et ad dialogum sincere gerendum promptos se praebeant.
86 For this cooperation, the following norms seem opportune: a) Progressing nations should greatly take care that as the end of progress they expressly and firmly aspire to the full human perfection of their citizens. Let them be mindful that progress arises and grows from labor and above all from the genius of the nations themselves, which must be founded primarily not on foreign resources alone, but on their own fully developed genius and on cultivating their own genius and tradition. In this matter, those who have greater influence on others ought to excel. b) The most grave duty of progressed nations is to help progressing peoples to fulfill the above offices. Therefore, let them perfect among themselves the mental and material accommodations which are required for establishing this universal cooperation. Thus in commerce with weaker and poorer nations let them sedulously regard the good of those; for these need the proceeds received from the sale of things produced by them for their own sustenance. c) It is the part of the international community to compose and stimulate development, yet in such a way that resources ordered for this are disposed of as effectively as possible and with full equity. It also pertains to this community, the principle of subsidiarity being preserved, to order economic ways throughout the world that they be developed to the norm of justice. Apt institutions are to be established to promote and order international affairs, especially with less progressed nations, and to compensate for defects which flow from excessive inequality of power between nations. Such ordering, together with technical, cultural, and monetary aids, ought to provide necessary subsidies to nations striving for development, that they may be able conveniently to attain the increase of their own economy. d) In many cases the need to recognize economic and social structures presses; but care is to be taken from solutions technically immaturely proposed, especially from those which, while they provide material advantages to man, oppose his spiritual character and progress. For man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God (Mt 4:4). But every part of the human family in itself and in its better traditions bears with it a part of the spiritual treasure entrusted by God to humanity, although many do not know from what source it proceeds.
¶489
De quibusdam opportunis normis
On certain opportune norms
Pro hac cooperatione sequentes normae opportunae videntur: a) Gentes progredientes valde cordi habeant ut tamquam finem progressus expresse et firmiter plenam perfectionem humanam suorum civium appetant. Meminerint ex labore ante omnia et ingenio ipsarum gentium oriri et crescere progressum quippe qui non solis opibus alienis, sed propriis plene explicandis necnon ingenio et traditione propria colendis imprimis inniti debeat. Qua in re illi excellant oportet, qui maiorem influxum in alios exercent. b) Gentium autem progressarum officium gravissimum est progredientes populos ad supradicta munera implenda adiuvandi. Quapropter mentales et materiales accommodationes, quae ad hanc universalem cooperationem stabiliendam requiruntur, apud seipsas perficiant. Ita in negotiatione cum debilioribus et pauperioribus nationibus bonum illarum sedulo respiciant; nam hae proventibus quos ex venditione rerum a se productarum accipiunt, ad propriam suam sustentationem indigent. c) Communitatis vero internationalis est incrementum componere et stimulare, ita tamen ut de opibus ad hoc ordinatis quam efficacissime et plena cum aequitate disponatur. Ad hanc quoque communitatem pertinet, principio subsidiarietatis utique servato, rationes oeconomicas in toto mundo ordinare ut ad normam iustitiae explicentur. Condantur instituta apta ad promovenda et ordinanda negotia internationalia, praesertim cum nationibus minus progressis et ad compensandos defectus qui ex nimia inaequalitate potentiae inter nationes manant. Huiusmodi ordinatio, cum adiumentis technicis, culturalibus et nummariis coniuncta, nationibus ad progressum intendentibus subsidia necessaria praestare debet ut incrementa oeconomiae suae convenienter consequi valeant. d) In multis casibus urget necessitas recognoscendi oeconomicas socialesque structuras; sed cavendum est a solutionibus technicis immature propositis, imprimis ab illis quae, dum homini commoda materialia praebent, eius spirituali indoli ac profectui adversantur. Nam non in solo pane vivit homo, sed in omni verbo quod procedit de ore Dei (Mt 4,4). Quaelibet autem humanae familiae pars in seipsa et in suis melioribus traditionibus aliquam partem thesauri spiritualis a Deo humanitati concrediti secumfert, etsi multi nesciunt ex qua origine procedat.
87 International cooperation becomes very necessary relating to those peoples who today rather frequently, besides so many other difficulties, are especially pressed by those which arise from rapid population increase. The necessity urges that, by full and eager cooperation of all, especially of richer nations, it be explored how those things which are necessary for food and for suitable instruction of men can be prepared and communicated with the whole human community. Some peoples, however, could make their conditions of life much better if, instructed with due instruction, they would pass from ancient methods for producing agricultural goods to new technical arts, applying them with necessary prudence to their conditions, a better social order being moreover restored and the distribution of land possession more equitably ordered. Governments indeed have the rights and duties, as to the problem of population in their nation, within the limits of their competence; for example, concerning social legislation and legislation concerning the family, concerning the migration of farmers to cities, concerning information regarding the state and needs of the nation. Since the minds of men are so vehemently agitated concerning this problem today, it is also to be desired that concerning all these things catholic experts, especially in universities, skillfully prosecute and more widely develop studies and undertakings. Since, however, it is affirmed by many that the increase of the world's population, or at least of certain nations, is altogether to be diminished by all means and every kind of intervention of public authority, the Council exhorts all to take care from solutions, promoted or imposed publicly or privately, which contradict the moral law. For according to the inalienable right of man to marriage and to the generation of offspring, the deliberation concerning the number of offspring to be begotten depends on the right judgment of parents and can by no means be entrusted to the judgment of public authority. Since, however, the judgment of parents presupposes a rightly formed conscience, it is of great importance that access be given to all to cultivate a right and truly human responsibility which regards the divine law, the circumstances of things and times having been attended to; this indeed demands that pedagogical and social conditions be everywhere changed for the better and above all that religious formation or at least whole moral instruction be provided. Moreover, concerning scientific advances in exploring methods by which spouses can be helped in ordering the number of offspring, whose firmness is well tested and whose congruence with the moral order is known, let men be wisely informed.
¶490
De cooperatione internationali quoad incrementum incolarum
On international cooperation concerning population increase
Maxime necessaria evadit cooperatio internationalis relate ad illos populos qui hodie sat frequenter, praeter tot alias difficultates, ea peculiariter premuntur quae ex rapido incremento populationis oritur. Urget necessitas ut per plenam et impensam cooperationem omnium, praesertim ditiorum nationum, exploretur quomodo ea quae ad victum et ad congruam instructionem hominum necessaria sunt, parari et cum tota communitate humana communicari possint. Nonnulli vero populi suae vitae condiciones multo meliores reddere possent si, debita instructione exculti, a methodis antiquis pro bonis agrariis gignendis ad novas artes technicas transirent, eas cum necessaria prudentia suis condicionibus applicantes, ordine sociali meliori insuper instaurato et terrarum possessionis distributione aequius ordinata. Gubernii quidem sunt iura et officia, problema populationis in sua natione quod attinet, intra propriae competentiae limites; utputa in ordine ad legislationem socialem et ad familias respicientem, ad transitum ruricolarum ad urbes, ad informationes circa statum et necessitates nationis. Cum hodie mentes hominum de hoc problemate tam vehementer agitentur, optandum quoque est ut de his omnibus periti catholici, praesertim in Universitatibus, studia et incepta sollerter prosequantur et latius evolvant. Cum autem a multis affirmetur incolarum orbis incrementum, vel saltem quarumdam nationum, omnibus mediis et cuiusvis generis interventu auctoritatis publicae funditus omnino minuendum esse, Concilium omnes hortatur ut caveant a solutionibus, publice vel privatim promotis et quandoque impositis, quae legi morali contradicunt. Nam iuxta inalienabile hominis ius ad matrimonium et generationem prolis, deliberatio circa numerum prolis gignendae a recto iudicio parentum pendet ac nullo modo auctoritatis publicae iudicio committi potest. Cum autem parentum iudicium conscientiam recte formatam supponat, magni momenti est ut omnibus aditus praebeatur ad colendam rectam et vere humanam responsabilitatem quae legem divinam, attentis adiunctis rerum et temporum, respiciat; hoc vero exigit ut passim condiciones paedagogicae et sociales in melius mutentur et imprimis ut formatio religiosa vel saltem integra moralis institutio praebeatur. De progressibus porro scientificis in explorandis methodis quibus coniuges iuvari possint in ordinando numero prolis, quarum firmitas bene probata est et congruentia cum ordine morali comperta habetur, homines sapienter certiores fiant.
88 To build the international order with true respect for legitimate freedoms and amicable fraternity of all, Christians should willingly and with whole heart cooperate, and this the more because the greater part of the world still labors under such poverty that Christ Himself in the poor as if with a loud voice calls upon the charity of His disciples. Lest it be a scandal to men that some nations, whose greater number of citizens is often adorned with the Christian name, abound with an abundance of goods, while others are deprived of things necessary for life and are tormented by hunger, diseases, and all misery. For the spirit of poverty and charity is the glory and testimony of the Church of Christ. Let those Christians therefore be praised and helped, especially the young, who spontaneously offer themselves to provide aids to other men and peoples. Indeed, it is the part of the whole People of God, the bishops leading the way by word and example, to relieve the miseries of this time according to their strength, and this, as the ancient custom of the Church bore, not only from the superfluous but even from the substance. The manner of collecting and distributing subsidies, not being rigidly and uniformly ordered, is to be disposed in right order in dioceses, nations, and the whole world, with the united action of Catholics with other Christian brothers, wherever it seems opportune. For the spirit of charity does not forbid, but rather imposes, the provident and orderly exercise of social and charitable action. Therefore it is necessary that those who intend to devote themselves to serving progressing nations be also duly formed by suitable institutions.
¶491
De munere christianorum in subsidiis praestandis
On the office of Christians in providing subsidies
Ad ordinem internationalem cum vera observantia legitimarum libertatum et amica fraternitate omnium aedificandum, christiani libenter et toto corde cooperentur, idque eo magis quod maior pars mundi tanta adhuc egestate laborat ut in pauperibus Christus Ipse quasi alta voce caritatem suorum discipulorum evocet. Ne igitur scandalo sit hominibus aliquas nationes, quarum saepius maior numerus civium christiano nomine ornatur, bonorum copia abundare, dum aliae rebus ad vitam necessariis priventur ac fame, morbis omnimodaque miseria cruciantur. Sunt enim spiritus paupertatis et caritatis gloria et testimonium Ecclesiae Christi. Laudandi igitur et adiuvandi sunt illi christiani, iuvenes praesertim, qui sponte seipsos ad aliis hominibus et populis auxilia praestanda offerunt. Immo totius Populi Dei est, Episcopis verbo et exemplo praeeuntibus, miserias huius temporis pro viribus sublevare, idque, ut antiquus mos ferebat Ecclesiae, non ex superfluis tantum, sed etiam ex substantia. Modus subsidia colligendi et distribuendi, quin sit rigide et uniformiter ordinatus, recto tamen ordine disponatur in dioecesibus, nationibus et in universo mundo, coniuncta, ubicumque opportunum videtur, actione catholicorum cum ceteris fratribus christianis. Spiritus enim caritatis providum ordinatumque actionis socialis et caritativae exercitium nedum prohibeat, potius id imponit. Quare necesse est eos qui se ad nationibus progredientibus inserviendum devovere intendunt, idoneis etiam institutis apte efformari.
89 The Church, since by her divine mission she preaches the Gospel to all men and imparts the treasures of grace, everywhere contributes to establishing peace and laying a solid foundation for the fraternal association of men and peoples: namely the knowledge of the divine and natural law. Wherefore the Church ought to be altogether present in the international community itself to foster and excite cooperation among men; and indeed both through her public institutions and through the full and sincere collaboration of all Christians, inspired by the sole desire to serve all. Which will be more effectively attained if the faithful themselves, conscious of their human and Christian responsibility, already in their own sphere strive to excite the will readily to cooperate with the international community. Special care in this matter is to be spent on forming the young, both in religious and in civil education.
¶492
De praesentia efficaci Ecclesiae in communitate internationali
On the efficacious presence of the Church in the international community
Ecclesia, cum, divina sua missione innixa, omnibus hominibus Evangelium praedicat et thesauros gratiae elargitur, ubique terrarum ad pacem firmandam et solidum fundamentum ponendum consortionis fraternae hominum et populorum confert: cognitionem scilicet legis divinae et naturalis. Quapropter Ecclesia in ipsa communitate gentium omnino praesens esse debet ad cooperationem inter homines fovendam et excitandam; et quidem tam per suas institutiones publicas quam per plenam ac sinceram collaborationem omnium christianorum, solo desiderio omnibus inserviendi inspiratam. Quod efficacius attingetur si ipsi fideles, suae responsabilitatis humanae et christianae conscii, iam in proprio ambitu vitae voluntatem prompte cooperandi cum communitate internationali excitare satagunt. Cura peculiaris hac in re iuvenibus formandis impendatur, tam in educatione religiosa quam civili.
90 An excellent form of international activity of Christians is without doubt the cooperative work which, whether individually or associated, they provide in the Institutions themselves established or to be established for promoting cooperation between nations. To build the community of nations in peace and fraternity, various catholic international associations can also serve in many ways, which are to be strengthened, increased in number of well-formed cooperators, with the aids they need and with apt coordination of forces. For in our times, both the efficacy of actions and the necessity of dialogue demand associated undertakings. Such associations also contribute not a little to cultivating the universal sense, certainly congruent with Catholics, and to forming a consciousness of truly universal solidarity and responsibility. Finally, it is to be desired that Catholics, to rightly fulfill their office in the international community, strive to cooperate actively and positively both with separated brothers who together with them profess evangelical charity, and with all men thirsting for true peace. The Council, however, regard being had to the immensity of the miseries by which the greater part of the human race is still vexed, and to foster justice at the same time as the love of Christ toward the poor everywhere, judges it very opportune to create some universal organism of the Church, whose office be to excite the catholic community to promote the progress of needy regions and social justice between nations.
¶493
De partibus christianorum in institutionibus internationalibus
On the parts of Christians in international institutions
Praecellens quaedam forma navitatis internationalis christianorum absque dubio socia opera est quam, sive singuli sive consociati, in ipsis Institutis ad cooperationem inter nationes provehendam conditis vel condendis praestant. Communitati gentium in pace et fraternitate aedificandae insuper multipliciter inservire possunt variae consociationes catholicae internationales, quae roborandae sunt, auctis numero cooperatorum bene formatorum, subsidiis quibus indigent et apta virium coordinatione. Nostris enim temporibus et actionum efficacitas et colloquii necessitas consociata incepta postulant. Tales consociationes insuper haud parum conferunt ad universalem sensum excolendum, catholicis certe congruum, et ad formandam conscientiam vere universalis solidarietatis et responsabilitatis. Optandum denique est ut catholici, ad munus suum in communitate internationali rite implendum, actuose et positive cooperare studeant sive cum fratribus seiunctis qui una cum eis evangelicam caritatem profitentur, sive cum omnibus hominibus veram pacem sitientibus. Concilium vero, ratione habita immensitatis aerumnarum quibus maior pars generis humani etiam nunc vexatur, et ad iustitiam simul ac amorem Christi erga pauperes ubique fovendum, valde opportunum aestimat creationem alicuius Ecclesiae universalis organismi, cuius sit catholicorum communitatem excitare ut progressus indigentium regionum necnon iustitia socialis inter nationes promoveantur.
91 Those things which are set forth by this sacred Synod from the treasures of the doctrine of the Church intend to help all men of our time, whether they believe in God or do not explicitly acknowledge Him, that, perceiving more clearly their whole vocation, they may conform the world more to the eminent dignity of man, aspire to a more universal and more deeply founded fraternity, and, under the impulse of love, respond to the urgent demands of our age by generous and associated effort. Indeed, before the immense diversity both of the state of things and of forms of human culture in the world, this proposition in many of its parts intentionally carries a merely general character: indeed, although it puts forth doctrine already received in the Church, since it often concerns matters subject to unceasing evolution, it will still need to be prosecuted and amplified. We trust, however, that many things which we have set forth founded on the word of God and the spirit of the Gospel, can bring strong help to all, especially after adaptation to individual peoples and mentalities has been brought into act by Christ's faithful under the direction of pastors.
¶494
De munere singulorum fidelium et Ecclesiarum particularium
On the office of individual faithful and particular churches
Ea quae ab hac Sacra Synodo ex thesauris doctrinae Ecclesiae proponuntur, omnes homines nostrorum temporum, sive in Deum credant sive Eum non explicite agnoscant, adiuvare intendunt ut, suam integram vocationem clarius percipientes, mundum praecellenti dignitati hominis magis conforment, universalem altiusque fundatam fraternitatem appetant atque, sub impulsu amoris, generoso atque consociato conamine, urgentibus nostrae aetatis postulationibus respondeant. Sane coram immensa diversitate tum rerum status tum culturae humanae formarum in mundo, propositio haec in compluribus suis partibus consulto nonnisi indolem generalem prae se fert: immo, licet doctrinam iam in Ecclesia receptam proferat, cum non raro de rebus incessanti evolutioni subiectis agitur, adhuc prosequenda et amplianda erit. Confidimus vero multa quae verbo Dei et spiritu Evangelii innixi protulimus, omnibus validum adiutorium conferre posse, praesertim postquam adaptatio ad singulas gentes et mentalitates a christifidelibus sub Pastorum moderamine ad actum deducta fuerit.
92 The Church, by virtue of her mission of illuminating the whole world with the gospel message and of gathering all men of any nation, race, or culture into one Spirit, becomes a sign of that fraternity which permits and strengthens sincere dialogue. Which, however, requires that first of all in the Church herself we promote mutual esteem, reverence, and concord, all legitimate diversity being acknowledged, to ever more fruitfully institute dialogue among all who constitute one People of God, whether pastors or other Christ's faithful. For stronger are those things which unite the faithful than those which divide them: let there be unity in necessary things, freedom in doubtful things, charity in all things. Our mind at the same time embraces brothers not yet living in full communion with us and their communities, to whom we are nevertheless joined by confession of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit and by the bond of charity, mindful namely that the unity of Christians is also expected and desired today by many who do not believe in Christ. For by how much this unity, under the powerful power of the Holy Spirit, shall progress in truth and charity, by so much the more it will be a foretaste of unity and peace for the whole world. Wherefore, with united forces and in forms more and more apt for effectively attaining this glorious end, let us strive that, more and more conformed to the Gospel, we may fraternally cooperate to render service to the human family which, in Christ Jesus, is called into the family of the sons of God. We therefore also turn our mind to all who acknowledge God and in their traditions preserve precious religious and human elements, hoping that open dialogue may lead us all to faithfully receive the impulses of the Spirit and willingly fulfill them. The desire of such dialogue, which is led only by charity toward truth, due prudence being preserved, excludes no one on our part, neither those who cultivate the excellent goods of the human mind, but do not yet acknowledge their Author, nor those who oppose the Church and persecute her in various ways. Since God the Father is the beginning of all and the end, all are called to be brothers. And therefore, called by this same human and divine vocation, we can and ought to cooperate, without violence, without deceit, to build the world in true peace.
