De dialogo inter omnes homines
On dialogue between all men
Ecclesia, vi suae missionis universum orbem nuntio evangelico illuminandi et omnes homines cuiusvis nationis, stirpis vel culturae in unum Spiritum coadunandi, signum evadit illius fraternitatis quae sincerum dialogum permittit atque roborat. Quod autem requirit ut imprimis in ipsa Ecclesia mutuam aestimationem, reverentiam et concordiam promoveamus, omni legitima diversitate agnita, ad fructuosius semper colloquium inter omnes instituendum qui unum Populum Dei constituunt, sive pastores sive ceteri christifideles sint. Fortiora enim sunt ea quibus uniuntur fideles quam ea quibus dividuntur: sit in necessariis unitas, in dubiis libertas, in omnibus caritas. Animus autem noster simul complectitur fratres nondum nobiscum in plena communione viventes eorumque communitates, quibus tamen coniungimur confessione Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti ac vinculo caritatis, memores scilicet christianorum unitatem hodie etiam a multis in Christum non credentibus exspectari et desiderari. Quo magis enim haec unitas, sub potenti virtute Spiritus Sancti, in veritate et caritate proficiet, eo magis universo mundo erit praesagium unitatis et pacis. Quare, unitis viribus et in formis huic praeclaro fini hodie efficaciter assequendo magis magisque aptatis, studeamus ut, Evangelio in dies melius conformati, fraterne cooperemur ad servitium familiae humanae praestandum quae, in Christo Iesu, in familiam filiorum Dei vocatur. Animum nostrum proin etiam ad omnes convertimus qui Deum agnoscunt et in traditionibus suis pretiosa elementa religiosa et humana conservant, optantes ut apertum colloquium omnes nos adigat ad impulsiones Spiritus fideliter accipiendas et alacriter implendas. Desiderium talis colloquii, quod sola caritate erga veritatem ducatur, servata utique congrua prudentia, ex nostra parte neminem excludit, neque illos qui praeclara animi humani bona colunt, eorum vero Auctorem nondum agnoscunt, neque illos qui Ecclesiae opponuntur eamque variis modis persequuntur. Cum Deus Pater principium omnium exsistat et finis, omnes, ut fratres simus, vocamur. Et ideo, hac eadem humana et divina vocatione vocati, sine violentia, sine dolo ad aedificandum mundum in vera pace cooperari possumus et debemus.
93 Christians, mindful of the word of the Lord: "By this shall all men know that you are my disciples, if you have love one for another" (Jn 13:35), can desire nothing more ardently than to serve the men of this world ever more generously and effectively. Therefore, faithfully adhering to the Gospel and enjoying its forces, joined with all who love and cultivate justice, they have undertaken the great work on earth to be fulfilled, of which they must render an account to Him who will judge all on the last day. Not all who say "Lord, Lord" will enter the kingdom of heaven, but those who do the will of the Father and lay a strong hand to the work. The Father wills that we recognize Christ our brother in all men and love Him effectively, both by word and deed, thus bearing witness to the Truth, and with others communicating the mystery of the love of the heavenly Father. By this way, men will be aroused to a living hope throughout the whole world, which is the gift of the Holy Spirit, that at length in peace and highest happiness they may be received into the homeland which shines with the glory of the Lord.

De mundo aedificando et ad finem perducendo
On building and perfecting the world
Christiani, memores verbi Domini in hoc cognoscent omnes quia discipuli mei estis, si dilectionem habueritis ad invicem (Jn 13,35), nihil ardentius optare possunt quam ut hominibus mundi huius temporis semper generosius et efficacius inserviant. Itaque, Evangelio fideliter adhaerentes eiusque viribus fruentes, cum omnibus qui iustitiam diligunt et colunt coniuncti, ingens opus in his terris adimplendum susceperunt, de quo Ei, qui omnes iudicabit ultimo die, rationem reddere debent. Non omnes qui dicunt: Domine, Domine, intrabunt in regnum caelorum, sed ii qui faciunt voluntatem Patris validamque manum operi apponunt. Vult autem Pater ut in omnibus hominibus Christum fratrem agnoscamus et efficaciter diligamus, tam verbo quam opere, ita testimonium perhibentes Veritati, et cum aliis mysterium amoris Patris caelestis communicemus. Hac via in toto orbe terrarum homines ad vivam spem excitabuntur, quae Spiritus Sancti donum est, ut tandem aliquando in pace ac beatitudine summa suscipiantur, in patria quae gloria Domini effulget.

HOMILIA AD PATRES CONCILIARES HABITA A SUMMO PONTEFICE, MISSAE CONCELEBRATIONE PERAGENTE, IN ULTIMA OECUMENICAE SYNODI PUBLICA SESSIONE, IN QUA DECLARATIO DE LIBERTATE RELIGIOSA, DECRETA DE ACTIVITATE MISSIONALI ECCLESIAE ET DE PRESBYTERORUM MINISTERIO ET VITA, CONSTITUTIO PASTORALIS DE ECCLESIA IN MUNDO HUIUS TEMPORIS APPROBATA ET PROMULGATA SUNT
HOMILY OF THE SUPREME PONTIFF TO THE COUNCIL FATHERS, CELEBRATING THE CONCELEBRATION OF THE MASS, IN THE LAST PUBLIC SESSION OF THE ECUMENICAL SYNOD, IN WHICH THE DECLARATION ON RELIGIOUS LIBERTY, THE DECREES ON THE MISSIONARY ACTIVITY OF THE CHURCH AND ON THE MINISTRY AND LIFE OF PRESBYTERS, AND THE PASTORAL CONSTITUTION ON THE CHURCH IN THE MODERN WORLD WERE APPROVED AND PROMULGATED
¶498
Venerabiles Fratres,
Venerable Brothers,
Hodie Concilium Oecumenicum Vaticanum secundum concludimus, illudque maxime adhuc firmum validumque concludimus. Quod et hic conspectus vester frequentissimus demonstrat, et ordinata huius Consessus compago testatur, et legitimus Concilii laborum exitus confirmat, et mentium voluntatumque conspiratio veluti proclamat. Quodsi quaestiones non paucae, inter Concilii celebrationem positae, adhuc exspectant donec congruenter dissolvantur, id profecto significat Concilium labores suos, non in virium suarum defatigatione concludere, sed in ea potius vivacitate, quam universalis haec Synodus excitavit, quaeque, post dimissum Concilium, Deo iuvante, ad easdem quaestiones dissolvendas se totam convertet. Concilium hoc nostrum posteris eiusmodi Ecclesiae imaginem tradet, qualem haec aula repraesentat, sacris videlicet referta Pastoribus, qui eandem fidem profitentur, eandemque caritatem spirant; qui societate precationum, disciplinae, alacritatis inter se coniuncti sunt, qui - quod mirum sane est - unum hoc expetunt omnes, ut, non secus atque Christus, Magister et Dominus noster, se pro vita Ecclesiae et mundi salute devoveant. At Concilium posteris non tantum imaginem Ecclesiae tradit, sed etiam patrimonium eius doctrinae eiusque praeceptorum, hoc est depositum a Christo ipsi commissum; hoc gentes per saeculorum decursum semper meditatae sunt, in suum quasi sucum et sanguinem verterunt, moribusque suis quodammodo expresserunt; hoc nunc, pluribus partibus illustratum, in sua integritate statutum atque ordinatum est. Hoc, cum vivum sit ob divinam veritatis et gratiae virtutem, ex qua constat, idcirco, idoneum est putandum quod hominem quemlibet vivificet, qui pie illud accipiat, eoque vitam suam alat.
Today we conclude the Second Vatican Ecumenical Council, and we conclude it most firmly and validly. Which both this most numerous gathering of yours demonstrates, and the ordered structure of this Assembly testifies, and the legitimate outcome of the Council's labors confirms, and the conspiracy of minds and wills as it were proclaims. But if not a few questions, posed during the celebration of the Council, still await to be congruently resolved, this certainly signifies that the Council concludes its labors not in the exhaustion of its forces, but rather in that vitality which this universal Synod has excited, and which, after the Council is dismissed, will turn itself wholly to resolving the same questions, with God's help. This Council of ours will hand down to posterity such an image of the Church as this hall represents, namely filled with sacred Pastors, who profess the same faith and breathe the same charity; who are joined with one another in the society of prayers, discipline, alacrity; who—which is indeed wonderful—all seek this one thing, that, not otherwise than Christ, our Master and Lord, they may devote themselves for the life of the Church and the salvation of the world. But the Council hands down to posterity not only an image of the Church, but also the patrimony of her doctrine and her precepts, that is, the deposit entrusted to her by Christ; this nations through the course of the ages have always meditated, have turned as it were into their own juice and blood, and have in a way expressed in their morals; this now, in many parts illustrated, is established and ordered in its integrity. This, since it is living on account of the divine power of truth and grace from which it consists, is therefore to be deemed fit to give life to any man who piously receives it and nourishes his life by it.
¶499
Quid re vera hoc Concilium fuerit, quidve ipsum effecerit, nostrae huius extremae meditationis argumentum esset. Sed nimium id attentionis et temporis postularet; neque hac suprema sollemnique hora audemus res tanti ponderis brevi comprehendere. Malumus propterea gravia haec temporis momenta eiusmodi cogitationi tribuere, quae simul nostros animos ad humilitatem inclinet, simul ad summa optata nostra extollat. A nobismetipsis nempe quaerere malumus: quodnam fuerit religiosum nostri Concilii momentum: quo vocabulo significare mens est nostras cum Deo necessitudines, quae profecto declarant quare Ecclesia exsistat, quid credat, quid speret, quid amet, quid sit, quid faciat.
What this Council has truly been, or what it has effected, would be the subject of this our final meditation. But that would demand too much attention and time; nor at this last solemn hour do we dare to comprehend matters of such weight in a brief space. We prefer therefore to devote these grave moments of time to such a reflection which at once inclines our minds to humility and at the same time lifts them up to our highest desires. We prefer, namely, to ask ourselves: what has been the religious significance of our Council? With what word does our mind intend to signify our relations with God, which certainly declare why the Church exists, what she believes, what she hopes, what she loves, what she is, what she does?
¶500
Possumusne fateri nos laudem Deo comparavisse, eius cognitionem et amorem quaesivisse, in contentione eum contemplandi profecisse, in sollicitudine eum celebrandi, in arte eum hominibus nuntiandi, qui ad nos tamquam ad Pastores viarumque Dei Magistros respiciunt? Ita re vera esse, Nos sincere opinamur, hanc etiam ob causam, quod ex hoc primo ac praecipuo consilio secutum est propositum illud, in quo tota veluti ratio celebrandi Concilii sita fuit. Adhuc resonant in hac Petriana Basilica verba, quae in auspicali huius Concilii allocutione prolata fuerunt a Decessore Nostro fel. rec. Ioanne XXIII, quem iure optimo auctorem huius Oecumenicae Synodi agnoscere possumus. Tunc ille Pontifex ita locutus est: «Quod Concilii Oecumenici maxime interest hoc est, ut sacrum doctrinae christianae depositum efficaciore ratione custodiatur atque proponatur . . . Verum sane est, Christum Dominum hanc pronuntiasse sententiam: - «Quaerite primum regnum Dei et iustitiam eius» - Quae vox primum declarat, quo potissimum vires et cogitationes nostras dirigi oporteat» (Discorsi, 1962, p. 583). Atque proposito illi eventus reapse respondit. Quod eventum ut pro rei veritate aestimemus, probe recolendum est, qua aetate illud contigerit. Scilicet aetate contigit, qua, ut apud omnes constat, homines ad huius mundi dominatum potius quam ad Dei regnum sunt conversi; aetate dicimus, qua Dei oblivio in consuetudinem venit, quasi eam suadeant ipsi progredientium scientiarum processus; aetate qua actus praecipuus personae humanae, de seipsa de suaque libertate clariorem conscientiam adeptae, eo contendit ut plenam libertatem sibi vindicet, nulli legi obnoxiam, quae rerum naturalium ordinem transcendat; aetate, qua laicismi placita videntur ex hodiernis doctrinarum incrementis iure proficisci, eademque quasi norma sapientissima habentur, secundum quam humana societas sit ordinanda; aetate praeterea, qua humana ratio, eo venit, ut ea exprimat, quae perabsurda sint omnemque spem auferant; aetate denique, qua maximae ethnicarum gentium religiones perturbationibus atque mutationibus obnoxiae sunt, quas antea numquam sunt expertae. Hac igitur aetate nostrum Concilium celebratum est ad Dei laudem, in nomine Christi, afflante Spiritu Sancto, qui omnia scrutatur, quique etiamnunc Ecclesiam intus animat, ut sciamus quae a Deo donata sunt nobis (cfr. 1 Cor. 2, 10-12), scilicet efficiens, ut Ecclesia hominis vitam ac mundum penitus omnique ex parte cognoscat. Ope huius Concilii, doctrina theocentrica ac theologica, uti aiunt, de humana natura ac de mundo ad se hominum mentes convertit, quasi eos provocans, qui illam a nostrae aetatis ratione alienam atque extraneam putent; atque talia sibi arrogat, quae mundus primum quidem absurda iudicet, sed postea, ut fore confidimus, humana, sapientia ac salutaria ultro agnoscet: scilicet Deum esse. Utique Deus est; reapse exsistit; vivit; persona est; est providus, infinita bonitate praeditus, et quidem bonus non solum in se, sed maximopere etiam erga nos; est noster Creator, nostra veritas, nostra felicitas; adeo ut homo, cum mentem et cor suum in Deo defigere nititur, contemplationi vacando, actum animi sui eliciat, qui omnium nobilissimus ac perfectissimus est habendus; actum dicimus, a quo nostris etiam temporibus innumeri humanae navitatis campi suae dignitatis gradum sumere possunt ac debent.
Can we confess that we have procured praise for God, have sought His knowledge and love, have progressed in the struggle to contemplate Him, in solicitude to celebrate Him, in the art of announcing Him to men who look to us as to Pastors and Masters of the ways of God? We sincerely think that it is truly so, also for this reason, that from this first and principal counsel followed that purpose in which the whole, as it were, reason for celebrating the Council lay. Still resound in this Petrine Basilica the words which were uttered in the inaugural allocution of this Council by our predecessor of happy memory, John XXIII, whom we can rightly acknowledge as the author of this Ecumenical Synod. Then that Pontiff spoke thus: "What is of greatest interest to the Ecumenical Council is this, that the sacred deposit of Christian doctrine be preserved and set forth by a more effective manner... It is truly true that Christ the Lord pronounced this sentence: 'Seek first the kingdom of God and His justice.' This word 'first' declares to what especially our strength and thoughts ought to be directed" (Discourses, 1962, p. 583). And the event indeed responded to that purpose. That event, that we may estimate it according to the truth of the matter, we must well recall in what age it occurred. Namely, it occurred in an age in which, as is manifest to all, men are turned rather to the dominion of this world than to the kingdom of God; we say an age in which forgetfulness of God has come into custom, as if the processes of progressing sciences themselves suggest it; an age in which the principal act of the human person, having acquired a clearer consciousness concerning itself and its freedom, tends to claim full liberty for itself, subject to no law transcending the order of natural things; an age in which the opinions of laicism seem rightly to proceed from the advances of modern doctrines, and the same seem to be held as the wisest norm, according to which human society is to be ordered; moreover, an age in which human reason has come to express those things which are very absurd and take away all hope; finally, an age in which the greatest religions of the ethnic nations are subject to disturbances and changes, which they have never before experienced. In this age, therefore, our Council has been celebrated to the praise of God, in the name of Christ, with the Holy Spirit breathing, who searches all things, and who even now animates the Church within, that we may know what things are given us by God (cf. 1 Cor 2:10-12), namely, effecting that the Church profoundly and wholly knows human life and the world. By the help of this Council, the theocentric and theological doctrine, as they say, of human nature and of the world, turns the minds of men to itself, as it were provoking those who think it alien and extraneous from the reason of our age; and it claims such things for itself which the world indeed first judges absurd, but afterwards, as we trust, will freely acknowledge as human, wise, and salutary: namely that God is. Certainly God is; He truly exists; He lives; He is a person; He is provident, endowed with infinite goodness, and indeed good not only in Himself, but most greatly also toward us; He is our Creator, our truth, our happiness; so much so that man, when he strives to fix his mind and heart on God, giving himself to contemplation, elicits an act of his spirit which is to be held as most noble and perfect; we say an act from which even in our times innumerable fields of human activity can and ought to receive the grade of their dignity.
¶501
Dixerit aliquis Concilium minus versatum esse in pertractandis veritatibus divinis quam - idque praecipue - in consideranda Ecclesia, eius natura, eius compage eius oecumenico munere, eius opera apostolica et missionali. Haec enim pervetus societas religiosa, hoc est, Ecclesia studuit de se ipsa cogitare, quo melius se cognosceret, melius se definiret atque ex his mentem ac praecepta sua componeret. Hoc est verum. Sed haec sui ipsius recognitio non finis fuit, quem solum sibi proponeret, non fuit ostensio solius terreni cultus ingenii. Ecclesia enim, se ad se ipsam revocans, in intimas conscientiae suae latebras penetravit, non ut se oblectaret in eruditis pervestigationibus, quibus in psychologiam religiosam vel historiam rerum suarum inquireret, aut ut dedita opera iterum iura sua obtineret vel leges suas describeret, sed ut, in se ipsa vivens atque, per Spiritum Sanctum, efficax verbum Christi melius intellegeret, utque altius scrutaretur mysterium, id est consilium ac praesentiam Dei circa se et intra se, et ut ignem fidei in se magis magisque aleret, quae est vis arcana, qua firmitas eius et sapientia innititur, ignemque amoris, quo impellitur ad laudes Dei sine intermissione canendas, siquidem; ut ait S. Augustinus, cantare amantis est (Serm. 336; P.L. 38, 1472). Hoc ipsum ac quidem praecipuum propositum religiosum clare patefit e documentis Concilii, imprimis ex iis, quae agunt de divina Revelatione, de Liturgia, de Ecclesia, de Sacerdotibus, de Religiosis, de Laicis; quibus ostenditur quam limpida, egelida, dives sit spiritualis vena, quae, ob vivum Dei viventis contactum, ex Ecclesiae sinu erumpit et per hanc in aridas glebas huius terrae nostrae effunditur.
Someone might say that the Council has been less occupied in treating divine truths than—and this especially—in considering the Church, her nature, her structure, her ecumenical office, her apostolic and missionary work. For this very ancient religious society, that is, the Church, has studied to think about herself, that she might better know herself, better define herself, and from these compose her mind and precepts. This is true. But this self-recognition was not the only end she proposed to herself, was not the display of a merely earthly cultivation of genius. For the Church, recalling herself to herself, penetrated into the intimate recesses of her conscience, not to delight herself in learned investigations into religious psychology or the history of her affairs, nor to purposely obtain again her rights or describe her laws, but to, living in herself and, through the Holy Spirit, better understanding the effective word of Christ, and to more deeply scrutinize the mystery, that is the plan and presence of God concerning and within herself, and to more and more nourish the fire of faith in herself, which is the secret power on which her firmness and wisdom rest, and the fire of love by which she is impelled to sing the praises of God without ceasing, since, as St. Augustine says, "to sing belongs to the lover" (Serm. 336; P.L. 38, 1472). This very thing, and indeed the principal religious purpose, clearly appears from the documents of the Council, especially from those which treat of divine Revelation, of the Liturgy, of the Church, of Priests, of Religious, of Laity; by which it is shown how clear, warm, and rich is the spiritual vein which, from the living contact with the living God, bursts forth from the bosom of the Church and through her is poured upon the dry clods of this our earth.
¶502
Sed non licet quiddam praetermittere, quod summi momenti est, cum religiosam vim huius Concilii pervestigamus: hoc enim ei potissimum fuit, ut mundum huius aetatis nostrae scrutaretur. Numquam fortasse Ecclesia sicut huius Synodi tempore in eam necessitatem se sensit adductam, ut societatem hominum, qui circa sunt, cognosceret, quasi adiret, recte aestimaret, in eam se inferret, ei serviret et Evangelii nuntium traderet, atque ut eam veluti appeteret, quodammodo prosequens eam, quae velociter et continenter mutatur. Hic habitus mentis, ortus ex eo quod Ecclesia superiore aetate, praeterito ac maxime hoc saeculo, abfuit et disiuncta est a profano ingenii cultu, hic habitus mentis, quem munus Ecclesiae salvificum ac primarium nullo non tempore ingerit, in Concilio efficaciter et continenter obtinuit. Quam ob rem quibusdam ea suspicio est iniecta, ut putarent in hominibus et actis Synodi plus aequo ac nimis indulgenter praeponderasse illam rationem, quae ex «relativismi» doctrina inhaeret mundo externo, rebus, quae fugaci cursu geruntur, novis modis, qui in humano cultu invalescunt, necessitatibus, quae oriuntur, cogitatis aliorum; idque cum detrimento fidelitatis, quae a maioribus acceptae doctrinae debetur et cum damno religiosae mentis ac voluntatis, quas Concilii proprias esse oportet. Non equidem arbitramur ei tribuendam esse talem perniciem, si eius vera et recondita consilia et germani actus spectantur. Animadvertere potius cupimus, Concilii nostri religionem in primis fuisse caritatem; quam ob praecipuam declaratam voluntatem, Concilium a nemine notari poterit quasi irreligiosum fuerit, vel a sacro Evangelio defecerit, si meminerimus ipsum Christum nos docere, fore ut «in hoc cognoscant omnes, quia discipuli eius sumus, si dilectionem habuerimus ad invicem» (cfr. Io. 13, 35); si pariter sinamus haec Apostolorum verba intus in animis nostris resonare: «Religio munda et immaculata apud Deum et Patrem haec est: visitare pupillos et viduas in tribulatione eorum, et immaculatum se custodire ab hoc saeculo (Iac. 1, 27); et haec alia: Qui . . . non diligit fratrem suum, quem videt, Deum, quem non videt, quomodo potest diligere?» (1 Io. 4, 20).
But it is not lawful to omit something which is of greatest importance when we investigate the religious force of this Council: for it was especially this, that it should scrutinize the world of our age. Never perhaps has the Church, as in the time of this Synod, felt herself led into the necessity of knowing, as it were approaching, rightly estimating, inserting herself into, serving, and handing over the message of the Gospel to the human society which is around, and of as it were seeking it out, in a way pursuing it, which changes swiftly and continuously. This habit of mind, arising from the fact that the Church in the past age, especially this century, was absent and separated from profane cultivation of genius, this habit of mind, which the saving and principal office of the Church at all times ingests, has effectively and continuously obtained in the Council. Wherefore a suspicion has been instilled into some, that they thought that in the men and acts of the Synod there unduly and too indulgently prevailed that reason which inheres in the external world from the doctrine of "relativism," in things which pass with fleeting course, in new ways which prevail in human culture, in arising necessities, in the thoughts of others; and that to the detriment of the fidelity owed to the doctrine received from the fathers, and with damage to the religious mind and will, which ought to be proper to the Council. We do not think such perdition is to be attributed to it, if its true and recondite counsels and genuine acts are regarded. We rather wish to point out that the religion of our Council was first of all charity; on account of which manifest declared will, the Council can be noted by no one as if it were irreligious, or had failed from the sacred Gospel, if we remember that Christ himself teaches us that "by this all will know that you are his disciples, if you have love one for another" (cf. Jn 13:35); if we likewise let these words of the Apostle resound within our spirits: "Religion clean and undefiled before God and the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their tribulation, and to keep oneself unspotted from this world" (Jas 1:27); and this other: "If... he does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see?" (1 Jn 4:20).
¶503
Verum enimvero Ecclesia, in Concilio collecta, suam considerationem summopere intendit - praeterquam in semetipsam, atque in necessitudinem, quam cum Deo coniungitur - in hominem etiam, in hominem, sicuti reapse hoc tempore se conspiciendum praebet: hominem, dicimus, qui vivit; hominem, qui sibimetipsi uni provehendo deditus est; hominem, qui non modo sese dignum existimat, ad quem unum, veluti ad quoddam centrum, omne studium conferatur, sed etiam affirmare non veretur, se esse cuiusvis rei principium atque rationem. Totus homo phaenomenicus - ut recentioris aetatis verbo utamur - suis innumeris animi habitibus indutus, quibus in conspectum venit, se Concilii Patribus obiecit, qui et ipsi homines, immo omnes Pastores atque fratres sunt, intenta cura atque amanti caritate praediti: homo, qui suas luctuosas fortunas animose conqueritur; homo, qui et praeterito et nostro hoc tempore alios infra se positos existimat, ideoque semper fluxus atque fucatus, sui cupidus et ferox est; homo sibi displicens, qui risus edit et lacrimas fundit; homo ad omnia versatilis, ad quaslibet partes agendas facilis; homo in unam scientiae pervestigationem acriter intentus; homo, qui uti talis cogitat, amat, in laboribus desudat, semper ad aliquid animum advertit, sicut ille filius accrescens (Gen. 49, 22); homo, qui sacra quadam cum religione est considerandus, ob suae infantiae innocentiam, ob suae inopiae arcanum, ob pietatem, quam suae aegritudines movent; homo hinc sui ipsius tantum studiosus, hinc societati favens; homo simul laudator temporis acti, simul posterum tempus praestolans, illudque felicius quam praeteritum somnians; homo ex altera parte criminibus obnoxius, ex altera Sanctis moribus ornatus; et deinde deinceps. Humanitatis illud laicum atque profanum studium, immani qua est magnitudine, tandem aliquando prodit, idemque ad certamen, ut ita dicamus, Concilium lacessivit. Religio, id est cultus Dei, qui homo fieri voluit, atque religio - talis enim est aestimanda - id est cultus hominis, qui fieri vult Deus, inter se congressae sunt. Quid tamen accidit? Certamen, proelium, anathema? Id sane haberi potuerat, sed plane non accidit. Vetus illa de bono Samaritano narratio exemplum fuit atque norma, ad quam Concilii nostri spiritualis ratio directa est. Etenim, immensus quidam erga homines amor Concilium penitus pervasit. Perspectae et iterum consideratae hominum necessitates, quae eo molestiores fiunt, quo magis huius terrae filius crescit, totum nostrae huius Synodi studium detinuerunt. Hanc saltem laudem Concilio tribuite, vos, nostra hac aetate cultores humanitatis, qui veritates rerum naturam transcendentes renuitis, iidemque novum nostrum humanitatis studium agnoscite: nam nos etiam, immo nos prae ceteris, hominis sumus cultores.
For truly the Church, gathered in the Council, most greatly turned her consideration—besides into herself, and into the relation by which she is joined with God—also into man, into man as he must be contemplated in reality in this time: man, we say, who lives; man, who is devoted to his own promotion alone; man, who not only deems himself worthy to have all study turned to him alone, as to a certain center, but also does not fear to affirm himself to be the principle and reason of every thing. The whole phenomenal man—to use a word of the recent age—clothed with his innumerable mental habits, in which he comes into view, presented himself to the Fathers of the Council, who are themselves men, indeed all Pastors and brothers, with intent care and loving charity: man, who bravely complains of his own mournful fortunes; man, who judges others placed beneath himself both in the past and in our time, and therefore always unstable and feigned, desirous of himself and fierce; man displeasing to himself, who gives forth laughter and sheds tears; man versatile to all things, easy to play any parts; man sharply intent on one investigation of science; man who, as such, thinks, loves, sweats in labors, always attends to something, like the growing son (Gen 49:22); man who is to be considered with a certain sacred reverence, on account of the innocence of his infancy, on account of the mystery of his poverty, on account of the piety which his sufferings move; man hence only studious of himself, hence favoring society; man at once the praiser of past time, at the same time awaiting the future time, and dreaming it happier than the past; man on the one hand obnoxious to crimes, on the other hand adorned with holy morals; and so on in turn. That lay and profane study of humanity, with its immense magnitude, at last comes forth, and the same, as it were, challenged the Council to a contest. Religion, that is, the worship of God, who willed to become man, and religion—for such it is to be esteemed—that is, the worship of man, who wishes to become God, have met together. But what happened? A contest, a battle, an anathema? That could indeed have been, but it plainly did not happen. That old story of the good Samaritan was an example and norm, to which the spiritual reason of our Council was directed. For an immense love for men thoroughly pervaded the Council. The necessities of men, which become the more troublesome the more the son of this earth grows, having been perceived and again considered, occupied the whole study of our Synod. Give this praise at least to the Council, you, cultivators of humanity in our age, who refuse the truths transcending the nature of things, and at the same time acknowledge our new study of humanity: for we also, indeed we before others, are cultivators of man.
¶504
Quid enim amplissimus hic Consessus in humana natura perspexit, quidve, lumen Deitatis consecutus, cognoscere studuit? Os eius, duas quod semper facies praefert, penitus perspicere voluit, hominis scilicet infirmitate mitemque dignitatem, tum eius summum malum quo veluti morbo insanabili neque dubio ipse laborat, tum eius quod manet bonum, arcana quidem venustate singularique praestantia distinctum. Verum id est aperte fatendum, nostrum hoc Concilium, cum suum fecerit de homine iudicium, magis in serena hac eius fronte quam in tristi contuenda esse versatum; in quo quidem res omnes in optimam sane partem scienter esse interpretatum. Multum enim studii atque admirationis in nostrae aetatis homines Concilium contulit. Errores profecto sunt reiecti, quemadmodum caritas ipsa perinde atque veritas postulabant, sed homines, salvo semper observantiae et amoris praecepto, tantum de errore sunt admoniti. Ita nimirum factum est, ut pro morborum cognitione, quae animos frangeret, salutaria in medium proferrentur remedia plena solacii; ut Concilium, non infaustis usum ominibus, sed nuntiis spei ac fiduciae verbis, huiusce memoriae homines alloqueretur. Nam quae iis sunt bona, ea non solum debito cultu, sed multo etiam honore affecit, omniaque eius coepta confirmavit atque studia, ad integritatem quandam redacta, vehementer probavit. Videatis igitur - ut aliquot supponamus exempla - quomodo innumeri, qui hodie passim usurpentur, sermones ita in sacrum sint inducti ordinem rituum, ut humana ad Deum verba rursumque divina ad homines verba redderent; quomodo hominis, uti hominis, ea sit comprobata naturalis ad plurima assequenda iura proclivitas atque etiam sors illa, quae ipsius exsuperet, naturam; quomodo ea, quae summis ipse votis concupiscat, ut vivat nempe et sua dignitate et honesta libertate et doctrinae copia et renovato rerum socialium statu et iustitia et pace perfruatur, haec omnia dicimus quasi emendata ad uberiorem sint incitata profectum; quomodo universi demum homines ad Evangelii lumen in se recipiendum, voce quae pastores deceat sacrosque operarios, sint votati. Nimis breviter nunc Nos de plurimis iisque amplissimis quaestionibus loquimur, ad germanam hominis prosperitatem attinentibus, de quibus in Concilio actum est; nec Concilio propositum fuit omnes hodiernae vitae urgentiores quaestiones dissolvere; quarum nonnullae ad diligentiores investigationes reiciuntur, quas Ecclesia in posterum instituet; plures aliae vero nimis presse ac generatim pertractatae sunt, ac propterea subtiliores explicationes variasque effectiones admittunt. Nunc vero animadvertere iuvat, Ecclesiam per suum magisterium, quamvis nullum doctrinae caput sententiis dogmaticis extraordinariis definire voluerit, nihilominus circa plurimas quaestiones cum auctoritate doctrinam proposuisse suam, ad cuius normam homines hodie tenentur conscientiam suam suamque agendi rationem conformare. Ecclesia praeterea, ut ita dicamus, cum nostrorum temporum hominibus colloquium iniit; semperque auctoritatem virtutemque suam retinens, ipsam tamen loquendi rationem adhibuit facilem et amicam, quae caritatis pastoralis propria est. Voluit enim ab hominibus audiri atque intellegi. Quapropter, non ad solam hominis intellegentiam verba sua convertit, sed dicendi genere usa est, quod hodie communiter usurpatur in serendis colloquiis, quae quidem ex vitae usu, quo nituntur, et ex sinceris humanitatis sensibus, quibus afficiuntur, maiorem vim capiunt ad alliciendum et ad persuadendum. Ecclesia scilicet collocuta est cum nostrae aetatis hominibus, uti sunt.
For what did this most ample Assembly perceive in human nature, or what, having obtained the light of Deity, did it strive to know? It wished to see through its two faces, which it always presents, namely, the infirmity of man and his gentle dignity, then his greatest evil with which he labors as if with an incurable and no doubtful disease, then the good which remains in him, distinguished by hidden beauty and singular excellence. But it is frankly to be confessed, this Council of ours, when it made its judgment concerning man, was more concerned with contemplating this serene face than the sad one; in which indeed it knowingly interpreted all things for the best. For the Council bestowed much study and admiration on the men of our age. Errors indeed were rejected, as charity itself as well as truth demanded, but men were only admonished concerning error, the precept of reverence and love always being preserved. Thus it came about that, instead of a recognition of diseases which would break the spirit, salutary remedies full of solace were brought forth; that the Council, not using ill-omened prognostications, but with words of hope and trust, addressed the men of this memory. For the goods which are in them, it not only cultivated with due cult, but also honored with much praise, and it strongly approved all their undertakings and studies, reduced to a certain integrity. You see, therefore—to suppose a few examples—how innumerable discourses, which are currently used everywhere, have been introduced into the sacred order of rites, that they might render human words to God and again divine words to men; how that natural inclination of man, as man, to attain many rights has been approved, and also the lot which exceeds his nature; how those things which he desires with highest wishes, namely to enjoy life and his dignity and honest liberty and abundance of doctrine and renewed state of social things and justice and peace—we say all these, as it were corrected, have been incited to fuller progress; how finally all men, by a voice which befits sacred pastors and workers, have been consecrated to receive the light of the Gospel into themselves. We now speak too briefly of very many and very ample questions concerning the true prosperity of man, which have been treated in the Council; nor did the Council intend to dissolve all the more urgent questions of modern life; some of which are referred to more diligent investigations, which the Church will institute in the future; many others, however, have been treated too compendiously and generally, and therefore admit subtler explanations and various effects. But now it is pleasant to point out that the Church through her Magisterium, although she wished to define no head of doctrine by extraordinary dogmatic sentences, has nevertheless with authority set forth her doctrine concerning very many questions, to the norm of which men today are held to conform their conscience and their manner of acting. The Church, moreover, as we might say, entered into dialogue with the men of our time; and, always retaining her authority and power, she used a manner of speaking easy and friendly, which is proper to pastoral charity. She wished, therefore, to be heard and understood by men. Wherefore, she turned her words not only to the intellect of man, but used a kind of speech which is commonly used today in weaving dialogues, which indeed, from the use of life on which they rest and from the sincere sentiments of humanity by which they are affected, derive greater power to allure and persuade. The Church, namely, spoke with the men of our age as they are.
¶505
Aliud est etiam, quod consideratione dignum putamus: huiusmodi divitem doctrinae copiam eo unite spectare, ut homini serviat, in omnibus cius vitae adiunctis, in omni eius infirmitate, et in omni eius necessitate. Ecclesia quodammodo se professa est humani generis ancillam, et quidem eo tempore, quo ipsius magisterium ipsiusque pastorale regimen, ob sollemnes Concilii Oecumenici celebrationes, clariore lumine validioreque robore praedita se praestiterunt: immo vero ministerii exercendi propositum reapse praecipuum obtinuit locum. Quae omnia de Concilio diximus, quaeque dicere insuper possimus ad humanum ipsius momentum quod attinet, numquid inter Concilium celebrandum, Ecclesiae mentem deflexerunt ad hodiernae mentis culturam, quae tota in homine consistit? Dicendum est, Ecclesiam non a recto itinere decessisse, sed hoc in illam partem direxisse. At qui probe perpendant hoc praecipuum studium, quo Concilium bona humana et temporalia consideravit, facere iidem non poterunt, quin agnoscant huiusmodi studium pastorali illi sollicitudini tribuendum esse, quam Concilium tamquam propriam laborum suorum notam sequi voluit; itemque agnoscere debebunt, hoc idem studium numquam a vero religionis studio disiungi, sive ob caritatem, qua penitus afficitur (et ubi est caritas ibi Deus est!), sive ob arctam conexionem, a Concilio semper confirmatam et provectam, quae humana ac temporalia bona cum bonis vere spiritualibus, religiosis et aeternis habent. Ecclesia ad hominem et ad mundum se inclinat, sed simul etiam ad Dei regnum extollitur. Homines nostrorum temporum, quorum mentis habitus eos impellit ut rerum praestantiam ex earum commodis metiantur, magnum Concilii Oecumenici pondus atque momentum agnoscere debent, saltem hanc ob causam, quod ipsum ad hominum utilitatem unite spectavit. Nemo igitur umquam affirmet inutilem catholicam religionem esse, quae, cum suae actionis formam maxime consciam atque eficacem exprimit, hoc est cum Concilium Oecumenicum celebrat, plane simul declarat se pro homine esse eiusque utilitatibus favere. Quae cum ita sint, fatendum revera est, catholicam religionem et humanam vitam inter se amico foedere iungi, et utramque simul conspirare ad unum quoddam humanum bonum: religionem scilicet catholicam pro humano genere esse, humanique generis esse quodammodo vitam. Vita quidem dicenda est ob doctrinam excelsam eamque omni ex parte perfectam, quam ipsa de homine tradit (nonne homo, sibi relictus, sibimetipsi arcana res est?); quam sane doctrinam idcirco tradit, quia illam e scientia, quam de Deo habet, haurit. Etenim ut nos hominem, hominem verum, hominem integrum penitus noscamus, Deum ipsum antea cognoscamus necesse est. Ad hoc probandum satis nunc sit haec recolere Sanctae Catharinae Senensis flammantia verba: «In tua natura, aeterne Deus, naturam meam cognoscam». Tum catholica religio vita est, quia hominis naturam eiusque supremum finem ostendit, plenioremque ei sensum attribuit; vita denique est, quia veluti suprema lex vitae est habenda, et quia vitae ipsi talem arcanam vim inicit, ut eam vere divinam efficiat.
There is also another thing which we think worthy of consideration: that such rich abundance of doctrine tends to this one end, to serve man, in all circumstances of his life, in all his infirmity, and in all his necessity. The Church in a way professed herself to be the handmaid of the human race, and indeed at the time when her Magisterium and her pastoral governance, on account of the solemn celebrations of the Ecumenical Council, showed themselves endowed with clearer light and stronger power; indeed, the purpose of exercising the ministry truly obtained the principal place. All these things which we have said concerning the Council, and which we could further say concerning its human significance, did they turn the mind of the Church during the celebration of the Council toward the modern cultivation of the mind, which wholly consists in man? It must be said that the Church did not depart from the right way, but directed it in that direction. But those who rightly weigh this principal study, by which the Council considered human and temporal goods, cannot but acknowledge that such study is to be attributed to the pastoral solicitude which the Council wished to follow as a proper mark of its labors; likewise they must acknowledge that this same study is never separated from the true study of religion, whether on account of the charity by which it is thoroughly affected (and where charity is, there God is!), or on account of the close connection, always confirmed and promoted by the Council, which human and temporal goods have with truly spiritual, religious, and eternal goods. The Church bends toward man and the world, but at the same time is raised to the kingdom of God. The men of our times, whose mental habits impel them to measure the excellence of things from their conveniences, ought to acknowledge the great weight and importance of the Ecumenical Council, at least for this cause, because it tended to the utility of man. Let no one therefore ever affirm that the catholic religion is useless, which, when it expresses the most conscious and effective form of its action, that is, when it celebrates an Ecumenical Council, plainly declares at the same time that it is for man and favors his utilities. Which things being so, it must truly be confessed that the catholic religion and human life are joined by a friendly covenant, and that both conspire to one certain human good: namely, the catholic religion is for the human race, and the life of the human race is in a way for it. Indeed, it is to be called life on account of the excellent doctrine, and that perfect in every respect, which it hands down concerning man (is not man, left to himself, an arcane thing to himself?); which doctrine it therefore hands down because it draws it from the knowledge which it has of God. For that we may fully know man, true man, whole man, we must first know God Himself. To prove this, it now suffices to recall these burning words of St. Catherine of Siena: "In your nature, eternal God, I shall know my nature." Then the catholic religion is life, because it shows the nature of man and his supreme end, and attributes a fuller sense to him; finally, it is life, because it is to be held as the supreme law of life, and because it injects such a secret power into life itself as to make it truly divine.
¶506
Quodsi, Venerabiles Fratres ac dilecti filii, omnes, qui hic praesentes adestis, meminimus in vultu cuiusvis hominis, maxime si lacrimis ac doloribus effectus est translucidus, agnoscendum esse vultum Christi (cfr. Matth. 25, 40), Filii hominis; ac si in vultu Christi agnoscendus est vultus Patris caelestis, secundum illud: «Qui videt me, videt et Patrem» (Io. 14, 9), modus noster res humanas aestimandi mutatur in Christianismum, qui in Deum ut in medium totus dirigitur; ita ut rem hoc etiam modo enuntiare possimus: scilicet opus esse cognoscere hominem, ut cognoscatur Deus. Nonne igitur huius Concilii, quod in hominem praecipue ac studiose mentem intendit, est mundo huius aetatis nostrae proponere liberationem et consolationem, quibus, veluti per gradus possit ascendere? Nonne denique nos Concilium docet ratione simplici, nova, sollemni amare hominem, ut amemus Deum? Amare hominem, dicimus, non ut instrumentum, sed ut primum veluti finem, quo ad supremum finem, humanas res transcendentem, perveniamus. Itaque hoc Concilium totum vertitur in sua vi religiosa qua comprehenditur ac terminatur, cum aliud non sit quam invitamentum quoddam, vehemens et amicum, quo humanum genus vocatur ad inveniendum, ope fraterni amoris, illum Deum, «a quo averti cadere, in quem converti resurgere, in quo manere consistere . . ., in quem redire reviviscere, in qua habitare vivere est» (S. August., Sol. I, 1, 3; P.L. 32, 870). Hac profecto spe tenemur in exitu huius Concilii Oecumenici Vaticani Secundi et in exordio renovationis humanae ac religiosae, quam ut consideraret ac proveheret, illud sibi proposuit; hoc ut nobismet ipsis contingat, Venerabiles Fratres ac Patres Concilii, fore confidimus; idem obventurum esse speramus universo humano generi, quod magis amare et cui melius servire hic docti sumus.
But if, Venerable Brothers and beloved sons, all of you present here, we remember that in the face of any man, especially if it is made translucent by tears and sorrows, the face of Christ (cf. Mt 25:40), the Son of Man, is to be recognized; and if in the face of Christ the face of the heavenly Father is to be recognized, according to that: "He who sees me, sees the Father also" (Jn 14:9), our manner of evaluating human things is changed into Christianity, which is wholly directed to God as to its center; so that we can also express it in this way: namely, it is necessary to know man, to know God. Is it not, therefore, the part of this Council, which especially and studiously intended man, to propose liberation and consolation to the world of our age, by which as if by steps it may ascend? Does it not finally teach us by a simple, new, solemn way to love man, that we may love God? To love man, we say, not as an instrument, but as a first, as it were, end, by which we may come to the supreme end, transcending human things. Thus this Council is wholly turned in its religious force, by which it is comprehended and terminated, since it is nothing other than a certain invitation, vehement and friendly, by which the human race is called to find, by the help of fraternal love, that God, "from whom to turn away is to fall, to whom to be converted is to rise, in whom to remain is to stand... to whom to return is to revive, in whom to dwell is to live" (St. Augustine, Soliloquies I, 1, 3; P.L. 32, 870). With this hope, we are held at the end of this Second Vatican Ecumenical Council and at the beginning of the human and religious renewal, which it proposed to consider and promote; we trust that this may happen to ourselves, Venerable Brothers and Fathers of the Council; we hope that the same will befall the whole human race, which we have here learned to love more and to serve better.
¶507
Dum denique, ut haec feliciter eveniant, iterum invocamus deprecatores nostros Sanctos Ioannem Baptistam et Ioseph, Patronos oecumenicae Synodi, Sanctos Apostolos Petrum et Paulum, fundamenta ac praesidia Sanctae Ecclesiae, et una cum his Sanctum Ambrosium Episcopum, cuius festum diem hodie celebramus, per eum veluti coniungentes Ecclesiam Orientalem et Occidentalem, pariter et ex animo imploramus auxilium Beatae Mariae Virginis, Matris Christi, quae a Nobis etiam Mater Ecclesiae est appellata, atque una voce, unanimi Deo gratias agimus et gloriam eius praedicamus, qui est Deus vivus et verus, Deus unus et summus, Pater, Filius et Spiritus Sanctus. Amen.
Finally, that these things may happily come to pass, we again invoke as intercessors for us St. John the Baptist and St. Joseph, Patrons of the ecumenical Synod, the holy Apostles Peter and Paul, the foundations and defenses of the holy Church, and together with them St. Ambrose the Bishop, whose feast day we celebrate today, by him as it were joining the Eastern and Western Church, likewise we implore from the heart the help of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of Christ, who has been called by us also Mother of the Church, and with one voice, unanimous, we give thanks to God and proclaim His glory, who is the living and true God, the one and most high God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Amen.

ORATIO CLAUSIONIS PAULI PP. VI IN SOLEMNI SESSIONE CONCILII VATICANI II HABITA
Ad vos, eminentissimi Patres cardinales; ad vos, venerabiles fratres; ad vos, legati nationum; ad vos, viri urbis Romae; ad vos, auctoritates et cives universi terrarum orbis; ad vos, observatores a diversis christiana religione seiunctis communitatibus; ad vos, christifideles et filii hodie hic praesentes, etiam ad vos per orbem terrae dispersi nobiscumque fide et caritate coniuncti.
Your eminences, venerable brothers, representatives of governments, gentlemen of the city of Rome, authorities and citizens of the entire world! You, observers belonging to so many different Christian denominations, and you, faithful and sons here present, and you also scattered across the earth and united with us in faith and charity!
¶509
Audietis hac peracta sacra missa nonnulla legit documenta, quae, ut suum perficiat laborum cursum, concilium oecumenicum ad diversas hominum ordines dirigit, volens in ipsis significare immensam illam multitudinem formarum, quibus humana vita exprimitur. Audiétis insuper nostrum decretum, sive breviarium, quo Nos ipsum Concilium Vaticanum II terminatum et clausum esse declaramus. Hoc est momentum, breve momentum salutationis. Tum autem obmutescet vox nostra, conciliumque perfectum erit, haecque immensa et extraordinaria coetus concio dimittetur.
You will hear shortly, at the end of this holy Mass, a reading of some messages which, at the conclusion of its work, the ecumenical council is addressing to various categories of persons, intending to consider in them the countless forms in which human life finds expression. And you will also hear the reading of our official decree in which we declare terminated and closed the Second Vatican Ecumenical Council. This is a moment, a brief moment of greetings. Then, our voice will be silent. This council is completely terminated, this immense and extraordinary assembly is disbanded.
¶510
Quare salutatio nostra, quam vobis offerimus, peculiarem habet significationem; eam licet vobis quasi signare, non ut ab oratione avocentur, sed ut attentiore animo in hac praesenti celebratione maneatis.
Hence, this greeting which we address to you has particular significance, which we take the liberty of pointing out to you, not to distract you from prayer, but to occupy the better your attention in this present celebration.
¶511
Est haec salutatio primum omnium universalis. Dirigitur enim ad universos vos, qui huic sacro ritui adstatis et participatis: ad vos, venerabiles fratres episcopos; ad vos, legatos nationum; ad vos, populum Dei. Extenditur et dilatatur ad omnem mundum. Quomodo aliter eveniret, cum concilium oecumenicum, id est universale, dictum sit et sit? Quemadmodum campanae sonus per auras propagatur, ad singulos suae vibrationis ambitu comprehensos accedens, ita hoc momento pervenit nostra salutatio ad unumquemque vestrum. Ea ad eos, qui eam suscipiunt, et ad eos, qui eam non suscipiunt, velut supplicans resonat in auribus hominum. Nullus, in principio, ab hoc catholico Romanae urbis centro separatus habetur; in principio, omnes homines potest et debet salutare. Ecclesiae enim catholicae nemo exsul, nemo exclusus, nemo longinquus est. Quilibet, ad quem nostra salutatio dirigitur, vocatus, invitatus, et quadam ratione praesens est. Hic est sermo cordis amantis: omnis qui amatur praesens est! Atque nos, hoc praesertim tempore, vi universalis mandati pastoralis et apostolici, omnes, omnes homines diligimus.
This greeting is, before all, universal. It is addressed to all of you assisting and participating here in this sacred rite: to you, venerable brothers in the episcopate; to you, representatives of nations; to you, people of God. And it is extended and broadened to the entire world. How could it be otherwise if this council was said to be and is ecumenical, that is to say, universal? Just as the sound of the bell goes out through the skies, reaching each one within the radius of its sound waves, so at this moment does our greeting go out to each and every one of you. To those who receive it and to those who do not, it resounds pleadingly in the ear of every man. From this Catholic center of Rome, no one, in principle, is unreachable; in principle, all men can and must be reached. For the Catholic Church, no one is a stranger, no one is excluded, no one is far away. Every one to whom our greeting is addressed is one who is called, who is invited and who, in a certain sense, is present. This is the language of the heart of one who loves. Every loved one is present! And we, especially at this moment, in virtue of our universal pastoral and apostolic mandate, we love all, all men.
¶512
Hoc igitur dicimus vobis, bonae et fideles animae, quae, corpore ab hoc fidelium et gentium coetu absentes, spiritu hic praesentes estis cum vestris precibus. De vobis etiam cogitans, Papa, simul et vobiscum, hoc sublimis communionis universalis celebrat momentum.
Hence, we say this to you good and faithful souls who, absent in person from this gathering of believers and of nations, are here present in spirit with your prayer. The Pope is thinking of you too, and with you he celebrates this sublime moment of universal communion.
¶513
Hoc dicimus vobis, qui velut infirmitatum carceribus detenti patimini; quibus si deesset cordialis salutationis nostrae solacium, propter spiritualem solitudinem velut geminatum doleretis.
We say this to you, you who suffer like prisoners of your infirmities, to you who, if you were without the comfort of our heartfelt greeting, would, because of your spiritual solitude, experience a redoubling of your pain.
¶514
Hoc maxime dicimus vobis, fratres episcopi, qui nulla vestra culpa in concilio defuistis, et nunc vacua in vestrorum fratrum episcoporum ordinibus, multo autem magis in eorum et in nostro animo deseritis, vacuum quod ita nos affligit et iniquitates illas vituperat, quae vestram libertatem coartant; utinam hoc unum deesset quominus ad nostrum concilium veniretis.
This we say especially to you, brothers in the episcopate, who through no fault of your own were missing from the council and now leave voids in the ranks of your brother bishops and still more in their hearts and ours, a void which gives us such sufferings and which condemns the injustices which shackle your liberty — would that this were all that was wanting to enable you to come to our council.
¶515
Salvete, fratres, qui iniuste in silentio detinetis, in oppressione, in privatione legitimorum et sacrorum iurium, quae cuilibet honesto homini debentur, multo magis vobis, qui nihil aliud nisi bonum, pietatem et pacem perficitis. Vobis, fratribus impeditís et humiliatis, Ecclesia adest. Ecclesia vestris christifidelibus adest, et omnibus, qui vestram participabant condicionem! Idem faciat mundi etiam conscientia civilis!
Greetings to you, brothers, who are unjustly detained in silence, in oppression, and in the privation of the legitimate and sacred rights owed to every honest man, and much more to you who are the workmen of nothing but good, piety and peace. To hindered and humiliated brethren, the Church is with you. She is with your faithful and with all those who have a part in your painful condition! May this also be the civil conscience of the world!
¶516
Denique salutatio nostra universalis ad vos dirigitur, homines qui nos ignoratis, qui non intellegitis, qui nos non putatis utiles, necessarios, benevolos. Ad vos quoque dirigitur, homines qui, fortasse existimantes vos bene facere, nobis adversamini. Sincera salutatio, non arrogans salutatio, sed spe plena et, hodie, quaesumus, credite, aestimatione et amore referta.
Lastly, our universal greeting goes out to you, men who do not know us, men who do not understand us, men who do not regard us as useful, necessary or friendly. This greeting goes also to you, men who, while perhaps thinking they are doing good, are opposed to us. A sincere greeting, an unassuming greeting but one filled with hope and, today, please believe that it is filled with esteem and love.
¶517
Haec est salutatio nostra. Sed attentiores vos esse quaesumus, qui nos auditis. Considerare vos rogamus, quomodo nostra salutatio, aliter ac fieri solet in quotidiana vitae consuetudine, si forte distaremus vel colloquium intermitteremus, non ut relationem concluderet sed ut spiritualem coniunctionem, unde significationem suam et sonum ducit, confirmaret et, si opus esset, produceret. Nostra est salutatio non valedictoria quae separat, sed amicitiae quae manet, et quae, si hoc postuletur, nasci vult. Ac veluti in hac ipsa extrema interpretatione verbum internum invenit salutatio nostra, qua pervenire ad cor hominis cupit, ut tamquam suavis hospes illuc descendat et in interiori conscientiae silentio illa Domini solita et ineffabilia verba proferat: «Pacem meam do vobis, pacem relinquo vobis, non quomodo mundus dat pacem» — suum loquendi modum habet Christus in absconditis cordium —. Altera autem ex parte, vult esse coniunctio diversa et altior, cum non sit tantum mutuum colloquium inter eos qui sumus, verum etiam ex alio quodam praesente Domino, qui invisibilis quidem est, sed in ipsa tamen hominum conversatione operatur; illum autem invitat et rogat, ut suscitet in salutante et in salutato nova quaedam dona, quorum primum et maximum est caritas.
This is our greeting. But please be attentive, you who are listening to us. We ask you to consider how our greeting, differently from what ordinarily happens in day to day conversation, would serve to terminate a relationship of nearness or discourse. Our greeting tends to strengthen and, if necessary, to produce a spiritual relationship whence it draws its meaning and its voice. Ours is a greeting, not of farewell which separates, but of friendship which remains, and which, if so demanded, wishes to be born. It is even precisely in this last expression that our greeting, on the one hand, would desire to reach the heart of every man, to enter therein as a cordial guest and speak in the interior silence of your individual souls, the habitual and ineffable words of the Lord: “My peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you, but not as the world gives it” — Christ has His own special way of speaking in the secrets of hearts — and in the other hand, our greeting wants to be a different and higher relationship because it is not only a two-sided exchange of words among us people of this earth, but it also brings into the picture another present one, the Lord Himself, invisible but working in the framework of human relationships. It invites Him and begs of Him to arouse in him who greets and in him who is greeted new gifts of which the first and highest is charity.
¶518
Ecce, haec est salutatio nostra. Haec tamquam nova divinae caritatis scintilla in cordibus nostris assurgat, quae incendi possit principia, doctrinam et proposita, quae concilium ordinatione quadam edidit; quaeque, ita caritatis igne inflammata, in Ecclesia et in mundo realiter efficere possit eam cogitationum, actionum, agendi rationis, virium moralium, spei et laetitiae renovationem, quae ipsum concilii propositum fuit.
Behold, this is our greeting. May it rise as a new spark of divine charity in our hearts, a spark which may enkindle the principles, doctrine and proposals which the council has organized and which, thus inflamed by charity, may really produce in the Church and in the world that renewal of thoughts, activities, conduct, moral force and hope and joy which was the very scope of the council.
¶519
Quapropter in ideali ordine consistit salutatio nostra. An est somnium? Estne poesis? Estne sola locutio conventionalis, insubstantiali exaggeratione constans, ut saepe nobis contingit in cotidianis bene precandi formulis? Minime. Haec salutatio idealis est, sed non est irrealis. Hie enixe rogamus ut animum vestrum momentum aliud attendatis. Cum nos homines nostras cogitationes et vota ad idealem vitae notionem dirigimus, statim in utopiam, in falsam verbositatem, in augurationem vel deceptionem decidimus. Homo inexstinctum desiderium idealis et perfectae perfectionis servat, sed per se capax non est ad illam attingendum fortasse in conceptu, multo minus in experimento et realitate. Hoc novimus. Haec est hominis fabula, haec est fabula regis caduci.
Consequently, our greeting is in the ideal order. Is it a dream? Is it poetry? Is it only a conventional and meaningless exaggeration, as often happens in our day-to-day expression of good wishes? No. This greeting is ideal, but not unreal. Here we would ask for a further moment of your attention. When we men push our thoughts and our desires toward an ideal conception of life, we find ourselves immediately in a utopia, in rhetorical caricature, in illusion or delusion. Man preserves an unquenchable yearning toward ideal and total perfection, but of himself he is incapable of reaching it, perhaps not in concept or much less with experience or reality. This we know, it is the drama of man, the drama of the fallen king.
¶520
Sed videte quid hoc mane agatur. Dum oecumenicum concilium claudimus, Mariam Sanctissimam, Christi Matrem, proinde, ut alio tempore ediximus, Dei Matrem et nostram spiritualem Matrem, colimus. Mariam Sanctissimam, Immaculatam, ideoque innocentem, praeclaram, perfectam, colimus. Ipsa est mulier, vera mulier, quae simul idealis et realis est; creatura, in qua effigies Dei clara nitet absolutissima luce, absque ulla perturbatione, ut in qualibet alia hominis creatura evenit.
But note what is taking place here this morning. While we close the ecumenical council, we are honoring Mary Most Holy, the mother of Christ, and consequently, as we declared on another occasion, the mother of God and our spiritual mother. We are honoring Mary Most Holy, the Immaculate One, therefore innocent, stupendous, perfect. She is the woman, the true woman who is both ideal and real, the creature in whom the image of God is reflected with absolute clarity, without any disturbance, as happens in every other human creature.
¶521
Fortasse nonne in hanc Mariam, quae humilis soror nostra, simulque caelestis mater et regina, mundum et sacrum infinitae pulchritudinis speculum, oculos nostros convertentes, spiritualem concilii profectum et extremam nostram salutationem concludere possumus? Nonne hinc opus nostrum postconciliares exordium sumet? Nonne Mariae Immaculatae pulchritudo fiet nobis exemplar incitans ad virtutem, spes nos consolans?
Is it not perhaps in directing our gaze on this woman who is our humble sister and at the same time our heavenly mother and queen, the spotless and sacred mirror of infinite beauty, that we can terminate the spiritual ascent of the council and our final greeting? Is it not here that our post-conciliar work can begin? Does not the beauty of Mary Immaculate become for us an inspiring model, a comforting hope?
¶522
O fratres, o filii, o viri qui nos auditis, nos ita esse et nobis et vobis putamus. Hic est summus et, Deo dante, pretiosissimus noster nuntius.
Oh, brothers, sons, and gentlemen who are listening to us, we think it is so for us and for you. And this is our most exalted and, God willing, our most valuable greeting.
¶523
NUNTIUS AD MODERATORES RERUM PUBLICARUM
To Rulers (read by Achille Cardinal Lienart of Lille, France, assisted by Bernard Cardinal Alfrink of Utrecht, the Netherlands, and Giovanni Cardinal Colombo of Milan, Italy)
At this solemn moment, we, the Fathers of the 21st ecumenical council of the Catholic Church, on the point of disbanding after four years of prayer and work, with the full consciousness of our mission toward mankind, address ourselves respectively and confidently to those who hold in their hands the destiny of men on this earth, to all those who hold temporal power.
¶524
Hoc sollemni momento, Nos Patres Concilii XXI Oecumenici Ecclesiae catholicae, cum quattuor annis precibus et laboribus peractis iam sumus dimissuri, plena conscii missionis nostrae erga homines, reverenter et fidenter ad eos nos convertimus, qui in suis manibus tenent hominum sortem super terram, ad omnes qui politica potestate fruuntur.
We proclaim publicly: We do honor to your authority and your sovereignty, we respect your office, we recognize your just laws, we esteem those who make them and those who apply them. But we have a sacrosanct word to speak to you and it is this: Only God is great. God alone is the beginning and the end. God alone is the source of your authority and the foundation of your laws.
¶525
Palam profitemur: Nos vestram auctoritatem vestrumque principatum honoramus, vestrum officium observamus, vestras iustas leges agnoscimus, eos qui eas ferunt atque exsequuntur aestimamus. At sacrosanctum verbum vobis dicendum habemus: Solus Deus magnus est. Solus Deus est principium et finis. Solus Deus vestrae est auctoritatis fons et vestrarum legum fundamentum.
Your task is to be in the world the promoters of order and peace among men. But never forget this: It is God, the living and true God, who is the Father of men. And it is Christ, His eternal Son, who came to make this known to us and to teach us that we are all brothers. He it is who is the great artisan of order and peace on earth, for He it is who guides human history and who alone can incline hearts to renounce those evil passions which beget war and misfortune. It is He who blesses the bread of the human race, who sanctifies its work and its suffering, who gives it those joys which you can never give it, and strengthens it in those sufferings which you cannot console.
¶526
Munus vestrum est mundi esse effectores ordinis et pacis inter homines. At numquam hoc obliviscimini: Deus est, vivus et verus Deus, pater hominum. Et Christus est, eius Filius aeternus, qui hoc nobis notum facere venit nosque omnes fratres esse docuit. Ipse est magnus opifex ordinis et pacis in terris; Ipse enim historiam humanam dirigit, et solus potest corda inclinare ad renuntiandas illas malas cupiditates, quae bellum pariunt et mala. Ipse est qui panem hominum benedicit, eorum labores et dolores sanctificat, datque eos gaudia, quae ei vos dare numquam potestis, eosque confirmat in doloribus, quos vos solari nequitis.
In your earthly and temporal city, God constructs mysteriously His spiritual and eternal city, His Church. And what does this Church ask of you after close to 2,000 years of experiences of all kinds in her relations with you, the powers of the earth? What does the Church ask of you today? She tells you in one of the major documents of this council. She asks of you only liberty, the liberty to believe and to preach her faith, the freedom to love her God and serve Him, the freedom to live and to bring to men her message of life. Do not fear her. She is made after the image of her Master, whose mysterious action does not interfere with your prerogatives but heals everything human of its fatal weakness, transfigures it and fills it with hope, truth and beauty.
¶527
In vestra terrestri et temporali civitate, Deus suam spiritualem et aeternam civitatem aedificat, Ecclesiam suam. Et quid haec Ecclesia postulat a vobis, post duo fere annorum milia experimentorum omnium generis in suis necessitudinibus cum vobis, potestatibus huius terrae? Quid hodie Ecclesia a vobis petit? Unis vobis e vobis in magnis huius Concilii documentis rem aperit. Libertatem tantum rogat, libertatem credendi et suam fidem praedicandi, libertatem Deum suum amandi et Ipsi serviendi, libertatem vivendi et hominibus nuntium vitae suae deferendi. Nolite eam timere. Condita est ad imaginem Magistri sui, cuius arcana actio vestris praerogativis non officit, sed omne id quod humanum est a fatali imbecillitate medetur, illud transfigurat, et spe veritate pulchritudineque complet.
Allow Christ to exercise His purifying action on society. Do not crucify Him anew. This would be a sacrilege for He is the Son of God. This would be suicide for He is the Son of man. And we, His humble ministers, allow us to spread everywhere without hindrance the Gospel of peace on which we have meditated during this council. Of it, your peoples will be the first beneficiaries, since the Church forms for you loyal citizens, friends of social peace and progress.
¶528
Christum sinite suam purificantem actionem in societatem exercere. Nolite Ipsum iterum crucifigere. Id sacrilegium esset, ipse cum Filius Dei sit. Id suicidium esset, ipse cum Filius hominis sit. Et nos, Eius humiles ministri, nobis permittite ut passim sine impedimento Evangelium pacis, de quo in hoc Concilio meditati sumus, diffundamus. Eius populi vestri primi beneficiarii erunt, quandoquidem Ecclesia vobis cives fideles creat, amicos socialis pacis et progressionis.
On this solemn day when she closes the deliberations of her 21st ecumenical council, the Church offers you through our voice her friendship, her services, her spiritual and moral forces. She addresses to you all her message of salvation and blessing. Accept it, as she offers it to you with a joyous and sincere heart and pass it on to your peoples.
¶529
Hac sollemni die, qua XXI sui Concilii Oecumenici deliberationes claudit, Ecclesia vobis per nostram vocem suam amicitiam, sua officia, suas spirituales et morales vires offert. Ad vos totum suum salutis et benedictionis nuntium dirigit. Eum accipite, prout vobis eum laeto et sincero animo offert, et vestris populis transmittite.
¶530
NUNTIUS AD VIROS COGITATIONIS ET SCIENTIAE
To Men of Thought and Science (read by Paul Emile Cardinal Leger of Montreal, assisted by Antonio Cardinal Caggiano of Buenos Aires and Norman Cardinal Gilroy of Sydney, Australia)
A very special greeting to you, seekers after truth, to you, men of thought and science, the explorers of man, of the universe and of history, to all of you who are pilgrims enroute to the light and to those also who have stopped along the road, tired and disappointed by their vain search.
¶531
Plane peculiaris est vobis salutatio, vos veritatis inquisitores, vos viri cogitationis et scientiae, investigatores hominis, universi et historiae, vos omnes, qui ad lucem peregrinamini, atque illis etiam, qui in via subsistentes, fessi et vana indagatione irritati.
Why a special greeting for you? Because all of us here, bishops and Fathers of the council, are on the lookout for truth. What have our efforts amounted to during these four years except a more attentive search for and deepening of the message of truth entrusted to the Church and an effort at more perfect docility to the spirit of truth.
¶532
Cur peculiaris vobis salutatio? Quia omnes nos, hic, episcopi et Patres conciliares, veritatem intendimus exspectamusque. Quid valuerunt horum quattuor annorum conatus nostri, nisi ut attentior quaereretur ac profundius patefieret veritatis nuntius Ecclesiae concreditus, atque ut eniteremur ad perfectionem docilitatis erga Spiritum veritatis?
Hence our paths could not fail to cross. Your road is ours. Your paths are never foreign to ours. We are the friends of your vocation as searchers, companions in your fatigues, admirers of your successes and, if necessary, consolers in your discouragement and your failures.
¶533
Quare semitae nostrae inter se congredi non potuerunt quin coniungerentur. Via vestra nostra est. Vestra itinera numquam ab nostra aliena sunt. Nos amici sumus vestrae vocationis indagatorum, socii laborum vestrorum, miratores successuum vestrorum et, si opus fuerit, consolatores in animorum demissione et in vestris inceptis frustratis.
Hence for you also we have a message and it is this: Continue your search without tiring and without ever despairing of the truth. Recall the words of one of your great friends, St. Augustine: “Let us seek with the desire to find, and find with the desire to seek still more.” Happy are those who, while possessing the truth, search more earnestly for it in order to renew it, deepen it and transmit it to others. Happy also are those who, not having found it, are working toward it with a sincere heart. May they seek the light of tomorrow with the light of today until they reach the fullness of light.
¶534
Quapropter etiam vobis nuntium habemus atque hic est: Pergite inquisitionem vestram sine fatigatione et unquam de veritate desperantes. Memoriam revocate verborum magni cuiusdam amici vestri, S. Augustini: «Quaeramus inveniendi desiderio et inveniamus quaerendi desiderio.» Beati qui, veritatem possidentes, eam studiosius quaerunt ut renovent eam, altius perscrutentur et transmittant aliis. Beati quoque qui, nondum inventa, sincero animo ad eam contendunt. Lucem crastini diei cum hodiernae luce ita quaerant ut ad plenitudinem lucis perveniant.
But do not forget that if thinking is something great, it is first a duty. Woe to him who voluntarily closes his eyes to the light. Thinking is also a responsibility, so woe to those who darken the spirit by the thousand tricks which degrade it, make it proud, deceive and deform it. What other basic principle is there for men of science except to think rightly?
¶535
At nolite oblivisci: si cogitare magnum aliquid est, est tamen prius officium. Vae illi qui sponte claudit oculos suos ad lucem. Cogitare etiam responsalitas est, vae illis qui obscurant animum mille artibus, quae illi officiunt, quae superbire faciunt, quae decipiunt et depravant. Quodnam aliud principium pro viris scientiae exstat nisi ut recte cogitent?
For this purpose, without troubling your efforts, without dazzling brilliance, we come to offer you the light of our mysterious lamp which is faith. He who entrusted this lamp to us is the sovereign Master of all thought, He whose humble disciples we are, the only one who said and could have said: “I am the light of the world, I am the way, the truth and the life.”
¶536
Hac ratione, vestros conatus non turbantes, sine splendore vos praestringente, venimus vobis lucem nostrae mysteriosae lampadis offerre, quae fides est. Qui hanc lampadem nobis concredidit, est summus Magister omnis cogitationis, cuius humiles discipuli sumus, Unus qui dixit eique dicere licuit: «Ego sum lux mundi, via, veritas et vita.»
These words have meaning for you. Never perhaps, thank God, has there been so clear a possibility as today of a deep understanding between real science and real faith, mutual servants of one another in the one truth. Do not stand in the way of this important meeting. Have confidence in faith, this great friend of intelligence. Enlighten yourselves with its light in order to take hold of truth, the whole truth. This is the wish, the encouragement and the hope, which, before disbanding, is expressed to you by the Fathers of the entire world assembled at Rome in council.
¶537
Haec verba vobis dicunt aliquid. Numquam fortasse, Deo gratias, tam aperta fuit facultas ac hodie profundae necessitudinis inter veram scientiam veramque fidem, sibi invicem ancillantes in una veritate. Ne huic magno occursui obstaculum ponatis. Fiduciam habete in fide, hac magna intellegentiae amica. Eius lumine vos illuminate ut veritatem comprehendatis, omnem veritatem. Hoc votum est, hoc invitamentum, haec spes quae, priusquam concilium dimittatur, a Patribus universi orbis Romae in Concilium congregatis vobis exprimitur.
¶538
NUNTIUS AD ARTIFICES
To Artists (read by Leo Cardinal Suenens of Malines Brussels, Belgium, assisted by Lawrence Cardinal Shehan of Baltimore and Jaime Cardinal de Barros Camara of Rio de Janeiro)
We now address you, artists, who are taken up with beauty and work for it: poets and literary men, painters, sculptors, architects, musicians, men devoted to the theater and the cinema. To all of you, the Church of the council declares to you through our voice: if you are friends of genuine art, you are our friends.
¶539
Ad vos nunc convertimur, artifices, qui pulchritudine occupamini eique operam datis: poetae et litterarum scriptores, pictores, sculptores, architecti, musici, homines theatro ac cinematographiae dediti. Ad vos universos Ecclesia, Concilii Patrum nomine, nostra vobiscum voce loquitur: si verae artis estis amici, nostri estis amici.
The Church has long since joined in alliance with you. You have built and adorned her temples, celebrated her dogmas, enriched her liturgy. You have aided her in translating her divine message in the language of forms and figures, making the invisible world palpable. Today, as yesterday, the Church needs you and turns to you. She tells you through our voice: Do not allow an alliance as fruitful as this to be broken. Do not refuse to put your talents at the service of divine truth. Do not close your mind to the breath of the Holy Spirit.
¶540
Ecclesia iam dudum vobiscum quasi in societatem coniuncta est. Vos eius templa aedificastis et ornastis, dogmata celebravistis, liturgiam locupletastis. Eam adiuvistis, ut nuntium suum divinum transferret in linguam formarum et figurarum, rerum invisibilium efficientes veluti palpabiles. Hodie sicut heri Ecclesia vobis indiget et ad vos convertitur. Hoc vobis per nostram vocem dicit: Ne patiamini ut tam fructuosa societas dissolvatur. Nolite vestra ingenia divinae veritati servire recusare. Nolite animum vestrum obcludere Spiritui Sancto inspiranti.
This world in which we live needs beauty in order not to sink into despair. It is beauty, like truth, which brings joy to the heart of man and is that precious fruit which resists the wear and tear of time, which unites generations and makes them share things in admiration. And all of this is through your hands. May these hands be pure and disinterested. Remember that you are the guardians of beauty in the world. May that suffice to free you from tastes which are passing and have no genuine value, to free you from the search after strange or unbecoming expressions. Be always and everywhere worthy of your ideals and you will be worthy of the Church which, by our voice, addresses to you today her message of friendship, salvation, grace and benediction.
¶541
Hic mundus, in quo vivimus, eget pulchritudine, ne in desperationem ruat. Pulchritudo est, sicut et veritas, quae gaudium cordi hominis infert, estque pretiosus fructus, qui vetustati obsistit et consummationi, qui generationes coniungit et eas facit admirationis participes. Atque haec omnia manibus vestris perficiuntur. Sint hae purae et gratuitae. Mementote vos pulchritudinis in mundo esse custodes. Hoc sufficiat ut vos expediat a saporibus fluxis nihilque vere valentibus, ut vos expediat ab indagatione novarum ineptarumve formarum. Estote semper et ubique vestris idealibus digni, et eritis digni Ecclesia, quae, hac nostra voce, hodie ad vos mittit suum amicitiae, salutis, gratiae et benedictionis nuntium.

NUNTIUS AD MULIERES
To Women (read by Leon Cardinal Duval of Algiers, Algeria, assisted by Julius Cardinal Doepfner of Munich, Germany, and Raul Cardinal Silva of Santiago, Chile)
And now it is to you that we address ourselves, women of all states — girls, wives, mothers and widows, to you also, consecrated virgins and women living alone — you constitute half of the immense human family. As you know, the Church is proud to have glorified and liberated woman, and in the course of the centuries, in diversity of characters, to have brought into relief her basic equality with man. But the hour is coming, in fact has come, when the vocation of woman is being achieved in its fullness, the hour in which woman acquires in the world an influence, an effect and a power never hitherto achieved. That is why, at this moment when the human race is undergoing so deep a transformation, women impregnated with the spirit of the Gospel can do so much to aid mankind in not falling.
¶543
Et nunc ad vos convertimur, mulieres cuiuslibet condicionis — puellae, uxores, matres et viduae — ad vos etiam, virgines sacrae ac mulieres solae viventes — vos constituitis dimidium immensae familiae humanae. Perlibenter, ut nostis, Ecclesia gloriatur se mulierem glorificasse et liberasse atque, decursu saeculorum, pro varietate ingeniorum, effecisse ut praecipua eius cum viro aequalitas in lucem proferretur. Sed hora venit, immo iam venit, qua mulieris vocatio ad plenitudinem suam adducitur, hora qua mulier in mundo consecutura est vim, efficacitatem potentiamque, qualis numquam antea est consecuta. Quapropter, hoc momento, cum humanum genus tam profundam transformationem subit, mulieres, evangelico spiritu imbutae, plurimum possunt ad homines adiuvandos, ne ruant.
You women have always had as your lot the protection of the home, the love of beginnings and an understanding of cradles. You are present in the mystery of a life beginning. You offer consolation in the departure of death. Our technology runs the risk of becoming inhuman. Reconcile men with life and above all, we beseech you, watch carefully over the future of our race. Hold back the hand of man who, in a moment of folly, might attempt to destroy human civilization.
¶544
Vos mulieres vestrae sortis semper habuistis tutelam domus, amorem initiorum, cunarum sapientem curam. Adestis in vitae nascentis mysterio. In mortis autem digressu solacium praebetis. Periculum est ne technica nostra ratio evadat inhumana. Reconciliate homines cum vita et, ante omnia, vos obsecramus, diligenter prospicite futuro generis humani. Manum viri cohibete, qui momento dementiae suae humanam文明em delere temptaret.
Wives, mothers of families, the first educators of the human race in the intimacy of the family circle, pass on to your sons and your daughters the traditions of your fathers at the same time that you prepare them for an unsearchable future. Always remember that by her children a mother belongs to that future which perhaps she will not see.
¶545
Uxores, matres familiarum, primae humani generis educatrices in familiari convictu ac secretiore, tradite filiis et filiabus vestris maiorum vestrorum traditiones, simul autem eas parate ad inscrutabile futurum. Mementote semper matrem per suos liberos ad illud futurum pertinere, quod forsitan ipsa non videbit.
And you, women living alone, realize what you can accomplish through your dedicated vocation. Society is appealing to you on all sides. Not even families can live without the help of those who have no families. Especially you, consecrated virgins, in a world where egoism and the search for pleasure would become law, be the guardians of purity, unselfishness and piety. Jesus who has given to conjugal love all its plenitudes, has also exalted the renouncement of human love when this is for the sake of divine love and for the service of all.
¶546
Et vos, mulieres solae viventes, percipite quid efficere possitis per vestram deditam vocationem. Societas undique vos appellat. Ne familiae quidem sine auxilio vivere possunt earum qui carent familia. Maxime vos, virgines sacrae, in mundo, in quo egoismus et voluptatis appetitus lex fieret, estote custodes puritatis, abnegationis et pietatis. Iesus qui amori coniugali plenitudines suas omnes tribuit, etiam renuntiationem amoris humani extulit, cum haec divino amori et servitio omnium fiat.
Lastly, women in trial, who stand upright at the foot of the cross like Mary, you who so often in history have given to men the strength to battle unto the very end and to give witness to the point of martyrdom, aid them now still once more to retain courage in their great undertakings, while at the same time maintaining patience and an esteem for humble beginnings.
¶547
Postremo, vos mulieres in tribulatione, quae pedi Crucis veluti Maria erectae stabatis, vos quae in historia totiens viribus homines donastis, ut ad extremum usque pugnarent et usque ad martyrium testimonium perhiberent, eos hodie rursus adiuvate ad retinendam fortitudinem in magnis inceptis, servata simul patientia et erga humilia initia observantia.
Women, you do know how to make truth sweet, tender and accessible, make it your task to bring the spirit of this council into institutions, schools, homes and daily life. Women of the entire universe, whether Christian or non-believing, you to whom life is entrusted at this grave moment in history, it is for you to save the peace of the world.
¶548
Mulieres, vos veritatem suavem, mitem et accessibilem facere scitis, curate ut huius Concilii spiritus in instituta, scholas, domos et cotidianam vitam inducatur. Mulieres totius universi, sive christianae sive non credentes, quibus hoc gravi historiae momento vita concreditur, vestrum est pacem mundi servare.
¶549
NUNTIUS AD PAUPERES, INFIRMOS ET ADFLICTOS
To the Poor, the Sick and the Suffering (read by Paul Cardinal Meouchi, Maronite-rite patriarch of Antioch; assisted by Stefan Cardinal Wyszynski of Warsaw and Peter Cardinal Doi of Tokyo)
To all of you, brothers in trial, who are visited by suffering under a thousand forms, the council has a very special message. It feels on itself your pleading eyes, burning with fever or hollow with fatigue, questioning eyes which search in vain for the why of human suffering and which ask anxiously when and whence will come relief.
¶550
Ad vos omnes, fratres in tentatione constituti, quos mille modis vis doloris adit, peculiarem habet nuntium Concilium. Sentit in se vestros supplicantes oculos vel morbo ardentes vel lassitudine cavos, interrogantes oculos qui frustra humanorum dolorum quaerunt quare et sollicite rogant quando et unde veniet levamen.
Very dear brothers, we feel echoing deeply within our hearts as fathers and pastors your laments and your complaints. Our suffering is increased at the thought that it is not within our power to bring you bodily help nor the lessening of your physical sufferings, which physicians, nurses and all those dedicated to the service of the sick are endeavoring to relieve as best they can.
¶551
Carissimi fratres, penitus in cordibus nostris, ut patrum et pastorum, querelas vestras et conquestiones vestras resonantes sentimus. Dolor noster augetur cogitantes non esse in nostra potestate vobis ferre adiumentum corporale neque minorationem corporalium dolorum vestrorum, quam medici, aegrorum ministrae omnesque aegrorum servitio dediti, pro viribus mitigare student.
But we have something deeper and more valuable to give you, the only truth capable of answering the mystery of suffering and of bringing you relief without illusion, and that is faith and union with the Man of Sorrows, with Christ the Son of God, nailed to the cross for our sins and for our salvation. Christ did not do away with suffering. He did not even wish to unveil to us entirely the mystery of suffering. He took suffering upon Himself and this is enough to make you understand all its value. All of you who feel heavily the weight of the cross, you who are poor and abandoned, you who weep, you who are persecuted for justice, you who are ignored, you the unknown victims of suffering, take courage. You are the preferred children of the kingdom of God, the kingdom of hope, happiness and life. You are the brothers of the suffering Christ, and with Him, if you wish, you are saving the world.
¶552
At habemus altius aliquid et pretiosius vobis donandum, unicam veritatem doloris mysterio respondentem et vobis sine fallacia levamen afferentem: fidem nempe et coniunctionem cum Viro dolorum, cum Christo, Dei Filio, propter nostra peccata et propter nostram salutem Cruci affixo. Christus non abstulit dolorem. Neque nobis totum doloris aperire mysterium voluit. In se suscepit dolorem et hoc satis est ut vos omnem eius valorem intellegatis. Vos omnes qui crucis pondus graviter sentitis, vos pauperes et derelicti, vos qui fletis, vos qui propter iustitiam persecutionem patimini, vos qui ignoramini, vos ignotae victimae doloris, bono animo estote. Estis praedilecti filii regni Dei, regni spei, laetitiae et vitae. Estis fratres Christi dolentis et cum Ipso, si vultis, mundum salvificatis.
This is the Christian science of suffering, the only one which gives peace. Know that you are not alone, separated, abandoned or useless. You have been called by Christ and are His living and transparent image. In His name, the council salutes you lovingly, thanks you, assures you of the friendship and assistance of the Church, and blesses you.
¶553
Haec est christiana doloris scientia, unica quae pacem donat. Scitote vos non solos esse, seiunctos, derelictos aut inutiles. A Christo vocati estis estisque Eius viva et perspicua imago. In Eius nomine, Concilium vos amanter salutat, vobis gratias agit, amicitiae et adiumenti Ecclesiae vos certiores facit, vobisque benedicit.
¶554
NUNTIUS AD OPERARIOS
To Workers (read by Paul Cardinal Zoungrana of Ouagadougou, Upper Volta, assisted by Jose Cardinal Quintero of Caracas, Venezuela, and Jose Cardinal Bueno y Monreal of Seville, Spain)
In the course of this council, we, the Catholic bishops of the five continents, have, among many other subjects, reflected together on the grave questions posed for human conscience by the economic and social conditions of the contemporary world, the coexistence of nations, the problem of armaments, of war and peace. We are fully aware of the repercussions which the solution provided for these problems can have on the concrete life of the working men and women of the entire world. Thus, at the end of our deliberations, we wish to address to all of them a message of confidence, peace and friendship.
¶555
In hoc Concilii decursu, Nos, catholici Episcopi quinque continentium, inter multa alia simul cogitavimus de gravibus quaestionibus, quae humanae conscientiae ab oeconomicis et socialibus condicionibus hodierni mundi, a nationum coexistentia, a quaestione armorum, belli et pacis proponuntur. Probe conscii sumus repercussionum quas harum problematum solutio afferre potest in concretam operariorum et operariarum totius orbis vitam. Itaque, exeuntibus nostris deliberationibus, ad eos omnes nuntium fiduciae, pacis et amicitiae dirigere cupimus.
Very loved sons, rest assured first of all that the Church is aware of your sufferings, your struggles and your hopes, and that she appreciates highly the virtues which ennoble your souls — namely courage, dedication, professional conscience, love of justice — and that she recognizes fully the immense services which, each in his own place and in positions often the most obscure and the most ignored, you render to the whole of society. The Church is grateful to you for this and thanks you through our voice.
¶556
Dilectissimi filii, imprimis hoc certum habete: Ecclesiam dolores vestros, certationes vestras spesque vestras non latere eamque magni aestimare virtutes illas, quae animos vestros nobilitant — id est animum, dedicationem, conscientiam professionalem, iustitiae amorem — planeque agnoscere immensa officia, quae, unusquisque suo in loco et saepe in obscurissimis neglectissimisque muneribus, toti societati praestatis. Hac de re gratam esse Ecclesiam vobisque per nostram vocem gratias agere.
In these recent years, she has never ceased to keep before her eyes the increasingly complex problems of the working world and the echo which recent pontifical encyclicals have found in your ranks has proved to what degree the soul of the working man of our time was attuned to that of his highest spiritual leaders. Pope John XXIII who enriched the patrimony of the Church with his incomparable messages knew how to find the road to your heart. He, in his own person, gave a shining example of the Church's love for the working man as well as for truth, justice, liberty and charity, on which is founded the peace of the world. We wish also to be before you witnesses of this love of the Church for you working men, and we declare to you with all the conviction of our souls: The Church is your friend. Have confidence in her. In the past, regrettable misunderstandings have, over too long a period, maintained a spirit of mistrust and lack of understanding between us, and both the Church and the working class have suffered from this. Today the hour for reconciliation has sounded and the Church of the council invites you to celebrate this hour without suspicion.
¶557
Hisce proximis annis, numquam destitit ante oculos habere problemata, magis in dies implicata, operarii mundi necnon argumento fuit resonantis in vestris ordinibus recentium Litterarum Encyclicarum Pontificiarum, qua probatum est quatenus animus hodierni operarii cum animo summi sui ducis spiritalis consonaret. Summus Pontifex Ioannes XXIII, qui inaestimabilibus suis nuntiis Ecclesiae patrimonium auxit, viam ad cor vestrum invenire novit. Ipse sua in persona praeclarum dedit exemplum amoris Ecclesiae erga operarium necnon erga veritatem, iustitiam, libertatem et caritatem, in quibus pax mundi fundatur. Nos quoque huius amoris Ecclesiae in vos operarios testes esse cupimus vobisque omni animi nostri persuasione declaramus: Ecclesia amica vestra est. In ea fiduciam habete. Praeterito tempore, dolendi errores, nimium diu diffidentiae et inintellegentiae inter nos animum foverunt, et ex eo tam Ecclesia quam operaria classis passa est. Hodie reconciliatonis hora cecinit et Ecclesia Concilii vos invitat ut hanc horam sine suspicione celebretis.
The Church is ever seeking to understand you better. But on your part you must endeavor to understand what the Church means for you, working men, who are the chief artisans of the prodigious changes which the world is undergoing today. For you know full well that unless a mighty spiritual inspiration animates these changes, they will cause disaster for humanity instead of bringing it happiness. It is not hatred which serves the world. It is not only the bread of this earth which can satisfy man's hunger. Thus, accept the message of the Church. Accept the faith which she offers you to light your path. It is the faith of the successor of Peter and of the 2,000 bishops assembled in council. It is the faith of the Christian people. May it be your light. May it be your guide. May it bring you to the knowledge of Jesus Christ, your Companion in work, Master and Savior of the whole human race.
¶558
Ecclesia contendit semper ut vos melius intellegat. Sed vos, vicissim, eniti debetis ad intellegendum quid Ecclesia vobis operariis significet, qui praecipui estis effectores mirabilium mutationum, quibus hodie mundus subicitur. Optime enim nostis, nisi ingens spiritalis animatio has mutationes vivificet, hominibus potius exitium, quam laetitiam ea allaturas esse. Non odium mundo prodest. Non solus panis terrae famem hominis explere potest. Itaque nuntium Ecclesiae accipite. Accipite fidem, quam vobis offert, ut vestram illustret viam. Fides est Petri successoris et duorum milium episcoporum in Concilium congregationum. Fides est populi christiani. Haec vestra lux sit; haec sit vobis dux; haec vos perducat ad Iesum Christum cognoscendum, socium vestrum in labore, Magistrum et Salvatorem totius generis humani.
¶559
NUNTIUS AD IUVENES
To Youth (read by Gregorio Cardinal Agagianian of the Roman curia, assisted by Joseph Cardinal Ritter of St. Louis and Valerian Cardinal Gracias of Bombay)
Lastly, it is to you, young men and women of the world, that the council wishes to address its final message. For it is you who are to receive the torch from the hands of your elders and to live in the world at the period of the most gigantic transformations ever realized in its history. It is you who, receiving the best of the example of the teaching of your parents and your teachers, are to form the society of tomorrow. You will either save yourselves or you will perish with it.
¶560
Denique ad vos, iuvenes viri ac mulieres mundi, vult Concilium suum ultimum dirigere nuntium. Vos enim estis, qui facem de manibus maiorum accepturi estis et in mundo victuri tempore immanissimarum transformationum, quae umquam in historia factae sunt. Vos estis, qui, optimo accepto exemplorum ac magisterii parentum praeceptorumque vestrorum, societatem crastini diei formaturi estis. Aut vosmet ipsos servabitis, aut una cum ea peribitis.
For four years the Church has been working to rejuvenate her image in order to respond the better to the design of her Founder, the great Living One, the Christ who is eternally young. At the term of this imposing re-examination of life, she now turns to you. It is for you, youth, especially for you that the Church now comes through her council to enkindle your light, the light which illuminates the future, your future. The Church is anxious that this society that you are going to build up should respect the dignity, the liberty and the rights of individuals. These individuals are you. The Church is particularly anxious that this society should allow free expansion to her treasure ever ancient and ever new, namely faith, and that your souls may be able to bask freely in its helpful light. She has confidence that you will find such strength and such joy that you will not be tempted, as were some of your elders, to yield to the seductions of egoistic or hedonistic philosophies or to those of despair and annihilation, and that in the face of atheism, a phenomenon of lassitude and old age, you will know how to affirm your faith in life and in what gives meaning to life, that is to say, the certitude of the existence of a just and good God.
¶561
Iam per quadriennium Ecclesia laboravit, ut effigiem suam redderet iuveniliorem, eo melius ut Conditoris sui consilio responderet, Magni Viventis, Christi, qui est aeternum iuvenis. Exeunte hac magna recognitione vitae, nunc ad vos se vertit. Ad vos, iuvenes, potissimum nunc Ecclesia venit, per suum Concilium, ad vestram accendendam lucem, lucem quae futurum illuminat, vestrum futurum. Ecclesiae curae est ut societas, quam constructuri estis, dignitatem, libertatem et iura personarum observet. Hae personae vos estis. Peculiari sollicitudine Ecclesia urgetur ut eius thesaurus, antiquus semper semperque novus, nempe fides, libere in hac societate dilatetur, eiusque salutari lumine animi vestri libere frui possint. Firmam fiduciam habet vos tantam fortitudinem tantumque gaudium inventuros, ut — quemadmodum aliqui natu maiores vestri — neque philosophiarum egoisticarum aut voluptariae consectationis neque desperationis ac perditionis illecebris cedatis, et ut, coram atheismo, defatigationis et senectutis phaenomeno, fidem vestram in vitam et in id quod vitae sensum tribuit, scilicet certitudinem exsistentiae iusti et boni Dei, affirmare sciatis.
It is in the name of this God and of His Son, Jesus, that we exhort you to open your hearts to the dimensions of the world, to heed the appeal of your brothers, to place your youthful energies at their service. Fight against all egoism. Refuse to give free course to the instincts of violence and hatred which beget wars and all their train of miseries. Be generous, pure, respectful and sincere, and build in enthusiasm a better world than your elders had.
¶562
In huius Dei Eiusque Filii Iesu nomine vos adhortamur ut corda vestra amplitudinibus mundi aperiatis, ut fratrum vestrorum appellationi aurem praebeatis, ut vestras iuveniles vires eorum servitio addicatis. Pugnate contra omnem egoismum. Nolite liberum cursum instinctibus violentiae et odii permittere, quae bella pariunt et omnem miseriarum comitatum. Este generosi, puri, observantes, sinceri, et aedificate studio pleni mundum meliorem eo quem maiores vestri habuerunt.
The Church looks to you with confidence and with love. Rich with a long past ever living in her, and marching on toward human perfection in time and the ultimate destinies of history and of life, the Church is the real youth of the world. She possesses what constitutes the strength and the charm of youth, that is to say the ability to rejoice with what is beginning, to give oneself unreservedly, to renew one's self and to set out again for new conquests. Look upon the Church and you will find in her the face of Christ, the genuine, humble and wise Hero, the prophet of truth and love, the companion and friend of youth. It is in the name of Christ that we salute you, that we exhort and bless you.
¶563
Ecclesia vos respicit cum fiducia et cum amore. Dives longi praeteriti, in se semper viventis, et ad humanam perfectionem in tempore contendens et ad ultimos historiae vitaeque fines, Ecclesia vera est iuventus mundi. Ea possidet quod iuventutis robur ac venustatem constituit, scilicet facultatem gaudendi cum eo quod incipit, se sine ulla retractione donandi, se renovandi, et ad novas expugnationes proficiscendi. In Ecclesiam intuemini et in ea reperietis vultum Christi, germanum, humilem sapientemque Heroem, prophetam veritatis et amoris, comitem et amicum iuventutis. In Christi nomine vos salutamus, vos adhortamur, vobis benedicimus.
The Second Vatican Ecumenical Council, assembled in the Holy Spirit and under the protection of the Blessed Virgin Mary, whom we have declared Mother of the Church, and of St. Joseph, her glorious spouse, and of the Apostles SS. Peter and Paul, must be numbered without doubt among the greatest events of the Church. In fact it was the largest in the number of Fathers who came to the seat of Peter from every part of the world, even from those places where the hierarchy has been very recently established. It was the richest because of the questions which for four sessions have been discussed carefully and profoundly. And last of all it was the most opportune, because, bearing in mind the necessities of the present day, above all it sought to meet the pastoral needs and, nourishing the flame of charity, it has made a great effort to reach not only the Christians still separated from communion with the Holy See, but also the whole human family.

Apostolic Brief "In Spiritu Sancto" for the Closing of the Council, read at the closing ceremonies of December 8, 1965, by Archbishop Pericle Felici, General Secretary of the Council.
LITTERAE APOSTOLICAE IN SPIRITU SANCTO QUIBUS CONCILIUM VATICANUM II CLAUDITUR
Concilium Oecumenicum Vaticanum II, in Spiritu Sancto congregatum et sub praesidio Beatae Mariae Virginis, quam Matrem Ecclesiae declaramus, et sancti Ioseph, eiusdem gloriosi Sponsi, et sanctorum Apostolorum Petri et Pauli, inter maxima Ecclesiae eventa haud dubie est numerandum. Fuit enim numero maximum propter Patres, qui ad Petri sedem ex omnibus terrarum partibus convenerunt, iis etiam ex locis, ubi Episcoporum ordo recentissime est constitutus; ditissimum propter quaestiones, quae per quattuor sessiones accurate ac penitus sunt discussae; postremo aptissimum, quia, habita ratione hodiernarum necessitatum, in primis pastoralibus necessitatibus prospicere studuit et, caritatis flamma refovens, magnopere contendit ut non solum christianos, qui adhuc ab Apostolicae Sedis communione seiuncti sunt, sed universam etiam humanam familiam attingeret.
At last all which regards the holy ecumenical council has, with the help of God, been accomplished and all the constitutions, decrees, declarations and votes have been approved by the deliberation of the synod and promulgated by us. Therefore we decided to close for all intents and purposes, with our apostolic authority, this same ecumenical council called by our predecessor, Pope John XXIII, which opened October 11, 1962, and which was continued by us after his death.
¶565
Tandem Deo adiuvante, omnia, quae ad sacrum Concilium Oecumenicum spectant, perfecta sunt et universae Constitutiones, Decreta, Declarationes, suffragia synodali decreto comprobata ac a Nobis sunt promulgata. Quapropter idem Concilium Oecumenicum a fel. rec. Decessore Nostro Ioanne Papa XXIII indictum, die XI mensis octobris anno MCMLXII initum ac post eius mortem a Nobis continuatum, ex omni effectu, auctoritate Nostra Apostolica concludere statuimus.
We decided moreover that all that has been established synodally is to be religiously observed by all the faithful, for the glory of God and the dignity of the Church and for the tranquillity and peace of all men. We have approved and established these things, decreeing that the present letters are and remain stable and valid, and are to have legal effectiveness, so that they be disseminated and obtain full and complete effect, and so that they may be fully convalidated by those whom they concern or may concern now and in the future; and so that, as it be judged and described, all efforts contrary to these things by whomsoever or whatever authority, knowingly or in ignorance be invalid and worthless from now on.
¶566
Decernimus praetorea ut omnia, quae synodali ratione sancita sunt, ab omnibus fidelibus religiose serventur, ad Dei gloriam et Ecclesiae dignitatem, ad omnium hominum tranquillitatem et pacem. Haec denique Nos comprobavimus atque constituimus, decernentes has Litteras firmas, validas atque efficaces semper exstare ac permanere, atque ab iis, ad quos pertinent aut pertinere poterunt nunc et in posterum, plenarie ab omnibus observari, et, prout iudicatum et descriptum erit, ab iis, ad quos pertinent aut pertinere poterunt, obtineri; sicque rite iudicari et definiri oportere, atque irritum esse et inane, si secus super his a quoquam, quavis auctoritate, scienter vel ignoranter contigerit attentari.
Given in Rome at St. Peter's, under the [seal of the] ring of the fisherman, Dec. 8, on the feast of the Immaculate Conception of the Blessed Virgin Mary, the year 1965, the third year of our pontificate.
