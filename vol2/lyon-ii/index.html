Second Council of Lyon

Concilium Lugdunense II

1274

Editorial note

Primary dogmatic contribution: Formal Latin ecumenical definition of the Filioque as ecclesiastical doctrine — this is Lyon II's distinctive function and should not be conflated with Florence.

Functional distinction from Florence: Lyon II defines that the Filioque belongs to Latin faith. Florence (1439) defines how — with the precision of per modum unius principii and unica spiratio. These are sequential, not redundant. The chapter should state this explicitly to prevent the reader from treating the two councils as interchangeable on the Filioque.

Secondary contributions: purgatory; reunion with the Greek East (short-lived); papal election procedures.

Zelus fidei fervor devotionis et compassionis pietas excitare debent corda fidelium ut omnes qui christiano nomine gloriantur de sui contumelia redemptoris tacti dolore cordis intrinsecus potenter et patenter exsurgant ad terrae sanctae praesidium et adiutorium causae dei.
The zeal of faith, the fervor of devotion, and the compassion of piety ought to excite the hearts of the faithful, that all who glory in the Christian name, touched by grief of heart intrinsically concerning the contumely of their Redeemer, may rise up powerfully and openly to the defense of the Holy Land and to the aid of the cause of God.
¶1
Quis verae fidei luce perfusus pia mente revolvens eximia beneficia quae salvator noster in terra sancta humano genere contulit devotione non ferveat et caritate non ardeat et illi terrae sanctae quae funiculus est hereditatis dominicae ex intimis visceribus et toto mentis non compatiatur affectu.
Who, suffused with the light of true faith, piously revolving in mind the exceeding benefits which our Savior in the Holy Land conferred upon the human race, would not burn with devotion and ardently love, and from the inmost viscera and with the whole affection of mind not suffer with that Holy Land which is the cord of the Lord's inheritance?
¶2
Cuius cor non emollient ad compassionem ipsius a creatore nostro in terra ipsa tot iudicia caritatis ostensa.
Whose heart would not be softened to compassion for it, so many judgments of charity having been shown by our Creator in that land?
¶3
Sed heu proh dolor ipsa terra in qua dominus salutem nostram dignatus est operari et quam ut hominem commercio mortis suae redimeret proprio sanguine consecravit per nefandissimos hostes nominis christiani blasphemos et perfidos Sarracenos audacia secuta occupata diutius et temere detinetur et intrepide devastatur.
But alas, woe to sorrow, that land itself in which the Lord deigned to work our salvation, and which, to redeem a man by the commerce of His death, He consecrated with His own blood, by the most nefarious enemies of the Christian name, blasphemous and perfidious Saracens, with pursuing audacity, having been occupied long since and rashly is detained and intrepidly devastated.
¶4
Trucidatur in ea inhumaniter populus christianus et ad maiorem contumeliam creatoris et iniuriam et dolorem omnium qui fide catholica profitentur.
The Christian people are inhumanly slaughtered in it, and to the greater contumely of the Creator and the injury and grief of all who profess the catholic faith.
¶5
Ubi sit deus christianorum improperant multis opprobriis christicolas insultantes.
"Where is the God of the Christians?" they reproachfully say, insulting the worshippers of Christ with many reproaches.
¶6
Ista et alia quae circa hoc nec animus sufficit plene concipere vel lingua referre accenderunt cor nostrum et excitaverunt animum ut nos qui in transmarinis partibus praemissa non tantum audivimus sed oculis nostris aspeximus et manus nostrae contractaverunt exsurgeremus quantum se possibilitatis nostrae conatus extendit ad vindicandam iniuriam crucifixi illorum interveniente auxilio quos ad hoc zelus fidei et devotionis accendet.
These things and others which concerning this neither does the mind suffice fully to conceive nor the tongue to relate, have inflamed our heart and aroused our spirit, so that we who in transmarine parts have not only heard the aforesaid things but have seen with our eyes and our hands have handled them, might rise up as far as the effort of our possibility extends, to avenge the injury of the Crucified, by the aid of those whom the zeal of faith and devotion for this purpose will inflame.
¶7
Et quia praefatae terrae liberatio tangere debet omnes qui fidem catholicam profitentur concilium mandavimus evocari ut deliberatione habita in eodem cum praelatis regibus et principibus et aliis prudentibus viris illa statueremus et ordinaremus in Christo per quae dictae terrae liberatio proveniret et nihilominus reducerentur Graecorum populi ad ecclesiae unitatem qui superba cervice nitentes scindere quodammodo inconsutilem domini tunicam se a sedis apostolicae devotione ac oboedientia subtraxerunt et reformarentur mores qui sunt peccatis exigentibus et clero et populo quam plurimum deformati illo in praemissis omnibus nostros actus et consilia dirigente cui nihil est impossibile sed cum vult res difficiles faciles facit et aspera sua virtute complanans prava dirigit in directa.
And because the liberation of the aforesaid land ought to touch all who profess the catholic faith, we have commanded a council to be summoned, so that, deliberation having been held in it with prelates, kings, princes, and other prudent men, we might establish and ordain in Christ those things through which the liberation of the said land might come about, and nevertheless the peoples of the Greeks, who with proud neck striving in a manner to rend the seamless tunic of the Lord, have withdrawn themselves from the devotion and obedience of the Apostolic See, might be reduced to the unity of the Church, and morals which, sins demanding, are very much deformed both among clergy and people might be reformed, with Him directing our acts and counsels in all the aforesaid things to whom nothing is impossible, but when He wills, difficult things He makes easy, and making rough things smooth by His virtue, He directs crooked things into straight ways.
¶8
Sane ut praemissa perduci possent liberius ad effectum attendentes guerrarum pericula et viarum discrimina quae subire poterant illis quos ad idem concilium duximus evocandos nobis et nostris fratribus non parcentes sed appetentes labores spontaneos ut quietem aliis praeparare possemus ad civitatem Lugdunensem in qua credebamus ad concilium evocatos minori onere laborum et expensarum convenire accessimus cum fratribus nostris et curia nostra periculis variis ac diversis incommoditatibus et multis discriminibus praegravati ubi convenientibus tam per se quam per procuratores idoneos universis ad ipsum concilium evocatis frequenter de praefatae terrae subsidio deliberavimus cum eisdem et quod debuerint ad vindictam iniuriae salvatoris accensi pro ipsius terrae succursu vias excogitantes laudabiles probatu dederunt consilia et discreta.
Truly, so that the aforesaid things might be able more freely to be brought to effect, attending to the perils of wars and the dangers of roads which those were able to undergo whom we decided to summon to the same council, not sparing ourselves and our brothers but seeking voluntary labors so that we might be able to prepare quiet for others, we came with our brothers and our curia to the city of Lyon, in which we believed that those summoned to the council would convene with lesser burden of labors and expenses, having been burdened by various perils and diverse inconveniences and many dangers, where, all those summoned to the same council convening both in person and through suitable proctors, we frequently deliberated concerning the subsidy of the aforesaid land with them, and what ought to be done, inflamed for the vengeance of the injury of the Savior, devising ways for the succor of that land, they gave counsels laudable in proof and discreet.
¶9
Nos autem auditis eorum consiliis ipsorum voluntates et affectus laudabiles quos ostendunt de terrae liberatione praedictae merito commendamus.
We however, their counsels having been heard, their wills and laudable affections which they show concerning the liberation of the aforesaid land, deservedly commend.
¶10
Ne autem in humeros hominum onera gravia et importabilia imponere videamur quae digito nostro movere nolimus incipientes a nobis ipsis qui profitemur nos omnia quae habemus possidere ab unigenito dei filio Iesu Christo cuius munere vivimus cuius beneficio sustentamur quin etiam cuius sanguine redempti sumus nos et fratres nostri sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinales plenarie usque ad vi annos continuos de omnibus reditibus fructibus et proventibus nostris ecclesiasticis pro praedictae terrae subsidio decimam persolvamus approbante hoc sacro concilio statuentes et ordinantes quod usque ad praedictos vi annos ab instanti festo nativitatis beati Ioannis baptistae proximo continue numerandos ab omnibus personis ecclesiasticis quacumque dignitate praefulgeant seu cuiuscumque praeeminentiae conditionis vel ordinis aut status religionis vel ordinis quibus. Et eorum ecclesiis nulla privilegia vel indulgentias sub quacumque verborum forma vel expressione concessa volumus suffragari sed ea quae adhuc concessimus penitus revocamus de omnibus reditibus fructibus et proventibus ecclesiasticis anni cuiuslibet terminis infrascriptis medietas scilicet in festo nativitatis domini et alia medietas in festo beati Ioannis baptistae integre sine diminutione qualibet decima persolvatur.
But lest we seem to place heavy and insupportable burdens upon the shoulders of men which we are unwilling to move with our finger, beginning from ourselves, who profess that we possess all that we have from the only-begotten Son of God Jesus Christ, by whose gift we live, by whose benefit we are sustained, indeed even by whose blood we are redeemed, we and our brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman Church shall pay in full for up to six continuous years, from all our ecclesiastical incomes, fruits, and proceeds, the tenth for the subsidy of the aforesaid land, this sacred council approving, establishing and ordaining that up to the aforesaid six years, continuously to be counted from the approaching feast of the nativity of blessed John the Baptist next, by all ecclesiastical persons, whatever dignity they may shine with or of whatsoever preeminence, condition, or order, or status of religion or order they may be, and for their churches, no privileges or indulgences granted under whatever form of words or expression do we wish to avail, but those which we have thus far granted we altogether revoke, from all incomes, fruits, and ecclesiastical proceeds of each year, on the terms written below, namely one half on the feast of the nativity of the Lord and the other half on the feast of blessed John the Baptist, the tenth wholly without any diminution be paid.
¶11
Ut ei cuius negotium agitur reverentia debita in se ac sanctis suis et praecipue in virgine gloriosa quorum in his et aliis suffragio utimur servetur attentius sit que plenior terrae subventio praedictae constitutionem felicis recordationis Gregorii papae praedecessoris nostri contra blasphemos editam praecipimus inviolabiliter observari poenam que pecuniarum in ipsa statutam per potestates locorum in quibus blasphemia committitur ceteros que qui inibi iurisdictionem temporalem exercent coactiones si necesse fuerit per dioecesanos et aliorum locorum ordinarios ad exhibenda integraliter exigi et dicti subsidii collectoribus assignari in idem subsidium convertenda.
So that due reverence to Him whose business is being transacted may be preserved more attentively in Himself and in His saints, and especially in the glorious Virgin, whose suffrage we use in these and other things, and the fuller subvention of the aforesaid land may be provided, we command the constitution of blessed memory of Pope Gregory, our predecessor, published against blasphemers, to be inviolably observed, and the pecuniary penalty established in it by the powers of the places in which blasphemy is committed and others who exercise temporal jurisdiction in that place, by coercion if necessary through diocesans and ordinaries of other places, to be exacted integrally and assigned to the collectors of the said subsidy, to be converted into the same subsidy.
¶12
Confessoribus insuper ex parte ordinaria vel ex privilegio confessiones audientibus districte mandamus ut confitentibus sibi suggerant et iniungant quod de pecunia praedicta eidem terrae plenariam satisfactionem impendant inducant etiam confitentes ultimas voluntates quod in testamentis suis pro modo facultatum suarum aliquid de bonis suis pro subsidio terrae sanctae relinquant.
Moreover, to confessors hearing confessions, whether by ordinary authority or by privilege, we strictly command that they suggest and enjoin to those confessing to them that concerning the aforesaid money they make full satisfaction to the same land; let them also induce the confessing to make last wills, that in their testaments, according to the measure of their resources, they leave something of their goods for the subsidy of the Holy Land.
¶13
In singulis nihilominus ecclesiis truncum concavum poni praecipimus tribus clavibus consignatum prima penes episcopum secunda penes ecclesiae sacerdotem tertia penes aliquem religiosum laicum conservandis et in eo fideles quilibet iuxta quod dominus eorum mentibus inspiraverit suas eleemosynas ponere in remissionem suorum peccaminum moneantur et in ipsis ecclesiis semel in hebdomade pro remissione huiusmodi peccatorum et praesertim offerentium eleemosynas certa die quam tum sacerdos pronuntiet populo missa publice decantetur.
Nevertheless, in each church we command a hollow chest to be placed, secured with three keys, the first with the bishop, the second with the priest of the church, the third with some religious layperson to be kept, and that in it any faithful persons, according to what the Lord shall have inspired to their minds, are to be admonished to place their alms for the remission of their sins; and in the same churches, once in the week for the remission of such sins and especially of those offering alms, on a certain day which the priest shall announce to the people, a public Mass be chanted.
¶14
Praeter haec ut terrae sanctae plenius succurratur exhortamur et suadere intendimus monitis et exhortationibus reges et principes marchiones comites et barones potestates capitaneos et alios quoscumque terrarum duces ut ordinetur in terris suis cuiuslibet iurisdictioni eorum subiectis quod a singulis fidelibus unus denarius ad valorem turonensis vel unius sterlingi iuxta consuetudinem vel condicionem regionis et alia modica sine cuiuscumque gravamine in remissione peccaminum iniungentes annis singulis in ipsius terrae subsidium persolvatur ut sicut nullus excusare se potest quin teneatur compati statui miserabili terrae sanctae sic nec aliquis excutiatur subsidio nec a merito excludatur.
Besides these things, so that the Holy Land may be more fully succored, we exhort and intend to persuade by warnings and exhortations kings and princes, marquises, counts, barons, powers, captains, and any other leaders of lands, that it be ordained in their lands subject to their jurisdiction that by each of the faithful one denarius to the value of a tournois or of one sterling, according to the custom or condition of the region, and other small amounts without any burden of anyone, by way of remission of sins, be paid each year into the subsidy of that land, so that just as no one is able to excuse himself from being held to suffer with the miserable state of the Holy Land, thus also may no one be shaken off from the subsidy nor excluded from the merit.
¶15
Etiam ne quod provide ordinatum est de praedictae terrae subsidio impediri contingat fraude vel malitia vel ingenio alicuius excommunicamus et anathemizamus omnes et singulos qui scienter impedimentum praestiterint directe vel indirecte publice vel occulte quominus praestentur decimae sicut superius est expressum in praedictae terrae subsidium.
Lest also that which has been providently ordained concerning the subsidy of the aforesaid land may happen to be impeded by the fraud or malice or ingenuity of anyone, we excommunicate and anathematize all and each who knowingly shall have presented impediment, directly or indirectly, publicly or secretly, lest the tithes be paid as is above expressed into the subsidy of the aforesaid land.
¶16
Ceterum quia cursarii et piratae nimium impediunt capiendo et spoliando transeuntes ad illam et redeuntes de illa nos eos et principales adiutores et fautores eorum excommunicationis vinculo innodamus sub interminatione anathematis inhibentes ne quis cum eis communicet scienter in aliquo contractu venditionis vel emptionis.
Moreover, because corsairs and pirates greatly impede, by capturing and plundering those going to it and returning from it, we bind them and the principal helpers and favorers of them by the bond of excommunication, under the threat of anathema forbidding that anyone knowingly communicate with them in any contract of sale or purchase.
¶17
Iniungimus etiam rectoribus civitatum et locorum ut eos ab iniquitate revocent et compescant alioquin in terra ipsorum per ecclesiarum praelatos severitatem ecclesiasticam volumus exerceri.
We enjoin also upon the rectors of cities and places that they recall and restrain them from iniquity; otherwise, in their land we will that ecclesiastical severity be exercised by the prelates of churches.
¶18
Excommunicamus praeterea et anathemizamus eos falsos et impios christianos qui contra Christum et populum christianum Sarracenis arma et ferrum quibus christianos impugnant et lignamina galearum et aliorum vasorum navigalium deferunt et eos etiam qui galeas eis vendunt vel naves qui que in piraticis Sarracenorum navibus curam gubernationis exercent vel in machinis aut quibuslibet aliis aliquod eis impendunt consilium vel auxilium in christianorum dispendium specialiter terrae sanctae ipsos rerum suarum privatione mulctari et capientium servos fieri censemus.
Moreover, we excommunicate and anathematize those false and impious Christians who against Christ and the Christian people carry to Saracens arms and iron with which they attack Christians, and timbers of galleys and of other sailing vessels, and those also who sell galleys or ships to them, and those who exercise the care of navigation in the pirate ships of Saracens, or in engines or any other things bestow upon them any counsel or aid to the loss of Christians, specifically of the Holy Land, we decree to be mulcted by the privation of their own things and to become the slaves of capturers.
¶19
Praecipientes ut per omnes urbes maritimas diebus dominicis et festivis huiusmodi sententia publice innovetur et talibus ecclesiae gremium non aperiatur nisi totum quod de commercio tam damnato perceperint et tantundem de suo in subsidium terrae sanctae transmittant ut aequo iudicio in quo delinquunt puniantur.
Commanding that through all maritime cities on the Lord's days and feast days a sentence of this kind be publicly renewed, and to such persons the bosom of the Church be not opened unless they shall have sent as much as they have received from such damnable commerce and an equal amount from their own in subsidy of the Holy Land, so that by a just judgment they may be punished in that in which they transgress.
¶20
Quod si forte solvendo non fuerint sic alias reatus talium castigetur quod in poena ipsorum aliis interdicatur audacia similia praesumendi.
But if perhaps they shall not be able to pay, then let the guilt of such persons be so chastised that by their punishment others may be forbidden from the audacity of presuming similar things.
¶21
Prohibemus insuper omnibus christianis et sub anathemate interdicimus ne in terris Sarracenorum qui partes orientales inhabitant usque ad vi annos transmeant aut transvehant naves suas ut per hoc volentibus transfretare in subsidium terrae sanctae maior copia navigii praeparetur et Sarracenis subtrahatur auxilium quod eis consuevit ex hoc non modicum provenire.
Moreover, we forbid all Christians and under anathema we interdict that into the lands of Saracens who inhabit the eastern parts for up to six years they sail or transport their ships, so that by this for those wishing to cross in subsidy of the Holy Land a greater abundance of shipping may be prepared, and aid which has been accustomed to come to Saracens from this not moderately may be withdrawn.
¶22
Et quia ad hoc negotium prosequendum permaxime est necessarium ut principes et populi christiani ad pacem observent hac sancta universali synodo approbante statuimus ut in toto orbe inter christianos pax generaliter observetur ita quod per ecclesiarum praelatos discordantes ducantur ad plenam concordiam sive pacem aut ad firmam treugam per vi annos inviolabiliter observandam et qui acquiescere forte contempserint per excommunicationis sententiam in personas et interdictum in terras arctissime compellantur nisi tanta fuerit iniuriatorum malitia quod tali non debeant pace gaudere.
And because for this business to be carried out it is especially necessary that princes and Christian peoples observe peace, this holy universal synod approving, we establish that in the whole world among Christians peace be generally observed, so that through the prelates of churches those at variance be led to full concord or peace, or to a firm truce for six years inviolably to be observed, and those who perhaps shall have despised to acquiesce, by the sentence of excommunication against persons and interdict upon lands most strictly let them be compelled, unless there has been such a degree of malice of injuries that they ought not to enjoy such peace.
¶23
Quod si forte censuram ecclesiasticam vilipenderint poterunt non immerito formidare ne per auctoritatem ecclesiae contra eos tamquam perturbatores negotii crucifixi saecularis potentia inducatur.
But if perhaps they shall have despised ecclesiastical censure, they may not undeservedly fear that by the authority of the Church against them as disturbers of the business of the Crucified, secular power may be induced.
¶24
Nos igitur de omnipotentis dei misericordia et beatorum Petri et Pauli auctoritate confisi ex illa quam nobis licet indignis deus ligandi atque solvendi contulit potestate omnibus qui transfretandi pro subsidio terrae sanctae in propriis personis laborem subierint et in expensis plenam suorum peccaminum de quibus veraciter fuerint corde contriti et ore confessi veniam indulgemus et retributionem iustorum salutis aeternae pollicemur augmentum.
We therefore, trusting in the mercy of omnipotent God and in the authority of the blessed Peter and Paul, from that power which, though to us unworthy, God has conferred of binding and loosing, to all who for crossing for the subsidy of the Holy Land shall have undergone the labor in their own persons and in expenses, full remission of their sins concerning which they shall have been truly contrite of heart and of mouth confessed, we grant, and the retribution of the just and increase of eternal salvation we promise.
¶25
Eis autem qui non in propriis personis illic accesserint sed in suis dumtaxat expensis iuxta facultatem et qualitatem suam viros idoneos destinaverint et illis similiter qui licet in alienis expensis in propriis tamen personis accesserint plenam suorum concedimus veniam peccatorum.
To those however who shall have gone there not in their own persons but have sent suitable men at their own expense only, according to their resources and their quality, and likewise to those who, though at another's expense, yet in their own persons shall have gone, we grant full remission of their sins.
¶26
Huiusmodi quoque remissionis volumus et concedimus esse participes iuxta qualitatem subsidii et devotionis affectum omnes qui ad subventionem ipsius terrae de bonis suis congrue ministrabunt aut circa praedicta consilium et auxilium impenderint oportunum nec omnes illos qui pro subsidio terrae sanctae naves proprias exhibebunt aut eos qui propter hoc opus naves studuerint fabricare.
Of this remission also we will and concede that all be participants, according to the quality of the subsidy and the affection of devotion, who shall have suitably ministered from their goods to the subvention of that land, or concerning the aforesaid shall have bestowed opportune counsel and aid, and also all those who for the subsidy of the Holy Land shall have presented their own ships, or those who for this work shall have striven to build ships.
¶27
Omnibus etiam pie proficientibus in hoc opere pia et sancta universalis synodus orationum et beneficiorum suffragium impertitur ut eis digne proficiat ad salutem.
To all also piously proceeding in this work, the pious and holy universal synod bestows the suffrage of prayers and benefits, that it may profit them worthily unto salvation.
¶28
Non nobis sed domino damus gloriam et honorem et ipsi gratias reddamus quod ad tam sacrum concilium patriarcharum primatum archiepiscoporum episcoporum abbatum priorum praepositorum decanorum archidiaconorum et aliorum ecclesiarum praelatorum tam per se quam per procuratores idoneos nec capitulorum collegiorum et conventuum procuratorum ad vocationem nostram copiosa multitudo convenit.
Not to us, but to the Lord we give glory and honor, and to Him we render thanks, that for so sacred a council of patriarchs, primates, archbishops, bishops, abbots, priors, provosts, deans, archdeacons, and other prelates of churches, both in person and through suitable proctors, and of chapters, colleges, and convents of proctors, at our summons, a copious multitude has convened.
¶29
Sane licet pro felici prosecutione tanti negotii esset eorum consilium oportunum et in ipsorum tanquam dilectorum filiorum praesentia delectemur et quodammodo spirituali gaudio affluamus contra nonnullos tamen eorum propter varia incommoda quae ipsorum copiositas ingerit ne prae turba nimia se diutius comprimant et eorum absentia ipsis et ipsorum ecclesiis posset esse damnosa quadam provida pietate commoti de fratrum nostrorum consilio super hoc salubriter providere decrevimus ut sic eorum gravaminibus occurratur quod prosecutio huius negotii quod ferventi spiritu et sollicitudine indefessa prosequimur nullatenus derogetur.
Truly, although for the happy prosecution of so great a business their counsel would be opportune, and in their presence as of beloved sons we delight and in a manner abound in spiritual joy, yet against not a few of them, on account of the various disadvantages which their abundance introduces, lest they be too long constrained by the excessive crowd, and their absence might be damaging to themselves and their churches, moved by a certain provident piety, by the counsel of our brothers concerning this matter salubriously to provide, we have decreed that thus their burdens may be met, that the prosecution of this business, which with fervent spirit and indefatigable solicitude we pursue, may be in no way derogated.
¶30
Omnes igitur patriarchas primates archiepiscopos episcopos abbates et priores per nos nominatim et specialiter evocatos sic remanere decrevimus ut ante diffinitum concilium absque nostra speciali licentia non discedant.
All therefore patriarchs, primates, archbishops, bishops, abbots, and priors by us nominately and specially summoned, we have decreed thus to remain, that before the council is concluded, they may not depart without our special license.
¶31
Ceteris vero abbatibus et prioribus non mitratis et aliis abbatibus et prioribus qui per nos non fuerunt nominatim et specialiter evocati necnon praepositis decanis archidiaconis et aliis ecclesiarum praelatis ac quorumque praelatorum capitulorum collegiorum et conventuum procuratoribus recedendi ex dei et nostra benedictione clementer licentiam impertimur mandantes ut omnes taliter recedentes primitus prout infra scribitur procuratores sufficientes dimittant ad suscipienda nostra mandata et ea quae in praesenti nostro concilio ordinata sunt et in futurum auctore domino contigerit ordinari.
To the remaining abbots and priors not mitred, and other abbots and priors who were not by us nominately and specially summoned, and also provosts, deans, archdeacons, and other prelates of churches, and the proctors of chapters, colleges, and convents of whatsoever prelates, we grant license to depart by the blessing of God and ours, mercifully, commanding that all thus departing first, as is written below, leave sufficient proctors to receive our mandates and those things which in our present council have been ordained and in the future, the Lord being author, shall happen to be ordained.
¶32
Omnes scilicet de regno Franciae taliter recedentes quattuor de regno Alamaniae quattuor de regnis Yspaniarum quattuor de regno Angliae quattuor de regno Scotiae unum de regno Siciliae duos de Lambardia duos de Tuscia unum de terris ecclesiae unum de regno Norbegiae unum de regno Sueviae unum de regno Ungariae unum de regno Daciae unum de regno Boemiae unum de ducatu Poloniae unum procuratores sufficientes dimittant.
All namely from the kingdom of France thus departing, four; from the kingdom of Germany, four; from the kingdoms of Spain, four; from the kingdom of England, four; from the kingdom of Scotland, one; from the kingdom of Sicily, one; from Lombardy, two; from Tuscany, two; from the lands of the Church, one; from the kingdom of Norway, one; from the kingdom of Sweden, one; from the kingdom of Hungary, one; from the kingdom of Denmark, one; from the kingdom of Bohemia, one; from the duchy of Poland, one: let them leave sufficient proctors.
¶33
Ad haec ad nos ex quorundam relatione pervenit quod nonnulli archiepiscopi et episcopi alii que praelati ea occasione quod ipsos ad concilium mandavimus evocari petentes a subditis immoderatum subsidium multa extorserunt ab eis graves ipsis tallias imponentes quorum aliqui licet a subiectis multa exegerint ad concilium non venerunt.
To these things, it has come to us by the report of certain persons that not a few archbishops and bishops and other prelates, by the occasion that we commanded them to be summoned to the council, seeking immoderate subsidy from subjects, extorted many things from them, imposing grave tallages upon them; of whom some, although they have exacted many things from subjects, have not come to the council.
¶34
Verumtamen cum nostrae intentionis non exstiterit nec exsistat ut praelati ad concilium veniendo sociarent oboedire bonum cum gravamine subditorum monemus praelatos omnes et singulos eis firmiter innuentes quatinus nullus eorum occasione praemissa subditos suos talliis vel exactionibus gravare praesumat.
Nevertheless, since it has not been nor is of our intention that prelates by coming to the council should join obedience to good with the burden of subjects, we warn all and each of the prelates, firmly intimating to them that none of them on the aforesaid occasion presume to burden their subjects by tallages or exactions.
¶35
Si vero aliqui non venerunt ad concilium et a subditis suis ea occasione aliquid exegerint volumus et praecise mandamus quod ea occasione quae receperunt ab eis restituant sine mora.
If indeed any have not come to the council and on that occasion have exacted anything from their subjects, we will and precisely command that what they have received from them on that occasion they restore without delay.
¶36
Illi autem qui gravaverunt subditos ab eis subsidia immoderata petendo eisdem satisfacere sine qualibet difficultate procurent mandatum nostrum taliter impleturi quod non oporteat ut super hoc auctoritate nostra remedium apponatur.
Those however who have burdened subjects by seeking immoderate subsidies from them, let them take care to satisfy them without any difficulty, thus about to fulfill our command, that it may not be necessary that concerning this a remedy be applied by our authority.
¶37
Fideli ac devota professione fatemur quod spiritus sanctus aeternaliter ex patre et filio non tanquam ex duobus principiis sed tanquam ex uno principio non duabus spirationibus sed unica spiratione procedit.
With faithful and devout profession we confess that the Holy Spirit eternally proceeds from the Father and the Son, not as from two principles but as from one principle, not by two spirations but by one single spiration.
¶38
Hoc professa est hactenus praedicavit et docuit hoc firmiter tenet praedicat profitetur et docet sacrosancta Romana ecclesia mater omnium fidelium et magistra.
This the sacrosanct Roman Church, the mother of all the faithful and teacher, has thus far professed, preached, and taught; this she firmly holds, preaches, professes, and teaches.
¶39
Hoc habet orthodoxorum patrum atque doctorum Latinorum pariter et Graecorum incommutabilis et vera sententia.
This the immutable and true sentence of orthodox fathers and doctors, both Latin and Greek, has.
¶40
Sed quia nonnulli propter irrefragabilis praemissae ignorantiam veritatis in errores varios sunt prolapsi nos huiusmodi erroribus viam praecludere cupientes sacro approbante concilio damnamus et reprobamus omnes qui negare praesumpserint aeternaliter spiritum sanctum ex patre et filio procedere sive etiam temerario ausu asserere quod spiritus sanctus ex patre et filio tanquam ex duobus principiis et non tanquam ex uno procedat.
But because not a few, on account of the ignorance of the irrefragable aforesaid truth, have fallen into various errors, we, wishing to preclude the way to such errors, with the sacred council approving, condemn and reprove all who have presumed to deny that the Holy Spirit proceeds eternally from the Father and the Son, or also with temerarious daring to assert that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son as from two principles and not as from one.
¶41
Ubi periculum maius intenditur ibi procul dubio est plenius consulendum.
Where greater peril is intended, there without doubt more fully provision must be made.
¶42
Quam gravibus autem sit onusta dispendiis quot et quantis sit plena periculis ecclesiae Romanae prolixa vacatio exacti temporis consideratio edocet et considerata prudenter illius discrimina manifestant.
How grave a burden of losses, how many and how great perils the prolonged vacancy of the Roman Church holds, the consideration of past time teaches, and, prudently considered, the dangers of that situation manifest.
¶43
Hinc nos evidens evocat ratio ut dum reformandis etiam minoribus nostra sollerter vacet intentio ea quae periculosiora sunt nequaquam absque remedio reformationis accomode relinquamus.
Hence evident reason summons us, that while our intention diligently attends to reforming even lesser matters, we may by no means leave those things which are more perilous without a remedy of reformation.
¶44
Ideo que omnia quae pro vitanda discordia in electione Romani pontificis a nostris sunt praedecessoribus et praecipue a felicis recordationis Alexandro papa tertio salubriter instituta omnino immota in sua firmitate manere censentes nihil enim illis detrahere intendimus sed quod experientia deesse probavit praesenti constitutione supplere.
And therefore, all things which for avoiding discord in the election of the Roman pontiff by our predecessors, and especially by Pope Alexander III of happy memory, were salubriously instituted, judging altogether immovable to remain in their firmness (for we intend to detract nothing from them), but that which experience has proved to be lacking, we supply by the present constitution.
¶45
Sacro concilio approbante statuimus ut si eumdem pontificem in civitate in qua cum sua curia residebat diem claudere contingat extremum cardinales qui fuerint in civitate ipsa praesentes absentes expectare decem diebus tantummodo teneantur.
With the sacred council approving, we establish that if it happen that the same pontiff closes his last day in the city in which he was residing with his curia, the cardinals who shall be present in that city are held to await the absent ones for ten days only.
¶46
Quibus elapsis sive absentes venerint sive non extunc omnes conveniant in palatio in quo idem pontifex habitabat contenti singuli singulis tantummodo servientibus clericis vel laicis prout duxerint eligendum. Illis tamen quibus patens necessitas id suggerit indulgeri duos habere permittimus eiusdem electionis arbitrio reservato.
Which having elapsed, whether the absent ones have come or not, from then let all convene in the palace in which the same pontiff dwelt, each one content with only one serving cleric or layperson, as they shall have decided to choose. Nevertheless to those whom open necessity suggests it, we permit to have two, the judgment of the election itself being reserved.
¶47
In eodem autem palatio unum conclave nullo intermedio pariete seu alio velamine omnes habitent in communi quod servato libero ad secretam cameram aditu ita claudatur undique ut nullus illud intrare valeat vel exire nulli ad eosdem cardinales aditus pateat vel facultas secrete loquendi cum eis nec ipsi aliquos ad se venientes admittant nisi eos qui de voluntate omnium cardinalium inibi praesentium pro iis tantum quae ad electionis instantis negotium pertinent vocarentur.
In the same palace moreover, one conclave, with no intervening wall or other veil, let all inhabit in common, which, free access to a secret chamber being preserved, so be closed on all sides that no one may be able to enter it or leave it; to no one let there be open approach or faculty of speaking secretly with them, nor let them themselves admit any coming to them except those who by the will of all the cardinals present in that place are summoned for those things only which pertain to the business of the election being imminent.
¶48
Nulli etiam fas sit ipsis cardinalibus vel eorum alicui nuntium mittere vel scripturam. Qui vero contra fecerit scripturam mittendo vel nuntium aut cum aliquo ipsorum secreto loquendo ipso facto sententiam excommunicationis incurrat.
Nor let it be lawful for anyone to send a nuncio or a writing to the same cardinals or to any of them. Whoever indeed shall have acted against this, by sending a writing or a nuncio or by speaking secretly with any of them, by the very fact let him incur the sentence of excommunication.
¶49
In conclavi tamen praedicto aliqua fenestra competens dimittatur per quam eisdem cardinalibus ad victum necessaria commode ministrentur sed per eam nulli ad ipsos patere possit ingressus.
In the aforesaid conclave however, some competent window shall be left, through which to the same cardinals things necessary for sustenance may be conveniently ministered, but through it may no entrance be able to lie open to them.
¶50
Verum si quod absit infra tres dies postquam ut praedicitur conclave praedictum iidem cardinals intraverint non fuerit ipsi ecclesiae de pastore provisum per spatium quinque dierum immediate sequentium singulis diebus tam in prandio quam in cena uno solo ferculo sint contenti. Quibus provisione non facta decursis extunc tantummodo panis vinum et aqua ministrentur eisdem donec eadem provisio subsequatur.
But if (which may God forbid) within three days after, as has been said, the same cardinals shall have entered the aforesaid conclave, provision shall not have been made for the church concerning a pastor, for the space of five days immediately following, on each day both at lunch and at dinner, let them be content with one single dish. Which, provision not having been made, having elapsed, from then only bread, wine, and water be ministered to them until the same provision follows.
¶51
Provisionis quoque huiusmodi pendente negotio dicti cardinales nihil de camera papae recipiant nec de aliis eidem ecclesiae tempore vacationis obvenientibus undecunque sed ea omnia ipsa vacatione durante sub eius cuius fidei et diligentiae camera eadem est commissa custodia maneant per eum dispositioni futuri pontificis reservanda.
With the business of such provision also pending, the said cardinals shall receive nothing from the chamber of the pope nor from other things coming to the same church during the time of vacancy from whatever source; but all those things, during the same vacancy, under the custody of him to whose faith and diligence the same chamber has been entrusted, remain, to be reserved by him for the disposition of the future pontiff.
¶52
Qui autem aliquid receperint teneantur extunc a perceptione quorumlibet redituum ad ipsos spectantium abstinere donec de receptis taliter plenariam satisfactionem impendant.
Those who shall have received anything, let them be held from then to abstain from the perception of any incomes pertaining to themselves until for such received things they make full satisfaction.
¶53
Iidem quoque cardinales accelerandae provisioni sic vacent attentius quod se nequaquam de alio negotio intromittant nisi forsan necessitas adeo urgens incideret quod eos oporteret de terra ipsius ecclesiae defendenda vel eius parte aliqua providere vel nisi aliquod tam grande tam evidens periculum immineret quod omnibus et singulis cardinalibus praesentibus concorditer videretur illi celeriter occurrendum.
The same cardinals also shall attend so attentively to the acceleration of provision that they by no means intrude themselves concerning any other business, unless perhaps such an urgent necessity should fall that it would be necessary for them to provide concerning the defense of the land of the same church or some part of it, or unless some so great, so evident peril should impend that it would appear concordantly to all and each of the cardinals present that it must be quickly met.
¶54
Sane si aliquis de praedictis cardinalibus conclave praedictum ut supra exprimitur non intraverit aut intrans absque manifesta causa infirmitatis exierit ipso minime requisito nec in eiusdem electionis negotio ulterius admittendo per alios ad eligendum substituendum pontificem libere procedatur.
Truly, if any of the aforesaid cardinals shall not have entered the aforesaid conclave as expressed above, or having entered, shall have left without manifest cause of infirmity, he not being required, nor thereafter being admitted to the business of the same election, by the others let it be freely proceeded to elect a substitute pontiff.
¶55
Si vero infirmitate superveniente idem conclave aliquem ex eis exire contingat ipsa etiam infirmitate durante poterit eius suffragio non requisito procedi. Sed si ad alios post sanitatem sibi redditam seu antea redire voluerit vel etiam si alii absentes quos per decem dies diximus expectandos supervenerint re integra videlicet antequam eidem ecclesiae sit de pastore provisum in eodem negotio in illo statu in quo ipsum invenerint admittantur praemissa tam de clausura quam de servientibus cibo ac potu et reliquis cum aliis servaturi.
If indeed, with infirmity supervening, it happen that the same conclave is left by any of them, during the infirmity itself his suffrage not being required, it may be proceeded. But if after health restored to him or before he wishes to return, or also if other absent ones whom we have said ought to be awaited for ten days shall have arrived while the matter is intact, namely before provision has been made for the same church concerning a pastor, in the same business, in that state in which they shall have found it, let them be admitted, about to observe the aforesaid things, both concerning enclosure and concerning servants, food and drink, and the rest, with the others.
¶56
Porro si quando Romanum pontificem extra civitatem praedictam in qua erat cum sua curia residens contigerit ab hac luce migrare teneantur cardinales in civitate in cuius territorio seu districtu idem pontifex obiit convenire nisi sit forsitan interdicta vel contra ecclesiam Romanam in aperta rebellione persistat. Quo casu in alia viciniori conveniant quae similiter nec interdicto subiaceat nec sit ut praedicitur aperte rebellis.
Moreover, if at any time it happen that the Roman pontiff departs from this light outside the aforesaid city in which he was residing with his curia, let the cardinals be held to convene in the city in whose territory or district the same pontiff died, unless perhaps it is under interdict or persists in open rebellion against the Roman Church. In which case, let them convene in another, nearer, which likewise is neither subject to interdict nor, as has been said, openly rebellious.
¶57
In hac etiam civitate tam quoad exspectationem absentium quam quoad habitationem communem clausuram et cetera omnia in domo episcopali vel alia qualibet eisdem cardinalibus deputanda eadem observentur quae superius obeunte dicto pontifice in ea in qua cum sua residebat curia sunt expressa.
In this city also, both with respect to the awaiting of the absent and with respect to common habitation, enclosure, and all other things in the episcopal house or any other to be assigned to the same cardinals, let those same things be observed which above, when the said pontiff dies in that city in which he resided with his curia, are expressed.
¶58
Praeterea quia parum est iura condere nisi sit qui eadem tueatur adiciendo sancimus ut dominus alii que rectores et officiales civitatis illius in qua Romani pontificis celebranda fuerit electio auctoritate nostra et eiusdem approbatione concilii potestate sibi tradita praemissa omnia et singula plene ac inviolabiliter sine fraude ac dolo aliquo faciant observari nec cardinales ultra quam praemittitur artare praesumant.
Moreover, because it is little to establish laws unless there be someone who may protect them, by adding we sanction that the lord and other rectors and officials of that city in which the election of the Roman pontiff shall be celebrated, by our authority and by the approval of the same council, power being given to them, cause all and each of the aforesaid things to be fully and inviolably without any fraud and deceit observed, nor presume to constrain the cardinals beyond what is set forth above.
¶59
Super iis autem taliter observandis statim audito summi pontificis obitu coram clero et populo civitatis ipsius ad hoc specialiter convocandis praestent corporaliter iuramentum.
Concerning these things moreover thus to be observed, immediately upon the death of the supreme pontiff having been heard, before the clergy and people of that city specially to be summoned for this, let them render bodily oath.
¶60
Quod si forte in praemissis vel circa ea fraudem commiserint aut ipsa diligenter non observaverint cuiuscumque sint praeeminentiae conditionis aut status omni cessante privilegio eo ipso excommunicationis sint vinculo innodati et perpetuo sint infames nec unquam eis portae dignitatis pateant nec ad aliquod publicum officium admittantur.
But if perhaps in the aforesaid things or concerning them they shall have committed fraud or shall not have diligently observed them, of whatsoever preeminence, condition, or status they may be, all privilege ceasing, by that very fact let them be bound by the bond of excommunication and be perpetually infamous; nor ever to them may the gates of dignity lie open, nor to any public office let them be admitted.
¶61
Ipsos insuper feudis et bonis ceteris que quae ab eadem Romana vel quibuslibet aliis ecclesiis obtinent ipso facto decrevimus esse privatos ita quod ad ecclesias ipsas plene ac libere revertantur administratorum earumdem ecclesiarum arbitrio sine contradictione aliqua disponenda. Civitas vero praedicta non solum sit interdicto supposita sed et pontificali dignitate privata.
Moreover, we have decreed them, by the very fact, to be deprived of fiefs and goods and other things which they hold from the same Roman Church or from any other churches, so that they may fully and freely revert to those churches, to be disposed by the judgment of the administrators of the same churches without any contradiction. The aforesaid city moreover shall be not only subject to interdict but also deprived of pontifical dignity.
¶62
Ceterum quia cum arbitrium vel inordinatus captivat affectus vel ad certum aliquid obligationis cuiusque necessitas adigit cessat electio dum libertas adimitur eligendi cardinales eosdem obsecrantes per viscera misericordiae dei nostri per aspersionem sui pretiosi sanguinis obtestamur ut pensantes attentius quid eis imminet cum agitur de creatione vicarii Iesu Christi successoris Petri rectoris universalis ecclesiae gregis dominici directoris omni privatae affectionis inordinatione deposita et cuiuslibet pactionis conventionis obligationis necessitate necnon condicti et intendimenti contemplatione cessantibus non in se reciprocent considerationis intuitum vel in suos.
Moreover, because when arbitrary desire or inordinate affection captivates, or the necessity of some obligation compels one to something certain, election ceases while the liberty of choosing is taken away, we, beseeching the same cardinals by the bowels of the mercy of our God, by the sprinkling of His precious blood, adjure them, that pondering more attentively what impends over them when the business concerns the creation of the vicar of Jesus Christ, the successor of Peter, the rector of the universal church, the director of the lordly flock, with all inordination of private affection laid aside, and with the necessity of any pact, convention, obligation, agreement, and intention ceasing, they may not look back upon the consideration of themselves or their own.
¶63
Non quae sua sunt quaerant non commodis privatis intendant sed nullo artante ipsorum in eligendo iudicium nisi deo puris et liberis mentibus nuda electionis conscientia utilitatem publicam libere prosequantur omni conatu et sollicitudine prout possibilitas patitur id acturi tantummodo ut eorum ministerio acceleretur utilis et pernecessaria totius mundi provisio idoneo celeriter eidem ecclesiae sponso dato.
Let them not seek their own things, let them not intend private advantages, but with no one constraining their judgment in electing except God, with pure and free minds, with the bare conscience of election, let them freely pursue the public utility, with every effort and solicitude, as possibility permits, about to act toward this only, that by their ministry the useful and most necessary provision of the whole world may be accelerated, a suitable sponsor quickly given to the same church.
¶64
Qui autem secus egerint divinae subiaceant ultioni eorum culpa nisi gravi propter hoc peracta poenitentia nullatenus abolenda.
Whoever however shall have acted otherwise, let him be subject to divine vengeance, his fault by no means to be abolished unless for this grave penance shall have been performed.
¶65
Et nos nihilominus pactiones conventiones obligationes condicta et intendimenta omnia sive iuramenti sive cuiuslibet alterius sint vinculo firmitatis innexa cassamus irritamus et viribus decernimus omnino carere ita ut nullus ad illa observanda quomodolibet sit astrictus nec quisquam ex eorum transgressione notam vereatur fidei non servatae sed non indignae laudis titulum potius mereatur cum lex etiam humana testetur deo magis transgressiones huiusmodi quam iurisiurandi observationes acceptas.
And we nevertheless annul, invalidate, and decree altogether to lack force all pacts, conventions, obligations, agreements, and intentions, even if they are bound by the bond of oath or of any other firmness, so that no one may be bound in any way to observe them, nor may anyone for the transgression of them fear the mark of faith not having been kept, but rather may he deserve a title of not unworthy praise, since even human law testifies that such transgressions are more acceptable to God than the observances of oaths.
¶66
Quia vero fidelibus non est tam de sollicita quantumcumque inventione fidendum quam de instantia orationis humilis et devotae sperandum huic adicimus sanctioni ut in omnibus civitatibus ceteris que locis insignibus ubi primum de memorati pontificis obitu certitudo claruerit a clero et populo solemnibus pro eo exequiis celebratis singulis diebus donec de ipsius ecclesiae provisione indubitatus rumor pertulerit veritatem humiles preces fundantur ad dominum apud eum devotis orationibus insistatur ut ipse qui concordiam facit in sublimibus suis sic efficiat eorumdem cardinalium corda in eligendo concordia quod provisio celer concors et utilis prout animarum salus exigit et totius requirit orbis utilitas ex ipsorum unanimitate sequatur.
Because however the faithful ought not so much to trust in any diligent invention as to hope in the instance of humble and devout prayer, we add to this sanction that in all cities and other notable places as soon as concerning the death of the aforesaid pontiff certitude shall have become clear, after solemn exequies for him have been celebrated by the clergy and people, on each day until the undoubted rumor shall have borne the truth concerning the provision of the same church, humble prayers be poured out to the Lord, with devout prayers be insisted upon with Him, that He who makes concord in His high places may so effect the hearts of the same cardinals in choosing concord that a provision speedy, concordant, and useful, as the salvation of souls demands and the utility of the whole world requires, may follow from their unanimity.
¶67
Et ne tam salubre praesentis sanctionis edictum ignorantiae negligi praetextu contingat districte praecipimus ut patriarchae archiepiscopi episcopi et alii ecclesiarum praelati ceteri que quibus concessum est proponere verbum dei clerum et populum propter hoc specialiter frequentius congregandos in suis sermonibus ad supplicum precum suffragia pro celeri et felici exitu tanti negotii frequentanda sollerter hortentur et ipsis eadem auctoritate non solum orationum frequentiam sed et observantiam prout circumstantiae pensandae suaserint ieiuniorum indicant.
And lest so salutary a command of the present sanction may happen to be neglected under the pretext of ignorance, we strictly command that patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, and other prelates of churches, and the rest to whom it is conceded to propose the word of God, the clergy and people for this purpose specially to be more frequently gathered, in their sermons to the suffrages of suppliant prayers for the swift and happy outcome of so great a business to be frequently offered, skillfully exhort, and by the same authority to them not only frequency of prayers but also the observance of fasts, as the circumstances to be considered may persuade, do they enjoin.
¶68
Ut circa electiones postulationes et provisiones ecclesiasticas viam malitiis prout est possibile praecludamus nec diutius periculose vacent ecclesiae vel personatuum dignitatum et aliorum ecclesiasticorum beneficiorum provisio differatur edicto perpetuo providemus ut si quando aliqui electionibus postulationibus vel provisionibus se opponunt proponendo aliqua contra electionis postulationis seu provisionis formam aut personas eligentium vel electi sive illius cui provisio erat facienda vel facta et propter hoc contigerit appellari appellantes in instrumento publico seu litteris super appellatione confectis omnia et singula exprimant quae in formam intendunt obicere vel personas coram personis authenticis aut persona quae super hoc testimonium perhibeant veritati corporali praestito iuramento quod credunt ea quae sic exprimunt esse vera et se posse probare.
That concerning elections, postulations, and ecclesiastical provisions we may preclude the way to malicious acts as far as is possible, and lest churches or the provision of personates, dignities, and other ecclesiastical benefices be dangerously vacant longer, by perpetual edict we provide that if at any time anyone opposes themselves to elections, postulations, or provisions, proposing anything against the form of election, postulation, or provision, or against the persons of those electing or of the elect or of him for whom provision was to be made or has been made, and on account of this it happen to be appealed, those appealing in a public instrument or in letters drawn up concerning the appeal express all and each thing which they intend to object concerning the form or the persons, before authentic persons or a person who may bear testimony concerning the truth, a bodily oath having first been given that they believe those things which they thus express to be true and that they are able to prove them.
¶69
Alioquin tam opponentes quam tempore appellationis interpositae vel postmodum adhaerentes eisdem obiciendi aliqua quae non fuerint in huiusmodi litteris vel instrumentis expressa potestatem sibi noverint interdictam nisi aliquid postea forsan emerserit vel super antiquis supervenerit probandi facultas aut aliqua antiqua in opponentium notitiam de novo pervenerint quae appellantes appellationis emissae tempore verisimiliter ignorare potuerint et etiam ignorarint.
Otherwise, let those both opposing and at the time of appeal interposed or afterward adhering to them know the power of objecting anything which shall not have been expressed in such letters or instruments to be forbidden to themselves, unless something afterward perhaps shall have emerged or the faculty of proving concerning old matters shall have supervened, or some old matters shall have newly come to the knowledge of those opposing which those appealing at the time of the appeal emitted could probably have been ignorant of and indeed were ignorant of.
¶70
Super huiusmodi autem ignorantia et superveniente facultate probandi fidem per proprium praestandum corporaliter faciant iuramentum hoc adiciendo in iuramento eodem quod ad ea probanda credunt se sufficientes probationes habere.
Concerning ignorance of this kind moreover and concerning the supervening faculty of proving, let them make faith by a bodily oath to be given, adding in the same oath that they believe themselves to have sufficient proofs for proving those things.
¶71
Illa sane quae felicis recordationis Innocentius papa iv contra non plene probantes ea quae in formam vel personam obiecerant statuit in suo volumus robore permanere.
Those things indeed which Pope Innocent IV of happy memory established against those not fully proving the things which they had objected concerning the form or the person, we wish to remain in their force.
¶72
Avaritiae caecitas et damnandae ambitionis improbitas aliquorum animos occupantes eos in illam temeritatem impellunt ut quae sibi a iure interdicta noverunt exquisitis fraudibus usurpare conentur.
The blindness of avarice and the impropriety of damnable ambition, occupying the minds of some, impel them into that temerity that they strive to usurp by exquisite frauds those things which they know to be forbidden to themselves by law.
¶73
Nonnulli siquidem ad regimen ecclesiarum electi quia eis iure prohibente non licet se ante confirmationem electionis celebratae de ipsis administrationi ecclesiarum ad quas vocantur ingerere ipsam sibi tanquam procuratoribus seu oeconomis committi procurant.
Indeed, certain persons elected to the governance of churches, because by the prohibiting law it is not lawful for them to thrust themselves into the administration of the churches to which they are called before the confirmation of the election celebrated concerning them, procure that it be committed to themselves as to proctors or stewards.
¶74
Cum itaque non sit malitiis hominum indulgendum nos latius providere volentes hac generali constitutione sancimus ut nullus de cetero administrationem dignitatis ad quam electus est priusquam celebrata de ipso electio confirmetur sub oeconomatus vel procurationis nomine aut alio de novo quaesito colore in spiritualibus vel temporalibus per se vel per alium pro parte vel in totum gerere vel recipere aut illi se immiscere praesumat omnes illos qui secus fecerint iure si quod eis per electionem quaesitum fuerit decernentes eo ipso privatos.
Since therefore it is not to be indulged to the malice of men, we, wishing to provide more fully, by this general constitution sanction that no one henceforth presume to take up or receive the administration of the dignity to which he has been elected, before the election concerning him is confirmed, under the name of stewardship or proctorship or another newly sought color, in spiritual or temporal things, by himself or through another, in part or in whole, or to intermix himself with it, decreeing that all who shall have acted otherwise are by that very fact deprived of whatever right, if any, had been acquired for them through election.
¶75
Quam sit ecclesiis ipsarum dispendiosa vacatio quam periculosa etiam esse soleat animabus non solum iura testantur sed et magistra rerum efficax experientia manifestat.
How costly the vacancy of churches is to the churches themselves, how perilous also it is accustomed to be to souls, not only the laws testify but also the effective mistress of things, experience, manifests.
¶76
Cupientes itaque competentibus remediis vacationum diuturnitatibus obviare hoc perpetuo decreto statuimus ut si quando fuerit electio in aliqua ecclesia celebrata electores electionem ipsam quam citius commode poterunt electo praesentare ac petere consensum ipsius electus vero illum adhibere infra mensem a tempore praesentationis huiusmodi teneantur.
Desiring therefore by competent remedies to meet the long durations of vacancies, by this perpetual decree we establish that if at any time an election shall have been celebrated in any church, the electors are held to present that same election to the elect as soon as they conveniently can, and to seek his consent, and the elect himself to give it, within a month from the time of such presentation.
¶77
Quem si electus ipse praestare ultra distulerit iure si quod ei ex sua electione fuerat acquisitum extunc se noverit eo ipso privatum nisi forsan ea sit electae personae conditio ut electioni de se celebratae absque superioris sui licentia ex prohibitione seu quavis provisione sedis apostolicae consentire non possit.
If the elect himself shall have delayed to give it beyond that time, from then let him know himself to be deprived by that very fact of whatever right, if any, had been acquired for him from his election, unless perhaps the condition of the elected person be such that he is not able to consent to the election celebrated concerning him without the license of his superior, on account of a prohibition or any provision of the Apostolic See.
¶78
Quo casu idem electus seu electores ipsius consentiendi licentiam ab eius superiore cum ea celeritate quam superioris ipsius praesentia vel absentia permiserit petere studeant et habere. Alioquin si lapso tempore pro eiusdem superioris praesentia vel absentia ut praemittitur moderando huiusmodi licentiam eos nequaquam obtinere contingat electores extunc ad electionem aliam procedendi liberam habeant facultatem.
In which case, let the same elect or his electors strive to seek and obtain the license of consenting from his superior with that swiftness which the presence or absence of his superior permits. Otherwise, if with the time elapsed, to be moderated as is set forth above according to the presence or absence of the same superior, it happen that they by no means obtain such license, from then let the electors have free faculty to proceed to another election.
¶79
Ceterum quivis electus infra tres menses post consensum electioni de se celebratae praestitum confirmationem electionis ipsius petere non omittat. Quod si iusto impedimento cessante infra huiusmodi trimestre tempus omiserit electio eadem eo ipso viribus vacuetur.
Moreover, any elect shall not omit to seek the confirmation of his election within three months after the consent to the election celebrated concerning him has been given. But if, just impediment ceasing, within the space of three months he shall have omitted, the same election by that very fact shall be voided of force.
¶80
Perpetuae sanctionis oraculo declaramus quod scienter in electionibus nominantes indignum propter suffragium in scrutinio praestitum nisi adeo in eo perstiterint quod ex eo votis eorum communis electio subsequatur nequaquam eligendi potestate privantur licet pro eo quod indignum nominando scienter contra conscientias suas agunt et divinam vindictam et apostolicae ultionis motum quem qualitas facti suaserit possint non immerito formidare.
By the oracle of perpetual sanction we declare that those who knowingly in elections nominate an unworthy person on account of a suffrage given in a scrutiny, unless they have so persisted in it that from it a common election follows from their votes, are by no means deprived of the power of electing, although for the fact that by knowingly nominating an unworthy person they act against their own consciences, they may be able not undeservedly to fear both divine vengeance and the motion of apostolic vengeance, which the quality of the fact will have persuaded.
¶81
Nulli licere decernimus postquam in scrutinio nominaverit aliquem et electio fuerit subsecuta vel postquam praestiterit electioni de ipso ab aliis celebratae consensum illum super electione ipsa nisi ex causis postea emergentibus impugnare vel nisi ei morum ipsius antea celata de novo pandatur improbitas seu alicuius alterius latentis vitii vel defectus quae verisimiliter ignorare potuerit veritas reveletur. De huiusmodi autem ignorantia fidem proprio faciat iuramento.
We decree that after anyone has nominated someone in a scrutiny and an election has followed, or after he has given consent to an election celebrated by others concerning him, it is not lawful for him to impugn that same election unless on account of causes subsequently emerging, or unless the impropriety of his morals previously hidden, or the truth of some other latent vice or defect which he could probably have been ignorant of, be newly revealed to him. Concerning ignorance of this kind, let him make faith by his own oath.
¶82
Si quando contigerit duabus electionibus celebratis partem alteram eligentium duplo maiorem numero inveniri contra electores qui partem reliquam sic excedunt ad extenuationem zeli meriti vel auctoritatis ipsorum reliquis vel electo ab eis aliquid opponendi omnem praesenti decreto interdicimus facultatem. Si quid autem opponere voluerint quod votum illius cui opponitur nullum redderet ipso iure id eis non intelligimus interdictum.
If at any time it happen that with two elections celebrated, one part of the electors is found to be greater by double in number, against the electors who thus exceed the remaining part by double, or against the elect by them, we forbid by the present decree all faculty of opposing anything for the lessening of zeal, merit, or authority of the same. But if they wish to oppose anything which would render the vote of him against whom it is opposed null by the law itself, we do not understand that to be forbidden to them.
¶83
Quamvis constitutio felicis recordationis Alexandri papae iv praedecessoris nostri causas electionum episcopalium seu super electionibus episcoporum exortas non immerito maioribus causis annumerans cognitiones ipsarum per appellationes quaslibet devolvi asserat ad apostolicae sedis examen nos tamen et temerariam appellantium audaciam et effrenatam appellationum frequentiam refrenare volentes hac generali constitutione duximus providendum ut si extra iudicium in praedictis electionibus vel in aliis de dignitatibus episcopatu maioribus celebratis expressa causa manifeste frivola contigerit appellari per appellationem huiusmodi nequaquam ad sedem eamdem negotium devolvatur.
Although the constitution of Pope Alexander IV of happy memory, our predecessor, not undeservedly numbering the causes of episcopal elections or those arising concerning episcopal elections among the greater causes, asserts that their cognitions are devolved by any appeals to the examination of the Apostolic See, we, nevertheless wishing to restrain both the temerarious audacity of those appealing and the unbridled frequency of appeals, by this general constitution have decided to provide that if outside a judgment in the aforesaid elections or in others concerning greater dignities celebrated, it happen to be appealed from a manifestly frivolous expressed cause, the business by no means be devolved by such an appeal to the same See.
¶84
Sed cum in electionum earumdem negotiis in iudicio vel extra iudicium appellatur in scriptis ex causa probabili quae probata deberet legitima reputari ad sedem ipsam huiusmodi negotia deferantur.
But when in the business of the same elections, in a judgment or outside a judgment, an appeal is made in writing from a probable cause, which if proven ought to be considered legitimate, let such businesses be carried to the same See.
¶85
Ceterum in praemissis casibus liceat partibus ab huiusmodi appellationibus nulla tamen interveniente pravitate recedere antequam praefatae sedi fuerint praesentatae.
Moreover, in the aforesaid cases, let it be lawful for the parties to withdraw from such appeals, no malice however intervening, before they have been presented to the aforesaid See.
¶86
Inferiores autem iudices quorum erat ipsarum causarum cognitio appellatione cessante an in hoc pravitas intercesserit ante omnia diligenter inquirant et si eam intercessisse reppererint se de causis ipsis nullatenus intromittant sed praefigant dictis partibus terminum peremptorium competentem in quo cum omnibus actis et monimentis suis apostolico se conspectui repraesentent.
Inferior judges however, whose it was the cognition of the same cases, the appeal ceasing, before all things let them diligently inquire whether malice has intervened in this, and if they find that it has intervened, let them by no means intrude themselves concerning the same cases, but let them set a peremptory competent term for the said parties, in which with all the acts and their documents they may present themselves to the apostolic sight.
¶87
Si forte inter cetera quae obiciuntur electo aut postulato seu alias promovendo ad aliquam dignitatem evidentem scientiae vel alium personae defectum opponi contingat in discussione obiectorum illum statuimus ordinem incommutabiliter observandum ut promovendus super defectu ipso ante omnia subiciatur examini cuius eventus examinandis aliis aut dabit initium aut negabit.
If perhaps among other things which are objected to the elect or postulant or otherwise to be promoted to any dignity, it happen that an evident defect of knowledge or other defect of the person be opposed, in the discussion of the objections we establish that order to be inviolably observed, that he who is to be promoted be subjected to examination concerning the defect itself before all things, the outcome of which will give either a beginning or a denial to the other things to be examined.
¶88
Ceterum si praemissi examinis exitus huiusmodi oppositiones docuerit veritate destitui opponentes omnino a prosecutione causae in qua talia obiecerunt excludimus et perinde puniri decernimus ac penitus in probatione omnium quae obiecerant defecissent.
Moreover, if the outcome of the aforesaid examination shall have taught that such oppositions are destitute of truth, we exclude those opposing altogether from the prosecution of the cause in which they have objected such things, and we decree them to be punished just as if they had altogether failed in the proof of all things which they had objected.
¶89
Sciant cuncti qui clericos vel quaslibet alias personas ecclesiasticas ad quos in aliquibus ecclesiis monasteriis aut aliis piis locis spectat electio pro eo quod rogati seu alias inducti eum pro quo rogabantur sive inducebantur eligere noluerunt vel consanguineos eorum aut ipsas ecclesias monasteria seu loca cetera beneficiis sive aliis bonis suis per se vel per alios spoliando seu alias iniuste persequendo gravare praesumpserint se ipso facto excommunicationis sententia innodatos.
Let all know that those who, because those whom they were asked or otherwise induced to elect were unwilling to elect the person for whom they were asked or induced, or their blood relatives, or have presumed to burden the same churches, monasteries, or other places by despoiling them by themselves or through others of benefices or other goods, or by otherwise unjustly persecuting them, are by that very fact bound by the sentence of excommunication.
¶90
Generali constitutione sancimus universos et singulos qui regalia custodiam sive guardiam advocationis vel defensionis titulum in ecclesiis monasteriis sive quibuslibet aliis piis locis de novo usurpare conantes bona ecclesiarum monasteriorum aut locorum ipsorum vacantium occupare praesumunt quantaecunque dignitatis honore praefulgeant clericos etiam ecclesiarum monachos monasteriorum et personas ceteras locorum eorumdem qui haec fieri procurant eo ipso excommunicationis sententiae subiacere.
By general constitution we sanction that all and each who, attempting to usurp newly the regalia, custody, or guard, or the title of advocacy or defense, in churches, monasteries, or any other pious places, presume to occupy the goods of vacant churches, monasteries, or the same places, of whatsoever dignity, honor, they may shine with, and also clerics of churches, monks of monasteries, and other persons of the same places who procure these things to be done, by that very fact lie under the sentence of excommunication.
¶91
Illos vero clericos qui se ut debent talia facientibus non opponunt de proventibus ecclesiarum seu locorum ipsorum pro tempore quo praemissa sine debita contradictione permiserint aliquid percipere districtius inhibemus.
Those clerics however who do not oppose themselves to those doing such things as they ought, we more strictly forbid to receive anything from the proceeds of the churches or the same places for the time in which they shall have permitted the aforesaid things without due contradiction.
¶92
Qui autem ab ipsarum ecclesiarum ceterorum que locorum fundatione vel ex antiqua consuetudine iura sibi huiusmodi vendicant ab illorum abusu sic prudenter abstineant et suos ministros in eis sollicite faciant abstinere quod ea quae non pertinent ad fructus sive reditus provenientes vacationis tempore non usurpent nec bona cetera quorum se asserunt habere custodiam dilabi permittant sed in bono statu conservent.
Those who claim such rights to themselves from the foundation of the same churches or other places or from ancient custom, so prudently abstain from their abuse, and cause their ministers to abstain solicitously, that they neither usurp those things which pertain not to the fruits or incomes coming at the time of vacancy, nor permit other goods whose custody they assert themselves to have to be dissipated, but preserve them in good state.
¶93
Licet canon a felicis recordationis Alexandro papa iii praedecessore nostro editus inter cetera statuerit ut nullus regimen ecclesiae parochialis suscipiat nisi vigesimum quintum annum aetatis attigerit ac scientia et moribus commendandus exsistat quod que talis ad regimen assumptus huiusmodi si monitus non fuerit praefixo a canonibus tempore in presbyterum ordinatus a regiminis eiusdem amoveatur officio et alii conferatur quia tamen in observatione canonis memorati se multi exhibent negligentes nos periculosam negligentiam volentes iuris executione suppleri praesenti decreto statuimus ut nullus ad regimen parochialis ecclesiae assumatur nisi sit idoneus scientia moribus et aetate decernentes collationes de parochialibus ecclesiis hiis qui non attigerunt vigesimum quintum annum de cetero faciendas viribus omnino carere.
Although a canon issued by Pope Alexander III of happy memory, our predecessor, established among other things that no one should receive the governance of a parish church unless he has attained the twenty-fifth year of age and exists to be commended in knowledge and morals, and that such a person, assumed to such governance, if he has not been admonished within the time prefixed by the canons to be ordained a priest, be removed from the office of the same governance and it be conferred upon another; because however many show themselves negligent in the observance of the aforesaid canon, we, wishing the perilous negligence to be supplied by the execution of the law, by the present decree establish that no one be assumed to the governance of a parish church unless he is suitable in knowledge, morals, and age, decreeing that collations of parish churches henceforth to be made to those who have not attained the twenty-fifth year altogether lack force.
¶94
Is etiam qui ad huiusmodi regimen assumetur ut gregis sibi crediti diligentius gerere curam possit in parochiali ecclesia cuius rector exstiterit residere personaliter teneatur et infra annum a sibi commissi regiminis tempore numerandum se faciat ad sacerdotium promoveri. Quod si infra idem tempus promotus non fuerit ecclesia sibi commissa nulla etiam praemissa monitione sit praesentis constitutionis auctoritate privatus.
He also who is assumed to such governance, so that he may be able more diligently to bear the care of the flock entrusted to him, shall be held to reside personally in the parish church of which he is rector, and within a year to be counted from the time of the governance committed to him, cause himself to be promoted to the priesthood. But if within the same time he shall not have been promoted, let the church committed to him, not even any admonition having been given, be deprived by the authority of the present constitution.
¶95
Super residentia vero ut praemittitur facienda possit ordinarius gratiam dispensative ad tempus facere prout causa rationabilis id exposcet.
Concerning residence indeed to be done as is set forth above, the ordinary may make grace dispensationally for a time, as reasonable cause shall demand.
¶96
Nemo deinceps parochialem ecclesiam alicui non constituto in aetate legitima et sacerdotio commendare praesumat nec tali etiam nisi unam et evidenti necessitate vel utilitate ipsius ecclesiae suadente. Huiusmodi autem commendam ut praemittitur rite factam declaramus ultra semestris temporis spatium non durare statuentes quicquid secus de commendis ecclesiarum parochialium actum fuerit esse irritum ipso iure.
No one henceforth presume to commend a parish church to anyone not constituted in legitimate age and in the priesthood; nor to such a person except one, and only when evident necessity or utility of the same church persuades it. Such a commendation moreover rightly made as is set forth above, we declare not to last beyond the space of a half-year, establishing that whatever shall have been otherwise done concerning commendations of parish churches is by the law itself invalid.
¶97
Eos qui clericos parochiae alienae absque superioris ordinandorum licentia scienter seu affectata ignorantia vel quocumque alio figmento quaesito praesumpserint ordinare per annum a collatione ordinum decernimus esse suspensos iis quae iura statuunt contra taliter ordinatos in suo robore duraturis.
Those who shall have presumed knowingly, or by affected ignorance, or by any other sought fiction, to ordain clerics of another's parish without the license of the superior of those to be ordained, we decree to be suspended for a year from the collation of orders, those things which the laws establish against such ordained persons to remain in their force.
¶98
Clericis quoque parochiae taliter suspensorum postquam eorum suspensio fuerit manifesta absque ipsorum etiam licentia interim recipiendi ordines ab aliis vicinis episcopis alias tamen canonice liberam concedimus facultatem.
To clerics also of the parish of such suspended persons, after their suspension shall have been manifest, without their license in the meantime, we grant free faculty to receive orders from other neighboring bishops, yet otherwise canonically.
¶99
Altercationis antiquae praesentis declarationis oraculo decidentes bigamos omni privilegio clericali declaramus esse nudatos et coercioni fori saecularis addictos consuetudine contraria non obstante. Ipsis quoque sub anathemate prohibemus deferre tonsuram vel habitum clericalem.
By the oracle of the present declaration, deciding an ancient altercation, we declare that bigamists are deprived of every clerical privilege and bound to the coercion of the secular forum, contrary custom notwithstanding. We also forbid them under anathema to bear the tonsure or clerical habit.
¶100
Si canonici a divinis cessare voluerint prout in ecclesiis aliquibus sibi ex consuetudine vel alias vendicant antequam ad cessationem huiusmodi quoquo modo procedant in instrumento publico vel patentibus litteris sigillorum suorum aut alterius authentici munimine roboratis cessationis ipsius causam exprimant et illud vel illas ei contra quem cessare intendunt assignent scituri quod si hoc praetermisso cessaverint vel causa quam expresserint non fuerit inventa canonica omnia quae de quibuscumque proventibus illius ecclesiae in qua cessatum fuerit cessationis tempore perceperunt restituent. Illa vero quae pro eodem tempore debentur eisdem nullo modo percipient sed ipsi ecclesiae cedere ac nihilominus ei contra quem cessaverant de damnis et iniuriis satisfacere tenebuntur.
If canons wish to cease from divine things, as in certain churches they claim for themselves by custom or otherwise, before they proceed in any way to such cessation, let them express the cause of that same cessation in a public instrument or in open letters fortified by the protection of their seals or of another authentic person, and assign that or those to him against whom they intend to cease, about to know that if they shall have ceased without this or the cause which they expressed shall not have been found canonical, they shall restore all things which they have received from any proceeds of that church in which it was ceased, at the time of cessation. Those things indeed which are owed for the same time to them, they shall by no means receive, but they shall yield them to the church; and nevertheless they shall be held to satisfy him against whom they ceased concerning damages and injuries.
¶101
Si autem causa eadem canonica fuerit iudicata is qui occasionem cessationi dederat ad omne interesse dictis canonicis et ecclesiae cui debitum officium eius est culpa subtractum ad certam quantitatem taxandam et in divini cultus augmentum convertendam superioris arbitrio condemnetur.
If however the same cause shall have been judged canonical, he who gave occasion to the cessation is to be condemned by the judgment of the superior to all interest of the said canons and of the church to which the debt of its office is by his fault subtracted, to be taxed to a certain quantity and to be converted to the increase of divine worship.
¶102
Ceterum detestabilem abusum horrendae indevotionis illorum qui crucis beatae virginis aliorum ve sanctorum imagines seu statuas irreverenti ausu tractantes eas in aggravationem cessationis huiusmodi prosternunt in terram urticis spinis que supponunt penitus reprobantes aliquid tale de cetero fieri districtius prohibemus statuentes ut in eos qui contra fecerint ultrix procedat dura sententia quae delinquentes sic graviter puniat quod alios a similium praesumptione compescat.
Moreover, the detestable abuse of horrible indevotion of those who, handling the images or statues of the cross, of the blessed Virgin, or of other saints with irreverent daring, cast them down to the ground for the aggravation of such cessation and put them under nettles and thorns, altogether reproving, we more strictly forbid that anything of the kind be done henceforth, establishing that a harsh avenging sentence proceed against those who shall have acted against it, which may so gravely punish those delinquent that it restrains others from the presumption of similar things.
¶103
Ordinarii locorum subditos suos plures dignitates vel ecclesias quibus animarum cura imminet obtinentes seu personatum aut dignitatem cum alio beneficio cui cura similis est annexa districte compellant dispensationes auctoritate quarum huiusmodi ecclesias personatus seu dignitates canonice tenere se asserunt infra tempus pro facti qualitate ipsorum ordinariorum moderandum arbitrio exhibere. Quod si forte iusto impedimento cessante nullam dispensationem infra idem tempus contigerit exhiberi ecclesiae beneficia personatus seu dignitates quae sine dispensatione aliqua eo ipso illicite detineri constabit per eos ad quos eorumdem collatio pertinet libere personis idoneis conferantur.
Let ordinaries of places strictly compel their subjects who are obtaining several dignities or churches to which the cure of souls is imminent, or a personate or dignity with another benefice to which a similar cure is annexed, to exhibit the dispensations by authority of which they assert themselves canonically to hold such churches, personates, or dignities, within a time to be moderated by the judgment of the same ordinaries according to the quality of the fact. But if perhaps, just impediment ceasing, it happen that no dispensation is exhibited within the same time, let the churches, benefices, personates, or dignities, which without any dispensation by that very fact will be established to be illicitly retained, be freely conferred upon suitable persons by those to whom the collation of them pertains.
¶104
Ceterum si dispensatio exhibita sufficiens evidenter appareat exhibens nequaquam in beneficiis huiusmodi quae canonice obtinet molestetur. Provideat tamen ordinarius qualiter nec animarum cura in eisdem ecclesiis personatibus seu dignitatibus negligatur nec beneficia ipsa debitis obsequiis defraudentur.
Moreover, if a dispensation exhibited shall evidently appear sufficient, let him who exhibits it by no means be molested concerning the benefices of this kind which he canonically obtains. Yet let the ordinary provide that neither the cure of souls in the same churches, personates, or dignities be neglected, nor the benefices themselves be defrauded of the due services.
¶105
Si vero de dispensationis exhibitae sufficientia dubitetur super hoc erit ad sedem apostolicam recurrendum cuius est aestimare quem modum sui beneficii esse velit.
If indeed there be doubt concerning the sufficiency of the dispensation exhibited, concerning this there shall be recourse to the Apostolic See, to which it belongs to estimate what manner of its benefit it wishes to be.
¶106
In conferendis insuper personatibus dignitatibus et aliis beneficiis curam habentibus animarum annexam iidem ordinarii diligentiam illam observent ut personatum dignitatem vel aliquod beneficium similem curam habens alicui plura similia obtinenti non ante conferre praesumant quam eis super obtentis dispensatio evidenter sufficiens ostendatur. Qua etiam ostensa ita demum ad collationem procedi volumus si appareat per eamdem quod is cui est collatio facienda huiusmodi personatum dignitatem vel beneficium retinere licite valeat cum obtentis vel si ea quae sic obtinet libere ac sponte resignet. Aliter autem de personatibus dignitatibus et beneficiis talibus facta collatio nullius penitus sit momenti.
Moreover, in conferring personates, dignities, and other benefices having the cure of souls annexed, the same ordinaries shall observe that diligence that they presume not to confer a personate, dignity, or any benefice having similar cure upon anyone obtaining several such things, before a dispensation evidently sufficient concerning those obtained is shown to them. Which also having been shown, thus at length we wish them to proceed to collation if it appears through the same that he to whom collation is to be made may lawfully retain such personate, dignity, or benefice together with those obtained, or if he freely and spontaneously resigns those which he thus obtains. Otherwise, a collation made concerning such personates, dignities, and benefices shall be of no force whatsoever.
¶107
Properandum nobis visum est ut malitiosis litium protractionibus occurratur quod speramus efficaciter provenire si eos qui circa iudicia suum ministerium exhibent ad id congruis remediis dirigamus.
It has seemed to us that we must hasten so that we may meet malicious protractions of lawsuits, which we hope will come about effectively if we direct to that end by suitable remedies those who offer their ministry in judgments.
¶108
Cum igitur ea quae ad hoc salubriter fuerant circa patronos causarum legali sanctione provisa desuetudine abolita videantur nos sanctionem eandem praesentis redivivae constitutionis suffragio cum aliqua tamen adiectione necnon et moderamine renovantes statuimus ut omnes et singuli advocationis officium in foro ecclesiastico sive apud sedem apostolicam sive alibi exercentes praestent tactis sacrosanctis evangeliis iuramentum quod in omnibus causis ecclesiasticis et aliis in eodem foro tractandis quarum assumpserunt patrocinium vel assument omni virtute sua omni que ope id quod verum et iustum existimaverint suis clientulis inferre procurent nihil in hoc studii quod eis sit possibile relinquentes quod que in quacunque parte iudicii eis innotuerit improbam fore causam quam in sua fide receperant amplius non patrocinabuntur eidem immo ab ea omnino recedent a communione illius se penitus separantes reliquis quae circa haec sunt in eadem sanctione statuta inviolabiliter observandis.
Since therefore those things which had been salutarily provided concerning the patrons of causes by legal sanction seem to have been abolished by desuetude, we, renewing the same sanction by the support of the present revived constitution, with some addition however and moderation, establish that all and each exercising the office of advocacy in the ecclesiastical forum, whether before the Apostolic See or elsewhere, give, having touched the holy Gospels, an oath that in all ecclesiastical causes and others to be treated in the same forum, of which they have assumed or will assume the patronage, with all their virtue and with all their aid they will strive to bring to their clients that which they judge to be true and just, leaving nothing in this study which is possible for them; and that in whatever part of the judgment it shall become known to them that the cause which they had received in their faith is improper, they will not further give patronage to it; indeed they will withdraw altogether from it, separating themselves altogether from its communion, with the other things established in the same sanction concerning these matters to be inviolably observed.
¶109
Procuratores insuper iuramento simili astringantur.
Moreover, let proctors be bound by a similar oath.
¶110
Huiusmodi quoque iuramentum tam advocati quam procuratores in foro in quo idem assumpserunt officium teneantur annis singulis iterare.
Also, both advocates and proctors shall be held to repeat such an oath each year in the forum in which they have assumed the same office.
¶111
Qui vero ad eamdem sedem veniunt vel ad curiam cuiuslibet ecclesiastici iudicis in qua nondum tale praestiterant iuramentum accedunt in aliquibus singularibus causis patrocinium vel procurationis ministerium praestituri praestent in singulis causis eisdem mota controversia simile iuramentum.
Those who come to the same See or to the court of any ecclesiastical judge in which they have not yet given such oath, about to give patronage or the ministry of proctorship in any singular causes, shall give a similar oath in each of the same causes, after controversy has been moved.
¶112
Advocati autem et procuratores qui iuxta praedictam formam iurare noluerint executionem officiorum suorum huiusmodi noluntate durante sibi noverint interdictam. Quod si iuramentum praestitum violare praesumpserint praeter reatum periurii consiliarii etiam qui scienter iniquam causam foverint divinam et nostram maledictionem incurrant a qua non aliter liberentur nisi duplum eius restituerint quod pro tam iniquis advocatione procuratione vel consilio receperunt ac nihilominus de damnis quae per iniqua huiusmodi ministeria partibus irrogarunt illis satisfacere teneantur.
Advocates and proctors who are unwilling to swear according to the aforesaid form, let them know that the execution of such offices is forbidden to themselves while such unwillingness lasts. But if they shall have presumed to violate the oath given, besides the guilt of perjury, counselors also who knowingly have fostered an unjust cause shall incur the divine and our malediction, from which they shall not otherwise be liberated unless they have restored double of that which they have received for such unjust advocacy, proctorship, or counsel, and nevertheless they shall be held to satisfy the parties concerning the losses which they have inflicted by such unjust ministries.
¶113
Ceterum ne cupiditatis ardor aliquos ad haec salubria statuta contemnenda praecipiet districtius inhibemus ne aliquis advocatus in quacunque causa ultra viginti procurator vero ultra duodecim libras turonensium recipere salarii nomine vel etiam sub palmarii colore praesumant. Qui autem ultra receperint nequaquam dominium eorum quae praedictam quantitatem excedunt acquirant sed ad restitutionem integram teneantur illorum ita quod nihil horum ad quae restituenda eos teneri praemisimus in fraudem praesentis constitutionis remitti possit eisdem. Et insuper advocati constitutionem praesentem taliter violantes ab advocationis officio triennio suspendantur. Procuratores vero extunc sibi sciant cuiuslibet procurationis in iudicio licentiam denegatam.
Moreover, lest the ardor of cupidity should impel anyone to contemn these salutary statutes, we more strictly forbid that any advocate in any cause presume to receive beyond twenty, and a proctor beyond twelve, pounds of tournois by way of salary or even under the color of a palmarium. Those who receive beyond shall by no means acquire dominion of those things which exceed the aforesaid quantity, but shall be held to complete restitution of them, in such a way that none of those things to the restitution of which we have said they are bound may be remitted to them in fraud of the present constitution. And moreover, advocates thus violating the present constitution shall be suspended from the office of advocacy for three years. Proctors indeed from then shall know that license of any proctorship in judgment is denied to them.
¶114
Absolutionis beneficium ab excommunicationis sententia vel quamcumque revocationem ipsius aut suspensionis seu etiam interdicti per vim vel metum extorta praesentis constitutionis auctoritate omnino viribus vacuamus. Ne autem sine vindicta violentiae crescat audacia eos qui absolutionem sive revocationem huiusmodi vi vel metu extorserint excommunicationis sententiae decernimus subiacere.
The benefit of absolution from a sentence of excommunication or any revocation of it, or of suspension, or even of interdict, extorted by force or fear, we altogether void of force by the authority of the present constitution. Lest however without vengeance the audacity of violence may grow, we decree that those who shall have extorted absolution or such revocation by force or fear lie under the sentence of excommunication.
¶115
Statutum felicis recordationis Clementis papae iv praedecessoris nostri de dignitatibus et beneficiis in curia Romana vacantibus nequaquam per alium quam per Romanum pontificem conferendis decrevimus taliter moderandum ut ii ad quos eorumdem beneficiorum et dignitatum spectat collatio statuto non obstante praedicto demum post mensem a die quo dignitates seu beneficia ipsa vacaverint numerandum ea conferre valeant tantummodo per seipsos vel ipsis agentibus in remotis per suos vicarios generales in eorum dioecesibus exsistentes quibus id canonice sit commissum.
The statute of Pope Clement IV of happy memory, our predecessor, concerning dignities and benefices vacant at the Roman Curia, not to be conferred by anyone other than the Roman pontiff, we have decreed thus to be moderated, that those to whom the collation of the same benefices and dignities pertains, the aforesaid statute notwithstanding, after a month from the day on which the dignities or benefices themselves shall have been vacant, to be counted, may confer them only by themselves, or, if they are acting in remote places, through their vicars general existing in their dioceses, to whom this has been canonically committed.
¶116
Hoc consultissimo prohibemus edicto universos et singulos praelatos ecclesias sibi commissas bona immobilia seu iura ipsarum laicis submittere subicere seu supponere absque capituli sui consensu et sedis apostolicae licentia speciali non concedendo bona ipsa vel iura in emphitheosim seu alias alienando in forma et casibus a iure permissis sed constituendo vel recognoscendo seu profitendo ab illis ea tanquam a superioribus se tenere seu ab ipsis eadem advocando prout in quibusdam partibus vulgariter dicitur avoer vel ipsos patronos sive advocatos ecclesiarum seu bonorum ipsorum perpetuo aut ad tempus non modicum statuendo.
By this most well-advised edict we forbid all and each prelate to submit, subject, or subordinate the churches committed to themselves, their immovable goods or rights, to laypersons without the consent of their chapter and special license of the Apostolic See, by granting the goods or rights themselves in emphyteusis or otherwise alienating them in the form and cases permitted by law, but by constituting or recognizing or professing that they hold them from those persons as from superiors, or by avowing the same from them, as in certain parts is vulgarly called "avoer," or by establishing them as perpetual or for no short time patrons or advocates of the churches or of the same goods.
¶117
Contractus autem omnes etiam iuramenti poenae vel alterius cuiuslibet firmitatis adiectione vallatos quos de talibus alienationibus sine huiusmodi licentia et consensu contigerit celebrari et quicquid ex eis secutum fuerit decernimus adeo viribus omnino carere ut nec ius aliquod tribuant nec praescribendi etiam causam parent. Et nihilominus praelatos qui secus egerint ipso facto ab officio et administratione clericos etiam qui scientes contra inhibitionem praedictam aliquid esse praesumptum id superiori denuntiare neglexerint a perceptione beneficiorum quae in ecclesia sic gravata obtinent triennio statuimus esse suspensos.
All contracts moreover, even fortified by the addition of an oath, a penalty, or any other firmness, which shall happen to be celebrated concerning such alienations without such license and consent, and whatever shall have followed from them, we decree to so altogether lack force that they neither give any right nor even furnish cause of prescription. And nevertheless we establish that prelates who shall have acted otherwise are by the very fact suspended from office and administration, and clerics also who, knowing that something has been presumed against the aforesaid inhibition, have neglected to denounce it to a superior, are suspended from the perception of the benefices which they obtain in the church thus burdened for three years.
¶118
Laici vero qui praelatos vel capitula ecclesiarum seu alias personas ecclesiasticas ad submissiones huiusmodi faciendas hactenus compulerunt nisi post competentem monitionem remissa submissione quam per vim vel metum exegerant ecclesias et bona ecclesiastica eis submissa taliter in sua libertate dimittant illi etiam qui de cetero praelatos vel personas easdem ad talia facienda compulerint cuiuscumque sint conditionis aut status excommunicationis sint sententia innodati.
Laypersons indeed who have compelled prelates or chapters of churches or other ecclesiastical persons to make such submissions, unless after competent admonition, the submission which they extorted by force or fear having been remitted, they so dismiss the churches and ecclesiastical goods thus submitted in their liberty, and also those who henceforth shall have compelled the same prelates or persons to do such things, of whatsoever condition or status they may be, are by that very fact bound by the sentence of excommunication.
¶119
Ex contractibus praeterea super praemissis huiusmodi licentia et consensu intervenientibus hactenus initis vel quos in futurum iniri continget seu occasione illorum laici ultra id quod eis ex natura contractuum ipsorum vel adhibita in illis lege permittitur aliquid non usurpent. Qui vero secus egerint nisi legitime moniti ab huiusmodi usurpatione destiterint restituendo etiam quae taliter usurparant eo ipso sententiam excommunicationis incurrant et extunc ad supponendum terram ipsorum si opus fuerit ecclesiastico interdicto libere procedatur.
Moreover, from contracts entered into thus far or which shall happen to be entered into in the future concerning the aforesaid, with such license and consent intervening, or on occasion of them, let laypersons not usurp anything beyond that which is permitted to them from the nature of the contracts themselves or from a law applied in them. Those who shall have acted otherwise, unless after being legitimately admonished they have desisted from such usurpation, also restoring those things which they had thus usurped, by that very fact incur the sentence of excommunication, and from then let it be freely proceeded to subject their land, if necessary, to ecclesiastical interdict.
¶120
Religionum diversitatem nimiam ne confusionem induceret generale concilium consulta prohibitione vitavit. Sed quia non solum importuna petentium inhiatio illarum postmodum multiplicationem extorsit verum etiam aliquorum praesumptuosa temeritas diversorum ordinum praecipue mendicantium quorum nondum approbationes meruere principium effrenatam quasi multitudinem adinvenit repetita constitutione districtius inhibentes ne aliquis de cetero novum ordinem aut religionem inveniat vel habitum novae religionis assumat cunctas affatim religiones et ordines mendicantes post dictum concilium adinventos qui nullam confirmationem sedis apostolicae meruerunt perpetuae prohibitioni subicimus et quatenus processerant revocamus.
The general council, by a well-advised prohibition, avoided excessive diversity of religions lest it might induce confusion. But because not only the importunate gaping of those seeking has extorted the multiplication of them afterward, but also the presumptuous temerity of some has invented an unbridled multitude of diverse orders, especially of the mendicants, whose approvals have not yet deserved a beginning, by a repeated constitution more strictly inhibiting that anyone henceforth invent a new order or religion or assume the habit of a new religion, we subject fully all the mendicant religions and orders invented after the said council, which have deserved no confirmation from the Apostolic See, to perpetual prohibition, and in so far as they have proceeded, we revoke them.
¶121
Confirmatos tamen per sedem eamdem post idem concilium institutos quibus ad congruam sustentationem reditus aut possessiones habere professio sive regula vel constitutiones quaelibet interdicunt sed per quaestum publicum tribuere victum solet incerta mendicitas modo subsidere decernimus infrascripto ut professoribus eorumdem ordinum ita liceat in illis remanere si velint quod nullum deinceps ad eorum professionem admittant nec de novo domum aut aliquem locum acquirant nec domos seu loca quae habent alienare valeant sine sedis eiusdem licentia speciali. Nos enim ea dispositioni sedis apostolicae reservamus in terrae sanctae subsidium vel pauperum aut alios pios usus per locorum ordinarios vel eos quibus sedes ipsa commiserit convertenda. Si vero secus praesumptum fuerit nec personarum receptio nec domorum vel locorum acquisitio aut ipsorum ceterorum que bonorum alienatio valeat et nihilominus contrarium facientes sententiam excommunicationis incurrant.
Those however confirmed by the same See after the same council, which were instituted, to which the profession or rule or any constitutions forbid to have incomes or possessions for congruous sustenance, but uncertain mendicancy is accustomed to provide food by public quest, we decree henceforth to subsist in the following manner: that it be lawful for the professors of the same orders to remain in them if they wish, but that they admit no one henceforth to their profession, nor acquire any house or any place anew, nor be able to alienate the houses or places which they have without special license of the same See. For we reserve them to the disposition of the Apostolic See, to be converted into the subsidy of the Holy Land or of the poor or other pious uses, by the ordinaries of the places or by those whom the same See shall have commissioned. If indeed it shall have been presumed otherwise, neither the reception of persons nor the acquisition of houses or places or the alienation of the same or of other goods shall be valid, and nevertheless those doing the contrary shall incur the sentence of excommunication.
¶122
Personis quoque ipsorum ordinum omnino interdicimus quoad extraneos praedicationis et audiendae confessionis officium ac etiam sepulturam.
Also we altogether forbid to the persons of the same orders the office of preaching and hearing confessions, and also burial, with respect to outsiders.
¶123
Sane ad praedicatorum et minorum ordines quos evidens ex eis utilitas ecclesiae universali proveniens perhibet approbatos praesentem non patimur constitutionem extendi.
Truly, we do not suffer the present constitution to be extended to the orders of Preachers and Minors, which evident utility coming from them to the universal Church attests to be approved.
¶124
Ceterum Carmelitarum et eremitarum sancti Augustini ordines quorum institutio dictum concilium generale praecessit in suo statu manere concedimus donec de ipsis fuerit aliter ordinatum. Intendimus siquidem tam de illis quam de reliquis etiam non mendicantibus ordinibus prout animarum saluti et eorum statui expedire viderimus providere.
Moreover, we concede the orders of Carmelites and Hermits of Saint Augustine, whose institution preceded the said general council, to remain in their state, until it shall have been otherwise ordained concerning them. For we intend to provide both concerning them and concerning the remaining orders, even not mendicant, as we shall see to be expedient for the salvation of souls and their state.
¶125
Ad haec personis ordinum ad quos constitutio praesens extenditur transeundi ad reliquos ordines approbatos licentiam concedimus generalem ita quod nullus ordo ad alium vel conventus ad conventum se ac loca totaliter transferat sedis eiusdem permissione super hoc specialiter non obtenta.
To these things, we grant general license to persons of the orders to which the present constitution extends of passing over to the remaining approved orders, in such a way that no order transfers itself totally to another or convent to convent and places, without permission of the same See specially obtained concerning this.
¶126
Exigit perversorum audacia ut non simus sola delictorum prohibitione contenti sed poenam etiam delinquentibus imponamus. Constitutionem itaque felicis recordationis Innocentii papae iv praedecessoris nostri editam super non recipiendis in pecunia procurationibus et super receptione munerum visitantibus eorum que familiis interdicta quam multorum fertur temeritas praeterire volentes inviolabiliter observari eam decrevimus poenae adiectione iuvandam statuentes ut universi et singuli qui ob procurationem sibi ratione visitationis debitam exigere pecuniam vel etiam a volente recipere aut alias constitutionem ipsam recipiendo munera sive visitationis officio non impenso procurationem in victualibus aut aliquid aliud procurationis occasione violare praesumpserint duplum eius quod receperint ecclesiae a qua id receptum fuerit infra mensem reddere teneantur alioquin extunc patriarchae archiepiscopi episcopi duplum ipsum ultra praedictum tempus restituere differentes ingressum sibi ecclesiae interdictum. Inferiores vero ab officio et beneficio noverint se suspensos quousque de duplo huiusmodi gravatis ecclesiis plenariam satisfactionem impendant nulla eis in hoc dantium remissione liberalitate seu gratia valitura.
The audacity of perverse persons demands that we should not be content with the mere prohibition of delicts, but also impose punishment upon those delinquent. The constitution therefore of Pope Innocent IV of happy memory, our predecessor, issued concerning not receiving procurations in money and concerning the reception of gifts being forbidden to visitors and their families, which the temerity of many is said to pass over, wishing it to be inviolably observed, we have decreed to be aided by the addition of a penalty, establishing that all and each who, on account of a procuration owed to themselves by reason of visitation, presume to exact money or also to receive it from one willing to give, or otherwise to violate the same constitution by receiving gifts, or by not having performed the office of visitation to receive a procuration in victuals or anything else on occasion of a procuration, shall be held to restore double of that which they have received to the church from which it was received, within a month; otherwise, from then, patriarchs, archbishops, bishops delaying to restore the double beyond the aforesaid time, let them know that the entrance of the church is forbidden to themselves. Inferiors indeed shall know themselves to be suspended from office and benefice until they make full satisfaction concerning such double to the churches burdened, no remission of the givers, liberality, or grace availing for them in this.
¶127
Decet domum domini sanctitudo decet ut cuius in pace factus est locus eius sit cultus cum debita veneratione pacificus.
Holiness befits the house of the Lord; it befits that His place, which has been made in peace, be peaceful with due veneration.
¶128
Sit itaque ad ecclesias humilis et devotus ingressus sit in eis quieta conversatio deo grata inspicientibus placida quae considerantes non solum instruat sed reficiat.
Let there be therefore to churches a humble and devout entrance, in them a quiet conversation, pleasing to God, pleasing to those inspecting, which not only instructs but also refreshes those considering.
¶129
Convenientes ibidem nomen illud quod est super omne nomen a quo aliud sub coelo non est datum hominibus in quo salvos fieri credentes oporteat nomen videlicet Iesu Christi qui salvum faciet populum suum a peccatis eorum exhibitione reverentiae specialis attollant et quod generaliter scribitur ut in nomine Iesu omne genu flectatur singuli singulariter in seipsis implentes praecipue dum aguntur missarum sacra mysteria gloriosum illud nomen quandocumque recolitur flectant genua cordis sui quod vel capitis inclinatione testentur.
Let those convening there lift up with the showing of special reverence that name which is above every name, than which no other is given under heaven to men in which believing it is fitting that they be saved, the name namely of Jesus Christ, who shall save His people from their sins; and that which is written generally, "that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow," let each singly in themselves fulfill, especially when the sacred mysteries of the Mass are being performed, whenever that glorious name is recalled, let them bow the knees of their heart, which let them show forth by the inclination of the head.
¶130
Attendantur in locis ipsis intentis praecordiis sacra solemnia devotis animis orationibus intendatur.
Let the sacred solemnities be attended to in the same places with intent hearts; to prayers with devout minds let attention be given.
¶131
Nullus in locis eisdem in quibus cum pace ac quiete vota convenit celebrari seditionem excitet conclamationem moveat impetum ve committat. Cessent in illis universitatum et societatum quarumlibet consilia conciones et publica parlamenta. Cessent vana et multo fortius foeda et prophana colloquia. Cessent confabulationes quaelibet. Sint postremo quaecunque alia divinum turbare possunt officium aut oculos divinae maiestatis offendere ab ipsis prorsus extranea ne ubi peccatorum est venia postulanda ibi peccandi detur occasio aut deprehendantur peccata committi.
Let no one in the same places, in which with peace and quiet it is fitting that vows be celebrated, excite sedition, raise a clamor, or commit an assault. Let there cease in them the counsels, meetings, and public parliaments of any universities and societies. Let there cease vain, and much more strongly, foul and profane conversations. Let there cease any confabulations. Finally, let whatever other things can disturb the divine office or offend the eyes of the divine majesty be altogether foreign from them, lest where the forgiveness of sins is to be sought, there occasion of sinning be given, or sins be discovered to be committed.
¶132
Cessent in ecclesiis earum que cimiteriis negotiationes et praecipue nundinarum ac fori cuiusque tumultus. Omnis in eis saecularium iudiciorum strepitus conquiescat. Nulla inibi causa per laicos criminalis maxime agitetur. Sint loca eadem a laicorum cognitionibus aliena.
Let there cease in churches and their cemeteries trading and especially the tumult of fairs and of any market. In them let all noise of secular judgments be quieted. Let no criminal cause, especially by laypersons, be agitated in them. Let the same places be foreign to the cognitions of laypersons.
¶133
Ordinarii locorum haec faciant observari suadenda suadeant interdicta huius canonis auctoritate compescant ad haec alios etiam in ecclesiis ipsis magis assiduos et ad praemissa idoneos deputando. Et nihilominus processus iudicum saecularium ac specialiter sententiae prolatae in eisdem locis omni careant robore firmitatis. Qui vero praemissas inhibitiones animo petulanti contempserint praeter processum ordinariorum et deputandorum ab ipsis divinae ultionis et nostrae poterunt acrimoniam formidare donec suum confessi reatum a similibus firmato proposito deliberaverint abstinere.
Let the ordinaries of places cause these things to be observed: let them persuade what is to be persuaded; let them restrain with the authority of this canon what is forbidden; for these things also deputing others more assiduous in the churches themselves and suitable to the aforesaid. And nevertheless let the processes of secular judges, and especially sentences pronounced in the same places, lack all force of firmness. Those who shall have despised the aforesaid inhibitions with a petulant spirit, besides the process of ordinaries and those to be deputed by them, may fear the acrimony of divine vengeance and ours until, having confessed their guilt, they shall have resolved with fixed purpose to abstain from similar things.
¶134
Usurarum voraginem quae animas devorat et facultates exhaurit compescere cupientes constitutionem Lateranensis concilii contra usurarios editam sub divinae maledictionis interminatione praecipimus inviolabiliter observari.
Desiring to restrain the voracious whirlpool of usuries which devours souls and exhausts resources, we command the constitution of the Lateran Council issued against usurers, under the threat of divine malediction, to be inviolably observed.
¶135
Et quia quo minor feneratoribus aderit fenerandi commoditas eo magis adimetur fenus exercendi libertas hac generali constitutione sancimus ut nec collegium nec alia universitas vel singularis persona cuiuscunque sit dignitatis conditionis aut status alienigenas et alios non oriundos de terris ipsorum publice pecuniam fenebrem exercentes aut exercere volentes ad hoc domos in terris suis conducere vel conductas habere aut alias habitare permittat sed huiusmodi usurarios manifestos omnes infra tres menses de terris suis expellant numquam aliquos tales de cetero admissuri. Nemo illis ad fenus exercendum domos locet vel sub alio titulo quocunque concedat. Qui vero contrarium fecerint si personae fuerint ecclesiasticae patriarchae archiepiscopi episcopi suspensionis minores vero personae singulares excommunicationis collegium autem seu alia universitas interdicti sententiam ipso facto se noverint incursuros. Quam si per mensem animo sustinuerint indurato terrae ipsorum quandiu in eis iidem usurarii commorantur extunc ecclesiastico subiaceant interdicto. Ceterum si laici fuerint per suos ordinarios ab huiusmodi excessu omni privilegio cessante per censuram ecclesiasticam compescantur.
And because the less facility of lending will be at hand to moneylenders, the more the liberty of exercising usury will be taken away, by this general constitution we sanction that neither a college nor any other university or singular person, of whatsoever dignity, condition, or status, permit foreigners and others not native from their lands, publicly exercising or wishing to exercise usurious money, to hire houses in their lands for this purpose, or to have them hired, or otherwise to dwell; but let them expel all manifest usurers of this kind from their lands within three months, at no time about to admit any such persons thereafter. Let no one let houses to them for exercising usury, or concede them by any other title. Those who shall have acted against this, if they are ecclesiastical persons, let them know themselves to have incurred by the very fact the sentence of suspension; inferior singular persons, of excommunication; a college or other university, of interdict. Which if they shall have sustained with hardened spirit for a month, from then let their lands, as long as the same usurers dwell in them, lie under ecclesiastical interdict. Moreover, if they are laypersons, let them be restrained by their ordinaries from such excess, all privilege ceasing, through ecclesiastical censure.
¶136
Quamquam usurarii manifesti de usuris quas receperant satisfieri expressa quantitate vel indistincte in ultima voluntate mandaverint nihilominus tamen eis sepultura ecclesiastica denegetur donec vel de usuris ipsis fuerit prout patiuntur facultates eorum plenarie satisfactum vel illis quibus est facienda restitutio si praesto sint ipsi aut alii qui eis possint acquirere vel eis absentibus loci ordinario aut eius vices gerenti sive rectori parochiae in qua testator habitat coram aliquibus fidedignis de ipsa parochia quibus quidem ordinario vicario et rectori praedicto modo cautionem huiusmodi eorum nomine liceat praesentis constitutionis auctoritate recipere ita quod illis proinde actio acquiratur aut servo publico de ipsius ordinarii mandato idonee de restitutione facienda sit cautum. Ceterum si receptarum usurarum sit quantitas manifesta illam semper in cautione praedicta exprimi volumus alioquin aliam recipientis cautionem huiusmodi arbitrio moderandam. Ipse tamen scienter non minorem quam verisimiliter creditur moderetur et si secus fecerit ad satisfactionem residui teneatur.
Although manifest usurers, concerning the usuries which they have received, may have commanded satisfaction to be made, by expressed quantity or indistinctly, in their last will, nevertheless let ecclesiastical burial be denied to them until either concerning the usuries themselves full satisfaction shall have been made, as their resources permit, or to those to whom restitution is to be made, if they are present, or to others who are able to acquire for them, or, with them absent, to the ordinary of the place or his vicar or the rector of the parish in which the testator dwells, before some trustworthy persons from that same parish, it shall have been sufficiently provided, by the authority of the present constitution, concerning such caution; to which ordinary, vicar, and rector, in the manner aforesaid, we permit to receive such caution in their name, in such a way that an action is thereby acquired for them, or to a public servant, at the command of the ordinary, it shall have been appropriately cautioned concerning restitution to be made. Moreover, if the quantity of usuries received is manifest, we wish that always be expressed in the aforesaid caution; otherwise, that the caution for receiving the other be moderated by the judgment of the ordinary. Yet he himself shall not knowingly moderate it to less than is believed with probability, and if he shall have acted otherwise, he shall be held to satisfaction of the remainder.
¶137
Omnes autem religiosos et alios qui manifestos usurarios contra praesentis sanctionis formam ad ecclesiasticam admittere ausi fuerint sepulturam poenae in Lateranensi concilio contra usurarios promulgatae statuimus subiacere.
Moreover, we establish that all religious and others who shall have dared to admit manifest usurers to ecclesiastical burial against the form of the present sanction lie under the penalty promulgated in the Lateran Council against usurers.
¶138
Nullus manifestorum usurariorum testamentis intersit aut eos ad confessionem admittat sive ipsos absolvat nisi de usuris satisfecerint vel de satisfaciendo pro suarum viribus facultatum praestent ut praemittitur idoneam cautionem. Testamenta quoque manifestorum usurariorum aliter facta non valeant sed sint irrita ipso iure.
Let no one of manifest usurers be present at testaments, or admit them to confession, or absolve them, unless concerning usuries they shall have satisfied or concerning satisfying according to the strength of their resources they shall have given, as is set forth above, suitable caution. Testaments also of manifest usurers otherwise made shall not be valid, but shall be invalid by the law itself.
¶139
Etsi pignorationes quas vulgaris elocutio represalias nominat in quibus alius pro alio praegravatur tanquam graves legibus et aequitati naturali contrariae civili sint constitutione prohibitae ut tamen earum prohibitio in personis ecclesiasticis tanto amplius timeatur quanto in illis specialius inhibentur eas concedi contra personas praedictas seu bona ipsarum aut quantumcumque generaliter praetextu cuiusvis consuetudinis quam potius reputamus abusum forte concessas ad illas extendi praesenti decreto districtius inhibemus.
Although pledges which the common expression calls "represaliae" (reprisals), in which one person is burdened for another, as contrary to laws and natural equity, are prohibited by civil constitution, so that nevertheless their prohibition may be so much the more feared in ecclesiastical persons as they are more specially forbidden, we more strictly inhibit by the present decree that they be granted or extended against the aforesaid persons or their goods, or however generally under pretext of any custom which we rather repute to be an abuse, perhaps granted, to be extended to them.
¶140
Illi autem qui contra fecerint adversus personas easdem pignorationes seu represalias concedendo vel extendendo ad eas nisi praesumptionem huiusmodi revocaverint a concessionis vel extensionis tempore infra mensem si personae singulares fuerint sententiam excommunicationis incurrant si vero universitas ecclesiastico subiaceat interdicto.
Those who shall have acted against this, by granting or extending such pledges or reprisals against the same ecclesiastical persons, unless they shall have revoked such presumption within a month from the time of the grant or extension, if they are singular persons, incur the sentence of excommunication; if indeed a university, let it lie under ecclesiastical interdict.
¶141
Constitutionem felicis recordationis Innocentii papae iv praedecessoris nostri quae prohibet participantes excommunicatis ea participatione quae solam minorem excommunicationem inducit monitione canonica non praemissa maiori excommunicatione ligari decernens promulgatam aliter excommunicationis sententiam non tenere ad tollendum omnem ambiguitatis scrupulum declarantes decernimus ita demum monitionem esse canonicam in hoc casu si aliis rite servatis eos qui monentur exprimat nominatim.
The constitution of Pope Innocent IV of happy memory, our predecessor, which prohibits that those participating with excommunicated persons by that participation which induces only the lesser excommunication are to be bound by the greater excommunication without canonical admonition having preceded, decreeing that a sentence of excommunication otherwise promulgated does not stand, for removing every scruple of ambiguity, declaring, we decree that in this case a canonical admonition is such only if, other things having been duly observed, it expresses those who are admonished by name.
¶142
Statuimus quoque ut inter monitiones quas ut canonicae promulgetur excommunicationis sententia statuunt iura permitti iudices sive monitionibus tribus utantur sive una pro omnibus observent aliquorum dierum competentia intervalla nisi facti necessitas ea suaserit aliter moderanda.
We establish also that among the admonitions which the laws establish that a sentence of excommunication be promulgated as canonical, judges observe competent intervals of some days, whether they use three admonitions or one for all, unless the necessity of the fact, to be otherwise moderated, shall have persuaded it.
¶143
Praesenti generali declaramus edicto beneficium relaxationis ad cautelam quoad interdicti sententias in civitates castra vel quaelibet alia loca generaliter promulgatas locum aliquatenus non habere.
By the present general edict we declare that the benefit of relaxation as a precaution concerning sentences of interdict generally promulgated over cities, castles, or any other places has by no means a place.
¶144
Quicunque pro eo quod in reges principes barones nobiles ballivos vel quoslibet ministros eorum aut quoscunque alios excommunicationis suspensionis seu interdicti sententia fuerit promulgata licentiam alicui dederint occidendi capiendi seu alias in personis aut bonis suis vel suorum gravandi eos qui tales sententias protulerunt sive quorum sunt occasione prolatae vel easdem sententias observantes seu taliter excommunicatis communicare nolentes nisi licentiam ipsam re integra revocaverint vel si ad bonorum captionem occasione ipsius licentiae sit processum nisi bona ipsa sint infra octo dierum spatium restituta aut satisfactio pro ipsis impensa in excommunicationis sententiam incidant ipso facto.
Whoever, because a sentence of excommunication, suspension, or interdict has been promulgated against kings, princes, barons, nobles, bailiffs, or any ministers of them, or any others, shall have given license to anyone to kill, capture, or otherwise burden in their persons or goods, or the goods of their dependents, those who have pronounced such sentences, or those on whose account they were pronounced, or those observing such sentences, or those unwilling to communicate with the excommunicated in such a way, unless they shall have revoked the license itself while the matter is still whole, or if it has been proceeded to the capture of goods on occasion of the same license, unless the goods themselves are restored within the space of eight days or satisfaction has been made for them, by the very fact incur the sentence of excommunication.
¶145
Eadem quoque sint sententia innodati omnes qui ausi fuerint praedicta licentia data uti vel aliquid praemissorum ad quae committenda licentiam dari prohibuimus alias committere suo motu.
Let those also be bound by the same sentence who shall have dared to use the aforesaid license when given, or to commit on their own motion any of the aforesaid things which we have forbidden license to be given to commit.
¶146
Qui autem in eadem sententia permanserint duorum mensium spatio extunc ab ea non possint nisi per sedem apostolicam absolutionis beneficium obtinere.
Those who shall have remained in the same sentence for the space of two months, from then may not obtain the benefit of absolution from it except through the Apostolic See.
¶147

This completes the remaining reform decrees of the Second Council of Lyon (Concilium Lugdunense II) of 1274.
