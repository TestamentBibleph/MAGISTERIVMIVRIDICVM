Second Lateran Council

Concilium Lateranense II

1139

Editorial note

Disciplinary. Schism of Anacletus II resolved. Clerical marriage formally invalidated (not merely prohibited). Limited independent dogmatic contribution.

SECOND LATERAN COUNCIL A.D. 1139
CANON I
Statuimus ut si quis simoniace ordinatus fuerit ab officio omnino cadat quod illicite usurpavit.
We decree that if anyone shall have been ordained simoniacally, let him entirely fall from the office which he has illicitly usurped.
¶1
CANON II
Si quis praebendam vel prioratum seu decanatum aut honorem vel promotionem aliquam ecclesiasticam seu quodlibet sacramentum ecclesiasticum utpote chrisma vel oleum sanctum consecrationes altarium vel ecclesiarum interveniente exsecrabili ardore avaritiae per pecuniam acquisivit honore male acquisito careat.
If anyone shall have acquired a prebend or a priory or a deanery or an honor or any ecclesiastical promotion or any ecclesiastical sacrament—such as chrism or holy oil, the consecrations of altars or of churches—by the intervention of the execrable ardor of avarice through money, let him lack the honor acquired by evil means.
¶2
Et emptor atque venditor et interventor nota infamiae percellantur.
And let the buyer and the seller and the intermediary be struck with the mark of infamy.
¶3
Et nec pro pastu nec sub obtentu alicuius consuetudinis ante vel post a quoquam aliquid exigatur vel ipse dare praesumat quoniam simoniacum est.
And let nothing be exacted from anyone either for sustenance or under the pretext of any custom before or after, nor let him presume to give anything, because it is simoniacal.
¶4
Sed libere et absque imminutione aliqua collata sibi dignitate atque beneficio perfruatur.
But let him freely and without any diminution enjoy the dignity and benefice conferred upon him.
¶5
CANON III
A suis episcopis excommunicatos ab aliis suscipi modis omnibus prohibemus.
Those excommunicated by their own bishops we prohibit in every manner from being received by others.
¶6
Qui vero excommunicato antequam ab eo qui eum excommunicaverit absolvatur scienter communicare praesumpserit pari sententiae teneatur obnoxius.
But whoever shall have presumed knowingly to communicate with an excommunicated person before he is absolved by the one who excommunicated him, let him be held liable to the same sentence.
¶7
CANON IV
Praecipimus etiam quod tam episcopi quam clerici in statu mentis in habitu corporis deo et hominibus placere studeant et nec in superfluitate scissura aut colore vestium nec in tonsura intuentium quorum forma et exemplum esse debent offendant aspectum sed potius quae eos deceat sanctitatem prae se ferant.
We command also that both bishops and clerics strive to please God and men in the state of their mind and in the habit of their body, and that they offend not the gaze of onlookers—whose form and example they ought to be—by excessiveness, slits, or color of garments, nor by tonsure, but rather that they bear before them the sanctity which befits them.
¶8
Quod si moniti ab episcopis emendari noluerint ecclesiasticis careant beneficiis.
But if, having been admonished by the bishops, they shall have refused to be corrected, let them lack ecclesiastical benefices.
¶9
CANON V
Illud autem quod in sacro Chalcedoniensi constitutum est concilio irrefragabiliter conservari praecipimus ut videlicet decedentium bona episcoporum a nullo omnino hominum diripiantur sed ad opus ecclesiae et successoris sui in libera oeconomi et clericorum permaneant potestate.
That moreover which was established in the sacred Council of Chalcedon we command to be preserved irrefragably: namely, that the goods of deceased bishops be seized by no men at all, but remain for the work of the church and of his successor in the free power of the steward and of the clerics.
¶10
Cesset igitur de cetero illa detestabilis et saeva rapacitas.
Let that detestable and savage rapacity therefore cease hereafter.
¶11
Si quis autem amodo hoc attentare praesumpserit excommunicationi subiaceat.
But if anyone shall have presumed to attempt this from now on, let him be subject to excommunication.
¶12
Qui vero morientium presbyterorum vel clericorum bona rapuerint simili sententiae subiiciantur.
But those who shall have seized the goods of dying presbyters or clerics, let them be subjected to the same sentence.
¶13
CANON VI
Decernimus etiam ut ii qui in ordine subdiaconatus et supra uxores duxerint aut concubinas habuerint officio atque ecclesiastico beneficio careant.
We decree also that those who in the order of subdeacon and above have taken wives or have had concubines shall lack office and ecclesiastical benefice.
¶14
Cum enim ipsi templum dei vasa domini sacrarium spiritus sancti debeant esse et dici indignum est eos cubilibus et immunditiis deservire.
For since they themselves ought to be and to be called the temple of God, the vessels of the Lord, the sanctuary of the Holy Spirit, it is unworthy that they serve beds and uncleanlinesses.
¶15
CANON VII
Ad haec praedecessorum nostrorum Gregorii vii Urbani et Paschalis Romanorum pontificum vestigiis inhaerentes praecipimus ut nullus missas eorum audiat quos uxores vel concubinas habere cognoverit.
To these things, adhering to the footsteps of our predecessors, the Roman pontiffs Gregory VII, Urban, and Paschal, we command that no one hear the masses of those whom he shall have known to have wives or concubines.
¶16
CANON VIII
Ut autem lex continentiae et deo placens munditia in ecclesiasticis personis et sacris ordinibus dilatetur statuimus quatenus episcopi presbyteri diaconi subdiaconi regulares canonici et monachi atque conversi professi qui sanctum transgredientes propositum uxores sibi copulare praesumpserint separentur.
So that the law of continence and the purity pleasing to God may be spread abroad among ecclesiastical persons and in sacred orders, we decree that bishops, presbyters, deacons, subdeacons, regular canons, and monks, and professed conversi who, transgressing the holy purpose, shall have presumed to bind wives to themselves, be separated.
¶17
Huiusmodi namque copulationem quam contra ecclesiasticam regulam constat esse contractam matrimonium non esse censemus.
For such a copulation, which is known to have been contracted contrary to the ecclesiastical rule, we judge not to be a marriage.
¶18
Qui etiam ab separati pro tantis excessibus condignam poenitentiam agant.
Let those also, having been separated, perform condign penance for such great excesses.
¶19
Id ipsum quoque de sanctimonialibus feminis si quod absit nubere attentaverint observari decernimus.
The same also concerning consecrated women we decree to be observed if—which may be absent—they shall have attempted to marry.
¶20
CANON IX
Prava autem consuetudo prout accepimus et detestabilis inolevit quoniam monachi et regulares canonici post susceptum habitum et professionem factam spreta beatorum magistrorum Benedicti et Augustini regula leges temporales et medicinam gratia lucri temporalis addiscunt.
But a wicked custom, as we have heard, and detestable has grown up, because monks and regular canons, after having received the habit and made profession, the rule of the blessed masters Benedict and Augustine having been spurned, learn temporal laws and medicine for the sake of temporal gain.
¶21
Avaritiae namque flammis accensi se patronos causarum faciunt.
For, inflamed with the flames of avarice, they make themselves patrons of cases.
¶22
Et cum psalmodiae et hymnis vacare debeant gloriosae vocis confisi munimine allegationum suarum varietate iustum et iniustum fas nefas que confundunt.
And whereas they ought to be free for psalmody and hymns, relying upon the protection of a glorious voice, by the variety of their allegations they confuse the just and the unjust, right and wrong.
¶23
Attestantur vero imperiales constitutiones absurdum immo et opprobrium esse clericis si peritos se velint disceptationum esse forensium.
But imperial constitutions attest that it is absurd—indeed even a disgrace—for clerics if they wish to be skilled in forensic disputations.
¶24
Huiusmodi temeratores graviter feriendos apostolica auctoritate decernimus.
We decree by apostolic authority that those who violate [this] be severely stricken.
¶25
Ipsi quoque neglecta animarum cura ordinis sui propositum nullatenus attendentes pro detestanda pecunia sanitatem pollicentes humanorum curatores se faciunt corporum.
These also, the care of souls having been neglected, paying no attention at all to the purpose of their order, for the sake of detestable money, promising health, make themselves healers of human bodies.
¶26
Cum que impudicus oculus impudici cordis sit nuntius illa de quibus loqui erubescit honestas non debet religio pertractare.
And since the unchaste eye is the messenger of the unchaste heart, that which honesty blushes to speak of, religion ought not to treat.
¶27
Ut ergo ordo monasticus et canonicus deo placens in sancto proposito inviolabiliter conservetur ne hoc ulterius praesumatur apostolica auctoritate interdicimus.
Therefore, so that the monastic and canonical order, pleasing to God, may be preserved inviolably in its holy purpose, we forbid by apostolic authority that this be presumed any further.
¶28
Episcopi autem abbates et priores tantae enormitati consentientes et non corrigentes propriis honoribus spolientur et ab ecclesiae liminibus arceantur.
Moreover, bishops, abbots, and priors consenting to such great enormity and not correcting [it], let them be despoiled of their own honors and be thrust from the thresholds of the church.
¶29
CANON X
Decimas ecclesiarum quas in usu pietatis concessas esse canonica demonstrat auctoritas a laicis possideri apostolica auctoritate prohibemus.
The tithes of churches, which the canonical authority shows to have been granted for use in piety, we forbid by apostolic authority to be possessed by laypersons.
¶30
Sive enim ab episcopis vel regibus vel quibuslibet personis eas acceperint nisi ecclesiae reddiderint sciant se sacrilegii crimen committere et periculum aeternae damnationis incurrere.
For whether they have received them from bishops or kings or any persons whatsoever, unless they shall have restored them to the church, let them know that they commit the crime of sacrilege and incur the peril of eternal damnation.
¶31
CANON XI
Praecipimus etiam ut laici qui ecclesias tenent aut eas episcopis restituant aut excommunicationi subiaceant.
We command also that laypersons who hold churches either restore them to the bishops or be subject to excommunication.
¶32
CANON XII
Innovamus autem et praecipimus ut nullus in archidiaconum vel decanum nisi diaconus vel presbyter ordinetur.
We renew also and command that no one be ordained as archdeacon or dean unless a deacon or a presbyter.
¶33
Archidiaconi vero decani vel praepositi qui infra ordines praenominatos exsistunt si inoboedientes ordinari contempserint honore suscepto priventur.
Moreover, archdeacons, deans, or provosts who exist below the aforementioned orders, if they shall have despised to be ordained as disobedient, let them be deprived of the honor received.
¶34
Prohibemus autem ne adolescentibus vel infra sacros ordines constitutis sed qui prudentia et merito vitae clarescunt praedicti concedantur honores.
Moreover, we forbid the aforementioned honors to be granted to adolescents or to those constituted below the sacred orders, but to those who are conspicuous for prudence and merit of life.
¶35
CANON XIII
Praecipimus etiam ne conductitiis presbyteris ecclesiae committantur et unaquaeque ecclesia cui facultas suppetit proprium habeat sacerdotem.
We command also that churches not be committed to hired presbyters, and that each church to which the faculty suffices have its own priest.
¶36
CANON XIV
Praecipimus etiam ut presbyteri clerici monachi peregrini et mercatores et rustici euntes et redeuntes et in agricoltura persistentes et animalia cum quibus aratur et semina portant ad agrum et oves omni tempore securi sint.
We command also that presbyters, clerics, monks, pilgrims, and merchants, and peasants going and returning, and persisting in agriculture, and the animals with which they plow and which carry seed to the field, and sheep, be safe at all times.
¶37
Treguam autem ab occasu solis in quarta feria usque ad ortum solis in secunda feria et ab adventu domini usque ad octavas epiphaniae et a quinquagesima usque ad octavam paschae ab omnibus inviolabiliter observari praecipimus.
Moreover, we command that the truce be observed inviolably by all from sunset on the fourth day of the week [Wednesday] until sunrise on the second day of the week [Monday], and from the advent of the Lord [Advent] until the octave of Epiphany, and from Quinquagesima until the octave of Easter.
¶38
Si quis autem treguam frangere tentaverit post tertiam commonitionem si non satisfecerit episcopus suus in eum excommunicationis sententiam dictet et scriptam episcopis vicinis annuntiet.
But if anyone shall have attempted to break the truce, after the third admonition if he shall not have made satisfaction, let his bishop pronounce sentence of excommunication against him and let him announce it in writing to the neighboring bishops.
¶39
Episcoporum autem nullus excommunicatum in communionem suscipiat immo scripto susceptam sententiam quisque confirmet.
Moreover, let none of the bishops receive an excommunicated person into communion; rather, let each confirm the sentence received in writing.
¶40
Si quis autem hoc violare praesumpserit ordinis sui periculo subiacebit.
But if anyone shall have presumed to violate this, let him be subject to the peril of his order.
¶41
CANON XV
Et quoniam funiculus triplex difficile rumpitur praecipimus ut episcopi ad solum deum et salutem populi habentes respectum omni tepiditate seposita ad pacem firmiter tenendam mutuum sibi consilium et auxilium praebeant neque hoc alicuius amore aut odio praetermittant.
And since a threefold cord is broken with difficulty, we command that bishops, having regard only to God and to the salvation of the people, all tepidity having been set aside, provide mutual counsel and aid to one another for firmly keeping the peace, and that they omit this on account of no one's love or hatred.
¶42
Quod si quis in hoc dei opere tepidus inventus fuerit damnum propriae dignitatis incurrat.
But if anyone shall have been found tepid in this work of God, let him incur the loss of his own dignity.
¶43
CANON XVI
Porro detestabilem et probrosam divinis et humanis legibus per scripturam in veteri et novo testamento abdicatam illam inquam insatiabilem foeneratorum rapacitatem damnamus et ab omni ecclesiastica consolatione sequestramus praecipientes ut nullus archiepiscopus nullus episcopus vel cuiuslibet ordinis abbas seu quivis in ordine et clero nisi cum summa cautela usurarios recipere praesumat.
Furthermore, the detestable and shameful [practice], condemned by divine and human laws through Scripture in the Old and New Testament—that insatiable rapacity of usurers, I say—we condemn and we sequester from all ecclesiastical consolation, commanding that no archbishop, no bishop, nor any abbot of whatever order, nor anyone in orders and clergy, presume to receive usurers except with the greatest caution.
¶44
Sed in tota vita infames habeantur et nisi resipuerint christiana sepultura priventur.
But let them be held infamous throughout their whole life, and unless they shall have repented, let them be deprived of Christian burial.
¶45
CANON XVII
Detestabiles autem illas nundinas vel ferias in quibus milites ex condicto convenire solent et ad ostentationem virium suarum et audaciae temerarie congrediuntur unde mortes hominum et animarum pericula saepe proveniunt omnino fieri interdicimus.
But those detestable markets or fairs in which soldiers are accustomed to meet by agreement and to contend rashly for the display of their strength and their audacity, from which deaths of men and perils of souls often arise, we entirely forbid to be held.
¶46
Quod si quis eorum ibidem mortuus fuerit quamvis ei poscenti poenitentia et viaticum non negetur ecclesiastica tamen careat sepultura.
But if anyone of them shall have died there, although penance and viaticum be not denied to him seeking it, let him nevertheless lack ecclesiastical burial.
¶47
CANON XVIII
Item placuit ut si quis suadente diabolo huius sacrilegii reatum incurrerit quod in clericum vel monachum violentas manus iniecerit anathematis vinculo subiaceat et nullus episcoporum illum praesumat absolvere nisi mortis urgente periculo donec apostolico conspectui praesentetur et eius mandatum suscipiat.
Likewise it pleased [the council] that if anyone, the devil persuading, shall have incurred the guilt of this sacrilege that he has laid violent hands upon a cleric or monk, let him be subject to the bond of anathema, and let none of the bishops presume to absolve him unless the peril of death urges [it], until he be presented to the apostolic sight and receive his mandate.
¶48
CANON XIX
Praecipimus etiam ut in eos qui ad ecclesiam vel coemeterium confugerint nullus omnino manum mittere audeat.
We command also that no one at all dare to lay a hand upon those who flee to a church or cemetery.
¶49
Quod si fecerit excommunicetur.
But if he shall have done so, let him be excommunicated.
¶50
CANON XX
Indubitatum est quoniam honores ecclesiastici sanguinis non sunt sed meriti et ecclesia dei non hereditario iure aliquem neque secundum carnem successorem expectat sed ad sua regimina et officiorum suorum dispensationes honestas sapientes et religiosas personas exposcit.
It is indubitable that ecclesiastical honors are not of blood but of merit, and the church of God does not expect anyone by hereditary right nor a successor according to the flesh, but demands for its governance and the dispensations of its offices honest, wise, and religious persons.
¶51
Propterea auctoritate prohibemus apostolica ne quis ecclesias praebendas praeposituras capellanias aut aliqua ecclesiastica officia hereditario iure valeat vindicare aut expostulare praesumat.
Therefore, we forbid by apostolic authority that anyone be able to claim churches, prebends, provostships, chaplaincies, or any ecclesiastical offices by hereditary right, or presume to demand them.
¶52
Quod si quis improbus aut ambitionis reus attentare praesumpserit debita pena mulctabitur et postulatis carebit.
But if anyone, impious or guilty of ambition, shall have presumed to attempt [this], let him be punished with due penalty and shall lack the things demanded.
¶53
CANON XXI
Sane coniunctiones consanguineorum omnino fieri prohibemus.
Indeed, we entirely forbid unions of blood-relatives to be made.
¶54
Huiusmodi namque incestum qui fere stimulante humani generis inimico in usum versus est sanctorum patrum instituta et sacrosancta dei detestatur ecclesia.
For the holy fathers and the sacrosanct church of God detest this incest—which, the enemy of the human race being the prompter, has nearly come into use.
¶55
Leges etiam saeculi de tali contubernio natos infames pronuntiant et ab hereditate repellunt.
The secular laws also pronounce those born from such cohabitation infamous and repel them from inheritance.
¶56
CANON XXII
Pessimam siquidem et depopulatricem et horrendam incendiorum malitiam auctoritate dei et beatorum apostolorum Petri et Pauli omnino detestamur et interdicimus.
The wicked, depopulating, and horrible malice of arson we utterly detest and forbid by the authority of God and the blessed apostles Peter and Paul.
¶57
Haec etenim pestis et hostilis vastitas omnes alias depraedationes exsuperat.
For this pestilence and hostile devastation surpasses all other plunderings.
¶58
Quae quantum populo dei sit damnosa quantum que detrimentum animabus et corporibus inferat nullus ignorat.
How ruinous it is to the people of God and how much detriment it inflicts upon souls and bodies, no one is ignorant.
¶59
Assurgendum est igitur et omni modo laborandum ut tanta clades tanta que pernicies pro salute populi eradicetur et exstirpetur.
Therefore, one must rise up and labor in every manner so that such a calamity and such a destruction may be eradicated and extirpated for the salvation of the people.
¶60
Si quis igitur post huius nostrae prohibitionis promulgationem malo studio sive pro odio sive pro vindicta ignem apposuerit vel apponi fecerit aut appositoribus consilium vel auxilium scienter tribuerit excommunicetur.
If therefore anyone, after the promulgation of this our prohibition, with evil purpose or for the sake of hatred or vengeance, shall have set fire or caused it to be set, or shall have knowingly given counsel or aid to those setting it, let him be excommunicated.
¶61
Et si mortuus fuerit incendiarius christianorum careat sepultura.
And if the arsonist shall have died, let him lack Christian burial.
¶62
Nec absolvatur nisi prius damno cui intulit secundum facultatem suam resarcito iuret se ulterius ignem non appositurum.
Nor let him be absolved unless, the damage inflicted upon him whom he injured having first been restored according to his ability, he shall swear that he will not set fire again.
¶63
Poenitentia autem ei detur ut Hierosolymis aut in Hispania in servitio dei per annum integrum permaneat.
Moreover, let penance be given to him that he remain in Jerusalem or in Spain in the service of God for a whole year.
¶64
Si quis autem archiepiscopus vel episcopus hoc relaxaverit damnum restituat et per annum ab officio episcopali abstineat.
But if any archbishop or bishop shall have relaxed this, let him restore the loss and let him abstain from the episcopal office for a year.
¶65
CANON XXIII
Sane regibus et principibus facultatem faciendae iustitiae consultis archiepiscopis et episcopis non negamus.
We do not deny to kings and princes the faculty of doing justice, the archbishops and bishops having been consulted.
¶66
CANON XXIV
Presbyterorum filios a sacri altaris ministeriis removendos decernimus nisi aut in coenobiis aut in canonicis religiose fuerint conversati.
We decree that the sons of presbyters be removed from the ministries of the sacred altar unless they shall have been religiously conversant in monasteries or in canonries.
¶67
CANON XXV
Sane quia inter cetera unum est quod sanctam maxime perturbat ecclesiam falsa videlicet poenitentia confratres nostros et presbyteros admonemus ne falsis poenitentiis laicorum animas decipi et in infernum pertrahi patiantur.
Since among other things there is one which greatly disturbs the holy church—namely false penance—we admonish our brothers and presbyters that they not allow the souls of laypersons to be deceived by false penances and to be dragged into hell.
¶68
Falsam autem poenitentiam esse constat cum spretis pluribus de uno solo poenitentia agitur aut cum sic agitur de uno ut non discedatur ab alio.
But it is established that penance is false when, many things having been spurned, penance is done for one alone, or when it is so done for one that one does not withdraw from another.
¶69
Unde scriptum est qui totam legem observaverit offendit autem in uno factus est omnium reus scilicet quantum ad vitam aeternam.
Whence it is written: "Whoever shall have observed the whole law but offends in one point, has become guilty of all"—namely as regards eternal life.
¶70
Sicut enim si peccatis esset omnibus involutus ita si in uno tantum maneat aeternae vitae ianuam non intrabit.
For just as if he had been involved in all sins, so if he remains in only one, he shall not enter the gate of eternal life.
¶71
Falsa etiam fit poenitentia cum poenitens ab officio vel curiali vel negotiali non recedit quod sine peccato agi nulla ratione praevalet aut si odium in corde gestetur aut si offenso cuilibet non satisfiat aut si offendenti offensus non indulgeat aut si arma quis contra iustitiam gerat.
Penance also is made false if the penitent does not withdraw from an office—either curial or business-related—which cannot be carried on without sin by any reason, or if hatred is harbored in the heart, or if satisfaction is not made to an offended person, or if the offended person does not forgive the offender, or if anyone bears arms against justice.
¶72
CANON XXVI
Eos autem qui religiositatis speciem simulantes domini corporis et sanguinis sacramentum baptisma puerorum sacerdotium et ceteros ecclesiasticos ordines et legitimarum damnant foedera nuptiarum tamquam haereticos ab ecclesia dei pellimus et damnamus et per potestates exteras coerceri praecipimus.
But those who, simulating a form of religion, condemn the sacrament of the Lord's body and blood, the baptism of children, the priesthood and the other ecclesiastical orders, and the bonds of lawful marriages, as heretics we drive out and condemn from the church of God, and we command them to be coerced by external powers.
¶73
Defensores quoque ipsorum eiusdem damnationis vinculo innodamus.
We also bind the defenders of them with the same bond of damnation.
¶74
CANON XXVII
Illud quoque adicientes praecipimus ut pro chrismatis olei sacri et sepulturae acceptione nullum venditionis pretium exigatur.
That also we add, commanding that no price of sale be exacted for the reception of chrism, holy oil, and burial.
¶75
CANON XXVIII
Si quis praeposituras praebendas vel alia ecclesiastica beneficia de manu laici acceperit indigne suscepto careat beneficio.
If anyone shall have received provostships, prebends, or other ecclesiastical benefices from the hand of a layperson, let him lack the benefice received as unworthily received.
¶76
Iuxta namque decreta sanctorum patrum laici quamvis religiosi sint nullam tamen habent disponendi de ecclesiasticis facultatibus potestatem.
For according to the decrees of the holy fathers, laypersons, however religious they may be, nevertheless have no power of disposing concerning ecclesiastical faculties.
¶77
CANON XXIX
Ad haec perniciosam et detestabilem consuetudinem quarundam mulierum quae licet neque secundum regulam beati Benedicti neque Basilii aut Augustini vivant sanctimoniales tamen vulgo censeri desiderant aboleri decernimus.
To these things, we decree to be abolished the pernicious and detestable custom of certain women who, although they neither live according to the rule of blessed Benedict nor of Basil nor of Augustine, yet desire to be commonly regarded as consecrated women.
¶78
Cum enim iuxta regulam degentes in coenobiis tam in ecclesia quam in refectorio atque dormitorio communiter esse debeant propria sibi aedificant receptacula et privata domicilia in quibus sub hospitalitatis velamine passim hospites et minus religiosos contra sacros canones et bonos mores suscipere nullatenus erubescunt.
For whereas those living according to the rule in monasteries ought to be together in common both in church and in refectory and in dormitory, they build for themselves private receptacles and private dwellings in which, under the veil of hospitality, they do not at all blush to receive guests everywhere and persons less religious, contrary to the sacred canons and good morals.
¶79
Quia ergo omnis qui male agit odit lucem ac per hoc ipsae absconditae in iustorum tabernaculo opinantur se posse latere oculos iudicis cuncta cernentis hoc tam inhonestum detestandum que flagitium ne ulterius fiat omnimodis prohibemus et sub poena anathematis interdicimus.
Because therefore everyone who does evil hates the light, and by this, hidden in the tabernacle of the just, they think they are able to hide from the eyes of the Judge who sees all things, we entirely forbid and under penalty of anathema interdict that so dishonest and detestable a disgrace be done any further.
¶80
Simili modo prohibemus ne sanctimoniales simul cum canonicis vel monachis in ecclesia in uno choro conveniant ad psallendum.
In a similar manner we forbid that consecrated women gather together in church in one choir with canons or monks for singing psalms.
¶81
CANON XXX
Obeuntibus sane episcopis quoniam ultra tres menses vacare ecclesias prohibent patrum sanctiones sub anathemate interdicimus ne canonici de sede episcopali ab electione episcoporum excludant religiosos viros sed eorum consilio honesta et idonea persona in episcopum eligatur.
With bishops clearly dying, since the sanctions of the fathers forbid churches to be vacant beyond three months, we forbid under anathema that the canons of the episcopal see exclude religious men from the election of bishops; but rather, by their counsel let an honest and suitable person be chosen as bishop.
¶82
Quod si exclusis eisdem religiosis electio fuerit celebrata quod absque eorum assensu et convenientia factum fuerit irritum habeatur et vacuum.
But if, the same religious men having been excluded, an election shall have been celebrated—what shall have been done without their assent and agreement—let it be held invalid and void.
¶83
CANON XXXI
Artem autem illam mortiferam et deo odibilem ballistariorum et sagittariorum adversus christianos et catholicos exerceri de cetero sub anathemate prohibemus.
But that deadly art, hateful to God, of crossbowmen and archers, we forbid under anathema to be exercised hereafter against Christians and Catholics.
¶84
CANON XXXII
Ad haec ordinationes factas a Petro et aliis schismaticis et haereticis evacuamus et irritas esse censemus.
To these things, the ordinations made by Peter and other schismatics and heretics we declare void and we judge to be invalid.
¶85

This completes the Second Lateran Council (Concilium Lateranense II a. 1139) – all canons as presented in this manuscript tradition.
