SESSION 13 — 11 OCTOBER 1551
*SESSIO XIII — 11 OCTOBRIS 1551*
*DECRETUM DE SANCTISSIMO EUCHARISTIAE SACRAMENTO*
DECREE ON THE MOST HOLY SACRAMENT OF THE EUCHARIST
Sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem sanctae sedis apostolicae legato et nuntiis, ad consummationem salutaris de eucharistiae sacramento doctrinae, quae in praecedenti sessione inchoata fuit, ea quae sequuntur de communione sub utraque specie et de parvulorum communione definienda esse decrevit.
The sacred, oecumenical, and general Tridentine synod, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit, the same legate and nuncios of the Apostolic See presiding, for the completion of the salutary doctrine concerning the sacrament of the Eucharist, which was begun in the preceding session, has decreed that the following matters should be defined concerning the communion under both species and concerning the communion of infants.
¶237
CHAPTER I
Laicos et non celebrantes clericos divino praecepto non obligari ad communionem sub utraque specie
That the laity and clerics not celebrating are not bound by divine precept to communicate under both species
Eadem sancta ipsa synodus, a spiritu sancto qui spiritus est sapientiae et intellectus, spiritus consilii et pietatis edocta atque ipsius ecclesiae iudicium et consuetudinem secuta, declarat ac docet nullo divino praecepto laicos et clericos non conficientes obligari ad eucharistiae sacramentum sub utraque specie sumendum, neque ullo pacto (salva fide) dubitari posse quin illis alterius speciei communio ad salutem sufficiat.
The same holy synod, taught by the Holy Spirit, who is the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and piety, and following the judgment and custom of the church itself, declares and teaches that laypersons and clerics not celebrating are not bound by any divine precept to receive the sacrament of the Eucharist under both species, nor that it can be doubted in any way (faith being preserved) that communion under the species of bread alone suffices for them for salvation.
¶238
Nam etsi christus dominus in ultima coena venerabile hoc sacramentum in panis et vini speciebus instituit et apostolis tradidit, non tamen illa institutio et traditio eo tendunt, ut omnes christifideles statuto domini ad utramque speciem accipiendam adstringantur. Sed neque ex sermone illo apud Ioannem sexto recte colligitur utriusque speciei communionem a domino praeceptam esse, utcumque iuxta varias sanctorum patrum et doctorum interpretationes intelligatur.
For although Christ the Lord instituted this venerable sacrament in the species of bread and wine at the last supper and delivered it to the apostles, nevertheless that institution and delivery do not tend to this, that all the faithful of Christ are bound by the Lord's decree to receive both species. Nor is it rightly gathered from that discourse in John, chapter six, that communion under both species was commanded by the Lord, however it is understood according to the various interpretations of the holy fathers and doctors.
¶239
Namque qui dixit: "nisi manducaveritis carnem filii hominis et biberitis eius sanguinem, non habebitis vitam in vobis," dixit quoque: "si quis manducaverit ex hoc pane, vivet in aeternum." Et qui dixit: "qui manducat meam carnem et bibit meum sanguinem, habet vitam aeternam," dixit etiam: "panis quem ego dabo, caro mea est pro mundi vita." Et denique qui dixit: "qui manducat meam carnem et bibit meum sanguinem, in me manet et ego in illo," dixit nihilominus: "qui manducat hunc panem, vivet in aeternum."
For He who said: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink His blood, you shall not have life in you," also said: "If anyone eats of this bread, he shall live forever." And He who said: "He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life," also said: "The bread which I shall give is my flesh for the life of the world." And finally, He who said: "He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me and I in him," also said: "He who eats this bread shall live forever."
¶240
CHAPTER II
Potestatem ecclesiae in sacramentorum dispensatione esse conservandam
That the power of the church in the dispensation of the sacraments is to be preserved
Praeterea declarat hanc potestatem perpetuo in ecclesia fuisse, ut in sacramentorum dispensatione, salva illorum substantia, ea statueret vel mutaret quae suscipientium utilitati seu ipsorum sacramentorum venerationi pro rerum, temporum et locorum varietate magis expedire iudicaret. Id autem apostolus non obscure visus est innuisse cum ait: "sic nos existimet homo ut ministros christi et dispensatores mysteriorum dei."
Moreover, it declares that this power has always been in the church, that in the dispensation of the sacraments, their substance being preserved, it might establish or change those things which it judged to be more expedient for the utility of the recipients or for the veneration of the sacraments themselves, according to the variety of things, times, and places. Which the apostle did not obscurely seem to intimate when he said: "Let a man so account of us as of the ministers of Christ and the dispensers of the mysteries of God."
¶241
Atque ipsum quidem hac potestate usum esse satis constat, cum in multis aliis, tum in hoc ipso sacramento, cum ordinatis nonnullis circa eius usum, "cetera" inquit "cum venero disponam."
And that He Himself used this power is sufficiently evident, since in many other things, so also in this very sacrament, when He arranged certain things concerning its use, He said: "Other things I will set in order when I come."
¶242
Quare agnoscens sancta mater ecclesia hanc suam in administratione sacramentorum auctoritatem, licet ab initio christianae religionis non infrequens utriusque speciei usus fuisset, tamen progressu temporis, latissime iam mutata illa consuetudine, gravibus et iustis causis adducta, hanc consuetudinem sub altera specie communicandi approbavit et pro lege habendam decrevit, quam reprobare aut sine ipsius ecclesiae auctoritate pro libito mutare non licet.
Therefore, recognizing this authority of hers in the administration of the sacraments, holy mother church, although from the beginning of the Christian religion the use of both species was not infrequent, yet in the progress of time, that custom having been already very widely changed, having been induced by grave and just reasons, approved this custom of communicating under one species and decreed that it is to be held as law, which it is not lawful to reject or to change at will without the authority of the church itself.
¶243
CHAPTER III
Sub qualibet specie totum et integrum christum verumque sacramentum sumi
That the whole and entire Christ and a true sacrament are received under each species
Insuper declarat, quavis redemptor noster, ut antea dictum est, in suprema illa coena hoc sacramentum in duabus speciebus instituerit et apostolis tradiderit, tamen fatendum esse etiam sub altera tantum specie totum atque integrum christum verum que sacramentum sumi, ac propterea, quod ad fructum attinet, nulla gratia necessaria ad salutem eos defraudari qui unam speciem solam accipiunt.
Moreover, it declares that although our Redeemer, as has been said before, instituted this sacrament in two species at that last supper and delivered it to the apostles, nevertheless it is to be confessed that even under one species alone the whole and entire Christ and a true sacrament are received, and therefore that, with respect to its fruit, those who receive one species alone are not defrauded of any grace necessary for salvation.
¶244
CHAPTER IV
Parvulos ad communionem sacramentalem non obligari
That infants are not bound to sacramental communion
Denique eadem sancta synodus docet parvulos usu rationis carentes nulla obligari necessitate ad sacramentalem eucharistiae communionem, siquidem per baptismi lavacrum regenerati et christo incorporati, adeptam iam filiorum dei gratiam in illa aetate amittere non possunt. Neque ideo tamen damnanda est antiquitas, si eum morem in quibusdam locis aliquando servavit.
Finally, the same holy synod teaches that infants lacking the use of reason are bound by no necessity to sacramental communion of the Eucharist, since, having been regenerated by the laver of baptism and incorporated into Christ, they cannot in that age lose the grace of the sons of God already obtained. Nor, however, is antiquity to be condemned if it at any time observed that custom in some places.
¶245
Ut enim sanctissimi illi patres sui facti probabilem causam pro illius temporis ratione habuerunt, ita certe eos nulla salutis necessitate id fecisse sine controversia credendum est.
For just as those most holy fathers had a probable reason for their action according to the condition of that time, so it is to be believed without controversy that they did this by no necessity of salvation.
¶246
*CANONES DE COMMUNIONE SUB UTRAQUE SPECIE ET PARVULORUM*
CANONS ON THE COMMUNION UNDER BOTH SPECIES AND THE COMMUNION OF INFANTS
CANON I
Si quis dixerit ex dei praecepto vel ex necessitate salutis omnes et singulos christifideles utramque speciem sanctissimi eucharistiae sacramenti sumere debere: anathema sit.
If anyone says that by a precept of God or by necessity of salvation all and each of the faithful of Christ ought to receive both species of the most holy sacrament of the Eucharist: let him be anathema.
¶247
CANON II
Si quis dixerit sanctam ecclesiam catholicam non iustis causis et rationibus adductam fuisse ut laicos atque etiam clericos non conficientes sub una panis tantummodo specie communicaret, aut in eo errasse: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the holy catholic church was not induced by just causes and reasons to communicate laypersons and also clerics not celebrating under the species of bread only, or that she has erred in this: let him be anathema.
¶248
CANON III
Si quis negaverit totum et integrum christum, omnium gratiarum fontem et auctorem, sub una panis specie sumi, quia (ut quidam falso asserunt) non secundum ipsius christi institutionem sub utraque specie sumatur: anathema sit.
If anyone denies that the whole and entire Christ, the fountain and author of all graces, is received under the species of bread alone, because (as some falsely assert) it is not received according to the institution of Christ Himself under both species: let him be anathema.
¶249
CANON IV
Si quis dixerit parvulis, antequam ad annos discretionis pervenerint, necessariam esse eucharistiae communionem: anathema sit.
If anyone says that communion of the Eucharist is necessary for infants before they have reached the years of discretion: let him be anathema.

PROROGATION OF THE SESSION
*PROROGATIO SESSIONIS*
Eadem sacrosancta synodus statuit et decrevit proximam futuram sessionem habendam et celebrandam esse feria quinta post octavam festi nativitatis beatae Mariae virginis, quae erit decima septima mensis septembris proxime futuri. Hoc tamen adiecto, quod dictum terminum, ac unicuique sessioni in posterum praefigendum, ipsa sancta synodus pro eius arbitrio et voluntate, sicuti rebus concilii putaverit expedire, etiam in generali congregatione restringere et prorogare libere possit et valeat.
The same sacred synod has established and decreed that the next future session is to be held and celebrated on the Thursday after the octave of the feast of the nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary, which will be the 17th day of the month of September next following. This however being added, that the same holy synod, according to its judgment and will, as it shall think expedient for the affairs of the council, may be able and have power freely to restrict and prorogate the said term, and each term to be prefixed in the future, even in a general congregation.

SESSION 14 — 25 NOVEMBER 1551
*SESSIO XIV — 25 NOVEMBRIS 1551*
*DECRETUM DE SACRAMENTIS POENITENTIAE ET EXTREMAE UNCTIONIS*
DECREE ON THE MOST HOLY SACRAMENTS OF PENANCE AND EXTREME UNCTION
Sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem sanctae sedis apostolicae legato et nuntiis, quamvis in decreto de iustificatione, propter locorum cognationem, necessaria quadam ratione multus de poenitentiae sacramento sermo interpositus fuerit, tanta nihilominus circa illud nostra hac aetate diversorum errorum est multitudo, ut non parum publicae utilitatis retulerit de eo exactiorem et pleniorem diffinitionem tradidisse, in qua demonstratis et convulsis, spiritus sancti praesidio, universis erroribus, catholica veritas perspicua et illustris fieret; quam nunc sancta haec synodus christianis omnibus perpetuo servandam proponit.
The sacred, oecumenical, and general Tridentine synod, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit, the same legates of the holy Apostolic See presiding, although in the decree on justification, on account of a certain necessary reason arising from the connection of the matters, much discourse was interposed concerning the sacrament of penance, nevertheless such a multitude of various errors concerning it exists in this our age that it would be of no little public utility to have delivered a more exact and full definition of it, in which, all errors having been demonstrated and refuted with the help of the Holy Spirit, the catholic truth might be made clear and illustrious; which the holy synod now proposes to be perpetually observed by all Christians.
¶252
CHAPTER I
De necessitate et institutione sacramenti poenitentiae
On the necessity and institution of the sacrament of penance
Si ea regeneratis omnibus gratitudo erga deum esset, ut iustitiam in baptismo ipsius beneficio et gratia susceptam constanter tuerentur, non fuisset opus aliud ab ipso baptismo sacramentum ad peccatorum remissionem esse institutum. Quoniam autem deus, dives in misericordia, cognovit figmentum nostrum, illis etiam vitae remedium contulit qui sese postea in peccati servitutem et daemonis potestatem tradidissent, sacramentum videlicet poenitentiae, quo lapsis post baptismum beneficium mortis christi applicatur.
If such gratitude toward God were in all the regenerate that they would constantly defend the justice received in baptism by His benefit and grace, there would not have been need for another sacrament besides baptism itself to be instituted for the remission of sins. But since God, rich in mercy, has known our frame, He has also bestowed a remedy for life upon those who afterwards delivered themselves into the servitude of sin and into the power of the devil, namely the sacrament of penance, by which the benefit of the death of Christ is applied to those who have fallen after baptism.
¶253
Fuit quidem poenitentia universis hominibus, qui se mortali aliquo peccato inquinassent, quovis tempore, ad gratiam et iustitiam assequendam necessaria, illis etiam qui baptismi sacramento ablui petivissent, ut, perversitate abiecta et emendata, tantam dei offensionem cum peccati odio et pio animi dolore detestarentur.
Penance, indeed, was necessary for all men who had defiled themselves with any mortal sin at any time, for attaining grace and justice, even for those who had sought to be washed by the sacrament of baptism, so that, their perversity having been cast aside and amended, they might detest so great an offense against God with hatred of sin and with a pious sorrow of mind.
¶254
Unde propheta ait: "convertimini et agite poenitentiam ab omnibus iniquitatibus vestris, et non erit vobis in ruinam iniquitas." Dominus etiam dixit: "nisi poenitentiam egeritis, omnes similiter peribitis." Et princeps apostolorum Petrus, peccatoribus baptismo initiandis poenitentiam commendans, dicebat: "poenitentiam agite et baptizetur unusquisque vestrum."
Whence the prophet says: "Turn yourselves and do penance for all your iniquities, and iniquity shall not be your ruin." The Lord also said: "Unless you do penance, you shall all likewise perish." And the prince of the apostles, Peter, commending penance to sinners about to be initiated by baptism, said: "Do penance and be baptized every one of you."
¶255
Porro nec ante adventum christi poenitentia erat sacramentum, nec est post adventum illius cuiquam ante baptismum.
Moreover, before the coming of Christ, penance was not a sacrament, nor is it for anyone after His coming before baptism.
¶256
Dominus autem sacramentum poenitentiae tunc praecipue instituit, cum a mortuis excitatus insufflavit in discipulos suos dicens: "accipite spiritum sanctum: quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis; et quorum retinueritis, retenta sunt." Quo tam insigni facto et verbis tam perspicuis potestatem remittendi et retinendi peccata, ad reconciliandos fideles post baptismum lapsos, apostolis et eorum legitimis successoribus fuisse communicatam universorum patrum consensus semper intellexit, et Novatianos, remittendi potestatem olim pertinaciter negantes, magna ratione ecclesia catholica, tanquam haereticos, explosit atque condemnavit.
But the Lord instituted the sacrament of penance then especially, when, raised from the dead, He breathed upon His disciples saying: "Receive the Holy Spirit: whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them; and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained." By which signal deed and so clear words the consent of all the fathers has always understood that the power of remitting and retaining sins was communicated to the apostles and their lawful successors for reconciling the faithful who have fallen after baptism. And the catholic church, with good reason, drove out and condemned the Novatians, who formerly pertinaciously denied the power of remission.
¶257
Quare verissimum hunc illorum verborum domini sensum sancta haec synodus probans et recipiens, damnat eorum commentitias interpretationes, qui verba illa ad potestatem praedicandi verbum dei et christi evangelium annunciandi contra huiusmodi sacramenti institutionem falso detorquent.
Therefore this holy synod, approving and receiving this most true sense of those words of the Lord, condemns the fictitious interpretations of those who, against the institution of this sacrament, falsely twist those words to the power of preaching the word of God and of announcing the gospel of Christ.
¶258
CHAPTER II
De differentia sacramenti poenitentiae et baptismatis
On the difference between the sacrament of penance and that of baptism
Caeterum hoc sacramentum multis rationibus a baptismo differre dignoscitur. Nam praeterquam quod materia et forma, quibus sacramenti essentia perficitur, longissime dissidet, constat certe baptismi ministrum iudicem esse non oportere, cum ecclesia in neminem iudicium exerceat, qui non prius in ipsam per baptismi ianuam fuerit ingressus. "Quid enim mihi" (inquit apostolus) "de iis qui foris sunt iudicare?" Secus est de domesticis fidei, quos christus dominus lavacro baptismi sui corporis membra semel effecit.
Moreover, this sacrament is known to differ from baptism in many respects. For besides that the matter and form by which the essence of the sacrament is perfected differ very widely, it is certain that the minister of baptism is not required to be a judge, since the church exercises judgment on no one who has not first entered into it through the door of baptism. "For what have I to do," says the apostle, "with those who are outside?" It is otherwise with those of the household of faith, whom Christ the Lord once made members of His own body by the laver of baptism.
¶259
Nam hos, si se postea crimine aliquo contaminaverint, non iam repetito baptismo ablui (cum id in ecclesia catholica nulla ratione liceat), sed ante hoc tribunal tanquam reos sisti voluit, ut per sacerdotum sententiam non semel, sed quoties ab admissis peccatis ad ipsum poenitentes confugerent, possent liberari.
For these, if they afterwards stain themselves with some crime, He willed not to be cleansed by a repeated baptism (since this in the catholic church is by no means allowed), but to be brought as defendants before this tribunal, that by the sentence of the priests they might be able to be absolved not once but as often as, penitent, they should flee to it from their sins committed.
¶260
Alius praeterea est baptismi et alius poenitentiae fructus. Per baptismum enim, christum induentes, nova prorsus in illo efficimur creatura, plenam et integram peccatorum omnium remissionem consequentes; ad quam tamen novitatem et integritatem per sacramentum poenitentiae sine magnis nostris fletibus et laboribus, divina id exigente iustitia, pervenire nequaquam possumus, ut merito poenitentia laboriosus quidam baptismus a sanctis patribus dictus fuerit. Est autem hoc sacramentum poenitentiae lapsis post baptismum ad salutem necessarium, ut nondum regeneratis ipse baptismus.
Moreover, the fruit of baptism is one; that of penance, another. For through baptism, putting on Christ, we are made altogether a new creature in Him, obtaining a full and entire remission of all sins; to which newness and integrity, however, through the sacrament of penance, we cannot arrive without great tears and labors on our part, divine justice so demanding, so that penance has rightly been called by the holy fathers a laborious kind of baptism. But this sacrament of penance is necessary for salvation for those who have fallen after baptism, as baptism itself is for those not yet regenerated.
¶261
CHAPTER III
De partibus et fructu sacramenti poenitentiae
On the parts and fruit of the sacrament of penance
Docet praeterea sancta synodus sacramenti poenitentiae formam, in qua praecipue ipsius vis sita est, in illis ministri verbis positam esse: "Ego absolvo te," et cetera, quibus, de ecclesiae sanctae more, preces quaedam laudabiliter adiunguntur, ad ipsius tamen formae essentiam nequaquam spectant neque ad ipsius sacramenti administrationem sunt necessariae.
Moreover, the holy synod teaches that the form of the sacrament of penance, in which its power chiefly consists, is placed in those words of the minister: "I absolve you," and certain others, to which, according to the custom of the holy church, certain prayers are laudably joined, which nevertheless do not pertain to the essence of that form nor are necessary for the administration of the sacrament.
¶262
Sunt autem quasi materia huius sacramenti ipsius poenitentis actus, nempe contritio, confessio et satisfactio. Qui, quatenus in poenitente ad integritatem sacramenti et ad plenam que et perfectam peccatorum remissionem ex dei institutione requiruntur, hac ratione poenitentiae partes dicuntur.
But the acts of the penitent himself, namely contrition, confession, and satisfaction, are as it were the matter of this sacrament. Which, insofar as they are required in the penitent for the integrity of the sacrament and for full and perfect remission of sins by the institution of God, are for this reason called the parts of penance.
¶263
Sane vero res et effectus huius sacramenti, quantum ad eius vim et efficaciam pertinet, reconciliatio est cum deo, quam interdum in viris piis et cum devotione hoc sacramentum percipientibus conscientiae pax ac serenitas cum vehementi spiritus consolatione consequi solet.
But the thing and effect of this sacrament, as far as it pertains to its power and efficacy, is reconciliation with God, which sometimes in pious men and those receiving this sacrament with devotion is accustomed to be followed by peace and serenity of conscience with vehement consolation of the spirit.
¶264
CHAPTER IV
De contritione
On contrition
Contritio, quae primum locum inter dictos poenitentis actus habet, animi dolor ac detestatio est de peccato commisso, cum proposito non peccandi de caetero.
Contrition, which holds the first place among the aforesaid acts of the penitent, is a sorrow of soul and a detestation for sin committed, with the purpose of not sinning in the future.
¶265
Fuit autem quovis tempore ad impetrandam veniam peccatorum hic contritionis motus necessarius, et in homine post baptismum lapso, ita demum ad remissionem peccatorum praeparat, si cum fiducia divinae misericordiae et voto praestandi reliqua coniunctus sit, quae ad rite suscipiendum hoc sacramentum requiruntur.
This movement of contrition was necessary at any time for obtaining the remission of sins, and in a man fallen after baptism, it then at length prepares him for the remission of sins if it be joined with trust in divine mercy and with the desire of performing the remaining things which are required for rightly receiving this sacrament.
¶266
Declarat igitur sancta synodus hanc contritionem non solum cessationem a peccato et vitae novae propositum et inchoationem, sed veteris etiam odium continere, iuxta illud: "proiicite a vobis omnes iniquitates vestras in quibus praevaricati estis, et facite vobis cor novum et spiritum novum." Et certe qui illos sanctorum clamores consideravert: "tibi soli peccavi et malum coram te feci"; "laboravi in gemitu meo, lavabo per singulas noctes lectum meum"; "recogitabo tibi omnes annos meos in amaritudine animae meae"—et alios huius generis, facile intelliget eos ex vehementi quodam anteactae vitae odio et ingenti peccatorum detestatione manasse.
Therefore the holy synod declares that this contrition contains not only a cessation from sin and a purpose and beginning of a new life, but also a hatred of the old, according to that: "Cast away from you all your iniquities by which you have transgressed, and make to yourselves a new heart and a new spirit." And certainly whoever has considered the cries of the saints: "To You only have I sinned, and have done evil before You"; "I have labored in my groaning, I will wash my bed every night"; "I will recount to You all my years in the bitterness of my soul"—and other such sayings, will easily understand that they flowed from a vehement hatred of their past life and a great detestation of sins.
¶267
Docet praeterea, etsi contritionem hanc aliquando charitate perfectam esse contingat hominem que deo reconciliare priusquam hoc sacramentum actu suscipiatur, ipsam nihilominus reconciliationem ipsi contritioni sine sacramenti voto (quod in illa includitur) non esse adscribendam.
It teaches moreover that although contrition may sometimes be perfected by charity and reconcile man to God before this sacrament is actually received, nevertheless that reconciliation is not to be ascribed to the contrition itself without the desire of the sacrament, which is included in it.
¶268
Illam vero contritionem imperfectam, quae attritio dicitur, quoniam vel ex turpitudinis peccati consideratione vel ex gehennae et poenarum metu communiter concipitur, si voluntatem peccandi excludat cum spe veniae, declarat non solum non facere hominem hypocritam et magis peccatorem, verum etiam donum dei esse et spiritus sancti impulsum, non adhuc quidem inhabitantis, sed tantum moventis, quo poenitens adiutus viam sibi ad iustitiam parat.
But that imperfect contrition, which is called attrition, because it is commonly conceived either from the consideration of the baseness of sin or from the fear of hell and of punishments, if it excludes the will to sin, with hope of pardon, the synod declares not only does not make a man a hypocrite and a greater sinner, but is even a gift of God and an impulse of the Holy Spirit, not yet indeed indwelling, but only moving, by which the penitent, being helped, prepares a way for himself towards justice.
¶269
Et quamvis sine sacramento poenitentiae per se ad iustificationem perducere peccatorem nequeat, tamen eum ad dei gratiam in sacramento poenitentiae impetrandam disponit. Hoc enim timore utiliter concussi Ninivitae, ad Ionae praedicationem, plenam terroribus, poenitentiam egerunt et misericordiam a domino impetrarunt.
And although without the sacrament of penance it cannot by itself lead the sinner to justification, yet it disposes him to obtain the grace of God in the sacrament of penance. For the Ninevites, profitably shaken by this fear, at the preaching of Jonah, full of terrors, did penance and obtained mercy from the Lord.
¶270
Quamobrem falso quidam calumniantur catholicos scriptores, quasi tradiderint sacramentum poenitentiae absque bono motu suscipientium gratiam conferre, quod nunquam ecclesia dei docuit neque sensit.
Wherefore, some falsely calumniate catholic writers as if they had handed down that the sacrament of penance confers grace without a good movement of those receiving it, which the church of God never taught nor thought.
¶271
Sed et falso docent contritionem esse extortam et coactam, non liberam et voluntariam.
But they also falsely teach that contrition is forced and coerced, not free and voluntary.
¶272
CHAPTER V
De confessione
On confession
Ex institutione sacramenti poenitentiae iam explicata, universa ecclesia semper intellexit institutam etiam esse a domino integram peccatorum confessionem et omnibus post baptismum lapsis iure divino necessariam existere, quia dominus noster Iesus christus, e terris ascensurus ad coelos, sacerdotes sui ipsius vicarios reliquit tanquam praesides et iudices, ad quos omnia mortalia crimina, in quae christi fideles ceciderint, deferantur, quo pro potestate clavium remissionis aut retentionis peccatorum sententiam pronuncient.
From the institution of the sacrament of penance, already explained, the whole church has always understood that a full confession of sins was also instituted by the Lord, and is necessary by divine law for all who have fallen after baptism, because our Lord Jesus Christ, about to ascend from earth to heaven, left priests as His own vicars, as presidents and judges, to whom all mortal crimes into which the faithful of Christ may have fallen are to be brought, so that, according to the power of the keys, they may pronounce the sentence of remission or retention of sins.
¶273
Constat enim sacerdotes iudicium hoc incognita causa exercere non potuisse, nec aequitatem quidem illos in poenis iniungendis servare potuisse, si in genere dumtaxat et non potius in specie ac sigillatim sua ipsi peccata declarassent. Ex his colligitur oportere a poenitentibus omnia peccata mortalia, quorum post diligentem sui discussionem conscientiam habent, in confessione recenseri, etiamsi occultissima illa sint et tantum adversus duo ultima decalogi praecepta commissa, quae nonnunquam animum gravius sauciant et periculosiora sunt iis quae in manifesto admittuntur.
For it is evident that priests could not have exercised this judgment without a known cause, nor could they preserve equity in imposing punishments, if they had declared their sins only in general and not rather specifically and one by one. Whence it follows that penitents are bound to recount in confession all mortal sins of which, after a diligent self-examination, they have consciousness, even if they are most secret and committed only against the last two precepts of the decalogue, which sometimes wound the soul more grievously and are more dangerous than those which are committed openly.
¶274
Nam venialia, quibus a gratia dei non excludimur et in quae frequentius labimur, quanquam recte et utiliter citra que omnem praesumptionem in confessione dicantur (quod piorum hominum usus demonstrat), taceri tamen citra culpam multis que aliis remediis expiari possunt.
For venial sins, by which we are not excluded from the grace of God and into which we fall more frequently, although they may be rightly and usefully and without any presumption told in confession, as the practice of pious persons shows, may yet be omitted without fault and be expiated by many other remedies.
¶275
Verum cum universa mortalia peccata, etiam cogitationis, homines irae filios et dei inimicos reddant, necessum est omnium etiam veniam cum aperta et verecunda confessione a deo quaerere.
But since all mortal sins, even those of thought, make men children of wrath and enemies of God, it is necessary to seek pardon for all also with an open and modest confession.
¶276
Itaque dum omnia quae memoriae occurrunt peccata christi fideles confiteri student, procul dubio omnia misericordiae divinae ignoscenda exponunt. Qui vero secus faciunt et scienter aliqua retinent, nihil divinae bonitati per sacerdotem remittendum proponunt.
Thus, while the faithful of Christ strive to confess all the sins that come to memory, without doubt they lay open all to divine mercy to be forgiven. But those who do otherwise and knowingly conceal some, lay nothing before divine goodness to be forgiven by the priest.
¶277
Si enim erubescat aegrotus vulnus medico detegere, quod ignorat medicina non curat.
For if the sick person is ashamed to show his wound to the physician, the medicine does not cure what it does not know.
¶278
Colligitur praeterea etiam eas circunstantias in confessione explicandas esse quae speciem peccati mutant, quod sine illis peccata ipsa nec a poenitentibus integre exponantur nec iudicibus innotescant, et fieri nequeat ut de gravitate criminum recte censere possint et poenam quam oportet pro illis poenitentibus imponere.
Moreover, it is gathered that even those circumstances which change the species of sin are to be explained in confession, because without them the sins themselves are neither fully set forth by the penitents nor become known to the judges, and it cannot be that they can rightly judge of the gravity of the crimes and impose the fitting penalty upon the penitents.
¶279
Unde alienum a ratione est docere circunstantias has ab hominibus ociosis excogitatas fuisse, aut unam tantum circunstantiam confitendam esse, nempe peccasse in fratrem.
Wherefore it is foreign to reason to teach that these circumstances have been devised by idle men, or that only one circumstance is to be confessed, namely that one has sinned against a brother.
¶280
Sed et impium est confessionem, quae hac ratione fieri praecipitur, impossibilem dicere aut carnificinam illam conscientiarum appellare; constat enim nihil aliud in ecclesia a poenitentibus exigi, quam ut postquam quisque diligentius se excusserit et conscientiae suae sinus omnes et latebras exploraverit, ea peccata confiteatur quibus se dominum et deum suum mortaliter offendisse meminerit; reliqua autem peccata, quae diligenter cogitanti non occurrunt, in universum eadem confessione inclusa esse intelliguntur; pro quibus fideliter cum propheta dicimus: "ab occultis meis munda me, domine."
But it is also impious to call the confession which is commanded to be done in this way impossible, or a torture of consciences; for it is evident that nothing else is required in the church from penitents than that, after each has more diligently examined himself and explored all the folds and recesses of his conscience, he confess those sins by which he remembers that he has mortally offended his Lord and God; but that the remaining sins, which do not occur to one thinking diligently, are understood to be included in the same confession in general; for which we faithfully say with the prophet: "Cleanse me from my secret sins, O Lord."
¶281
Ipsa vero huiusmodi confessionis difficultas ac peccata detegendi verecundia gravis quidem videri posset, nisi tot tantis que commodis et consolationibus levaretur, quae omnibus digne ad hoc sacramentum accedentibus per absolutionem certissime conferuntur.
But the difficulty itself of such confession and the shame of revealing sins could indeed seem grave, if it were not lightened by so many and such great benefits and consolations which are most certainly conferred by absolution upon all who worthily approach this sacrament.
¶282
CHAPTER VI
De ministro huius sacramenti et de absolutione
On the minister of this sacrament, and on absolution
Circa ministrum autem huius sacramenti declarat sancta synodus falsas esse et a veritate evangelii penitus alienas doctrinas omnes, quae ad alios quosvis homines praeter episcopos et sacerdotes clavium ministerium perniciose extendunt, putantes verba illa domini: "quaecumque alligaveritis super terram, erunt ligata et in coelo, et quaecumque solveritis super terram, erunt soluta et in coelo"; et: "quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis, et quorum retinueritis, retenta sunt"—ad omnes christifideles indifferenter et promiscue, contra institutionem huius sacramenti, ita fuisse dicta, ut quivis potestatem habeat remittendi peccata: publica quidem per correptionem, si correptus acquieverit; secreta vero per spontaneam confessionem cuicunque factam.
Concerning the minister of this sacrament, the holy synod declares that false are all doctrines which perniciously extend the ministry of the keys to any other men besides bishops and priests, thinking that the words of the Lord: "Whatsoever you shall bind upon earth shall be bound also in heaven, and whatsoever you shall loose upon earth shall be loosed also in heaven"; and: "Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them; and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained"—were so said to all the faithful of Christ indifferently and promiscuously, contrary to the institution of this sacrament, that everyone has the power of remitting sins: public indeed by correction, if the one corrected acquiesces; secret, however, by spontaneous confession made to anyone.
¶283
Docet quoque etiam sacerdotes qui peccato mortali tenentur, per virtutem spiritus sancti in ordinationem collatam, tanquam christi ministros, functionem remittendi peccata exercere, eos que prave sentire qui in malis sacerdotibus hanc potestatem non esse contendunt.
It also teaches that even priests who are held by mortal sin, by virtue of the Holy Spirit conferred in ordination, as ministers of Christ exercise the function of remitting sins, and that they think wrongly who contend that this power does not exist in evil priests.
¶284
Quamvis autem absolutio sacerdotis alieni beneficii sit dispensatio, tamen non est solum nudum ministerium vel annunciandi evangelium vel declarandi remissa esse peccata, sed ad instar actus iudicialis, quo ab ipso velut a iudice sententia pronunciatur.
Moreover, although the absolution of a priest is a dispensation of another's benefit, it is not, however, a mere ministry of announcing the gospel or of declaring that sins are remitted, but in the manner of a judicial act, by which sentence is pronounced by him as by a judge.
¶285
Atque ideo non debet poenitens adeo sibi de sua ipsius fide blandiri, ut etiamsi nulla illi adsit contritio aut sacerdoti animus serio agendi et vere absolvendi desit, putet tamen se propter suam solam fidem vere et coram deo esse absolutum. Nec enim fides sine poenitentia remissionem ullam peccatorum praestaret, nec is esset nisi salutis suae negligentissimus, qui sacerdotem ioco se absolventem cognosceret et non alium serio agentem sedulo requireret.
And therefore the penitent ought not so to flatter himself on his own faith that, even if there is no contrition in him nor a serious intention on the part of the priest to act and truly absolve, he should nevertheless think that he is truly and before God absolved on account of his faith alone. For faith without penance would not confer any remission of sins, nor would he be anything but most neglectful of his own salvation who should know that a priest was absolving him in jest and should not diligently seek another who acts seriously.
¶286
Quoniam igitur natura et ratio iudicii illud exposcit, ut sententia in subditos dumtaxat feratur, persuasum semper in ecclesia dei fuit et verissimum esse synodus haec confirmat, nullius momenti absolutionem eam esse debere, quam sacerdos in eum profert in quem ordinariam aut subdelegatam non habet iurisdictionem.
Since, therefore, the nature and reason of judgment demand that sentence be pronounced only on subjects, it has always been persuaded in the church of God, and this synod confirms it as most true, that that absolution is of no effect which a priest pronounces upon one over whom he has not ordinary or subdelegated jurisdiction.
¶287
CHAPTER VII
De casuum reservatione
On the reservation of cases
Magnopere vero ad christiani populi disciplinam pertinere sanctissimis patribus nostris visum est, ut atrociora quaedam et graviora crimina non a quibusvis sed a summis dumtaxat sacerdotibus absolverentur; unde merito pontifices maximi, pro suprema potestate sibi in ecclesia universa tradita, causas aliquas criminum graviores suo potuerunt peculiari iudicio reservare. Nec dubitandum est (quando omnia quae a deo sunt ordinata sunt) quin hoc idem episcopis omnibus in sua cuique dioecesi, in aedificationem tamen non in destructionem, liceat pro illis in subditos tradita supra reliquos inferiores sacerdotes auctoritate, praesertim quoad illa quibus excommunicationis censura annexa est.
It seemed to our most holy fathers to pertain greatly to the discipline of the Christian people that certain more atrocious and more grave crimes be absolved not by any priests but only by the highest priests; whence the supreme pontiffs, by the supreme power entrusted to them in the universal church, have been able to reserve certain more grave cases of crimes to their own special judgment. Nor is it to be doubted (since all things that are from God are ordained) that this same is permitted to all bishops in their respective dioceses, for edification however, not for destruction, by the authority over their subjects handed down to them above the inferior priests, especially with respect to those to which a censure of excommunication is attached.
¶288
Hanc autem delictorum reservationem consonum est divinae auctoritati non tantum in externa politia sed etiam coram deo vim habere.
Moreover, it is consonant with divine authority that this reservation of crimes have force not only in external polity but also before God.
¶289
Veruntamen pie admodum, ne hac ipsa occasione aliquis pereat, in eadem ecclesia dei custoditum semper fuit, ut nulla sit reservatio in articulo mortis, atque ideo omnes sacerdotes quoslibet poenitentes a quibusvis peccatis et censuris absolvere possunt; extra quem articulum sacerdotes, cum nihil possint in casibus reservatis, id unum poenitentibus persuadere nitantur, ut ad superiores et legitimos iudices pro beneficio absolutionis accedant.
Nevertheless, it has been piously preserved in the same church of God, that there be no reservation in the article of death, and therefore that all priests are able to absolve any penitents from any sins and censures; outside that article, however, since priests can do nothing in reserved cases, let them strive to persuade this one thing to penitents, that they go to their superiors and lawful judges for the benefit of absolution.
¶290
CHAPTER VIII
De satisfactionis necessitate et fructu
On the necessity and fruit of satisfaction
Demum, quoad satisfactionem, quae ex omnibus poenitentiae partibus quemadmodum a patribus nostris christiano populo fuit perpetuo tempore commendata, ita una maxime nostra aetate summo pietatis praetextu impugnatur ab iis qui speciem pietatis habent, virtutem autem eius abnegarunt, sancta synodus declarat falsum omnino esse et a verbo dei alienum, culpam a domino nunquam remitti quin universa etiam poena condonetur.
Finally, concerning satisfaction, which, of all the parts of penance, has been commended to the Christian people by our fathers at all times, and especially is impugned in this our age by those who, having the appearance of piety but having denied its power, under the greatest pretext of piety, the holy synod declares that it is altogether false and foreign to the word of God that the fault is never remitted by the Lord without the whole punishment also being remitted.
¶291
Perspicua enim et illustria in sacris litteris exempla reperiuntur, quibus praeter divinam traditionem hic error quam manifestissime revincitur. Sane et divinae iustitiae ratio exigere videtur, ut aliter ab eo in gratiam recipiantur qui ante baptismum per ignorantiam deliquerint; aliter vero qui, semel a peccati et daemonis servitute liberati et accepto spiritus sancti dono, scientes templum dei violare et spiritum sanctum contristare non formidaverint.
For clear and illustrious examples are found in the sacred writings by which, besides divine tradition, this error is most manifestly refuted. Indeed the reason of divine justice seems to demand that those who sinned before baptism through ignorance be received into grace in one way; those who, having once been freed from the servitude of sin and of the devil, and having received the gift of the Holy Spirit, knowingly have not feared to violate the temple of God and to grieve the Holy Spirit, in another way.
¶292
Et divinam clementiam decet, ne ita nobis absque ulla satisfactione peccata dimittantur, ut occasione accepta, peccata leviora putantes, velut iniurii et contumeliosi spiritui sancto, in graviora labamur, thesaurizantes nobis iram in die irae. Procul dubio enim magnopere a peccato revocant et quasi freno quodam cohercent hae satisfactoriae poenae, cautiores que et vigilantiores in futurum poenitentes efficiunt; medentur quoque peccatorum reliquiis et vitiosos habitus, male vivendo comparatos, contrariis virtutum actionibus tollunt.
And it befits divine clemency that sins be not so remitted to us without any satisfaction, that, taking occasion, thinking sins lighter, as it were injurious and contumelious to the Holy Spirit, we should fall into more grievous ones, treasuring up for ourselves wrath in the day of wrath. For beyond doubt these satisfactory punishments greatly recall from sin and coerce as with a certain bridle, and make penitents more cautious and vigilant for the future; they also heal the remnants of sins and remove vicious habits acquired by evil living by actions of contrary virtues.
¶293
Neque vero securior ulla via in ecclesia dei unquam existimata fuit ad amovendam imminentem a domino poenam, quam ut haec poenitentiae opera homines cum vero animi dolore frequentent.
Nor has any safer way ever been thought in the church of God for averting the penalty impending from the Lord than that men frequently perform these works of penance with true sorrow of soul.
¶294
Accedit ad haec quod, dum satisfaciendo patimur pro peccatis, christo Iesu, qui pro peccatis nostris satisfecit, ex quo omnis nostra sufficientia est, conformes efficimur, certissimam quoque inde arrham habentes quod, si compatimur, et conglorificabimur.
To this is added that, while we make satisfaction by suffering for sins, we are made conformed to Christ Jesus, who made satisfaction for our sins, from whom is all our sufficiency, having also a most certain pledge that, if we suffer with Him, we shall also be glorified with Him.
¶295
Neque vero ita nostra est satisfactio haec, quam pro peccatis nostris exsolvimus, ut non sit per christum Iesum; nam qui ex nobis tanquam ex nobis nihil possumus, eo cooperante qui nos confortat, omnia possumus. Ita non habet homo unde glorietur, sed omnis gloriatio nostra in christo est, in quo vivimus, in quo meremur, in quo satisfacimus, facientes fructus dignos poenitentiae, qui ex illo vim habent, ab illo offeruntur patri et per illum acceptantur a patre.
Nor, however, is this our satisfaction which we pay for our sins so our own that it is not through Christ Jesus. For we who from ourselves can do nothing, with Him cooperating who strengthens us, can do all things. Thus man has not whereof to glory, but all our glorying is in Christ, in whom we live, in whom we merit, in whom we make satisfaction, bringing forth fruits worthy of penance, which have their power from Him, are offered to the Father by Him, and through Him are accepted by the Father.
¶296
Debent ergo sacerdotes domini, quantum spiritus et prudentia suggesserit, pro qualitate criminum et poenitentium facultate, salubres et convenientes satisfactiones iniungere, ne si forte peccatis conniveant et indulgentius cum poenitentibus agant, levissima quaedam opera pro gravissimis delictis iniungendo, alienorum peccatorum participes efficiantur.
Therefore the priests of the Lord ought, as the Spirit and prudence suggest, to enjoin salutary and fitting satisfactions according to the quality of the crimes and the ability of the penitents, lest, if they perhaps connive at sins and deal too indulgently with penitents, by enjoining very light works for very grievous crimes, they become partakers of the sins of others.
¶297
Habeant autem prae oculis, ut satisfactio quam imponunt non sit tantum ad novae vitae custodiam et infirmitatis medicamentum, sed etiam ad praeteritorum peccatorum vindictam et castigationem; nam claves sacerdotum non ad solvendum dumtaxat, sed et ad ligandum concessas etiam antiqui patres et credunt et docent.
But let them have before their eyes that the satisfaction which they impose be not only for the keeping of a new life and a medicine for infirmity, but also for the vengeance and chastisement of past sins. For the ancient fathers both believe and teach that the keys of the priests are given not only for loosing but also for binding.
¶298
Nec propterea existimarunt sacramentum poenitentiae esse forum irae vel poenarum, sicut nemo unquam catholicus sensit ex huiusmodi nostris satisfactionibus vim meriti et satisfactionis domini nostri Iesu christi vel obscurari vel aliqua ex parte imminui; quod dum novatores intelligere nolunt, ita optimam poenitentiam novam vitam esse docent, ut omnem satisfactionis vim et usum tollant.
Nor for this reason did they think that the sacrament of penance is a forum of wrath or of punishments, just as no Catholic ever thought that by our satisfactions the virtue and satisfaction of our Lord Jesus Christ is obscured or in any part diminished; which the innovators, unwilling to understand, so teach that the best penance is a new life, that they take away all the power and use of satisfaction.
¶299
Docet praeterea tantam esse divinae munificentiae largitatem, ut non solum poenis sponte a nobis pro vindicando peccato susceptis aut sacerdotis arbitrio pro mensura delicti impositis, sed etiam (quod maximum amoris argumentum est) temporalibus flagellis a deo inflictis et a nobis patienter toleratis, apud deum patrem per christum Iesum satisfacere valeamus.
Moreover, it teaches that so great is the bounty of divine munificence, that we are able to satisfy before God the Father through Christ Jesus not only by punishments voluntarily undertaken by us for avenging sin, or imposed by the priest according to the measure of the offense, but also (which is the greatest proof of love) by temporal afflictions inflicted by God and patiently borne by us.
¶300
CHAPTER IX
De sacramento extremae unctionis
On the sacrament of extreme unction
Visum est autem sanctae synodo praecedenti doctrinae de poenitentia adiungere ea quae sequuntur de sacramento extremae unctionis, quod non modo poenitentiae, sed et totius christianae vitae, quae perpetua poenitentia esse debet, consummativum existimatum est a patribus.
It has seemed good to the holy synod to add to the preceding doctrine on penance those things which follow concerning the sacrament of extreme unction, which has been esteemed by the fathers not only as the completion of penance but also of the whole Christian life, which ought to be perpetual penance.
¶301
Primum itaque circa illius institutionem declarat et docet quod clementissimus redemptor noster, qui servis suis quovis tempore voluit de salutaribus remediis adversus omnia omnium hostium tela esse prospectum, quemadmodum auxilia maxima in sacramentis aliis praeparavit, quibus christiani conservare se integros dum viverent ab omni graviori spiritus incommodo possint, ita extremae unctionis sacramento finem vitae tanquam firmissimo quodam praesidio munivit.
First, therefore, concerning its institution, it declares and teaches that our most merciful Redeemer, who wished at all times to have provision for His servants with salutary remedies against all the weapons of all enemies, as He prepared the greatest helps in the other sacraments by which Christians may be able to keep themselves whole while they live from all graver spiritual disadvantage, so He fortified the end of life with the sacrament of extreme unction as with a most firm defense.
¶302
Nam etsi adversarius noster occasiones per omnem vitam quaerat et captet ut devorare animas nostras quoquo modo possit, nullum tamen tempus est quo vehementius ille omnes suae versutiae nervos intendat ad perdendos nos penitus et a fiducia etiam si possit divinae misericordiae deturbandos, quam cum impendere nobis exitum vitae prospicit.
For although our adversary seeks and snatches opportunities through the whole life to devour our souls in any way he can, yet there is no time in which he more vehemently strains all the sinews of his cunning to ruin us utterly and to drive us from trust in divine mercy if he can, than when he sees the end of life approaching us.
¶303
Instituta est autem sacra haec unctio infirmorum tanquam vere et proprie sacramentum novi testamenti a christo domino nostro, apud Marcum quidem insinuatum, per Iacobum autem apostolum ac domini fratrem fidelibus commendatum ac promulgatum.
Moreover, this holy unction of the infirm was instituted as a true and proper sacrament of the New Testament by our Lord Christ, insinuated indeed in Mark, but commended and promulgated to the faithful by James the apostle and brother of the Lord.
¶304
"Infirmatur" inquit "quis in vobis? inducat presbyteros ecclesiae et orent super eum, ungentes eum oleo in nomine domini; et oratio fidei salvabit infirmum et alleviabit eum dominus; et si in peccatis sit, dimittentur ei." Quibus verbis, ut ex apostolica traditione per manus accepta ecclesia didicit, docet materiam, formam, proprium ministrum et effectum huius salutaris sacramenti.
"Is anyone sick among you?" he says. "Let him bring in the priests of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord; and the prayer of faith shall save the sick man, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he be in sins, they shall be forgiven him." By which words, as the church learned from the apostolic tradition received by hand, it teaches the matter, the form, the proper minister, and the effect of this salutary sacrament.
¶305
Intellexit enim ecclesia materiam esse oleum ab episcopo benedictum; nam unctio aptissime spiritus sancti gratiam, qua invisibiliter anima aegrotantis inungitur, repraesentat; formam deinde esse illa verba: "per istam unctionem et suam piissimam misericordiam, indulgeat tibi dominus quidquid deliquisti," etc.
For the church understood that the matter is oil blessed by a bishop; for the unction most fittingly represents the grace of the Holy Spirit with which the soul of the sick person is invisibly anointed; the form, then, is those words: "Through this anointing and His most tender mercy, may the Lord forgive you whatever you have done wrong," etc.
¶306
Res porro et effectus huius sacramenti illis verbis explicatur: "et oratio fidei salvabit infirmum et alleviabit eum dominus; et si in peccatis sit, dimittentur ei." Res etenim haec gratia est spiritus sancti, cuius unctio delicta, si qua sint adhuc expianda, ac peccati reliquias abstergit et aegroti animam alleviat et confirmat, magnam in eo divinae misericordiae fiduciam excitando, qua infirmus sublevatus et morbi incommoda ac labores levius fert et tentationibus daemonis, calcaneo insidiantis, facilius resistit, et sanitatem corporis interdum, ubi saluti animae expedierit, consequitur.
Moreover, the thing and effect of this sacrament are explained by those words: "And the prayer of faith shall save the sick man, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he be in sins, they shall be forgiven him." For the thing itself is the grace of the Holy Spirit, whose unction blots out sins, if any still remain to be expiated, and the remnants of sin, and raises up and confirms the soul of the sick man, exciting great confidence in divine mercy in him, by which the sick man, being lifted up, bears more lightly the inconveniences and labors of the disease and more easily resists the temptations of the devil lying in wait for his heel, and sometimes, when it is expedient for the salvation of the soul, obtains health of the body.
¶307
Iam vero quod attinet ad praescriptionem eorum qui et suscipere et ministrare hoc sacramentum debent, haud obscure fuit illud etiam in verbis praedictis traditum. Nam et ostenditur illic proprios huius sacramenti ministros esse ecclesiae presbyteros, quo nomine eo loco non aetate seniores aut primores in populo intelligendi veniunt, sed aut episcopi aut sacerdotes ab ipsis rite ordinati per impositionem manuum presbyterii.
Now, concerning the prescription of those who ought both to receive and to minister this sacrament, that also was not obscurely handed down in the aforesaid words. For it is shown there that the proper ministers of this sacrament are the priests of the church, by which name in that place are to be understood not older men by age or chief men among the people, but either bishops or priests rightly ordained by them through the imposition of the hands of the priesthood.
¶308
Declaratur etiam esse hanc unctionem infirmis adhibendam, illis vero praesertim qui tam periculose decumbunt ut in exitu vitae constituti videantur; unde et sacramentum exeuntium nuncupatur.
It is also declared that this unction is to be applied to the infirm, especially to those who lie so dangerously that they are seen to be in the end of life; whence it is also called the sacrament of the dying.
¶309
Quod si infirmi post susceptam hanc unctionem convaluerint, iterum huius sacramenti subsidio iuvari poterunt, cum in aliud simile vitae discrimen inciderint.
But if the sick, after receiving this unction, recover, they can be helped by the aid of this sacrament again when they fall into another similar peril of life.
¶310
Quare nulla ratione audiendi sunt qui contra tam apertam et dilucidam apostoli Iacobi sententiam docent hanc unctionem vel figmentum esse humanum vel ritum a patribus acceptum, nec mandatum dei nec promissionem gratiae habentem; et qui illam iam cessasse asserunt, quasi ad gratiam curationum dumtaxat in primitiva ecclesia referenda esset; et qui dicunt ritum et usum quem sancta Romana ecclesia in huius sacramenti administratione observat, Iacobi apostoli sententiae repugnare atque ideo in alium commutandum esse; et denique qui hanc extremam unctionem a fidelibus sine peccato contemni posse affirmant.
Wherefore, by no means are they to be heard who, against so open and clear a sentence of the apostle James, teach that this unction is either a human invention or a rite received from the fathers, having neither the command of God nor the promise of grace; and who assert that it has now ceased, as if it were to be referred only to the grace of healings in the primitive church; and who say that the rite and use which the holy Roman church observes in the administration of this sacrament is contrary to the sentence of the apostle James and therefore to be changed into another; and finally, who affirm that this extreme unction can be despised by the faithful without sin.
¶311
Haec enim omnia manifestissime pugnant cum perspicuis tanti apostoli verbis. Nec profecto ecclesia Romana, aliarum omnium mater et magistra, aliud in hac administranda unctione (quantum ad ea quae huius sacramenti substantiam perficiunt) observat quam quod beatus Iacobus praescripsit. Neque vero tanti sacramenti contemptus absque ingenti scelere et ipsius spiritus sancti iniuria esse posset.
For all these things most manifestly fight with such clear words of so great an apostle. Nor, indeed, does the Roman church, the mother and teacher of all other churches, observe anything else in administering this unction, as far as pertains to those things which perfect the substance of this sacrament, than what blessed James prescribed. Nor could the contempt of so great a sacrament be without enormous crime and injury to the Holy Spirit Himself.
¶312
*CANONES DE SACRAMENTO POENITENTIAE*
CANONS ON THE SACRAMENT OF PENANCE
CANON I
Si quis dixerit in catholica ecclesia poenitentiam non esse vere et proprie sacramentum, pro fidelibus quoties post baptismum in peccata labuntur ipsi deo reconciliandis a christo domino nostro institutum: anathema sit.
If anyone says that in the catholic church penance is not truly and properly a sacrament, instituted by Christ our Lord for reconciling the faithful to God as often as they fall into sin after baptism: let him be anathema.
¶313
CANON II
Si quis, sacramenta confundens, ipsum baptismum poenitentiae sacramentum esse dixerit, quasi haec duo sacramento distincta non sint, atque ideo poenitentiam non recte secundam post naufragium tabulam appellari: anathema sit.
If anyone, confounding the sacraments, says that baptism itself is the sacrament of penance, as if these two sacraments are not distinct, and therefore that penance is not rightly called the second plank after shipwreck: let him be anathema.
¶314
CANON III
Si quis dixerit verba illa domini salvatoris: "accipite spiritum sanctum: quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis; et quorum retinueritis, retenta sunt," non esse intelligenda de potestate remittendi et retinendi peccata in sacramento poenitentiae, sicut ecclesia catholica ab initio semper intellexit, detorserit autem, contra institutionem huius sacramenti, ad auctoritatem praedicandi evangelium: anathema sit.
If anyone says that those words of the Lord the Savior: "Receive the Holy Spirit: whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them; and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained," are not to be understood concerning the power of remitting and retaining sins in the sacrament of penance, as the catholic church has always understood from the beginning, but twists them, contrary to the institution of this sacrament, to the authority of preaching the gospel: let him be anathema.
¶315
CANON IV
Si quis negaverit ad integram et perfectam peccatorum remissionem requiri tres actus in poenitente, quasi materiam sacramenti poenitentiae, videlicet contritionem, confessionem et satisfactionem, quae tres poenitentiae partes dicuntur; aut dixerit duas tantum esse poenitentiae partes, terrores scilicet incussos conscientiae agnito peccato et fidem conceptam ex evangelio vel absolutione, qua credit quis sibi per christum remissa peccata: anathema sit.
If anyone denies that for full and perfect remission of sins three acts in the penitent, namely contrition, confession, and satisfaction, are required, as the matter of the sacrament of penance, which are called the three parts of penance; or says that there are only two parts of penance, namely the terrors of a stricken conscience when sin is recognized, and the faith conceived from the gospel or from absolution, by which one believes that sins are remitted to him through Christ: let him be anathema.
¶316
CANON V
Si quis dixerit eam contritionem quae paratur per discussionem, collectionem et detestationem peccatorum, qua quis recogitat annos suos in amaritudine animae suae, ponderando peccatorum suorum gravitatem, multitudinem, foeditatem, amissionem aeternae beatitudinis et aeternae damnationis incursum, cum proposito melioris vitae, non esse verum et utilem dolorem, nec praeparare ad gratiam, sed facere hominem hypocritam et magis peccatorem; demum illam esse dolorem coactum et non liberum ac voluntarium: anathema sit.
If anyone says that that contrition which is prepared by the discussion, collection, and detestation of sins, by which one recounts his years in the bitterness of his soul, weighing the gravity, multitude, foulness of his sins, the loss of eternal beatitude, and the incurrence of eternal damnation, with the purpose of a better life, is not a true and useful sorrow, nor prepares for grace, but makes a man a hypocrite and a greater sinner; finally, that it is a coerced and forced, not free and voluntary, sorrow: let him be anathema.
¶317
CANON VI
Si quis negaverit confessionem sacramentalem vel institutam vel ad salutem necessariam esse iure divino; aut dixerit modum secrete confitendi soli sacerdoti, quem ecclesia catholica ab initio semper observavit et observat, alienum esse ab institutione et mandato christi et inventum esse humanum: anathema sit.
If anyone denies that sacramental confession was instituted or is necessary for salvation by divine law; or says that the manner of confessing secretly to a priest alone, which the catholic church has always observed from the beginning and observes, is foreign to the institution and command of Christ and is a human invention: let him be anathema.
¶318
CANON VII
Si quis dixerit in sacramento poenitentiae ad remissionem peccatorum necessarium non esse iure divino confiteri omnia et singula peccata mortalia, quorum memoria cum debita et diligenti praemeditatione habeatur, etiam occulta et quae sunt contra duo ultima decalogi praecepta, et circumstantias quae peccati speciem mutant; sed eam confessionem tantum esse utilem ad erudiendum et consolandum poenitentem, et olim observatam fuisse tantum ad satisfactionem canonicam imponendam; aut dixerit eos qui omnia peccata confiteri student nihil relinquere velle divinae misericordiae ignoscendum; aut demum non licere confiteri peccata venialia: anathema sit.
If anyone says that in the sacrament of penance for the remission of sins it is not necessary by divine law to confess all and each mortal sin of which memory with due and diligent meditation is had, even those which are secret and those which are against the last two precepts of the decalogue, and the circumstances which change the species of the sin; but that such confession is only useful for instructing and consoling the penitent, and was formerly observed only for imposing canonical satisfaction; or says that those who strive to confess all sins wish to leave nothing to divine mercy to be forgiven; or finally, that it is not lawful to confess venial sins: let him be anathema.
¶319
CANON VIII
Si quis dixerit confessionem omnium peccatorum, qualem ecclesia servat, esse impossibilem et traditionem humanam a piis abolendam; aut ad eam non teneri omnes et singulos utriusque sexus christifideles iuxta magni consilii lateranensis constitutionem semel in anno, et ob id suadendum esse christifidelibus ut non confiteantur tempore quadragesimae: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the confession of all sins, such as the church observes, is impossible and a human tradition to be abolished by the pious; or that all and each of the faithful of both sexes are not bound to it according to the constitution of the great Lateran council once a year, and therefore that the faithful are to be persuaded not to confess during Lent: let him be anathema.
¶320
CANON IX
Si quis dixerit absolutionem sacramentalem sacerdotis non esse actum iudicialem, sed nudum ministerium pronunciandi et declarandi remissa esse peccata confitenti, modo tantum credat se esse absolutum, aut sacerdos non serio sed ioco absolvat; aut dixerit non requiri confessionem poenitentis, ut sacerdos ipsum absolvere possit: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the sacramental absolution of a priest is not a judicial act, but a mere ministry of pronouncing and declaring that sins are forgiven to the confessing person, provided he only believe that he is absolved, or that the priest absolve not seriously but in jest; or says that the confession of the penitent is not required for the priest to be able to absolve him: let him be anathema.
¶321
CANON X
Si quis dixerit sacerdotes qui in peccato mortali sunt potestatem ligandi et solvendi non habere; aut non solos sacerdotes esse ministros absolutionis, sed omnibus et singulis christifidelibus esse dictum: "quaecumque ligaveritis super terram, erunt ligata et in coelo, et quaecumque solveritis super terram, erunt soluta et in coelo"; et: "quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis; et quorum retinueritis, retenta sunt"—quorum verborum virtute quilibet absolvere possit peccata: publica quidem per correptionem dumtaxat, si correptus acquieverit; secreta vero per spontaneam confessionem: anathema sit.
If anyone says that priests who are in mortal sin do not have the power of binding and loosing; or that not only priests are the ministers of absolution, but that it was said to all and each of the faithful of Christ: "Whatsoever you shall bind upon earth shall be bound also in heaven, and whatsoever you shall loose upon earth shall be loosed also in heaven"; and: "Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them; and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained"—by the force of which words anyone can absolve sins: public indeed by correction only, if the one corrected acquiesces; secret, however, by spontaneous confession made to anyone: let him be anathema.
¶322
CANON XI
Si quis dixerit episcopos non habere ius reservandi sibi casus, nisi quoad externam politiam, atque ideo casuum reservationem non prohibere quominus sacerdos a reservatis vere absolvat: anathema sit.
If anyone says that bishops do not have the right to reserve cases to themselves, except in respect of external polity, and therefore that the reservation of cases does not prevent a priest from truly absolving from reserved cases: let him be anathema.
¶323
CANON XII
Si quis dixerit totam poenam simul cum culpa remitti semper a deo, satisfactionem que poenitentium non esse aliam quam fidem qua apprehendunt christum pro eis satisfecisse: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the whole punishment is always remitted together with the guilt by God, and that the satisfaction of penitents is nothing other than the faith by which they apprehend that Christ has satisfied for them: let him be anathema.
¶324
CANON XIII
Si quis dixerit pro peccatis, quoad poenam temporalem, minime deo per christi merita satisfieri poenis ab eo inflictis et patienter toleratis vel a sacerdote iniunctis, neque sponte susceptis, ut ieiuniis, orationibus, eleemosynis vel aliis etiam pietatis operibus, atque ideo optimam poenitentiam esse tantum novam vitam: anathema sit.
If anyone says that for sins, as regards temporal punishment, satisfaction is by no means made to God through the merits of Christ by penalties inflicted by Him and patiently borne, or by those enjoined by the priest, nor even by those spontaneously undertaken, such as fasts, prayers, alms, or other works of piety, and therefore that the best penance is only a new life: let him be anathema.
¶325
CANON XIV
Si quis dixerit satisfactiones, quibus poenitentes per christum Iesum peccata redimunt, non esse cultus dei, sed traditiones hominum, doctrinam de gratia et verum dei cultum atque ipsum beneficium mortis christi obscurantes: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the satisfactions by which penitents redeem their sins through Christ Jesus are not a cult of God, but traditions of men obscuring the doctrine of grace and the true cult of God and the very benefit of the death of Christ: let him be anathema.
¶326
CANON XV
Si quis dixerit claves ecclesiae esse datas tantum ad solvendum, non etiam ad ligandum, et propterea sacerdotes, dum imponunt poenas confitentibus, agere contra finem clavium et contra institutionem christi; et fictionem esse quod virtute clavium, sublata poena aeterna, poena temporalis plerunque exsolvenda remaneat: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the keys of the church are given only for loosing, not also for binding, and therefore that priests, when they impose penalties on those confessing, act against the end of the keys and against the institution of Christ, and that it is a fiction that when the eternal punishment is removed by the virtue of the keys, a temporal punishment often remains to be paid: let him be anathema.
¶327
*CANONES DE EXTREMA UNCTIONE*
CANONS ON EXTREME UNCTION
CANON I
Si quis dixerit extremam unctionem non esse vere et proprie sacramentum, a christo domino nostro institutum et a beato Iacobo apostolo promulgatum, sed ritum tantum acceptum a patribus aut figmentum humanum: anathema sit.
If anyone says that extreme unction is not truly and properly a sacrament instituted by our Lord Christ and promulgated by blessed James the apostle, but only a rite received from the fathers or a human invention: let him be anathema.
¶328
CANON II
Si quis dixerit sacram infirmorum unctionem non conferre gratiam, nec remittere peccata, nec alleviare infirmos, sed iam cessasse, quasi olim tantum fuerit gratia curationum: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the sacred unction of the infirm does not confer grace, nor remit sins, nor relieve the infirm, but has already ceased, as if it was formerly only the grace of healings: let him be anathema.
¶329
CANON III
Si quis dixerit extremae unctionis ritum et usum quem observat sancta Romana ecclesia repugnare sententiae beati Iacobi apostoli, ideo que eum mutandum posse que a christianis absque peccato contemni: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the rite and use of extreme unction which the holy Roman church observes is repugnant to the sentence of blessed James the apostle, and therefore that it ought to be changed, and that it can be despised by Christians without sin: let him be anathema.
¶330
CANON IV
Si quis dixerit presbyteros ecclesiae, quos beatus Iacobus adducendos esse ad infirmum inungendum hortatur, non esse sacerdotes ab episcopo ordinatos, sed aetate seniores in quavis communitate, ob id que proprium extremae unctionis ministrum non esse solum sacerdotem: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the priests of the church, whom blessed James exhorts to be brought to anoint the sick, are not priests ordained by bishops, but older men by age in any community, and for this reason that the proper minister of extreme unction is not a priest alone: let him be anathema.
¶331
*DECRETUM DE REFORMATIONE*
DECREE ON THE REFORMATION
Cum proprie episcoporum munus sit subditorum omnium vitia redarguere, hoc illis praecipue cavendum erit, ne clerici, praesertim ad animarum curam constituti, criminosi sint, neve inhonestam vitam ipsis conniventibus ducant. Nam si eos pravis et corruptis moribus esse permittant, quo pacto laicos de ipsorum vitiis redarguent, qui uno ab eis sermone convinci possent quod clericos ipsis patiantur esse deteriores? Qua etiam libertate laicos corripere poterunt sacerdotes, cum tacite sibi ipsi respondeant eadem se admisisse quae corripiunt? Monebunt propterea episcopi suos clericos, in quocumque ordine fuerint, ut conversatione, sermone et scientia commisso sibi dei populo praeeant, memores eius quod scriptum est: "sancti estote, quia et ego sanctus sum." Et iuxta apostoli vocem: "nemini dent ullam offensionem, ut non vituperetur ministerium eorum; sed in omnibus exhibeant se sicut ministros dei," ne illud prophetae dictum impleatur in eis: "sacerdotes dei contaminant sancta et reprobant legem."
Since it is properly the office of bishops to rebuke the vices of all their subjects, this will be especially to be guarded by them, that clerics, especially those constituted over the care of souls, be not criminal, nor lead a dishonest life, with them conniving. For if they permit them to be of depraved and corrupt morals, how will they rebuke laypersons concerning their vices, who could be convicted by one word from them that they suffer clerics to be worse? With what freedom, also, will priests be able to correct laypersons, when they silently answer to themselves that they themselves have committed the same things which they correct? Therefore bishops will admonish their clerics, in whatever order they may be, that in conversation, speech, and knowledge they go before the people of God committed to them, mindful of that which is written: "Be holy, because I also am holy." And according to the voice of the apostle: "Give no offense to anyone, that their ministry be not blamed; but in all things let them show themselves as ministers of God," lest that saying of the prophet be fulfilled in them: "The priests of God pollute the holy things and make the law void."
¶332
Ut autem ipsi episcopi id liberius exequi ac quoquam praetextu desuper impediri nequeant, eadem sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus, praesidentibus in ea eisdem apostolicae sedis legato et nunciis, hos qui sequuntur canones statuendos et decernendos duxit.
So that the bishops themselves may be able to do this more freely and by no pretext be hindered above, the same sacred, oecumenical, and general Tridentine synod, the same legate and nuncios of the Apostolic See presiding, has judged that the following canons are to be established and decreed.
¶333
Cum honestius ac tutius sit subiecto debitam praepositis obedientiam impendendo in inferiori ministerio deservire, quam cum praepositorum scandalo graduum altiorum appetere dignitatem, ei cui ascensus ad sacros ordines a suo praelato ex quacunque causa, etiam ob occultum crimen, quomodolibet etiam extraiudicialiter, fuerit interdictus, aut qui a suis ordinibus seu gradibus vel dignitatibus ecclesiasticis fuerit suspensus, nulla contra ipsius praelati voluntatem concessa licentia de se promoveri faciendo aut ad priores ordines, gradus, dignitates sive honores restitutio suffragetur.
Since it is more honest and safer to serve in a lower ministry by paying due obedience to superiors than to seek the dignity of higher grades with scandal of superiors, to him whose ascent to sacred orders has been forbidden by his prelate for any cause, even on account of a hidden crime, in any way even extrajudicially, or who has been suspended from his orders or grades or ecclesiastical dignities, no license granted against the will of the prelate for promoting himself or for restoration to former orders, grades, dignities, or honors shall avail.
¶334
Et quoniam nonnulli episcopi ecclesiarum quae in partibus infidelium consistunt, clero carentes et populo christiano, cum fere vagabundi sint et permanentem sedem non habeant, non quae Iesu christi sed alienas oves, inscio proprio pastore, quaerentes, dum per hanc sanctam synodum se pontificalia officia in alterius dioecesi nisi de loci ordinarii expressa licentia et in personas eidem ordinario subiectas tantum exercere prohibitos vident, in legis fraudem et contemptum, quasi episcopalem cathedram in loco nullius dioecesis sua temeritate eligunt, et quoscunque ad se venientes, etiam si suorum episcoporum seu praelatorum literas commendatitias non habeant, clericali charactere insignire et ad sacros etiam presbyteratus ordines promovere praesumunt, quo plerunque fit ut minus idonei et rudes ac ignari et qui a suo episcopo tanquam inhabiles et indigni reiecti fuerint ordinentur, nec divina officia peragere nec ecclesiastica sacramenta recte valeant ministrare: nemo episcoporum, qui titulares vocantur, etiam si in loco nullius dioecesis etiam exempto aut aliquo monasterio cuiusvis ordinis resederint aut moram traxerint, vigore cuiusvis privilegii sibi de promovendo quoscunque ad se venientes pro tempore concessi, alterius subditum, etiam praetextu familiaritatis continuae, commensalitatis suae, absque sui proprii praelati expresso consensu aut literis dimissoriis, ad aliquos sacros aut minores ordines vel primam tonsuram promovere seu ordinare valeat.
And since some bishops of churches situated in the lands of the infidels, lacking clergy and Christian people, since they are almost vagabonds and have no permanent seat, seeking not the things of Jesus Christ but the sheep of others, the own pastor not knowing, while they see themselves forbidden by this holy synod to exercise pontifical offices in another's diocese without the express license of the ordinary of the place and only upon persons subject to the same ordinary, in fraud and contempt of the law, as if with rashness they choose an episcopal see in a place of no diocese, and presume to mark with the clerical character and to promote even to sacred orders of presbyterate any who come to them, even if they have no commendatory letters from their own bishops or prelates, whence it often comes to pass that the less suitable, rude, ignorant, and those rejected by their own bishop as unfit and unworthy are ordained, and can neither perform divine offices nor rightly administer ecclesiastical sacraments: let no bishop who are called titular, even if they reside or stay in a place of no diocese, however exempt, or in any monastery of any order, by the force of any privilege granted to them for promoting any who come to them for the time, be able to promote or ordain a subject of another, even under pretext of continuous familiarity and table fellowship, to any sacred or minor orders or first tonsure, without the express consent or dimissory letters of his own proper prelate.
¶335
Contrafaciens ab exercitio pontificalium per annum, taliter vero promotus ab executione ordinum sic susceptorum, donec suo praelato visum fuerit, ipso iure sint suspensi.
He who acts contrary shall be suspended from the exercise of pontificals for a year, and he thus promoted from the execution of the orders thus received, until it shall seem good to his prelate, by the law itself.
¶336
Episcopus quoscunque suos clericos, praesertim in sacris constitutos, absque suo praecedenti examine et commendatitiis literis quacumque auctoritate promotos, licet tanquam habiles ab eo a quo ordinati sunt probatos, quos tamen ad divina officia celebranda seu ecclesiastica sacramenta ministranda minus idoneos et capaces repererit, a susceptorum ordinum exercitio ad tempus, de quo ei videbitur, suspendere et illis ne in altari aut aliquo ordine ministrent interdicere possit.
A bishop shall be able to suspend from the exercise of the orders received for a time, according to his judgment, and to forbid them to minister at the altar or in any order, any of his clerics, especially those constituted in sacred orders, promoted by any authority without his prior examination and commendatory letters, although approved as suitable by him from whom they were ordained, whom he shall have found to be less suitable and capable for celebrating divine offices or administering ecclesiastical sacraments.
¶337
Omnes ecclesiarum praelati, qui ad corrigendos subditorum excessus diligenter intendere debent et a quibus nullus clericus per huius sanctae synodi statuta cuiusvis privilegii praetextu tutus censetur quominus iuxta canonicas sanctiones visitari, puniri et corrigi possit, si in ecclesiis suis resederint, quoscunque saeculares clericos, qualitercunque exemptos, qui alias suae iurisdictioni subessent, de eorum excessibus, criminibus et delictis, quoties et quando opus fuerit, etiam extra visitationem, tanquam ad hoc apostolicae sedis delegati, corrigendi et castigandi facultatem habeant; quibuscunque exemptionibus, declarationibus, consuetudinibus, sententiis, iuramentis, concordiis (quae suos tantum obligent auctores) ipsis clericis ac eorum consanguineis, capellanis, familiaribus, procuratoribus et aliis quibuslibet, ipsorum exemptorum contemplatione et intuitu, minime suffragantibus.
All prelates of churches, who ought diligently to attend to correcting the excesses of subjects, and from whom no cleric, by the statutes of this holy synod, is deemed safe under pretext of any privilege, so that he cannot be visited, punished, and corrected according to canonical sanctions, if they reside in their churches, shall have the faculty of correcting and chastising, even outside visitation, as often and when there shall be need, any secular clerics, however exempt, who would otherwise be subject to their jurisdiction, concerning their excesses, crimes, and offenses, as in this matter delegated by the Apostolic See, any exemptions, declarations, customs, sentences, oaths, agreements (which bind only their authors) by no means availing the same clerics and their blood relatives, chaplains, familiars, proctors, and any others for the consideration and regard of those exempt.
¶338
Insuper, cum nonnulli qui sub praetextu quod super bonis et rebus ac iuribus suis diversae eis iniuriae ac molestiae inferantur, certos iudices per literas conservatorias deputari obtinent, qui illos a molestiis et iniuriis huiusmodi tueantur ac defendant et in possessione seu quasi bonorum, rerum ac iurium suorum manu teneant et conservent, neque super illis eos molestari permittant, eiusmodi literas in plerisque contra concedentis mentem in reprobum sensum detorqueant; idcirco nemini omnino, cuiuscunque dignitatis et conditionis sit, etiam si capitulum fuerit, conservatoriae literae cum quibuscunque clausulis aut decretis et quorumcunque iudicum deputatione, quocunque etiam alio praetextu aut colore concessae, suffragentur ad hoc, ut coram suo episcopo sive alio superiore ordinario in criminalibus et mixtis causis accusari et conveniri ac contra eum inquiri et procedi non possit, aut quominus, si qua iura ei ex concessione competierint, super illis libere valeat apud iudicem ordinarium conveniri. In civilibus etiam causis, si ipse actor exstiterit, aliquem ei apud suos conservatores iudices in iudicium trahere minime liceat. Quod si in iis causis in quibus ipse reus fuerit contigerit, ut electus ab eo conservator ab actore suspectus esse dicatur, aut si qua inter ipsos iudices, conservatorem et ordinarium, controversia super competentia iurisdictionis orta fuerit, nequaquam in causa procedatur, donec per arbitros in forma iuris electos super suspicione aut iurisdictionis competentia fuerit iudicatum. Familiaribus vero eius qui huiusmodi literis conservatoriis tueri se solent, nihil illae prosint, praeterquam duobus dumtaxat, si tamen illi propriis eius sumptibus vixerint. Nemo etiam similium literarum beneficio ultra quinquennium gaudere possit. Non liceat quoque conservatoribus iudicibus ullum habere tribunal erectum. In causis vero mercedum aut miserabilium personarum, huius sanctae synodi super hoc decretum in suo robore permaneat. Universitates autem generales ac collegia doctorum seu scholarium et regularia loca, nec non hospitalia actu hospitalitatem servantia, ac universitatum, collegiorum, locorum et hospitalium huiusmodi personae, in praesenti canone minime comprehensae, sed exemptae omnino sint et esse intelligantur.
Moreover, since some who, under the pretext that various injuries and molestations are inflicted upon them concerning their goods, things, and rights, obtain certain judges by letters called conservatory letters, to be deputed, who may protect and defend them from such molestations and injuries, and may hold and preserve them in possession or quasi-possession of their goods, things, and rights, and may not permit them to be molested concerning them, twist such letters, in most cases, contrary to the mind of the one granting, into a reprobate sense; therefore, let letters of conservation, with whatever clauses or decrees and the deputation of any judges, granted under any other pretext or color, by no means avail anyone of whatsoever dignity and condition, even if it be a chapter, for this purpose: that he cannot be accused and sued before his bishop or other superior ordinary in criminal and mixed causes, and that inquisition and proceedings cannot be held against him, or that, if any rights may have belonged to him by concession, he may not be able freely to sue concerning them before an ordinary judge. Moreover, in civil causes, if he himself shall be the actor, let it by no means be lawful for him to draw anyone to judgment before his conservatory judges. But if in those causes in which he shall be the defendant it shall happen that the conservator elected by him is said to be suspect by the actor, or if any controversy concerning the competence of jurisdiction shall have arisen between the conservator judges themselves and the ordinary, the cause is by no means to be proceeded in until it shall have been judged by arbiters elected in the form of law concerning the suspicion or the competence of jurisdiction. As to his familiars who are accustomed to protect themselves by such conservatory letters, they shall profit them nothing except for two only, provided, however, that they have lived at his own expense. No one also may enjoy the benefit of similar letters beyond five years. Nor may conservator judges have any tribunal erected. In causes, however, of salaries or of miserable persons, let the decree of this holy synod concerning this remain in its force. Universities, however, general colleges of doctors or scholars, and regular places, and also hospitals actually keeping hospitality, and the persons of such universities, colleges, places, and hospitals, are by no means comprehended in this present canon, but are altogether to be exempt and to be understood as exempt.
¶339
Quia vero, etsi habitus non facit monachum, oportet tamen clericos vestes proprio ordini congruentes semper deferre, ut per decentiam habitus extrinseci morum honestatem intrinsecam ostendant, tanta autem hodie aliquorum inolevit temeritas religionis que contemptus, ut propriam dignitatem et honorem clericalem parvi pendentes, vestes etiam publice deferant laicales, pedes in diversis ponentes, unum in divinis, alterum in carnalibus: propterea omnes ecclesiasticae personae, quantumcunque exemptae, quae aut in sacris fuerint aut dignitates, personatus, officia aut beneficia qualiacunque ecclesiastica obtinuerint, si postea quam ab episcopo suo etiam per edictum publicum moniti fuerint, honestum habitum clericalem illorum ordini ac dignitati congruentem et iuxta episcopi ordinationem et mandatum non detulerint, per suspensionem ab ordinibus, ac officio et beneficio, ac fructibus, redditibus et proventibus ipsorum beneficiorum, nec non si semel correpti denuo in hoc deliquerint, etiam per privationem officiorum et beneficiorum huiusmodi coerceri possint et debeant, constitutionem Clementis V in concilio Viennensi editam quae incipit: "Quoniam" innovando et ampliando.
Since, however, even if the habit does not make the monk, yet it is fitting that clerics always wear clothes suitable to their order, that by the decency of external habit they may show the honesty of internal morals, such great temerity and contempt of religion has grown strong in some today that, esteeming their own dignity and clerical honor lightly, they even publicly wear lay garments, placing their feet in different places, one in divine things, the other in carnal: therefore all ecclesiastical persons, however exempt, who either have been constituted in sacred orders or have obtained any dignities, personates, offices, or benefices of any kind, if after they have been warned by their bishop even by public edict they shall not have worn an honest clerical habit suitable to their order and dignity and according to the ordination and command of the bishop, they may and ought to be coerced by suspension from orders, office, and benefice and from the fruits, incomes, and proceeds of those benefices, and if, having been once corrected, they shall have again offended in this, also by deprivation of such offices and benefices, renewing and amplifying the constitution of Clement V issued in the council of Vienne which begins: "Quoniam."
¶340
Cum etiam qui per industriam occiderit proximum suum et per insidias ab altari avelli debeat, qui sua voluntate homicidium perpetraverit, etiam si crimen id nec ordine iudiciario probatum nec alia ratione publicum, sed occultum fuerit, nullo tempore ad sacros ordines promoveri possit, nec illi aliqua ecclesiastica beneficia, etiam si cura non habeant animarum, conferri liceat, sed omni ordine ac beneficio et officio ecclesiastico perpetuo careat.
Also, he who has killed his neighbor by industry and by ambush ought to be taken away from the altar; he who has voluntarily committed homicide, even if that crime has not been proven by judicial order nor public in any other way but is secret, cannot be promoted to sacred orders at any time, nor may any ecclesiastical benefices, even if they have no cure of souls, be conferred upon him, but let him lack every order, benefice, and ecclesiastical office perpetually.
¶341
Si vero homicidium non ex proposito sed casu vel vim vi repellendo, ut quis se a morte defenderet, fuisse commissum narretur, quam ob causam etiam ad sacrorum ordinum et altaris ministerium et beneficia quaecunque ac dignitates iure quodammodo dispensatio debeatur, committatur loci ordinario, aut ex causa metropolitano seu viciniori episcopo, qui non nisi causa cognita et probatis precibus ac narratis, nec aliter, dispensare possit.
If, however, homicide is reported to have been committed not of set purpose but by chance or by repelling force, as when one would defend himself from death, on account of which cause some dispensation is in a manner due by law for sacred orders, for the ministry of the altar, and for any benefices and dignities, let it be entrusted to the ordinary of the place, or from cause to the metropolitan or the nearest bishop, who may dispense only after the cause has been known and the prayers have been proved and the facts set forth, and not otherwise.
¶342
Praeterea qui nonnulli, quorum etiam aliqui veri sunt pastores ac proprias oves habent, alienis etiam ovibus praeesse quaerunt, et ita alienis subditis quandoque intendunt ut suorum curam negligant. Quicunque etiam episcopali praeditus dignitate, qui alienos subditos puniendi privilegium habuerit, contra clericos sibi non subiectos, praesertim in sacris constitutos, quorumcunque etiam atrocium criminum reos, nisi cum proprii ipsorum clericorum episcopi, si apud ecclesiam suam resederit, aut personae ab ipso episcopo deputandae interventu, nequaquam procedere debeat; alias processus et inde secuta quaecunque viribus omnino careant.
Moreover, some, of whom even some are true pastors and have their own sheep, seek to be over also the sheep of others, and thus sometimes attend to subjects of others that they neglect the care of their own. Whoever, also, endowed with episcopal dignity has had the privilege of punishing subjects of others, is by no means to proceed against clerics not subject to him, especially those constituted in sacred orders, even those guilty of any atrocious crimes, unless with the intervention of the bishop of the clerics themselves, if he resides in his church, or of a person to be deputed by the bishop himself; otherwise, the process and whatever follows from it shall altogether lack force.
¶343
Et quia iure optimo distinctae fuerunt dioeceses et parochiae, ac unicuique gregi proprii attributi pastores et inferiorum ecclesiarum rectores, qui suarum quisque ovium curam habeant, ut ordo ecclesiasticus non confundatur, aut una et eadem ecclesia duarum quodammodo dioecesum fiat non sine gravi eorum incommodo qui illi subditi fuerint, beneficia unius dioecesis, etiam si parochiales ecclesiae, vicariae perpetuae aut simplicia beneficia seu praestimonia aut praestimoniales portiones fuerint, etiam ratione augendi cultum divinum aut numerum beneficiatorum aut alia quacunque de causa, alterius dioecesis beneficio aut monasterio seu collegio vel loco etiam pio perpetuo non uniantur; decretum huius sanctae synodi super huiusmodi unionibus in hoc declarando.
And because by best right dioceses and parishes have been distinguished, and to each flock its own pastors have been assigned, and rectors of inferior churches, each having the care of his own sheep, lest the ecclesiastical order be confused, or one and the same church become in a manner of two dioceses, not without grave disadvantage to those who are subject to them, let benefices of one diocese, even if they be parish churches, perpetual vicarages, or simple benefices, or prebends or prebendal portions, even for the sake of increasing divine worship or the number of beneficiaries or any other cause, not be perpetually united to a benefice, monastery, college, or pious place of another diocese; the decree of this holy synod concerning unions of this kind in this declaring.
¶344
Regularia beneficia, in titulum regularibus professis provideri consueta, cum per obitum aut resignationem vel alias illa in titulum obtinentis vacare contigerit, religiosis tantum illius ordinis vel iis qui habitum omnino suscipere et professionem emittere teneantur, et non aliis (ne vestem lino lana que contextam induant) conferantur.
Regular benefices, accustomed to be provided in title only to regulars who have made profession, when it shall happen that they become vacant by death or resignation or otherwise of the one holding them in title, are to be conferred only on religious of that order or on those who are bound to receive the habit altogether and to make profession, and not on others (lest they wear a garment woven of linen and wool).
¶345
Quia vero regulares de uno ad alium ordinem translati facile a suo superiore licentiam standi extra monasterium obtinere solent, ex quo vagandi et apostatandi occasio tribuitur, nemo cuiuscunque ordinis praelatus vel superior, vigore cuiusvis facultatis, aliquem ad habitum et professionem admittere possit, nisi ut in ordine ipso ad quem transfertur, sub sui superioris obedientia, in claustro perpetuo maneat; ac taliter translatus, etiamsi canonicorum regularium fuerit, ad beneficia saecularia, etiam curata, omnino incapax exsistat.
Since, however, regulars transferred from one order to another are accustomed easily to obtain license from their superior to stay outside the monastery, from which occasion of wandering and apostatizing is given, let no prelate or superior of any order, by virtue of any faculty, be able to admit anyone to habit and profession unless that he remain in cloister perpetually under the obedience of his superior in the order itself to which he is transferred; and let one thus transferred, even if he be of the regular canons, be altogether incapable of secular benefices, even those with cure.
¶346
Nemo etiam cuiusvis dignitatis ecclesiasticae vel saecularis, quacunque ratione, nisi ecclesiam, beneficium aut cappellam de novo fundaverit et construxerit, seu iam erectam, quae tamen sine sufficienti dote fuerit, de suis propriis et patrimonialibus bonis competenter dotaverit, ius patronatus impetrare aut obtinere possit aut debeat. In casu autem fundationis aut dotationis huiusmodi, institutio episcopo et non alteri inferiori reservetur. Non liceat praeterea patrono, cuiusvis privilegii praetextu, aliquem ad beneficia sui iuris patronatus nisi episcopo loci ordinario, ad quem provisio seu institutio ipsius beneficii, cessante privilegio, iure pertineret, quoquo modo praesentare; alias praesentatio ac institutio forsan secutae nullae sint et esse intelligantur.
Nor may anyone, of any ecclesiastical or secular dignity, by any reason, unless he has newly founded and built a church, benefice, or chapel, or has competently endowed from his own and patrimonial goods one already erected which yet is without sufficient dowry, be able or ought to obtain or acquire the right of patronage. In the case of such foundation or endowment, however, the institution is reserved to the bishop and not to another inferior. Moreover, it shall not be lawful for a patron, under pretext of any privilege, to present anyone to benefices of his right of patronage in any way to a bishop who is not the ordinary of the place to whom the provision or institution of that benefice would by right pertain, the privilege ceasing; otherwise, the presentation and the institution that may follow shall be null and be understood to be null.
¶347
Declarat praeterea sancta synodus in futura sessione, quam ad 25 diem ianuarii subsequentis anni 1552 habendam esse iam decrevit, una cum sacrificio missae agendum et tractandum etiam esse de sacramento ordinis et prosequendam esse materiam reformationis.
Moreover, the holy synod declares that in the future session, which it has already decreed to be held on the 25th day of January of the following year 1552, together with the sacrifice of the mass, it is also to treat and discuss concerning the sacrament of order, and to pursue the matter of reformation.

*SESSIO XV*
*DECRETUM DE PROROGATIONE SESSIONIS*
DECREE CONCERNING THE PROROGATION OF THE SESSION
Cum ex eo quod proximis sessionibus decretum fuit, sancta haec et universalis synodus per hos dies accuratissime diligentissime que tractaverit ea quae ad sanctissimum missae sacrificium et ad sacramentum ordinis spectant, ut hodierna sessione, quemadmodum spiritus sanctus suggessisset, decreta de his rebus et quatuor praeterea articulos ad sanctissimum eucharistiae sacramentum pertinentes in hac tandem sessione dilatos publicaret; atque interim affuturos esse putaverit ad hoc sacrosanctum concilium eos qui se protestantes vocant, quorum causa eorum publicationem articulorum distulerat, et ut libere ac sine cunctatione ulla huc venirent, fidem eis publicam sive salvumconductum concesserat; tamen cum illi nondum venerint, et eorum nomine supplicatum huic sanctae synodo fuerit, ut publicatio quae hodierna die facienda fuerat in sequentem sessionem differatur, certa spe allata affuturos eos esse omnino multo ante illam sessionem, salvoconductu amplioris formae interim accepto, eadem sancta synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, iisdem legato et nuntiis praesidentibus, nihil magis optans quam ex praestantissima natione Germanica omnes de religione dissensiones et schismata tollere ac eius quieti, paci otio que consulere, parata ipsos, si venerint, humaniter excipere et benigne audire, confidens que eos non fidei catholicae pertinaciter oppugnandae sed veritatis cognoscendae studio esse venturos, et (ut evangelicae veritatis studiosos decet) sanctae matris ecclesiae decretis et disciplinae ad extremum esse acquieturos, sequentem sessionem ad edenda et publicanda ea quae supra commemorata sunt in diem festum sancti Iosephi, qui erit die decima nona mensis martii, distulit, ut illi satis temporis et spatii habeant non solum ad veniendum, verum etiam ad ea quae voluerint antequam is dies veniat proponenda. Quibus, ut omnem diutius cunctandi causam adimat, fidem publicam sive salvumconductum eius qui recitabitur tenoris et sententiae libenter dat et concedit. Interea vero de matrimonii sacramento agendum et de eo praeter superiorum decretorum publicationem definiendum esse eadem sessione statuit et decernit, et prosequendam esse materiam reformationis.
Since from that which was decreed in the last sessions, this holy and universal synod has in these days most accurately and diligently treated those things which pertain to the most holy sacrifice of the mass and to the sacrament of order, that in today's session, as the Holy Spirit would have suggested, it might publish decrees concerning these matters and also the four articles pertaining to the most holy sacrament of the Eucharist, deferred at length in this session; and since it thought that those who call themselves Protestants would be present at this sacred council, for whose cause it had deferred the publication of the articles, and that they might come freely and without any delay, it had granted them public faith or safe-conduct; yet since they have not yet come, and it has been besought of this holy synod in their name that the publication which was to have been made today be deferred to the next session, certain hope having been brought that they will be present altogether before that session, a safe-conduct of ampler form having been received in the meantime, the same sacred synod, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit, the same legate and nuncios presiding, wishing nothing more than to remove all dissensions and schisms concerning religion from the most excellent German nation and to consult for its quiet, peace, and repose, ready, if they come, humanely to receive them and benignly to hear them, and trusting that they will come not for pertinaciously impugning the catholic faith but for the study of knowing the truth, and (as befits studious persons of evangelical truth) that they will at length acquiesce in the decrees and discipline of holy mother church, has deferred the next session for publishing and making known those things which are mentioned above to the feast of St. Joseph, which will be the 19th day of the month of March, that they may have sufficient time and space not only for coming but also for setting forth what they wish before that day comes. To whom, that it may remove all cause of longer delay, it freely gives and grants public faith or safe-conduct of the tenor and sentence which will be recited. In the meantime, however, it establishes and decrees that concerning the sacrament of matrimony it is to be treated and defined in the same session, beyond the publication of the superior decrees, and the matter of reformation is to be pursued.

DECREE CONCERNING THE CELEBRATION OF THE NEXT SESSION
Illustrissimi et reverendissimi domini, reverendi que patres, placet ne vobis ad laudem et gloriam sanctae individuae trinitatis, patris et filii et spiritus sancti, ad incrementum et exaltationem fidei et religionis christianae, sacrum oecumenicum et generale concilium Tridentinum, in spiritu sancto legitime congregatum, ab hodierno die qui est decimus octavus mensis ianuarii anni a nativitate domini 1562, cathedrae Romanae b(eati) Petri apostolorum principis consecrato, sublata quacumque suspensione, iuxta formam et tenorem litterarum sanctissimi domini nostri Pii IV pontificis maximi celebrari, et in eo, ea debito servato ordine tractari, quae proponentibus legatis ac praesidentibus ad horum temporum levandas calamitates, sedandas de religione controversias, coercendas linguas dolosas, depravatorum morum abusus corrigendos, ecclesiae veram atque christianam pacem conciliandam apta et idonea ipsi sanctae synodo videbuntur?

SESSION 18 — 26 FEBRUARY 1562
*SESSIO XVIII*
*DECRETUM DE ELECTIONE LIBRORUM ET DE INVITATIONE PROTESTANTIUM*
DECREE ON THE CHOICE OF BOOKS AND ON THE INVITATION OF THE PROTESTANTS
Sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem apostolicae sedis legatis, etsi in eum finem non absque peculiari spiritus sancti ductu et gubernatione convenerit, ut veram et antiquam de fide et sacramentis doctrinam exponeret et ut haeresibus omnibus et aliis gravissimis incommodis quibus dei ecclesia misere nunc exagitatur et in multas ac varias partes scinditur remedium afferret, hoc praesertim iam inde a principio in votis habuit, ut stirpitus convelleret zizania execrabilium errorum et schismatum quae inimicus homo his nostris calamitosis temporibus in doctrina fidei, usu et cultu sacrosanctae eucharistiae superseminavit, quam alioqui salvator noster in ecclesia sua tanquam symbolum reliquit eius unitatis et charitatis, qua christianos omnes inter se coniunctos et copulatos esse voluit.
The sacred, oecumenical, and general Tridentine synod, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit, the same legates of the Apostolic See presiding, although it has come together for that end not without the particular guidance and governance of the Holy Spirit, to set forth the true and ancient doctrine concerning faith and the sacraments, and to bring a remedy for all heresies and other very grievous disadvantages with which the church of God is now miserably agitated and rent into many and various parts, especially this, even from the beginning, it had in mind to pluck up by the roots the tares of execrable errors and schisms which the enemy man has oversown in these calamitous times in the doctrine of faith, in the use and cult of the most holy Eucharist, which otherwise our Savior left in His church as a symbol of that unity and charity by which He wished all Christians to be joined and bound together among themselves.
¶353
Censuit ut delecti ad hanc disquisitionem patres de censuris libris que quid facto opus esset diligenter considerarent, atque etiam ad eandem sanctam synodum suo tempore referrent.
It has judged that men chosen for this examination concerning the censures and books should diligently consider what is to be done concerning the censures and books, and that they should refer to the same holy synod in its own time.
¶354
Haec autem omnia ad notitiam quorumcumque deducta esse vult, prout etiam praesenti decreto deducit, ut si quis ad se pertinere aliquo modo putaverit quae vel de hoc librorum et censurarum negotio vel de aliis quae in hoc generali concilio tractanda praedixit, non dubitet a sancta synodo se benigne auditum iri.
All these things, however, it wishes to be brought to the knowledge of any persons, as also by the present decree it brings them, that if anyone thinks that anything pertains to him concerning this business of books and censures or concerning other things which it has said are to be treated in this general council, let him not doubt that he will be benignantly heard by the holy synod.
¶355
Quoniam vero eadem sancta synodus ex corde optat, deum que enixe rogat quae ad pacem sunt ecclesiae, ut universi communem matrem in terris agnoscentes, quae quos peperit oblivisci non potest, unanimes uno ore glorificemus deum et patrem domini nostri Iesu christi, per viscera misericordiae eiusdem dei et domini nostri omnes qui nobis cum communionem non habent ad concordiam et reconciliationem et ut ad hanc sanctam synodum veniant invitat atque hortatur, ut que charitatem, quod est vinculum perfectionis, amplectantur, pacem que christi exsultantem in cordibus suis prae se ferant, in quam vocati sunt in uno corpore.
Since, however, the same holy synod from the heart desires and earnestly prays to God for those things which are for the peace of the church, that all, acknowledging the common mother on earth, who cannot forget those whom she has begotten, may with one mind and one mouth glorify God and the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, by the bowels of the mercy of the same God and our Lord it invites and exhorts all who do not have communion with us to concord and reconciliation, and that they come to this holy synod, and that they embrace charity, which is the bond of perfection, and that they bear before themselves the peace of Christ rejoicing in their hearts, into which they have been called in one body.
¶356
Hanc ergo, non humanam sed spiritus sancti vocem audientes, ne obdurent corda sua, sed in suo sensu non ambulantes neque sibi placentes, ad tam piam et salutarem suae matris admonitionem excitentur et convertantur. Omnibus enim charitatis officiis sancta synodus eos, ut invitat, ita complectetur.
Hearing therefore this voice, not of men but of the Holy Spirit, let them not harden their hearts, nor walking in their own sense nor pleasing themselves, let them be aroused and converted to so pious and salutary an admonition of their mother. For with all offices of charity the holy synod, as it invites them, so it will embrace them.
¶357
Insuper eadem sancta synodus decrevit fidem publicam in congregatione generali concedi posse et eandem vim habituram eiusdem que roboris et momenti futuram ac si in publica sessione data et decreta fuisset.
Moreover, the same holy synod has decreed that public faith can be granted in a general congregation, and that it will have the same force and be of the same strength and moment as if it had been given and decreed in a public session.
¶358
Eadem sacrosancta Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem apostolicae sedis legatis, statuit et decernit proximam futuram sessionem habendam et celebrandam esse feria quinta post sacratissimum festum ascensionis domini, quae erit quartadecima mensis maii.
Likewise, the same sacred Tridentine synod, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit, the same legates of the Apostolic See presiding, has established and decreed that the next future session be held and celebrated on the Thursday after the most sacred feast of the Ascension of the Lord, which will be the 14th day of May.

SESSION 19 — 14 MAY 1562
*SESSIO XIX — 14 MAII 1562*
*DECRETUM DE PROROGATIONE SESSIONIS*
DECREE CONCERNING THE PROROGATION OF THE SESSION
Sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem apostolicae sedis legatis, universis fidem facit.... Eadem sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem sedis apostolicae legatis, omnibus et singulis aliis qui nobis cum in iis quae sunt fidei communionem non habent, ex quibuscumque regnis, nationibus, provinciis, civitatibus ac locis in quibus publice et impune praedicatur vel docetur sive creditur contrarium eius quod sancta Romana sentit ecclesia, dat fidem publicam sive salvumconductum sub eadem forma et eisdem verbis quibus datur Germanis.
The sacred, oecumenical, and general Tridentine synod, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit, the same legates of the Apostolic See presiding, makes known to all the faithful... DECREE CONCERNING THE COMMUNION UNDER BOTH SPECIES AND THE COMMUNION OF INFANTS, AND THE PROROGATION OF THE SESSION
Sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem apostolicae sedis legatis, cum de tremendo et sanctissimo eucharistiae sacramento varia diversis in locis errorum monstra nequissimi daemonis artibus circumferantur, ob quae in nonnullis provinciis multi a catholicae ecclesiae fide atque oboedientia videantur discessisse, censuit ea quae ad communionem sub utraque specie et parvulorum pertinent hoc loco exponenda esse.
Wherefore it forbids all the faithful of Christ that henceforth they dare to believe, teach, or preach concerning them otherwise than is explained and defined in these decrees.
¶365
Quapropter cunctis christifidelibus interdicit, ne posthac de iis aliter vel credere vel docere vel praedicare audeant quam est iis decretis explicatum atque definitum.
Therefore the same holy synod, taught by the Holy Spirit, who is the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and piety, and following the judgment and custom of the church itself, declares and teaches that laypersons and clerics not celebrating are not bound by any divine precept to receive the sacrament of the Eucharist under both species, nor that it can be doubted in any way (faith being preserved) that communion under the species of bread alone suffices for them for salvation.
¶366
CHAPTER I
Itaque sancta ipsa synodus, a spiritu sancto qui spiritus est sapientiae et intellectus, spiritus consilii et pietatis edocta atque ipsius ecclesiae iudicium et consuetudinem secuta, declarat ac docet nullo divino praecepto laicos et clericos non conficientes obligari ad eucharistiae sacramentum sub utraque specie sumendum, neque ullo pacto (salva fide) dubitari posse quin illis alterius speciei communio ad salutem sufficiat.
For although Christ the Lord instituted this venerable sacrament in the species of bread and wine at the last supper and delivered it to the apostles, nevertheless that institution and delivery do not tend to this, that all the faithful of Christ are bound by the Lord's decree to receive both species. Nor is it rightly gathered from that discourse in John, chapter six, that communion under both species was commanded by the Lord, however it is understood according to the various interpretations of the holy fathers and doctors.
¶367
Nam etsi christus dominus in ultima coena venerabile hoc sacramentum in panis et vini speciebus instituit et apostolis tradidit, non tamen illa institutio et traditio eo tendunt, ut omnes christifideles statuto domini ad utramque speciem accipiendam adstringantur. Sed neque ex sermone illo apud Ioannem sexto recte colligitur utriusque speciei communionem a domino praeceptam esse, utcumque iuxta varias sanctorum patrum et doctorum interpretationes intelligatur.
For He who said: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink His blood, you shall not have life in you," also said: "If anyone eats of this bread, he shall live forever." And He who said: "He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life," also said: "The bread which I shall give is my flesh for the life of the world." And finally, He who said: "He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me and I in him," also said: "He who eats this bread shall live forever."
¶368
Namque qui dixit: "nisi manducaveritis carnem filii hominis et biberitis eius sanguinem, non habebitis vitam in vobis," dixit quoque: "si quis manducaverit ex hoc pane, vivet in aeternum." Et qui dixit: "qui manducat meam carnem et bibit meum sanguinem, habet vitam aeternam," dixit etiam: "panis quem ego dabo, caro mea est pro mundi vita." Et denique qui dixit: "qui manducat meam carnem et bibit meum sanguinem, in me manet et ego in illo," dixit nihilominus: "qui manducat hunc panem, vivet in aeternum."
Moreover, it declares that this power has always been in the church, that in the dispensation of the sacraments, their substance being preserved, it might establish or change those things which it judged to be more expedient for the utility of the recipients or for the veneration of the sacraments themselves, according to the variety of things, times, and places. Which the apostle did not obscurely seem to intimate when he said: "Let a man so account of us as of the ministers of Christ and the dispensers of the mysteries of God."

CHAPTER II
Praeterea declarat hanc potestatem perpetuo in ecclesia fuisse, ut in sacramentorum dispensatione, salva illorum substantia, ea statueret vel mutaret quae suscipientium utilitati seu ipsorum sacramentorum venerationi pro rerum, temporum et locorum varietate magis expedire iudicaret. Id autem apostolus non obscure visus est innuisse cum ait: "sic nos existimet homo ut ministros christi et dispensatores mysteriorum dei."
And that He Himself used this power is sufficiently evident, since in many other things, so also in this very sacrament, when He arranged certain things concerning its use, He said: "Other things I will set in order when I come."
¶370
Atque ipsum quidem hac potestate usum esse satis constat, cum in multis aliis, tum in hoc ipso sacramento, cum ordinatis nonnullis circa eius usum, "cetera" inquit "cum venero disponam."
Therefore, recognizing this authority of hers in the administration of the sacraments, holy mother church, although from the beginning of the Christian religion the use of both species was not infrequent, yet in the progress of time, that custom having been already very widely changed, having been induced by grave and just reasons, approved this custom of communicating under one species and decreed that it is to be held as law, which it is not lawful to reject or to change at will without the authority of the church itself.
¶371
Quare agnoscens sancta mater ecclesia hanc suam in administratione sacramentorum auctoritatem, licet ab initio christianae religionis non infrequens utriusque speciei usus fuisset, tamen progressu temporum, latissime iam mutata illa consuetudine, gravibus et iustis causis adducta, hanc consuetudinem sub altera specie communicandi approbavit et pro lege habendam decrevit, quam reprobare aut sine ipsius ecclesiae auctoritate pro libito mutare non licet.
Moreover, it declares that although our Redeemer, as has been said before, instituted this sacrament in two species at that last supper and delivered it to the apostles, nevertheless it is to be confessed that even under one species alone the whole and entire Christ and a true sacrament are received, and therefore that, with respect to its fruit, those who receive one species alone are not defrauded of any grace necessary for salvation.
¶372
CHAPTER III
Insuper declarat, quavis redemptor noster, ut antea dictum est, in suprema illa coena hoc sacramentum in duabus speciebus instituerit et apostolis tradiderit, tamen fatendum esse etiam sub altera tantum specie totum atque integrum christum verum que sacramentum sumi, ac propterea, quod ad fructum attinet, nulla gratia necessaria ad salutem eos defraudari qui unam speciem solam accipiunt.
Finally, the same holy synod teaches that infants lacking the use of reason are bound by no necessity to sacramental communion of the Eucharist, since, having been regenerated by the laver of baptism and incorporated into Christ, they cannot in that age lose the grace of the sons of God already obtained. Nor, however, is antiquity to be condemned if it at any time observed that custom in some places.
¶373
CHAPTER IV
Denique eadem sancta synodus docet parvulos usu rationis carentes nulla obligari necessitate ad sacramentalem eucharistiae communionem, siquidem per baptismi lavacrum regenerati et christo incorporati, adeptam iam filiorum dei gratiam in illa aetate amittere non possunt. For just as those most holy fathers had a probable reason for their action according to the condition of that time, so it is to be believed without controversy that they did this by no necessity of salvation.
¶374

If anyone says that by a precept of God or by necessity of salvation all and each of the faithful of Christ ought to receive both species of the most holy sacrament of the Eucharist: let him be anathema.

SESSION 22 — 17 SEPTEMBER 1562
*SESSIO XXII*
*DECRETUM DE SACRIFICIO MISSAE*
DECREE CONCERNING THE SACRIFICE OF THE MASS
Sacrosancta oecumenica et generalis Tridentina synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata, praesidentibus in ea eisdem apostolicae sedis legatis, ut vetus, absoluta atque omni ex parte perfecta de magno eucharistiae mysterio in sancta catholica ecclesia fides atque doctrina retineatur et in sua puritate, propulsatis erroribus atque haeresibus, conservetur, de ea quatenus verum et singulare sacrificium est, spiritus sancti illustratione edocta, haec quae sequuntur docet, declarat et fidelibus populis praedicanda decernit.
Since under the former testament, (as the apostle Paul testifies), on account of the weakness of the levitical priesthood, there was no perfection, it was necessary (God the Father of mercies so ordaining) that another priest should arise according to the order of Melchisedech, our Lord Jesus Christ, who could perfect and bring to perfection all those who are to be sanctified. He therefore, our God and Lord, although He was about to offer Himself once on the altar of the cross, death intervening, to God the Father, that He might work eternal redemption for them, yet because His priesthood was not to be extinguished by death, in the last supper, on the night He was betrayed, that He might leave to His beloved spouse the church a visible sacrifice (as the nature of men requires) by which that bloody sacrifice once to be accomplished on the cross might be represented, and its memory might remain until the end of the age, and its saving virtue might be applied for the remission of those sins which we daily commit, declaring Himself constituted a priest forever according to the order of Melchisedech, He offered His body and blood under the species of bread and wine to God the Father, and under the symbols of the same things He gave them to the apostles (whom He then constituted priests of the new testament) to receive, and to their successors in the priesthood He commanded that they should offer them, by these words: "Do this in memory of me," and so on, as the catholic church has always understood and taught.
¶382
CHAPTER I
De institutione sanctissimi missae sacrificii
Concerning the institution of the most holy sacrifice of the Mass
Quoniam sub priori testamento, (teste apostolo Paulo), propter levitici sacerdotii imbecillitatem, consummatio non erat, oportuit (deo patre misericordiarum ita ordinante) sacerdotem alium secundum ordinem Melchisedech surgere, dominum nostrum Iesum christum, qui posset omnes quotquot sanctificandi essent consummare et ad perfectum adducere. Is igitur, deus et dominus noster, etsi semel se ipsum in ara crucis, morte intercedente, deo patri oblaturus erat, ut aeternam illis redemptionem operaretur, quia tamen per mortem sacerdotium eius exstinguendum non erat, in coena novissima, qua nocte tradebatur, ut dilectae sponsae suae ecclesiae visibile (sicut hominum natura exigit) relinqueret sacrificium, quo cruentum illud semel in cruce peragendum repraesentaretur, eius que memoria in finem usque saeculi permaneret atque illius salutaris virtus in remissionem eorum quae a nobis quotidie committuntur peccatorum applicaretur, sacerdotem secundum ordinem Melchisedech se in aeternum constitutum declarans, corpus et sanguinem suum sub speciebus panis et vini deo patri obtulit ac sub earundem rerum symbolis apostolis (quos tunc novi testamenti sacerdotes constituebat) ut sumerent tradidit, et eisdem eorum que in sacerdotio successoribus ut offerent praecepit, per haec verba: "hoc facite in meam commemorationem," et cetera, uti semper catholica ecclesia intellexit et docuit.
For having celebrated the old pasch which the multitude of the children of Israel immolated in memory of the departure from Egypt, He instituted the new pasch, Himself to be immolated by the church through priests under visible signs, in memory of His own passage from this world to the Father, when by the shedding of His blood He redeemed us and delivered us from the power of darkness and translated us into His kingdom.
¶383
Nam celebrato veteri pascha, quod in memoriam exitus de Aegypto multitudo filiorum Israel immolabat, novum instituit pascha, se ipsum ab ecclesia per sacerdotes sub signis visibilibus immolandum in memoriam transitus sui ex hoc mundo ad patrem, quando per sui sanguinis effusionem nos redemit eripuit que de potestate tenebrarum et in regnum suum transtulit.
And since in this divine sacrifice which is performed in the Mass, that same Christ is contained and is immolated in an unbloody manner, who once offered Himself in a bloody manner on the altar of the cross, the holy synod teaches that this sacrifice is truly propitiatory, and that by it, if we approach to God with a true heart and right faith, with fear and reverence, contrite and penitent, we may obtain mercy and find grace in opportune aid.
¶384
CHAPTER II
Quod missae sacrificium sit propitiatorium pro vivis et defunctis
That the sacrifice of the Mass is propitiatory both for the living and the dead
Et quoniam in divino hoc sacrificio quod in missa peragitur, idem ille christus continetur et incruente immolatur, qui in ara crucis semel se ipsum cruente obtulit, docet sancta synodus sacrificium istud vere propitiatorium esse, per ipsumque fieri, ut si cum vero corde et recta fide, cum metu ac reverentia, contriti ac poenitentes ad deum accedamus, misericordiam consequamur et gratiam inveniamus in auxilio opportuno.
For the Lord, placated by this oblation, granting grace and the gift of penance, remits crimes and sins, even great ones. For it is one and the same victim, the same now offering by the ministry of priests who offered Himself then on the cross, the manner only of offering being different. The fruit of this oblation (the bloody one, I say) is most abundantly received through this unbloody oblation; so far is it that this in any way derogates from that.
¶385
Huius quippe oblatione placatus dominus, gratiam et donum poenitentiae concedens, crimina et peccata etiam ingentia dimittit. Una enim eadem que est hostia, idem nunc offerens sacerdotum ministerio, qui se ipsum tunc in cruce obtulit, sola ratione diversa. Cuius quidem oblationis (cruentae inquam) fructus per hanc incruentam uberrime percipiuntur; tantum abest ut illi per hanc quovis modo derogetur.
Wherefore, it is rightly offered not only for the sins, punishments, satisfactions, and other necessities of the faithful living, but also for the dead in Christ not yet fully purged, according to the tradition of the apostles.
¶386
Quare non solum pro fidelium vivorum peccatis, poenis, satisfactionibus et aliis necessitatibus, sed et pro defunctis in christo nondum ad plenum purgatis, rite iuxta apostolorum traditionem offertur.
And although the church has been accustomed sometimes to celebrate masses in honor and memory of the saints, it does not teach that sacrifice is offered to them, but to God alone who crowned them. Whence neither does the priest usually say: "I offer sacrifice to you, Peter or Paul," but, giving thanks to God for their victories, he implores their patronage that they may deign to intercede for us in heaven whose memory we make on earth.
¶387
CHAPTER III
De missis in honorem sanctorum
On masses in honor of the saints
Et quamvis in honorem et memoriam sanctorum nonnullas interdum missas ecclesia celebrare consueverit, non tamen illis sacrificium offerri docet, sed deo soli qui illos coronavit. Unde nec sacerdos dicere solet: "offero tibi sacrificium, Petre vel Paule," sed deo de illorum victoriis gratias agens, eorum patrocinia implorat, ut ipsi pro nobis intercedere dignentur in coelis, quorum memoriam facimus in terris.
And since it is fitting that it be administered holily, and this is the most holy of all sacrifices, the catholic church, that it might be offered and received worthily and reverently, instituted the sacred canon many centuries ago, so pure from all error that nothing is contained in it which does not in the highest degree savor of sanctity and piety and raise the minds of those offering to God. For it consists both of the very words of the Lord and of the traditions of the apostles and also of the pious institutions of holy pontiffs.
¶388
CHAPTER IV
De canone missae
On the canon of the Mass
Et cum sancte administrari conveniat, sit que hoc omnium sanctissimum sacrificium, ecclesia catholica, ut digne reverenter que offerretur ac perciperetur, sacrum canonem multis ante saeculis instituit, ita ab omni errore purum, ut nihil in eo contineatur quod non maxime sanctitatem ac pietatem quandam redoleat mentes que offerentium in deum erigat. Is enim constat cum ex ipsis domini verbis tum ex apostolorum traditionibus ac sanctorum quoque pontificum piis institutionibus.
And since the nature of men is such that it cannot easily be raised to the meditation of divine things without external aids, therefore pious mother church has instituted certain rites, namely that some things in the Mass be pronounced in a subdued voice, others in a louder voice; it has also employed ceremonies, such as mystical blessings, lights, incense, vestments, and many other things of that kind from apostolic discipline and tradition, that by these visible signs of religion and piety the majesty of so great a sacrifice might be commended, and the minds of the faithful might be excited by these visible signs to the contemplation of the most exalted things which lie hidden in this sacrifice.
¶389
CHAPTER V
De ceremoniis et ritibus missae
On the ceremonies and rites of the Mass
Cum que natura hominum ea sit, ut non facile queat sine adminiculis exterioribus ad rerum divinarum meditationem sustolli, propterea pia mater ecclesia ritus quosdam, ut scilicet quaedam submissa voce, alia vero elatiore in missa pronuntiarentur, instituit, caeremonias item adhibuit, ut mysticas benedictiones, lumina, thymiamata, vestes, alia que id genus multa, ex apostolica disciplina et traditione, quo et maiestas tanti sacrificii commendaretur, mentes fidelium per haec visibilia religionis ac pietatis signa ad rerum altissimarum quae in hoc sacrificio latent contemplationem excitarentur.
The sacred synod would indeed wish that at each Mass the faithful who are present should communicate not only spiritually in affection but also sacramentally by the reception of the Eucharist, that the more abundant fruit of this most holy sacrifice might come to them; but it does not, however, if this is not always done, for that reason condemn those Masses in which the priest alone communicates sacramentally as private and illicit, but approves and indeed commends them; since those Masses also ought to be considered truly common, partly because in them the people communicate spiritually, partly because they are celebrated by a public minister of the church not for himself alone but for all the faithful who belong to the body of Christ.
¶390
CHAPTER VI
De modo celebrandi missam, et quod laici tantum spiritualiter communicent
On the manner of celebrating Mass, and that the laity should communicate only spiritually
Optaret quidem sacrosancta synodus ut in singulis missis fideles adstantes non solum spirituali affectu sed sacramentali etiam eucharistiae perceptione communicarent, quo ad eos sanctissimi huius sacrificii fructus uberior proveniret; nec tamen, si id non semper fiat, propterea missas illas in quibus solus sacerdos sacramentaliter communicat, ut privatas et illicitas, damnat, sed probat atque adeo commendat; siquidem illae quoque missae vere communes censeri debent, partim quod in eis populus spiritualiter communicat, partim vero quod a publico ecclesiae ministro non pro se tantum sed pro omnibus fidelibus qui ad corpus christi pertinent celebrentur.
Moreover, the holy synod admonishes that it is a precept of the church for priests to mix water with wine in offering the chalice, both because Christ the Lord is believed to have done this, and also because water came forth from His side together with blood, which sacrament is recalled by this mixture; and since in the Apocalypse of blessed John, the waters are said to be peoples, the union of the faithful people with Christ the head is represented.

CHAPTER VII
Quod aqua vino in calice offerendo miscenda sit
That water is to be mixed with the wine to be offered in the chalice
Monet deinde sancta synodus praeceptum esse ab ecclesia sacerdotibus ut aquam vino in calice offerendo miscerent, tum quod christum dominum ita fecisse credatur, tum etiam quia e latere eius aqua simul cum sanguine exierit, quod sacramentum hac mixtione recolitur; et cum aquae in apocalypsi beati Ioannis populi dicantur, ipsius populi fidelis cum capite christo unio repraesentatur.
Although the Mass contains great instruction for the faithful people, it has not seemed expedient to the fathers that it be celebrated everywhere in the vulgar tongue. Wherefore, the ancient rite of each church, approved by the holy Roman church, the mother and teacher of all churches, having been retained everywhere, lest the sheep of Christ be hungry, and lest the little ones ask for bread and there be none to break it to them, the holy synod commands pastors and each one having the care of souls that frequently during the celebration of Mass, either by themselves or by others, they explain something of those things which are read in the Mass, and among other things declare some mystery of this most holy sacrifice, especially on the Lord's days and feast days.
¶392
CHAPTER VIII
Quod missa non sit celebranda in lingua vulgari, et populus in eius mysteriis sit instruendus
That the Mass is not to be celebrated in the vulgar tongue, and that the people are to be instructed in its mysteries
Etsi missa magnam contineat populi fidelium eruditionem, non tamen expedire visum est patribus ut vulgari passim lingua celebraretur. Quamobrem, retento ubique cuiusque ecclesiae antiquo et a sancta Romana ecclesia, omnium ecclesiarum matre et magistra, probato ritu, ne oves christi esuriant neve parvuli panem petant et non sit qui frangat eis, mandat sancta synodus pastoribus et singulis curam animarum gerentibus, ut frequenter inter missarum celebrationem, vel per se vel per alios, ex his quae in missa leguntur aliquid exponant, atque inter cetera sanctissimi huius sacrificii mysterium aliquod declarent, diebus praesertim dominicis et festis.
If anyone says that the Mass does not offer a true and proper sacrifice to God, or that what is offered is nothing other than that Christ is given to us to eat: let him be anathema.

CANON I

CANON IX
Si quis dixerit ecclesiae Romanae ritum, quo submissa voce pars canonis et verba consecrationis proferuntur, damnandum esse; aut lingua tantum vulgari missam celebrari debere; aut aquam non miscendam esse vino in calice offerendo, eo quod sit contra christi institutionem: anathema sit.
How great care ought to be taken that the most holy sacrifice of the Mass be celebrated with all religious cult and veneration, anyone can easily estimate who has considered that in the sacred writings he is called cursed who does the work of God negligently. If we must necessarily confess that no other work can be treated by the faithful of Christ so holy and divine as this tremendous mystery, in which that life-giving victim by which we were reconciled to God the Father is daily immolated on the altar by priests, it is sufficiently evident also that all effort and diligence ought to be used in it, that it be performed with the greatest possible interior purity of heart and exterior devotion and piety.

*DECRETUM DE REFORMATIONE*
DECREE ON THE REFORMATION
Quanta cura adhibenda sit ut sacrosanctum missae sacrificium omni religionis cultu ac veneratione celebretur, quivis facile existimare poterit qui cogitarit maledictum in sacris litteris eum vocari qui facit opus dei negligenter. Quodsi necessario fatemur nullum aliud opus adeo sanctum ac divinum a christifidelibus tractari posse quam hoc ipsum tremendum mysterium, quo vivifica illa hostia qua deo patri reconciliati sumus in altari per sacerdotes quotidie immolatur, satis etiam apparet omnem operam et diligentiam in eo ponendam esse, ut quanta maxima fieri potest interiori cordis munditia et puritate atque exterioris devotionis ac pietatis specie peragatur.
Since therefore many things seem to have crept in, whether through the vice of the times or through the negligence and wickedness of men, which are alien to the dignity of so great a sacrifice, that due honor and cult may be restored to the glory of God and the edification of the faithful people, the holy synod decrees that the ordinaries of places, the bishops, diligently take care and be bound to prohibit and remove from the midst all those things which either avarice (the servitude of idols), or irreverence (which can scarcely be separated from impiety), or superstition (the false imitator of true piety), have introduced.
¶403
Cum igitur multa iam sive temporum vitio sive hominum incuria et improbitate irrepsisse videantur quae a tanti sacrificii dignitate aliena sunt, ut et debitus honor et cultus ad dei gloriam et fidelis populi aedificationem restituatur, decernit sancta synodus ut ordinarii locorum episcopi ea omnia prohibere atque e medio tollere sedulo curent ac teneantur quae vel avaritia (idolorum servitus), vel irreverentia (quae ab impietate vix seiuncta esse potest), vel superstitio (verae pietatis falsa imitatrix), induxit.
And that many things may be included in a few, first with respect to avarice, the conditions of wages of any kind, pacts, and whatever is given for celebrating new Masses, and also importunate and illiberal exactions of alms rather than requests, and other such things which are not far from simoniacal stain or certainly from filthy gain, let them altogether forbid.
¶404
Atque ut multa paucis comprehendantur, in primis quod ad avaritiam pertinet, cuiusvis generis mercedum conditiones, pacta et quidquid pro missis novis celebrandis datur, necnon importunas atque illiberales eleemosynarum exactiones potius quam postulationes, alia que huiusmodi quae a simoniaca labe vel certe a turpi quaestu non longe absunt, omnino prohibeant.
Then, that irreverence may be avoided, let each one in his own diocese forbid that it be lawful for any wandering and unknown priest to celebrate Masses. Moreover, let them not permit anyone who is publicly and notoriously criminal to minister at the holy altar or to be present at sacred things, nor suffer that this holy sacrifice be performed in private houses and altogether outside the church and the oratories dedicated to divine worship only, to be designated and visited by the same ordinaries, by any secular or regular priests, unless those who are present first declare that they are present with their mind and devout affection of heart, not only with their body, having decently composed the habit of their body.
¶405
Deinde, ut irreverentia vitetur, singuli in suis dioecesibus interdicant ne cui vago et ignoto sacerdoti missas celebrare liceat. Neminem praeterea cui publice et notorie criminosus sit aut sancto altari ministrare aut sacris interesse permittant, neve patiantur privatis in domibus atque omnino extra ecclesiam et ad divinum tantum cultum dedicata oratoria, ab eisdem ordinariis designanda et visitanda, sanctum hoc sacrificium a saecularibus aut regularibus quibuscumque peragi, ac nisi prius qui intersint decenter composito corporis habitu declaraverint se mente etiam ac devoto cordis affectu non solum corpore adesse.
From churches, moreover, let them banish those musical practices in which either organ or singing mixes anything lascivious or impure; likewise all secular actions, vain and indeed profane conversations, walking about, noise, clamor, that the house of God may truly seem and be able to be called the house of prayer.
¶406
Ab ecclesiis vero musicas eas ubi sive organo sive cantu lascivum aut impurum aliquid miscetur, item saeculares omnes actiones, vana atque adeo profana colloquia, deambulationes, strepitus, clamores arceant, ut domus dei vere domus orationis esse videatur ac dici possit.
Finally, that no place be given to superstition, let them take care by edict and proposed penalties that priests celebrate not at other than the due hours, nor that they use other rites or other ceremonies and prayers in the celebration of Mass besides those which have been approved by the church and received by frequent and laudable use.
¶407
Postremo, ne superstitioni locus aliquis detur, edicto et poenis propositis caveant ne sacerdotes aliis quam debitis horis celebrent, neve ritus alios aut alias caeremonias et preces in missarum celebratione adhibeant praeter eas quae ab ecclesia probatae ac frequenti et laudabili usu receptae fuerint.
Let them altogether remove from the church a certain number of masses and candles, which has been invented more from superstitious cult than from true religion, and let them teach the people what is and from whom especially proceeds this so precious and heavenly fruit of this most holy sacrifice.
¶408
Quarundam vero missarum et candelarum certum numerum, qui magis a superstitioso cultu quam a vera religione inventus est, omnino ab ecclesia removeant, doceant que populum quis sit et a quo potissimum proveniat huius sanctissimi sacrificii tam pretiosus ac coelestis fructus.
Let them also admonish the same people that they frequently come to their own parishes, at least on the Lord's days and the greater feasts.
¶409
Moneant etiam eundem populum ut frequenter ad suas parochias, saltem diebus dominicis et maioribus festis, accedant.
All these things, therefore, which have been enumerated summarily, are so set forth to all ordinaries of places that not only those same things, but whatever other things shall seem to pertain to this, they themselves, by the power given to them by the sacred synod and also as delegates of the Apostolic See, may prohibit, command, correct, establish, and by ecclesiastical censures and other penalties which shall be determined by their judgment, compel the faithful people to observe them inviolably, any privileges, exemptions, appeals, and customs whatsoever notwithstanding.
¶410
Haec igitur omnia quae summatim enumerata sunt omnibus locorum ordinariis ita proponuntur ut non solum ea ipsa, sed quaecumque alia huc pertinere visa fuerint, ipsi pro data sibi a sacrosancta synodo potestate, ac etiam ut delegati sedis apostolicae, prohibeant, mandent, corrigant, statuant, atque ad ea inviolate servanda censuris ecclesiasticis aliis que poenis quae illorum arbitrio constituentur, fidelem populum compellant, non obstantibus privilegiis, exemptionibus, appellationibus et consuetudinibus quibuscumque.
The sacrifice and priesthood are so conjoined by the ordinance of God that both have existed in every law. Since therefore in the New Testament the catholic church has received the visible sacrifice of the holy Eucharist from the institution of the Lord, it must also be confessed that there is in it a new, visible, and external priesthood, into which the old has been translated.

*SESSIO XXIII*
*DECRETUM DE SACRAMENTO ORDINIS*
DECREE CONCERNING THE SACRAMENT OF ORDER
Sacrificium et sacerdotium ita dei ordinatione coniuncta sunt, ut utrumque in omni lege exsisterit. Cum igitur in novo testamento sanctum eucharistiae sacrificium visibile ex domini institutione catholica ecclesia acceperit, fateri etiam oportet in ea novum esse visibile et externum sacerdotium, in quod vetus translatum est.
Moreover, the sacred scriptures show, and the tradition of the catholic church has always taught, that this priesthood was instituted by the same Lord our Savior, and that the power of consecrating, offering, and ministering His body and blood, as well as of remitting and retaining sins, was handed down to the apostles and their successors in the priesthood.
¶412
Hoc autem ab eodem domino salvatore nostro institutum esse atque apostolis eorum que successoribus in sacerdotio potestatem traditam consecrandi, offerendi et ministrandi corpus et sanguinem eius, nec non et peccata dimittendi et retinendi, sacrae litterae ostendunt et catholicae ecclesiae traditio semper docuit.
But since the ministry of so holy a priesthood is a divine matter, it was fitting that, so that it might be exercised more worthily and with greater veneration, there should be in the most ordered disposition of the church many and diverse orders of ministers who serve the priesthood by office, so distributed that those who are already marked with the clerical tonsure might ascend through the minor to the major orders.
¶413
Cum autem divina res sit tam sancti sacerdotii ministerium, consentaneum fuit, quo dignius et maiori cum veneratione exerceri posset, ut in ecclesiae ordinatissima dispositione plures et diversi essent ministrorum ordines, qui sacerdotio ex officio deservirent, ita distributi, ut qui iam clericali tonsura insigniti essent, per minores ad maiores ascenderent.
For the sacred scriptures make open mention not only of priests but also of deacons, and teach in most grave words what is especially to be observed in their ordination; and from the very beginning of the church, the names of the following orders and the proper ministries of each, namely subdeacons, acolytes, exorcists, readers, and doorkeepers, are known to have been in use, although not of equal grade. For subdiaconate is referred by the fathers and sacred councils to the major orders, in which also we read very frequently concerning the other minor orders.
¶414
Nam non solum de sacerdotibus sed et de diaconis sacrae litterae apertam mentionem faciunt, et quae maxime in illorum ordinatione attendenda sunt, gravissimis verbis docent; et ab ipso ecclesiae initio sequentium ordinum nomina atque uniuscuiusque eorum propria ministeria, subdiaconi scilicet, acolythi, exorcistae, lectoris et ostiarii, in usu fuisse cognoscuntur, quamvis non pari gradu. Nam subdiaconatus ad maiores ordines a patribus et sacris conciliis refertur, in quibus et de aliis inferioribus frequentissime legimus.
Since it is clear from the testimony of scripture, apostolic tradition, and the unanimous consent of the fathers that by sacred ordination, which is effected by words and outward signs, grace is conferred, no one ought to doubt that order is truly and properly one of the seven sacraments of the holy church. For the apostle says: "I admonish you to stir up the grace of God which is in you by the imposition of my hands." For "God has not given us the spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of sobriety."
¶415
Cum scripturae testimonio, apostolica traditione et patrum unanimi consensu perspicuum sit per sacram ordinationem, quae verbis et signis exterioribus perficitur, gratiam conferri, dubitare nemo debet ordinem esse vere et proprie unum ex septem sanctae ecclesiae sacramentis. Inquit enim apostolus: "admoneo te ut resuscites gratiam dei quae est in te per impositionem manuum mearum." "Non enim dedit nobis deus spiritum timoris, sed virtutis et dilectionis et sobrietatis."
Since, however, in the sacrament of order, as also in baptism and confirmation, a character is imprinted which can neither be destroyed nor taken away, the holy synod rightly condemns the opinion of those who say that the priests of the New Testament have only temporary power, and that once rightly ordained they can again become laymen if they do not exercise the ministry of the word of God.
¶416
Quoniam vero in sacramento ordinis, sicut et in baptismo et confirmatione, character imprimitur, qui nec deleri nec auferri potest, merito sancta synodus damnat eorum sententiam, qui asserunt novi testamenti sacerdotes temporariam tantummodo potestatem habere et semel rite ordinatos iterum laicos effici posse, si verbi dei ministerium non exerceant.
But if anyone affirms that all Christians indiscriminately are priests of the New Testament, or that all are endowed with equal spiritual power among themselves, he seems to do nothing else than confound the ecclesiastical hierarchy, which is as an army set in order, just as if against the teaching of blessed Paul "all are apostles, all prophets, all evangelists, all pastors, all are teachers."
¶417
Quodsi quis omnes christianos promiscue novi testamenti sacerdotes esse, aut omnes pari inter se potestate spirituali praeditos affirmet, nihil aliud facere videtur quam ecclesiasticam hierarchiam, quae est "ut castrorum acies ordinata," confundere, perinde ac si contra beati Pauli doctrinam "omnes apostoli, omnes prophetae, omnes evangelistae, omnes pastores, omnes sint doctores."
Therefore the holy synod declares that besides the other ecclesiastical grades, bishops, who have succeeded in the place of the apostles, especially pertain to this hierarchical order, and that they are placed (as the same apostle says) by the Holy Spirit to rule the church of God, that they are superior to priests, and that they can confer the sacrament of confirmation, ordain ministers of the church, and perform many other functions, of which the rest of the lower orders have no power.
¶418
Proinde sancta synodus declarat praeter ceteros ecclesiasticos gradus, episcopos qui in apostolorum locum successerunt, ad hunc hierarchicum ordinem praecipue pertinere et positos (sicut idem apostolus ait) a spiritu sancto regere ecclesiam dei, eos que presbyteris superiores esse, ac sacramentum confirmationis conferre, ministros ecclesiae ordinare atque alia pleraque peragere ipsos posse, quarum functionum potestatem reliqui inferioris ordinis nullam habent.
Moreover, the holy synod teaches that in the ordination of bishops, priests, and the other orders, neither the consent nor the vocation or authority of the people, or of any secular power and magistrate, is so required that without it the ordination is void; rather it decrees that those who are only called and instituted by the people, or by secular power and magistrate, to ascend to these ministries to be exercised, and who take them to themselves by their own rashness, are all to be held not as ministers of the church but as thieves and robbers who have not entered by the door.
¶419
Docet insuper sancta synodus in ordinatione episcoporum, sacerdotum et ceterorum ordinum, nec populi nec cuiusvis saecularis potestatis et magistratus consensum sive vocationem sive auctoritatem ita requiri, ut sine ea irrita sit ordinatio; quin potius decernit eos qui tantummodo a populo aut saeculari potestate ac magistratu vocati et instituti ad haec ministeria exercenda ascendunt, et qui ea propria temeritate sibi sumunt, omnes non ecclesiae ministros sed fures et latrones per ostium non ingressos habendos esse.
These are the things which in general it has seemed good to the sacred synod to teach the faithful of Christ concerning the sacrament of order. But it has determined to condemn those things which are contrary to these in certain and proper canons in the following manner, that all, using the rule of faith with Christ's help, may more easily recognize and hold the catholic truth in so many errors of darkness.
¶420
Haec sunt quae generatim sacrae synodo visum est christifideles de sacramento ordinis docere. His autem contraria certis et propriis canonibus in hunc qui sequitur modum damnare constituit, ut omnes, adiuvante christo, fidei regula utentes in tot errorum tenebris catholicam veritatem facilius agnoscere et tenere possint.
If anyone says that there is not in the New Testament a visible and external priesthood, or that there is no power of consecrating and offering the true body and blood of the Lord and of remitting and retaining sins, but only an office and bare ministry of preaching the gospel; or that those who do not preach are not priests at all: let him be anathema.

DECREE ON THE REFORMATION
Eadem sacrosancta Tridentina synodus, reformationis materiam prosequens, haec quae sequuntur in praesenti decernenda esse statuit ac decernit.
Since it is commanded by divine precept to all to whom the care of souls is committed to know their sheep, to offer sacrifice for them, to feed them by the preaching of the divine word, the administration of the sacraments, and the example of all good works, to bear paternal care for the poor and other miserable persons, and to attend to the other pastoral duties, all of which cannot be performed and fulfilled by those who do not watch over their flock nor attend to it, but desert it after the manner of hirelings, the sacred synod admonishes and exhorts them, mindful of the divine precepts and made a form of the flock, to feed and rule in judgment and truth.
¶430
Cum praecepto divino mandatum sit omnibus quibus animarum cura commissa est oves suas agnoscere, pro his sacrificium offerre, verbi que divini praedicatione, sacramentorum administratione ac bonorum omnium operum exemplo pascere, pauperum aliarum que miserabilium personarum curam paternam gerere et in cetera munia pastoralia incumbere, quae omnia nequaquam ab his praestari et impleri possunt qui gregi suo non invigilant neque assistunt, sed mercenariorum more deserunt, sacrosancta synodus eos admonet et hortatur, ut divinorum praeceptorum memores, facti que forma gregis, in iudicio et veritate pascant et regant.
Lest, however, those things which were holily and usefully previously enacted concerning residence under Paul III of happy memory be twisted into a sense alien from the mind of the sacred synod, as if by the force of that decree it were lawful to be absent for five continuous months, adhering to them, the sacred synod declares that all placed over patriarchal, primatial, metropolitan, and cathedral churches, under whatsoever name and title, even if they are cardinals of the holy Roman church, are bound to personal residence in their own church or diocese where they are bound to perform the office enjoined upon them, nor can they be absent except for the causes and in the manners written below.
¶431
Ne vero ea quae de residentia sancte et utiliter iam antea sub felicis recordationis Paulo tertio sancita fuerunt in sensus a sacrosanctae synodi mente alienos trahantur, ac si vigore illius decreti quinque mensibus continuis abesse liceat, illis inhaerendo, declarat sacrosancta synodus omnes patriarchalibus, primatialibus, metropolitanis ac cathedralibus ecclesiis quibuscumque quocumque nomine et titulo praefectos, etiam si sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinales sint, obligari ad personalem in sua ecclesia vel dioecesi residentiam, ubi iniuncto sibi officio defungi teneantur, neque abesse posse nisi ex causis et modis infrascriptis.
For since Christian charity, urgent necessity, due obedience, and evident utility of the church or of the commonwealth sometimes demand and require that some be absent, the same sacred synod decrees that these legitimate causes of absence are to be approved in writing by the most blessed Roman pontiff, or by the metropolitan, or, in his absence, by the senior resident suffragan bishop, who ought to prove the absence of the metropolitan, unless the absence shall have occurred on account of some duty and public office annexed to bishoprics, the causes of which, since they are notorious and sometimes sudden, it will not be necessary even to signify to the metropolitan.
¶432
Nam cum christiana charitas, urgens necessitas, debita obedientia ac evidens ecclesiae vel rei publicae utilitas aliquos nonnumquam abesse postulent et exigant, decernit eadem sacrosancta synodus has legitimae absentiae causas a beatissimo Romano pontifice, aut a metropolitano, vel eo absente, suffraganeo episcopo antiquiori residente, qui idem metropolitani absentiam probare debebit, in scriptis esse approbandas, nisi cum absentia inciderit propter aliquod munus et rei publicae officium episcopatibus adiunctum, cuius quoniam causae sunt notoriae et interdum repentinae, ne eas quidem significari metropolitano necesse erit.
Nevertheless, it will pertain to the same metropolitan with the provincial council to judge concerning the licenses given by him or by the suffragan, and to see that no one abuses that right and that those who err may be punished with canonical penalties.
¶433
Ad eundem tamen cum concilio provinciali spectabit iudicare de licentiis a se vel a suffraganeo datis, et videre ne quis eo iure abutatur, et ut poenis canonicis errantes puniantur.
In the meantime, let those about to depart remember so to provide for their sheep that, as far as can be done, they receive no loss from their absence.
¶434
Interea meminerint discessuri ita ovibus suis providendum, ut quantum fieri poterit ex ipsorum absentia nullum damnum accipiant.
Since, however, according to the judgment of the ancient canons, those who are absent for only a short time are not considered to be absent, because they are about to return immediately, the sacred synod wills that that space of absence each year, whether continuous or interrupted, beyond the aforesaid causes, should by no means exceed two, or at most three, months, and that regard be had that this be done for a just cause and without any detriment to the flock.
¶435
Quoniam autem qui aliquantisper tantum absunt ex veterum canonum sententia non videntur abesse, quia statim reversuri sunt, sacrosancta synodus vult illud absentiae spatium singulis annis, sive continuum sive interruptum, extra praedictas causas nullo pacto debere duos aut ad summum tres menses excedere, et haberi rationem ut id aequa ex causa fiat et absque ullo gregis detrimento.
Whether this be so, it leaves to the conscience of those who are absent, which it hopes will be religious and fearful, since hearts are open to God, whose work they are bound not to do fraudulently at their own peril.
¶436
Quod an ita sit, absentium conscientiae relinquit, quam sperat religiosam et timoratam fore, cum deo corda pateant, cuius opus non fraudulentur agere, suo periculo tenentur.
In the meantime, it admonishes and exhorts them in the Lord that during that space of time they be by no means absent from their own cathedral church on the days of the Lord's Advent, Lent, the Nativity, the Resurrection of the Lord, Pentecost, and also the Body of Christ, on which days the sheep ought especially to be refreshed and to rejoice in the Lord in the presence of the pastor, unless episcopal duties call them elsewhere in their own diocese.
¶437
Eosdem interim admonet et in domino hortatur, ne per illius temporis spatium, dominici adventus, quadragesimae, nativitatis, resurrectionis domini, pentecostes item et corporis christi diebus, quibus refici maxime et in domino gaudere pastoris praesentia oves debeant, ipsi ab ecclesia sua cathedrali ullo pacto absint, nisi episcopalia munia in sua dioecesi eos alio vocent.
If anyone, however (which may it never happen), shall have been absent contrary to the disposition of this decree, the sacred synod establishes that besides the other penalties against non-residents imposed and renewed under Paul III, and the guilt of mortal sin which he incurs, he does not make the fruits his own in proportion to the time of absence, nor can he with a safe conscience, no other declaration having followed, retain them for himself, but is bound, the same ceasing, by the ecclesiastical superior to cause them to be distributed to the fabric of the churches or to the poor of the place, any pact or composition prohibited which is called for fruits wrongly received, by which also the aforesaid fruits would be remitted to him in whole or in part.
¶438
Si quis autem (quod utinam numquam eveniat) contra huius decreti dispositionem abfuerit, statuit sacrosancta synodus praeter alias poenas adversus non residentes sub Paulo III impositas et innovatas, ac mortalis peccati reatum quem incurrit, eum pro rata temporis absentiae fructus suos non facere, nec tuta conscientia, alia etiam declaratione non secuta, illos sibi detinere posse, sed teneri, ipso cessante, per superiorem ecclesiasticum illos fabricae ecclesiarum aut pauperibus loci erogare, prohibita quacumque conventione vel compositione quae pro fructibus male perceptis appellatur, ex qua etiam praedicti fructus in totum aut pro parte ei remitterentur.
The same things, with respect to guilt, loss of fruits, and penalties, the sacred synod declares and decrees also concerning inferior curates and any others who obtain any ecclesiastical benefice having the cure of souls, yet in such a way that whenever it happens that they are absent, the cause having first been known and proved by the bishop, they leave a suitable vicar to be approved by the ordinary, with the assignment of the due salary. Moreover, let them not obtain license to depart in writing, to be granted freely, beyond two months unless from a grave cause. But if, having been cited by edict, they are contumacious, even not personally, the synod wills that it be free for ordinaries to compel them by ecclesiastical censures and by sequestration and subtraction of fruits and other remedies of law, even to deprivation, and that this execution cannot be suspended by any privilege, license, familiarity, exemption, even by reason of any benefice, pact, statute even confirmed by oath or by any authority, custom even immemorial (which is rather to be considered a corruption), or by appeal or inhibition even in the Roman curia or by virtue of the Eugidian constitution.
¶439
Eadem omnino etiam quoad culpam, amissionem fructuum et poenas de curatis inferioribus et aliis quibuscumque qui beneficium aliquod ecclesiasticum curam animarum habens obtinent sacrosancta synodus declarat et decernit, ita tamen ut quandocumque eos causa prius per episcopum cognita et probata abesse contigerit, vicarium idoneum ab ipso ordinario approbandum cum debita mercedis assignatione relinquant. Discedendi autem licentiam in scriptis, gratis que concedendam, ultra bimestre tempus nisi ex gravi causa non obtineant. Quodsi per edictum citati etiam non personaliter contumaces fuerint, liberum esse vult ordinariis per censuras ecclesiasticas, ac sequestrationem et subtractionem fructuum, alia que iuris remedia, etiam usque ad privationem compellere, nec exsecutionem hanc quolibet privilegio, licentia, familiaritate, exemptione etiam ratione cuiuscumque beneficii, pactione, statuto etiam iuramento vel quacumque auctoritate confirmato, consuetudine etiam immemorabili (quae potius corruptela censenda est), sive appellatione aut inhibitione, etiam in Romana curia vel vigore Eugenianae constitutionis, suspendi posse.
Finally, the holy synod commands both that decree under Paul III and this same decree to be published in provincial and episcopal councils. For it wishes those things which pertain so much to the duty of pastors and the salvation of souls to be frequently fixed in the ears and minds of all, that in the future, God helping, they be not abolished by any injury of times or by the oblivion or desuetude of men.
¶440
Postremo tam decretum illud sub Paulo III quam hoc ipsum in conciliis provincialibus et episcopalibus publicari sancta synodus praecipit. Cupit enim quae adeo ex pastorum munere animarum que salute sunt frequenter omnium auribus mentibus que infigi, ut in posterum, deo iuvante, nulla temporum iniuria aut hominum oblivione aut desuetudine aboleantur.
Those placed over cathedral or superior churches, by whatever name and title, even if they are cardinals of the holy Roman church, if they have not received the gift of consecration within three months, shall be bound to the restitution of the fruits received; if they have neglected to do this within the same number of months thereafter, let them be deprived of the churches by the law itself. Consecration, however, if it be done outside the Roman curia, let it be celebrated in the church to which they have been promoted, or in the province, if it can conveniently be done.
¶441
Ecclesiis cathedralibus seu superioribus quocumque nomine ac titulo praefecti, etiam si sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinales sint, si munus consecrationis intra tres menses non susceperint, ad fructuum perceptorum restitutionem teneantur; si intra totidem menses postea id facere neglexerint, ecclesiis ipso iure sint privati. Consecratio vero, si extra curiam Romanam fiat, in ecclesia ad quam promoti fuerint, aut in provincia, si commode fieri poterit, celebretur.
Bishops shall confer orders by themselves. But if they be hindered by illness, let them send their subjects to another bishop to be ordained, only after they have been proved and examined.
¶442
Episcopi per semetipsos ordines conferant. Quodsi aegritudine fuerint impediti, subditos suos non aliter quam iam probatos et examinatos ad alium episcopum ordinandos dimittant.
Let those not be initiated with first tonsure who have not received the sacrament of confirmation and have not been taught the rudiments of faith, and who do not know how to read and write, and of whom there is not a probable conjecture that they have chosen this kind of life not for the purpose of evading secular judgment by fraud, but to render faithful cult to God.
¶443
Prima tonsura non initientur qui sacramentum confirmationis non susceperint et fidei rudimenta edocti non fuerint, qui que legere et scribere nesciant, et de quibus probabilis coniectura non sit eos non saecularis iudicii fugiendi fraude, sed ut deo fidelem cultum praestent, hoc vitae genus elegisse.
Those to be promoted to minor orders shall have good testimony from the parish priest and from the schoolmaster in whose school they are educated. Those, however, who are to be assumed to each of the major orders shall approach the bishop a month before ordination, who shall commit to the parish priest or to another whom it shall seem more expedient to inquire diligently concerning the names, origin, age, morals, and life of those who wish to be promoted, having been publicly proposed in the church, from trustworthy persons, and shall transmit testimonial letters containing that inquisition to the bishop himself as soon as possible.
¶444
Ad minores ordines promovendi bonum a parocho et a magistro scholae in qua educantur testimonium habeant. Ii vero qui ad singulos maiores erunt assumendi per mensem ante ordinationem episcopum adeant, qui parocho aut alteri cui magis expedire videbitur committat, ut nominibus ac desiderio eorum qui volent promoveri, publice in ecclesia propositis, de ipsorum ordinandorum natalibus, aetate, moribus et vita, a fide dignis diligenter inquirat, et litteras testimoniales ipsam inquisitionem factam continentes ad ipsum episcopum quamprimum transmittat.
Let no one initiated with first tonsure or even constituted in minor orders be able to obtain a benefice before the fourteenth year. Let him also not enjoy the privilege of the forum unless he have an ecclesiastical benefice, or, bearing the clerical habit and tonsure, he serve some church at the command of the bishop, or be engaged in the seminary of clerics or in some school or university by license of the bishop as if in the way of receiving major orders.
¶445
Nullus prima tonsura initiatus aut etiam in minoribus ordinibus constitutus ante quartum decimum annum beneficium possit obtinere. Is etiam fori privilegio non gaudeat, nisi beneficium ecclesiasticum habeat, aut clericalem habitum et tonsuram deferens alicui ecclesiae ex mandato episcopi inserviat, vel in seminario clericorum aut in aliqua schola vel universitate de licentia episcopi quasi in via ad maiores ordines suscipiendos versetur.
In the case of married clerics, let the constitution of Boniface VIII which begins Clerici qui be observed, with the condition that only those clerics serve or minister to some church by the deputation of the bishop, and use the clerical habit and tonsure; no privilege or custom even immemorial availing anyone in this regard.
¶446
In clericis vero coniugatis servetur constitutio Bonifatii VIII quae incipit: "clerici qui," cum unicis modo hi clerici alicuius ecclesiae servitio vel ministerio ab episcopo deputati eidem ecclesiae serviant vel ministrent et clericali habitu et tonsura utantur; nemini quoad hoc privilegio vel consuetudine etiam immemorabili suffragante.
The holy synod, adhering to the footprints of the ancient canons, decrees that when the bishop intends to perform ordination, all who wish to approach the sacred ministry are to be summoned to the city on the Wednesday before the ordination itself, or when it shall seem good to the bishop. The bishop, however, having associated with himself priests and other prudent men skilled in the divine law and exercised in ecclesiastical sanctions, shall diligently investigate and examine the kind, person, age, instruction, morals, doctrine, and faith of those to be ordained.
¶447
Sancta synodus antiquorum canonum vestigiis inhaerendo decernit ut, quando episcopus ordinationem facere disposuerit, omnes qui ad sacrum ministerium accedere voluerint, feria quarta ante ipsam ordinationem vel quando episcopo videbitur, ad civitatem evocentur. Episcopus autem, sacerdotibus et aliis prudentibus viris peritis divinae legis ac in ecclesiasticis sanctionibus exercitatis sibi adscitis, ordinandorum genus, personam, aetatem, institutionem, mores, doctrinam et fidem diligenter investiget et examinet.
Ordinations of sacred orders shall be celebrated publicly at the times established by law and in the cathedral church, the canons of the church having been summoned and present for that purpose. But if in another place of the diocese, let the church of the place, as far as can be done, more worthy, always be approached, the local clergy being present. Moreover, let each one be ordained by his own bishop. But if he seek to be promoted by another, let this by no means be permitted to him, even under pretext of any general or special rescript or privilege, even at the established times, unless his probity and morals be commended by the testimony of his own ordinary.
¶448
Ordinationes sacrorum ordinum statutis a iure temporibus ac in cathedrali ecclesia, vocatis praesentibus que ad id ecclesiae canonicis, publice celebrentur. Si autem in alio dioecesis loco, praesente clero loci dignior quantum fieri poterit ecclesia semper adeatur. Unusquisque autem a proprio episcopo ordinetur. Quodsi quis ab alio promoveri petat, nullatenus id ei etiam cuiusvis generalis aut specialis rescripti vel privilegii praetextu, etiam statutis temporibus, permittatur, nisi eius probitas ac mores ordinarii sui testimonio commendentur.
If it be done otherwise, let the ordaining person be suspended from the collation of orders for a year, and the ordained person from the execution of the orders received as long as it shall seem expedient to his own ordinary.
¶449
Si secus fiat, ordinans a collatione ordinum per annum, et ordinatus a susceptorum ordinum exsecutione, quamdiu proprio ordinario videbitur expedire, sit suspensus.
A bishop cannot ordain his familiar, who is not his subject, unless he has stayed with him for three years and, all fraud ceasing, immediately confer a benefice on him; any custom even immemorial to the contrary notwithstanding.
¶450
Episcopus familiarem suum, non subditum, ordinare non possit, nisi per triennium se cum fuerit commoratus et beneficium, quacumque fraude cessante, statim re ipsa illi conferat; consuetudine quacumque etiam immemorabili in contrarium non obstante.
It shall not be lawful for abbots and any others, however exempt, dwelling within the boundaries of any diocese, even if they are said to be of no diocese or exempt, to confer tonsure or minor orders upon anyone who is not a regular subject to them; nor let the abbots themselves and other exempt persons, or any colleges or chapters, even of cathedral churches, grant dimissory letters to any secular clerics that they may be ordained by others; but the ordination of all these, all things contained in the decrees of this holy synod being preserved, shall pertain to the bishops within whose diocesan boundaries they exist; any privileges, prescriptions, or customs, even immemorial, notwithstanding.
¶451
Abbatibus ac aliis quibuscumque quantumvis exemptis non liceat in posterum intra fines alicuius dioecesis consistentibus, etiam si nullius dioecesis vel exempti esse dicantur, cuiquam qui regularis subditus sibi non sit tonsuram vel minores ordines conferre, nec ipsi abbates et alii exempti aut collegia vel capitula quaecumque etiam ecclesiarum cathedralium litteras dimissorias aliquibus clericis saecularibus, ut ab aliis ordinentur, concedant; sed horum omnium ordinatio, servatis omnibus quae in huius sanctae synodi decretis continentur, ad episcopos, intra quorum dioecesis fines exsistant, pertineat; non obstantibus quibusvis privilegiis, praescriptionibus aut consuetudinibus, etiam immemorabilibus.
It also commands the penalty imposed on those who, against the decree of this holy synod under Paul III, obtain dimissory letters from the chapter, the episcopal see being vacant, to be extended to those who should obtain the same letters not from the chapter but from any others succeeding in the jurisdiction of the bishop in the place of the chapter when the see is vacant. Let those granting dimissory letters contrary to the form of the decree be by the law itself suspended from office and benefice for a year.
¶452
Poenam quoque impositam his qui contra huius sanctae synodi sub Paulo III decretum a capitulo, episcopali sede vacante, litteras dimissorias impetrant, mandat extendi ad illos qui easdem litteras non a capitulo, sed ab aliis quibusvis in iurisdictione episcopi loco capituli, sede vacante, succedentibus obtinerent; concedentes autem dimissorias contra formam decreti ab officio et beneficio per annum sint ipso iure suspensi.
Minor orders shall be conferred on those who understand at least the Latin language, through intervals of time, unless it shall seem otherwise more expedient to the bishop, that they may be able to be taught how great the weight of this discipline is; and in each duty, according to the prescription of the bishop, let them exercise themselves in that church to which they shall be assigned, unless perhaps they be absent for the sake of study; and thus let them ascend from grade to grade, that with age, merit of life and greater doctrine may increase in them. Which the example of good morals, and assiduous ministry in the church, and greater reverence for priests and higher orders, and more frequent communion of the body of Christ than before, will especially approve.
¶453
Minores ordines iis qui saltem latinam linguam intelligant per temporum interstitia, nisi aliud episcopo expedire magis videretur, conferantur, ut eo accuratius quantum sit huius disciplinae pondus possint edoceri; ac in unoquoque munere iuxta praescriptum episcopi se exerceant, id que in ea cui adscripti erunt ecclesia, nisi forte ex causa studiorum absint, atque ita de gradu in gradum ascendant, ut in eis cum aetate vitae meritum et doctrina maior accrescat. Quod et bonorum morum exemplum et assiduum in ecclesia ministerium atque maior erga presbyteros et superiores ordines reverentia et crebrior quam antea corporis christi communio maxime comprobabunt.
Since from hence is the entrance to higher grades and most sacred mysteries, let no one be initiated in them whom the hope of knowledge does not show to be worthy of the greater orders.
¶454
Cum que hinc ad altiores gradus et sacratissima mysteria sit ingressus, nemo iis initietur quem non scientiae spes maioribus ordinibus dignum ostendat.
But these shall not be promoted to sacred orders until after a year from the reception of the last grade of minor orders, unless necessity or the utility of the church, in the judgment of the bishop, demand otherwise.
¶455
Hi vero non nisi post annum a susceptione postremi gradus minorum ordinum ad sacros ordines promoveantur, nisi necessitas aut ecclesiae utilitas, iudicio episcopi, aliud exposcat.
Let no one in the future be promoted to the order of subdiaconate before the twenty-second year of his age, to the diaconate before the twenty-third, to the presbyterate before the twenty-fifth. Let bishops know, however, that not all constituted in that age ought to be assumed to these orders, but only the worthy, and those whose life is proved to be aged. Regulars also shall be ordained neither at a lesser age nor without diligent examination by the bishop; any privileges whatsoever being altogether excluded in this regard.
¶456
Nullus in posterum ad subdiaconatus ordinem ante vigesimum secundum, ad diaconatus ante vigesimum tertium, ad presbyteratus ante vigesimum quintum aetatis suae annum promoveatur. Sciant tamen episcopi non singulos in ea aetate constitutos debere ad hos ordines assumi, sed dignos dumtaxat et quorum probata vita senectus sit. Regulares quoque nec in minori aetate nec sine diligenti episcopi examine ordinentur; privilegiis quibuscumque quoad hoc penitus exclusis.
Let subdeacons and deacons be ordained who have good testimony and are already proved in minor orders and instructed in letters and in those things which pertain to the exercise of the order, who hope, God being author, that they can be continent, let them serve the churches to which they shall be assigned, and know that it is most fitting that at least on the Lord's days and solemn days, if they have ministered at the altar, they receive sacred communion.
¶457
Subdiaconi et diaconi ordinentur habentes bonum testimonium et in minoribus ordinibus iam probati, ac litteris et iis quae ad ordinem exercendum pertinent instructi, qui sperent, deo auctore, se continere posse, ecclesiis quibus adscribentur inserviant, sciant que maxime decere si saltem diebus dominicis et solemnibus, cum altari ministraverint, sacram communionem perceperint.
Those promoted to the sacred order of subdiaconate, if they have not been engaged in it for at least a year, shall not be permitted to ascend to a higher grade, unless it shall seem otherwise to the bishop. Two sacred orders shall not be conferred on the same day, even on regulars; any privileges and indults whatsoever granted notwithstanding.
¶458
Promoti ad sacrum subdiaconatus ordinem, si per annum saltem in eo non sint versati, ad altiorem gradum, nisi aliud episcopo videatur, ascendere non permittantur. Duo sacri ordines non eodem die, etiam regularibus, conferantur; privilegiis ac indultis quibusvis concessis non obstantibus quibuscumque.
Those who shall have behaved piously and faithfully in the preceding ministries shall be assumed to the presbyterate order; let them have good testimony, and these are they who have not only ministered in the diaconate for at least one whole year, unless it shall seem otherwise to the bishop on account of the utility and necessity of the church, but also, a diligent examination having preceded, shall be proved suitable for teaching the people those things which are necessary for all to know for salvation and for administering the sacraments, and thus conspicuous by piety and chaste morals that a splendid example of good works and lessons of life may be able to be expected from them.
¶459
Qui pie et fideliter in ministeriis anteactis se gesserint ad presbyteratus ordinem assumantur; bonum habeant testimonium, et hi sint qui non modo in diaconatu ad minus annum integrum, nisi ob ecclesiae utilitatem ac necessitatem aliud episcopo videretur, ministraverint, sed etiam, ad populum docendum ea quae scire omnibus necessarium est ad salutem ac administranda sacramenta, diligenti examine praecedente, idonei comprobentur, atque ita pietate ac castis moribus conspicui, ut praeclarum bonorum operum exemplum et vitae monita ab eis possint exspectari.
Let the bishop take care that they celebrate Mass at least on the Lord's days and solemn feasts, but if they have care of souls, as often as they may satisfy their duty.
¶460
Curet episcopus ut ii saltem diebus dominicis et festis solemnibus, si autem curam habuerint animarum, tam frequenter ut suo muneri satisfaciant, missas celebrent.
Although presbyters receive the power of absolving from sins in their ordination, the holy synod nevertheless decrees that no one, even a regular, can hear the confessions of seculars, even of priests, nor be considered suitable for that purpose, unless he hold a parochial benefice, or be judged suitable by the bishops through examination, if they shall see it to be necessary, or otherwise, and obtain approval, which is to be given freely; any privileges and custom whatsoever, even immemorial, notwithstanding.
¶461
Quamvis presbyteri in sua ordinatione a peccatis absolvendi potestatem accipiant, decernit tamen sancta synodus nullum, etiam regularem, posse confessiones saecularium, etiam sacerdotum, audire, nec ad id idoneum reputari, nisi aut parochiale beneficium habeat, aut ab episcopis per examen, si illis videbitur esse necessarium, aut alias idoneus iudicetur et approbationem (quae gratis detur) obtineat; privilegiis et consuetudine quacumque, etiam immemorabili, non obstantibus.
Since no one ought to be ordained who in the judgment of his own bishop is not useful or necessary for his churches, the holy synod, adhering to the footprints of the sixth canon of the council of Chalcedon, establishes that let no one in the future be ordained who is not inscribed for that church or pious place for whose necessity or utility he is assumed, where he may perform his duties, nor wander about uncertain seats. But if he shall have left the place without consulting the bishop, let the exercise of sacred things be forbidden to him.
¶462
Cum nullus debeat ordinari qui iudicio sui episcopi non sit utilis aut necessarius suis ecclesiis, sancta synodus vestigiis sexti canonis concilii Chalcedonensis inhaerendo statuit ut nullus in posterum ordinetur qui illi ecclesiae aut pio loco pro cuius necessitate aut utilitate assumitur non adscribatur, ubi suis fungatur muneribus, nec incertis vagetur sedibus. Quodsi locum inconsulto episcopo deseruerit, ei sacrorum exercitium interdicatur.
Moreover, let no foreign cleric without commendatory letters from his own ordinary be admitted by any bishop to celebrate divine things and administer the sacraments.
¶463
Nullus praeterea clericus peregrinus sine commendatitiis sui ordinarii litteris ab ullo episcopo ad divina celebranda et sacramenta administranda admittatur.
That the functions of the holy orders from diaconate to ostiariate, laudably received in the church from the times of the apostles and for some time discontinued in many places, may be brought back into use according to the sacred canons, the holy synod, burning with the desire of restoring that ancient custom, decrees that in the future such ministries be exercised only by those constituted in the said orders, and it exhorts in the Lord all and each of the prelates of churches, and commands them, that as far as can conveniently be done in the cathedral, collegiate, and parochial churches of their diocese, if the people be numerous and the revenues of the church can bear it, they take care to have these functions restored, and that they assign stipends for those exercising these functions from the revenues of some simple benefices or from the fabric of the church, if the revenues suffice, or from both; for which if the ordinaries be neglectful, they may be mulcted in part or altogether deprived by judgment.
¶464
Ut sanctorum ordinum a diaconatu ad ostiariatum functiones ab apostolorum temporibus in ecclesia laudabiliter receptae et pluribus in locis aliquamdiu intermissae in usum iuxta sacros canones revocentur, sancta synodus illius prisci moris restituendi desiderio flagrans decernit ut in posterum huiuscemodi ministeria non nisi per constitutos in dictis ordinibus exerceantur, omnes que et singulos praelatos ecclesiarum in domino hortatur et illis praecipit, ut quantum fieri commode poterit in ecclesiis cathedralibus, collegiatis et parochialibus suae dioecesis, si populus frequens et ecclesiae proventus id ferre queant, huiusmodi functiones curent restituendas, et ex aliqua parte redituum aliquorum simplicium beneficiorum vel fabricae ecclesiae, si proventus suppetant, aut utriusque, illas functiones exercentibus stipendia assignent; quibus si negligentes fuerint ordinarii, iudicio aut ex parte mulctari aut in totum privari possint.
But if clerics, unmarried, be not at hand sufficient for exercising the four ministries of the minor orders, married men of proved life may also be sufficient, provided they be not bigamists, suitable for performing those duties, and who bear the tonsure and clerical habit in the church.
