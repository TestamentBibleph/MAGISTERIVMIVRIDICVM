SESSION 24 — 11 NOVEMBER 1563
*SESSIO XXIV*
*DECRETUM DE SACRAMENTO MATRIMONII*
DECREE ON THE SACRAMENT OF MATRIMONY
Matrimonii perpetuum indissolubilem que nexum primus humani generis parens, divini spiritus instinctu, pronuntiavit cum dixit: "hoc nunc os ex ossibus meis et caro de carne mea. Quamobrem relinquet homo patrem suum et matrem et adhaerebit uxori suae, et erunt duo in carne una."
But that by this bond two only are to be united and joined, our Lord Christ taught more openly when, referring to those last words as spoken by God, He said: "Therefore now they are not two, but one flesh"; and immediately He confirmed the firmness of the same bond, pronounced so long before by Adam, with these words: "What therefore God has joined together, let not man separate."
¶467
Hoc autem vinculo duos tantummodo copulari et coniungi christus dominus apertius docuit cum, postrema illa verba tamquam a deo prolata referens, dixit: "itaque iam non sunt duo, sed una caro"; statim que eiusdem nexus firmitatem ab adamo tanto ante pronuntiatam his verbis confirmavit: "Quod ergo deus coniunxit, homo non separet."
Moreover, the grace which might perfect that natural love and confirm the indissoluble union and sanctify the married persons, Christ Himself, the institutor and perfecter of the venerable sacraments, merited for us by His passion. Which the apostle Paul intimates, saying: "Men, love your wives, as Christ also loved the church and delivered Himself up for it"; soon after adding: "This sacrament is great, but I speak in Christ and in the church."
¶468
Gratiam vero quae naturalem illum amorem perficeret et indissolubilem unitatem confirmaret coniuges que sanctificaret, ipse christus, venerabilium sacramentorum institutor atque perfector, sua nobis passione promeruit. Quod Paulus apostolus innuit dicens: "viri, diligite uxores vestras, sicut christus dilexit ecclesiam et se ipsum tradidit pro ea"; mox subiungens: "sacramentum hoc magnum est; ego autem dico in christo et in ecclesia."
Since, therefore, matrimony in the evangelical law, through Christ, excels the ancient marriages in grace, deservedly our holy fathers and councils and the tradition of the universal church have always taught that it is to be numbered among the sacraments of the new law; against which impious men of this age raging not only have wrongly thought concerning this venerable sacrament, but also, introducing carnal liberty under the pretext of the gospel after their manner, have asserted in writing and word many things alien from the sense of the catholic church and from the custom approved from the time of the apostles, not without great loss of the faithful of Christ.
¶469
Cum igitur matrimonium in lege evangelica, per christum, veteribus connubiis gratia praestet, merito inter novae legis sacramenta annumerandum sancti patres nostri, concilia et universalis ecclesiae traditio semper docuerunt; adversus quam impii homines huius saeculi insanientes non solum perperam de hoc venerabili sacramento senserunt, sed de more suo, praetextu evangelii, libertatem carnis introducentes, multa ab ecclesiae catholicae sensu et ab apostolorum temporibus probata consuetudine aliena scripto et verbo asseruerunt, non sine magna christifidelium iactura.
To whose rashness the holy and universal synod, wishing to meet, has judged that the more noted heresies and errors of the aforesaid schismatics are to be exterminated, lest their pernicious contagion draw many to itself, decreeing these anathematisms against those heretics and their errors.
¶470
Quorum temeritati sancta et universalis synodus cupiens occurrere, insigniores praedictorum schismaticorum haereses et errores, ne plures ad se trahat perniciosa eorum contagio, exterminandos duxit, hos in ipsos haereticos eorum que errores decernens anathematismos.
If anyone says that matrimony is not truly and properly one of the seven sacraments of the evangelical law, instituted by Christ the Lord, but was invented by men in the church, and does not confer grace: let him be anathema.
¶471
*CANONES DE SACRAMENTO MATRIMONII*
CANONS ON THE SACRAMENT OF MATRIMONY
CANON I
Si quis dixerit matrimonium non esse vere et proprie unum ex septem legis evangelicae sacramentis, a christo domino institutum, sed ab hominibus in ecclesia inventum, neque gratiam conferre: anathema sit.
If anyone says that it is lawful for Christians to have several wives at the same time, and that this is not prohibited by any divine law: let him be anathema.
¶472
CANON II
Si quis dixerit licere christianis plures simul habere uxores, et hoc nulla lege divina esse prohibitum: anathema sit.
If anyone says that those degrees of consanguinity and affinity which are expressed in Leviticus can only impede the contracting of marriage and dissolve the contract; and that the church cannot dispense in some of those degrees or establish that more degrees impede and dissolve: let him be anathema.
¶473
CANON III
Si quis dixerit eos tantum consanguinitatis et affinitatis gradus qui Levitico exprimuntur posse impedire matrimonium contrahendum et dirimere contractum; nec posse ecclesiam in nonnullis illorum dispensare aut constituere ut plures impediant et dirimant: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the church could not establish impediments dissolving marriage, or that she has erred in establishing them: let him be anathema.
¶474
CANON IV
Si quis dixerit ecclesiam non potuisse constituere impedimenta matrimonium dirimentia, vel in iis constituendis errasse: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the bond of marriage can be dissolved on account of heresy, or troublesome cohabitation, or affected absence from the spouse: let him be anathema.
¶475
CANON V
Si quis dixerit propter haeresim aut molestam cohabitationem aut affectatam absentiam a coniuge dissolvi posse matrimonii vinculum: anathema sit.
If anyone says that a ratified but not consummated marriage is not dissolved by the solemn religious profession of either spouse: let him be anathema.
¶476
CANON VI
Si quis dixerit matrimonium ratum non consummatum per solemnem religionis professionem alterius coniugum non dirimi: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the church errs when she has taught and teaches that, according to evangelical and apostolic doctrine, the bond of marriage cannot be dissolved on account of the adultery of either spouse, and that both, or even the innocent who has given no cause for adultery, cannot contract another marriage while the other spouse is living, and that he who, having put away the adulteress, marries another, and she who, having put away the adulterer, marries another, commit adultery: let him be anathema.
¶477
CANON VII
Si quis dixerit ecclesiam errare cum docuit et docet, iuxta evangelicam et apostolicam doctrinam, propter adulterium alterius coniugum matrimonii vinculum non posse dissolvi, et utrumque vel etiam innocentem, qui causam adulterio non dedit, non posse altero coniuge vivente aliud matrimonium contrahere, moechari que eum qui dimissa adultera aliam duxerit et eam quae dimisso adultero alii nupserit: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the church errs when she decrees that for many causes separation may be made between spouses as to bed or as to cohabitation for a certain or uncertain time: let him be anathema.
¶478
CANON VIII
Si quis dixerit ecclesiam errare cum ob multas causas separationem inter coniuges quoad thorum seu quoad cohabitationem ad certum incertum ve tempus fieri posse decernit: anathema sit.
If anyone says that clerics constituted in sacred orders, or regulars who have solemnly professed chastity, can contract marriage, and that the contract is valid, the ecclesiastical law or vow notwithstanding, and that the contrary is nothing else than to condemn marriage; and that all can contract marriage who do not feel that they have the gift of chastity (even if they have vowed it): let him be anathema, since "God does not deny that which is rightly asked, nor does He suffer us to be tempted above that which we are able."
¶479
CANON IX
Si quis dixerit clericos in sacris ordinibus constitutos vel regulares castitatem solemniter professos posse matrimonium contrahere, contractum que validum esse, non obstante lege ecclesiastica vel voto, et oppositum nil aliud esse quam damnare matrimonium; posse que omnes contrahere matrimonium qui non sentiunt se castitatis (etiam si eam voverint) habere donum: anathema sit; cum "deus id recte petentibus non deneget, nec patiatur nos supra id quod possumus tentari."
If anyone says that the conjugal state is to be preferred to the state of virginity or celibacy, and that it is not better and more blessed to remain in virginity or celibacy than to be joined in marriage: let him be anathema.
¶480
CANON X
Si quis dixerit statum coniugalem anteponendum esse statui virginitatis vel coelibatus, et non esse melius ac beatius manere in virginitate aut coelibatu quam iungi matrimonio: anathema sit.
If anyone says that the prohibition of the solemnity of weddings at certain seasons of the year is a superstitious tyranny proceeding from the superstition of the heathen; or condemns the blessings and other ceremonies which the church uses in them: let him be anathema.
¶481
CANON XI
Si quis dixerit prohibitionem solemnitatis nuptiarum certis anni temporibus superstitionem esse tyrannicam ab ethnicorum superstitione profectam; aut benedictiones et alias caeremonias quibus ecclesia in illis utitur damnaverit: anathema sit.
If anyone says that matrimonial causes do not pertain to ecclesiastical judges: let him be anathema.
¶482
CANON XII
Si quis dixerit causas matrimoniales non spectare ad iudices ecclesiasticos: anathema sit.
Although it is not to be doubted that clandestine marriages, made with the free consent of those contracting, are true and real marriages as long as the church has not made them void, and therefore that those are rightly to be condemned, as the holy synod condemns them with anathema, who deny that they are true and real, and who falsely affirm that marriages contracted by children of the family without the consent of their parents are void, and that parents can make them void or valid; nevertheless holy church of God has always detested and prohibited them for most just reasons.
¶483
*DECRETUM DE REFORMATIONE MATRIMONII*
DECREE ON THE REFORMATION OF MARRIAGE
Tametsi dubitandum non est clandestina matrimonia, libero contrahentium consensu facta, rata et vera esse matrimonia, quamdiu ecclesia ea irrita non fecit, et proinde iure sint damnandi illi, ut eos sancta synodus anathemate damnat, qui ea vera ac rata esse negant, qui que falso affirmant matrimonia a filiis familias sine consensu parentum contracta irrita esse et parentes ea rata vel irrita facere posse; nihilominus sancta dei ecclesia ex iustissimis causis illa semper detestata est atque prohibuit.
But since the holy synod perceives that those prohibitions, on account of the disobedience of men, are now no longer profitable, and considers the grave sins which arise from the same clandestine marriages, especially of those who remain in a state of damnation while, having left the former wife with whom they had contracted clandestinely, they contract openly with another and live with her in perpetual adultery; to which evil, since the church does not judge of hidden things, it cannot be succored unless some more efficacious remedy be applied, therefore, adhering to the footsteps of the sacred Lateran council celebrated under Innocent III, it commands that in the future, before a marriage is contracted, it be announced publicly three times by the proper parish priest of those contracting, on three continuous feast days, during the solemnities of the mass, between whom the marriage is to be contracted; which announcements having been made, if no legitimate impediment be opposed, then let the celebration of the marriage proceed before the face of the church, where the parish priest, the man and woman having been interrogated and their mutual consent understood, shall either say: "I join you in matrimony in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit," or shall use other words according to the received rite of each province.
¶484
Verum cum sancta synodus animadvertat prohibitiones illas propter hominum inobedientiam iam non prodesse, et gravia peccata perpendat quae ex eisdem clandestinis coniugiis ortum habent, praesertim vero eorum qui in statu damnationis permanent dum priore uxore cum qua clam contraxerant relicta cum alia palam contrahunt et cum ea in perpetuo adulterio vivunt; cui malo cum ab ecclesia (quae de occultis non iudicat) succurri non possit nisi efficacius aliquod remedium adhibeatur, idcirco sacri Lateranensis concilii sub Innocentio III celebrati vestigiis inhaerendo praecipit ut in posterum, antequam matrimonium contrahatur, ter a proprio contrahentium parocho, tribus continuis diebus festivis in ecclesia inter missarum solemnia publice denuntietur inter quos matrimonium sit contrahendum; quibus denuntiationibus factis, si nullum legitimum opponatur impedimentum, ad celebrationem matrimonii in facie ecclesiae procedatur, ubi parochus, viro et muliere interrogatis et eorum mutuo consensu intellecto, vel dicat: "ego vos in matrimonium coniungo in nomine patris et filii et spiritus sancti," vel aliis utatur verbis iuxta receptum uniuscuiusque provinciae ritum.
But if at any time there be a probable suspicion that the marriage might be maliciously impeded if so many announcements preceded, then let either only one announcement be made, or at least let the marriage be celebrated in the presence of the parish priest and two or three witnesses; then before its consummation let announcements be made in the church, that if any impediments subsist they may be more easily discovered, unless the ordinary himself shall have judged that the aforesaid announcements are to be omitted, which the holy synod leaves to his prudence and judgment.
¶485
Quodsi aliquando probabilis fuerit suspicio matrimonium malitiose impediri posse si tot praecesserint denuntiationes, tunc vel una tantum denuntiatio fiat, vel saltem parocho et duobus vel tribus testibus praesentibus matrimonium celebretur; deinde ante illius consummationem denuntiationes in ecclesia fiant, ut si aliqua subsunt impedimenta facilius detegantur, nisi ordinarius ipse expedire iudicaverit ut praedictae denuntiationes remittantur, quod illius prudentiae et iudicio sancta synodus relinquit.
Those who shall attempt to contract marriage otherwise than in the presence of the parish priest or of another priest with the license of the parish priest or of the ordinary, and of two or three witnesses, the holy synod renders altogether incapable of so contracting, and decrees that such contracts are void and null, just as by the present decree it makes them void and annuls them. Moreover, it commands that the parish priest or other priest who shall have assisted with a smaller number of witnesses, and the witnesses who shall have been present at such a contract without the parish priest or other priest, and also the contracting parties themselves, be severely punished at the discretion of the ordinary.
¶486
Qui aliter quam praesente parocho vel alio sacerdote de ipsius parochi seu ordinarii licentia et duobus vel tribus testibus matrimonium contrahere attentabunt, eos sancta synodus ad sic contrahendum omnino inhabiles reddit, et huiusmodi contractus irritos et nullos esse decernit, prout eos praesenti decreto irritos facit et annullat. Insuper parochum vel alium sacerdotem qui cum minori testium numero, et testes qui sine parocho vel sacerdote huiusmodi contractui interfuerint, necnon ipsos contrahentes, graviter arbitrio ordinarii puniri praecipit.
Moreover, the same holy synod exhorts that spouses do not cohabit in the same house before the priestly blessing is received in the temple. And it establishes that the blessing be done by the proper parish priest, nor can license for making the aforesaid blessing be granted to another priest by anyone except by the same parish priest or by the ordinary, any custom even immemorial (which is rather to be called a corruption) or privilege notwithstanding. But if any parish priest or other priest, whether regular or secular, even if he contend that it is lawful for him by privilege or immemorial custom, shall have dared to join in marriage or bless the betrothed of another parish without the license of their parish priest, let him remain suspended by the law itself until he be absolved by the ordinary of that parish priest who ought to have been present at the marriage or from whom the blessing ought to have been received.
¶487
Praeterea eadem sancta synodus hortatur ut coniuges ante benedictionem sacerdotalem in templo suscipiendam in eadem domo non cohabitent. Statuit que benedictionem a proprio parocho fieri, neque a quoquam nisi ab ipso parocho vel ab ordinario licentiam ad praedictam benedictionem faciendam alii sacerdoti concedi posse, quacumque consuetudine etiam immemorabili (quae potius corruptela dicenda est) vel privilegio non obstante. Quodsi quis parochus vel alius sacerdos, sive regularis sive saecularis sit, etiam si id sibi ex privilegio vel immemorabili consuetudine licere contendat, alterius parochiae sponsos sine illorum parochi licentia matrimonio coniungere aut benedicere ausus fuerit, ipso iure tamdiu suspensus maneat, quamdiu ab ordinario eius parochi qui matrimonio interesse debebat seu a quo benedictio suscipienda erat absolvatur.
Let the parish priest have a book in which he describes the names of the spouses and witnesses and the day and place of the contracted marriage, which he carefully keeps with himself.
¶488
Habeat parochus librum in quo coniugum et testium nomina diem que et locum contracti matrimonii describat, quem diligenter apud se custodiat.
Finally, the holy synod exhorts spouses that, before they contract, or at least three days before the consummation of the marriage, they diligently confess their sins and piously approach the most holy sacrament of the Eucharist.
¶489
Postremo sancta synodus coniuges hortatur, ut antequam contrahant, vel saltem triduo ante matrimonii consummationem, sua peccata diligenter confiteantur et ad sanctissimum eucharistiae sacramentum pie accedant.
If any provinces use other laudable customs and ceremonies in this matter besides the aforesaid, the holy synod earnestly desires that they be altogether retained.
¶490
Si quae provinciae aliis ultra praedictas laudabilibus consuetudinibus et caeremoniis hac in re utuntur, eas omnino retineri sancta synodus vehementer optat.
Lest, however, these so salutary precepts escape anyone, it commands all ordinaries that as soon as they can they take care to have this decree published and explained to the people in each of the parish churches of their own diocese, and that this be done as often as possible in the first year, then indeed as often as they shall see expedient. Moreover, it decrees that such a decree in each parish begin to have its force after thirty days from the day of the first publication made in that parish.
¶491
Ne vero haec tam salubria praecepta quemquam lateant, ordinariis omnibus praecipit ut cum primum potuerint curent hoc decretum populo publicari ac explicari in singulis suarum dioecesum parochialibus ecclesiis, id que in primo anno quam saepissime fiat, deinde vero quoties expedire viderint. Decernit insuper ut huiusmodi decretum in unaquaque parochia suum robur post triginta dies habere incipiat a die primae publicationis in eadem parochia factae numerandos.
Experience teaches that on account of the multitude of prohibitions, marriages are often ignorantly contracted in prohibited cases, in which either they are continued not without great sin, or they are dissolved not without great scandal. Therefore the holy synod, wishing to provide for this disadvantage, and beginning from the impediment of spiritual relationship, establishes that only one, whether man or woman, or at most one and one woman, according to the institutes of the sacred canons, receive the baptized person from baptism, between whom and the baptized person himself and his father and mother, and also between the baptizer and the baptized person and the father and mother of the baptized person, only spiritual relationship be contracted.
¶492
Docet experientia propter multitudinem prohibitionum multoties in casibus prohibitis ignoranter contrahi matrimonia, in quibus vel non sine magno peccato perseveratur, vel ea non sine magno scandalo dirimuntur. Volens itaque sancta synodus huic incommodo providere et a cognationis spiritualis impedimento incipiens, statuit ut unus tantum sive vir sive mulier, iuxta sacrorum canonum instituta, vel ad summum unus et una, baptizatum de baptismo suscipiant, inter quos ac baptizatum ipsum et illius patrem et matrem, necnon inter baptizantem et baptizatum baptizati que patrem ac matrem tantum spiritualis cognatio contrahatur.
Let the parish priest, before he proceeds to confer baptism, diligently inquire of those to whom it pertains whom they have chosen to receive the baptized person from the sacred font, and let him admit only him or them to receive him, and let him describe their names in the book, and teach them what relationship they have contracted, that they may be able to excuse themselves by no ignorance. But if others beyond those designated have touched the baptized person, let them by no means contract spiritual relationship; the constitutions to the contrary notwithstanding. If it shall have been done otherwise through the fault or negligence of the parish priest, let him be punished at the discretion of the ordinary.
¶493
Parochus antequam ad baptismum conferendum accedat, diligenter ab iis ad quos spectabit sciscitetur quem vel quos elegerint ut baptizatum de sacro fonte suscipiant, et eum vel eos tantum ad illum suscipiendum admittat, et in libro eorum nomina describat, doceat que eos quam cognationem contraxerint, ne ignorantia ulla excusari valeant. Quodsi alii ultra designatos baptizatum tetigerint, cognationem spiritualem nullo pacto contrahant; constitutionibus in contrarium facientibus non obstantibus. Si parochi culpa vel negligentia secus factum fuerit, arbitrio ordinarii puniatur.
Let that relationship also which is contracted from confirmation not extend beyond the confirmator and the confirmed and his father and mother and the holder; all impediments of this spiritual relationship between other persons being altogether removed.
¶494
Ea quoque cognatio quae ex confirmatione contrahitur confirmantem et confirmatum illius que patrem et matrem ac tenentem non egrediatur; omnibus inter alias personas huius spiritualis cognationis impedimentis omnino sublatis.
The impediment of public honesty, where the espousals in whatever way shall not be valid, the holy synod altogether removes; where they shall be valid, let it not exceed the first degree, because in further degrees such a prohibition cannot be observed without loss.
¶495
Iustitiae publicae honestatis impedimentum, ubi sponsalia quacumque ratione valida non erunt, sancta synodus prorsus tollit; ubi autem valida fuerint, primum gradum non excedat, quoniam in ulterioribus gradibus iam non potest huiusmodi prohibitio absque dispendio observari.
Moreover, the holy synod, induced by these and other most grave causes, restricts the impediment which is introduced by reason of affinity contracted from fornication and dissolves a subsequent marriage, to those only who are joined in the first and second degree; but in further degrees it establishes that such affinity does not dissolve a subsequently contracted marriage.
¶496
Praeterea sancta synodus eisdem et aliis gravissimis de causis adducta impedimentum quod propter affinitatem ex fornicatione contractam inducitur et matrimonium postea factum dirimit, ad eos tantum qui in primo et secundo gradu coniunguntur restringit; in ulterioribus vero gradibus statuit huiusmodi affinitatem matrimonium postea contractum non dirimere.
If anyone shall have knowingly presumed to contract marriage within the prohibited degrees, let him be separated, and let him lack the hope of obtaining a dispensation; and let this take place much more in him who shall have dared not only to contract marriage but also to consummate it. But if he shall have done this ignorantly, if indeed he neglected the required solemnities in contracting marriage, let him be subject to the same penalties. For he is not worthy easily to experience the benignity of the church who has rashly despised her salutary precepts.
¶497
Si quis intra gradus prohibitos scienter matrimonium contrahere praesumpserit, separetur, ac spe dispensationis consequendae careat, id que in eo multo magis locum habeat qui non tantum matrimonium contrahere sed etiam consummare ausus fuerit. Quodsi ignoranter id fecerit, siquidem solemnitates requisitas in contrahendo matrimonio neglexerit, eisdem subiiciatur poenis. Non enim dignus est qui ecclesiae benignitatem facile experiatur cuius salubria praecepta temere contempsit.
If, however, the solemnities having been employed, some impediment be afterwards known to subsist of which he had probable ignorance, then dispensation can be more easily and freely granted with him.
¶498
Si vero solemnitatibus adhibitis impedimentum aliquod postea subesse cognoscatur, cuius ille probabilem ignorantiam habuit, tunc facilius cum eo et gratis dispensari poterit.
In contracting marriages, let either no dispensation be given at all, or rarely, and that for cause and freely. In the second degree, let it never be dispensed except between great princes and on account of a public cause.
¶499
In contrahendis matrimoniis vel nulla omnino detur dispensatio, vel raro, id que ex causa et gratis concedatur. In secundo gradu numquam dispensetur, nisi inter magnos principes et ob publicam causam.
The holy synod decrees that between a ravisher and the one ravished, as long as she remains in the power of the ravisher, no marriage can exist. But if the one ravished, separated from the ravisher and placed in a safe and free place, shall consent to have him as a husband, let the ravisher have her as a wife; and nevertheless the ravisher himself and all those who gave him counsel, aid, and favor shall be by the law itself excommunicated and perpetually infamous and incapable of all dignities. And if they be clerics, let them fall from their own grade. Moreover, let the ravisher be bound decently to endow the woman ravished, whether he has taken her as a wife or not, at the discretion of the judge.
¶500
Decernit sancta synodus inter raptorem et raptam, quamdiu ipsa in potestate raptoris manserit, nullum posse consistere matrimonium. Quodsi rapta a raptore separata et in loco tuto et libero constituta illum in virum habere consenserit, eam raptor in uxorem habeat; et nihilominus raptor ipse ac omnes illi consilium, auxilium et favorem praebentes sint ipso iure excommunicati ac perpetuo infames, omnium que dignitatum incapaces. Et si clerici fuerint, de proprio gradu decidant. Teneatur praeterea raptor mulierem raptam, sive eam in uxorem duxerit sive non, decenter arbitrio iudicis dotare.
Many are they who wander about and have uncertain abodes, and, being of wicked ingenuity, having left the first wife, take another, and often several, while she is living, in different places; to which evil the holy synod, wishing to meet, paternally warns all to whom it pertains not to easily receive this kind of wandering men to marriage. It also exhorts secular magistrates to restrain them severely; and commands parish priests not to be present at their marriages unless they shall have first made a diligent inquisition, and, the matter having been referred to the ordinary, have obtained license from him for doing this.
¶501
Multi sunt qui vagantur et incertas habent sedes, et ut improbi sunt ingenii, prima uxore relicta, aliam et plerumque plures illa vivente diversis in locis ducunt; cui morbo cupiens sancta synodus occurrere, omnes ad quos spectat paterne monet ne hoc genus hominum vagantium ad matrimonium facile recipiant. Magistratus etiam saeculares hortatur ut eos severe coerceant; parochis autem praecipit ne illorum matrimoniis intersint, nisi prius diligentem inquisitionem fecerint, et re ad ordinarium delata, ab eo licentiam id faciendi obtinuerint.
It is a grave sin for unmarried men to have concubines, but most grave, and committed in singular contempt of this great sacrament, also for married men to live in this state of damnation, and to dare sometimes to keep and retain them at home even with their wives. Wherefore, that the holy synod may provide opportune remedies for so great an evil, it establishes that such concubinarians, both unmarried and married, of whatever state, dignity, and condition, if after having been three times admonished by the ordinary, even ex officio, concerning this matter, they have not ejected their concubines and separated themselves from their association, they are to be struck with excommunication, from which they are not to be absolved until they have really obeyed the admonition given. But if they shall have remained in concubinage for a year, the censures having been neglected, let them be proceeded against severely by the ordinary according to the quality of the crime.
¶502
Grave peccatum est homines solutos concubinas habere, gravissimum vero et in huius magni sacramenti singularem contemptum admissum, uxoratos quoque in hoc damnationis statu vivere, ac audere eas quandoque domi etiam cum uxoribus alere et retinere. Quare, ut huic tanto malo sancta synodus opportunis remediis provideat, statuit huiusmodi concubinarios, tam solutos quam uxoratos, cuiuscumque status, dignitatis et conditionis exsistant, si postquam ab ordinario etiam ex officio ter admoniti ea de re fuerint, concubinas non eiecerint se que ab earum consuetudine non seiunxerint, excommunicatione feriendos esse, a qua non absolvantur donec re ipsa admonitioni factae paruerint. Quodsi in concubinatu per annum censuris neglectis permanserint, contra eos ab ordinario severe pro qualitate criminis procedatur.
Women, whether married or unmarried, who live publicly with adulterers or concubinaries, if after being three times admonished they have not obeyed, are to be severely punished by the ordinaries of places, even without anyone requiring it, ex officio, according to the measure of the fault, and to be expelled out of the town or diocese if it shall seem good to the same ordinaries, the secular arm having been invoked if necessary; other penalties inflicted against adulterers and concubinarians remaining in their force.
¶503
Mulieres, sive coniugatae sive solutae, quae cum adulteris seu concubinariis publice vivunt, si ter admonitae non paruerint, ab ordinariis locorum, nullo etiam requirente, ex officio, graviter pro modo culpae puniantur, et extra oppidum vel dioecesim, si id eisdem ordinariis videbitur, invocato si opus fuerit brachio saeculari, eiiciantur; aliis poenis contra adulteros et concubinarios inflictis in suo robore permanentibus.
The earthly affections and cupidities of temporal lords and magistrates so often blind the eyes of their minds that by threats and penalties they compel men and women living under their jurisdiction, especially the rich or those having hope of a great inheritance, to contract marriage unwillingly with those whom the lords or magistrates themselves have prescribed to them. Wherefore, since it is most wicked to violate the liberty of marriage, and injuries arise from those from whom rights are expected, the holy synod commands all, of whatsoever grade, dignity, and condition, under penalty of anathema which they incur by the very act, that they do not in any way, directly or indirectly, compel their subjects or any others so that they do not freely contract marriage.
¶504
Ita plerumque temporalium dominorum ac magistratuum mentis oculos terreni affectus atque cupiditates excaecant, ut viros et mulieres sub eorum iurisdictione degentes, maxime divites vel spem magnae haereditatis habentes, minis et poenis adigant cum iis matrimonium invitos contrahere quos ipsi domini vel magistratus illis praescripserint. Quare, cum maxime nefarium sit matrimonii libertatem violare et ab eis iniurias nasci a quibus iura exspectantur, praecipit sancta synodus omnibus, cuiuscumque gradus, dignitatis et conditionis exsistant, sub anathematis poena, quam ipso facto incurrant, ne quovis modo directe vel indirecte subditos suos vel quoscumque alios cogant quominus libere matrimonia contrahant.
From the advent of our Lord Jesus Christ until the day of Epiphany, and from Ash Wednesday until the octave of Easter inclusive, the holy synod commands that the ancient prohibitions of solemn weddings be diligently observed by all. In other times, however, it permits weddings to be solemnly celebrated, which bishops shall take care to be done with the modesty and honesty which befits. For matrimony is a holy thing and is to be treated holily.
¶505
Ab adventu domini nostri Iesu christi usque in diem epiphaniae, et a feria quarta cinerum usque in octavam paschatis inclusive, antiquas solemnium nuptiarum prohibitiones diligenter ab omnibus observari sancta synodus praecipit. In aliis vero temporibus nuptias solemniter celebrari permittit, quas episcopi, ut ea qua decet modestia et honestate fiant, curabunt. Sancta enim res est matrimonium et sancte tractandum.
Since the catholic church, taught by the Holy Spirit, from the sacred scriptures and the ancient tradition of the fathers, in sacred councils and lastly in this oecumenical synod, has taught that there is a purgatory, and that the souls detained there are helped by the suffrages of the faithful, especially by the acceptable sacrifice of the altar, the holy synod commands bishops that they diligently strive that the sound doctrine concerning purgatory, handed down by the holy fathers and sacred councils, be believed, held, taught, and everywhere preached by the faithful of Christ.

SESSION 25 — 3-4 DECEMBER 1563
*SESSIO XXV*
DECREE CONCERNING PURGATORY
*DECRETUM DE PURGATORIO*
Cum catholica ecclesia, spiritu sancto edocta, ex sacris litteris et antiqua patrum traditione in sacris conciliis et novissime in hac oecumenica synodo docuerit purgatorium esse, animas que ibi detentas fidelium suffragiis, potissimum vero acceptabili altaris sacrificio, iuvari, praecipit sancta synodus episcopis ut sanam de purgatorio doctrinam a sanctis patribus et sacris conciliis traditam a christifidelibus credi, teneri, doceri et ubique praedicari diligenter studeant.
But the more difficult and subtle questions, which do not make for edification and from which there is often no increase of piety, are to be excluded from popular sermons. Likewise, uncertain matters, or those which labor under the appearance of falsehood, are not to be published or treated. Those, however, which pertain to a certain curiosity or superstition or savor of filthy gain, as scandals and offenses of the faithful, let them forbid.
¶507
Apud rudem vero plebem difficiliores ac subtiliores quaestiones, quaeque ad aedificationem non faciunt et ex quibus plerumque nulla fit pietatis accessio, a popularibus concionibus secludantur. Incerta item vel quae specie falsi laborant, evulgari ac tractari non permittantur. Ea vero quae ad curiositatem quandam aut superstitionem spectant vel turpe lucrum sapiunt, tamquam scandala et fidelium offendicula, prohibeant.
Moreover, let bishops take care that the suffrages of the living faithful, namely the sacrifices of masses, prayers, alms, and other works of piety which have been accustomed to be performed by the faithful for other faithful departed, be done piously and devoutly according to the institutes of the church, and that those things which are owed for them from the foundations of testators or by any other reason be performed not perfunctorily but diligently and accurately by priests and the ministers of the church and by others who are bound to perform this.
¶508
Curent autem episcopi ut fidelium vivorum suffragia, missarum scilicet sacrificia, orationes, eleemosynae, alia que pietatis opera quae a fidelibus pro aliis fidelibus defunctis fieri consueverunt, secundum ecclesiae instituta pie et devote fiant, et quae pro illis ex testatorum fundationibus vel alia ratione debentur, non perfunctorie, sed a sacerdotibus et ecclesiae ministris et aliis qui hoc praestare tenentur, diligenter et accurate persolvantur.
The holy synod commands all bishops and others sustaining the office and care of teaching that, according to the use of the catholic and apostolic church received from the primitive times of the Christian religion, and according to the consent of the holy fathers and the decrees of the sacred councils, they above all diligently instruct the faithful concerning the intercession and invocation of saints, the honor of relics, and the legitimate use of images, teaching them that the saints, reigning together with Christ, offer their prayers to God for men; that it is good and useful to invoke them suppliantly, and to flee to their prayers, help, and assistance for the sake of obtaining benefits from God through His Son Jesus Christ our Lord, who alone is our Redeemer and Savior; that those who deny that the saints, enjoying eternal happiness in heaven, are to be invoked, or who assert either that they do not pray for men, or that their invocation for us to pray even for each individually is idolatry, or that it is contrary to the word of God and opposes the honor of the one mediator of God and men, Jesus Christ, or that it is foolish to supplicate with voice or mind those who reign in heaven: think impiously.
¶509
*DECRETUM DE INVOCATIONE, VENERATIONE ET RELIQUIIS SANCTORUM, ET DE SACRIS IMAGINIBUS*
DECREE CONCERNING THE INVOCATION, VENERATION, AND RELICS OF SAINTS, AND CONCERNING SACRED IMAGES
Mandat sancta synodus omnibus episcopis et ceteris docendi munus curam que sustinentibus, ut iuxta catholicae et apostolicae ecclesiae usum a primaevis christianae religionis temporibus receptum, sanctorum que patrum consensionem et sacrorum conciliorum decreta, in primis de sanctorum intercessione, invocatione, reliquiarum honore et legitimo imaginum usu fideles diligenter instruant, docentes eos sanctos, una cum christo regnantes, orationes suas pro hominibus deo offerre; bonum atque utile esse suppliciter eos invocare et ob beneficia impetranda a deo per filium eius Iesum christum dominum nostrum, qui solus noster redemptor et salvator est, ad eorum orationes, opem, auxilium que confugere; illos vero qui negant sanctos aeterna felicitate in coelo fruentes invocandos esse, aut qui asserunt vel illos pro hominibus non orare, vel eorum ut pro nobis etiam singulis orent invocationem esse idolatriam, vel pugnare cum verbo dei adversari que honori unius mediatoris dei et hominum Iesu christi, vel stultum esse in coelo regnantibus voce vel mente supplicare: impie sentire.
That the holy bodies of holy martyrs and of others living with Christ, which were the living members of Christ and the temple of the Holy Spirit, are to be venerated by the faithful, through which many benefits are bestowed by God on men, so that those who affirm that veneration and honor are not owed to the relics of the saints, or that they are uselessly honored by the faithful, and that the memorials of the saints are vainly frequented for the sake of obtaining their help: are altogether to be condemned, just as the church has long since condemned them and now also condemns them.
¶510
Sanctorum quoque martyrum et aliorum cum christo viventium sancta corpora, quae viva membra fuerunt christi et templum spiritus sancti, ab ipso ad aeternam vitam suscitanda et glorificanda, a fidelibus veneranda esse, per quae multa beneficia a deo hominibus praestantur, ita ut affirmantes sanctorum reliquiis venerationem atque honorem non deberi, vel eas alia que sacra monumenta a fidelibus inutiliter honorari atque eorum opis impetrandae causa sanctorum memorias frustra frequentari: omnino damnandos esse, prout iampridem eos damnavit et nunc etiam damnat ecclesia.
Moreover, that the images of Christ, of the Virgin Mother of God, and of the other saints are to be had and retained especially in temples, and that due honor and veneration is to be imparted to them, not that any divinity or virtue is believed to be in them, on account of which they are to be worshipped, or that something is to be asked from them, or that trust is to be placed in images, as was once done by the gentiles who placed their hope in idols; but because the honor which is shown to them is referred to the prototypes which they represent, so that through the images, which we kiss and before which we uncover our heads and prostrate ourselves, we adore Christ and venerate the saints whose likenesses they bear. That which was established by the decrees of councils, especially of the second Nicene synod, against the opponents of images.
¶511
Imagines porro christi, deiparae virginis et aliorum sanctorum in templis praesertim habendas et retinendas, eis que debitum honorem et venerationem impertiendam, non quod credatur inesse aliqua in iis divinitas vel virtus propter quam sint colendae, vel quod ab eis sit aliquid petendum, vel quod fiducia in imaginibus sit figenda, veluti olim fiebat a gentibus quae in idolis spem suam collocabant; sed quoniam honos qui eis exhibetur refertur ad prototypa quae illae repraesentant, ita ut per imagines, quas osculamur et coram quibus caput aperimus et procumbimus, christum adoremus et sanctos, quorum illae similitudinem gerunt, veneremur. Id quod conciliorum, praesertim vero secundae Nicaenae synodi, decretis contra imaginum oppugnatores est sancitum.
Moreover, let the bishops diligently teach that by the histories of the mysteries of our redemption, expressed in paintings or other likenesses, the people are instructed and confirmed in remembering and assiduously recollecting the articles of faith; then, that great fruit is perceived from all sacred images, not only because the people are reminded of the benefits and gifts which have been bestowed upon them by Christ, but also because the miracles of God and the salutary examples of the saints are set before the eyes of the faithful, so that for them they may give thanks to God, may compose their own life and manners to the imitation of the saints, and may be excited to adore and love God and to cultivate piety.
¶512
Illud vero diligenter doceant episcopi per historias mysteriorum nostrae redemptionis, picturis vel aliis similitudinibus expressas, erudiri et confirmari populum in articulis fidei commemorandis et assidue recolendis; tum vero ex omnibus sacris imaginibus magnum fructum percipi non solum quia admonetur populus beneficiorum et munerum quae a christo sibi collata sunt, sed etiam quia dei per sanctos miracula et salutaria exempla oculis fidelium subiiciuntur, ut pro iis deo gratias agant, ad sanctorum que imitationem vitam mores que suos componant, excitentur que ad adorandum ac diligendum deum et ad pietatem colendam.
If anyone, however, shall have taught or thought contrary to these decrees: let him be anathema.
¶513
Si quis autem his decretis contraria docuerit aut senserit: anathema sit.
But if any abuses have crept into these holy and salutary observances, the holy synod earnestly desires that they be altogether abolished, so that no images of false dogma and giving occasion of dangerous error to the rude be set up.
¶514
In has autem sanctas et salutares observationes si qui abusus irrepserint, eos prorsus aboleri sancta synodus vehementer cupit, ita ut nullae falsi dogmatis imagines et rudibus periculosi erroris occasionem praebentes statuantur.
But if sometimes it happens that histories and narratives of sacred scripture, when it will be expedient for the unlearned people, are expressed and figured, let the people be taught that for this reason the divinity is not figured as if it can be seen with bodily eyes or expressed by colors or figures.
¶515
Quodsi aliquando historias et narrationes sacrae scripturae, cum id indoctae plebi expediet, exprimi et figurari contigerit, doceatur populus non propterea divinitatem figurari quasi corporeis oculis conspici vel coloribus aut figuris exprimi possit.
Moreover, let all superstition be removed in the invocation of saints, the veneration of relics, and the sacred use of images; let all filthy gain be eliminated; finally, let all lasciviousness be avoided, so that images be not painted nor adorned with provocative beauty; and that men not abuse the celebration of saints and the visitation of relics to feasts and drunkenness, as if feast days in honor of the saints are to be spent in luxury and lasciviousness.
¶516
Omnis porro superstitio in sanctorum invocatione, reliquiarum veneratione et imaginum sacro usu tollatur; omnis turpis quaestus eliminetur; omnis denique lascivia vitetur, ita ut procaci venustate imagines non pingantur nec ornentur; et sanctorum celebratione ac reliquiarum visitatione homines ad commessationes atque ebrietates non abutantur, quasi festi dies in honorem sanctorum per luxum ac lasciviam agantur.
Finally, so great diligence and care is to be applied by bishops concerning these matters, that nothing disordered, or preposterously and tumultuously accommodated, nothing profane, nothing dishonest appear, since holiness befits the house of God.
¶517
Postremo tanta circa haec diligentia et cura ab episcopis adhibeatur, ut nihil inordinatum aut praepostere et tumultuarie accommodatum, nihil profanum, nihil inhonestum appareat, cum domum dei deceat sanctitudo.
So that these things may be more faithfully observed, the holy synod establishes that it is lawful for no one in any place or church, however exempt, to place or cause to be placed any unusual image, unless it has been approved by the bishop. Moreover, that no new miracles be admitted, nor new relics received, unless the same bishop recognizing and approving. Who as soon as he has discovered anything concerning these, having taken into counsel theologians and other pious men, let him do those things which he shall have judged to be consonant with truth and piety.
¶518
Haec ut fidelius observentur, statuit sancta synodus nemini licere ullo in loco vel ecclesia, etiam quomodolibet exempta, ullam insolitam ponere vel ponendam curare imaginem, nisi ab episcopo approbata fuerit. Nulla etiam admittenda esse nova miracula, nec novas reliquias recipiendas nisi eodem recognoscente et approbante episcopo. Qui simulatque de his aliquid compertum habuerit, adhibitis in consilium theologis et aliis piis viris, ea faciat quae veritati et pietati consentanea iudicaverit.
But if any doubtful or difficult abuse is to be extirpated, or absolutely any more grave question concerning these matters shall arise, let the bishop, before he resolves the controversy, await the opinion of the metropolitan and of the suffragan bishops in the provincial council, yet in such a way that nothing new or hitherto unusual in the church be decreed without the most holy Roman pontiff having been consulted.
¶519
Quodsi aliquis dubius aut difficilis abusus sit exstirpandus, vel omnino aliqua de his rebus gravior quaestio incidat, episcopus antequam controversiam dirimat, metropolitani et comprovincialium episcoporum in concilio provinciali sententiam exspectet; ita tamen ut nihil inconsulto sanctissimo romano pontifice novum aut in ecclesia hactenus inusitatum decernatur.
The same sacred synod, pursuing the matter of reformation, has judged that the following are to be established in the present session.
¶520
*DECRETUM DE REFORMATIONE*
DECREE ON THE REFORMATION
Eadem sacrosancta synodus reformationem prosequens, ea quae sequuntur statuenda esse censuit.
Since the suspicion of all avarice ought to be absent from the ecclesiastical order, let bishops and other ordainers, or their ministers, receive nothing for the conferral of any orders whatsoever, even of clerical tonsure, nor for dimissory or testimonial letters, nor for seal, nor for any other cause, even voluntarily offered, under any pretext.
¶521
CANON I
Quoniam ab ecclesiastico ordine omnis avaritiae suspicio abesse debet, nihil pro collatione quorumcumque ordinum, etiam clericalis tonsurae, nec pro litteris dimissoriis aut testimonialibus, nec pro sigillo, nec alia quacumque de causa, etiam sponte oblatum, episcopi et alii ordinum collatores aut eorum ministri quovis praetextu accipiant.
Notaries, however, in those places only in which there does not prevail the laudable custom of receiving nothing, may receive for each dimissory or testimonial letter only a tenth part of a gold coin, provided that no salary be established for them for exercising their office, nor can any emolument from the profits of the notaries for the same conferrals of orders come directly or indirectly to the bishop.
¶522
Notarii vero in his tantum locis in quibus non viget laudabilis consuetudo nihil accipiendi, pro singulis litteris dimissoriis aut testimonialibus decimam tantum unius aurei partem accipere possint, dummodo eis nullum salarium sit constitutum pro officio exercendo, nec episcopo ex notarii commodis aliquod emolumentum ex eisdem ordinum collationibus directe vel indirecte provenire possit.
For then it decrees that they are altogether bound to give their work freely, altogether nullifying and interdicting contrary taxes, statutes, and customs even immemorial of any places, which can rather be called abuses and favorers of simoniacal depravity. And those who shall have done otherwise, both givers and receivers, beyond divine vengeance, shall incur by the very fact the penalties inflicted by law.
¶523
Tunc enim gratis operam suam eos praestare omnino teneri decernit, contrarias taxas, ac statuta et consuetudines, etiam immemorabiles, quorumcumque locorum, quae potius abusus et corruptelae simoniacae pravitati faventes nuncupari possunt, penitus cassando et interdicendo. Et qui secus fecerint, tam dantes quam accipientes, ultra divinam ultionem, poenas a iure inflictas ipso facto incurrant.
Since it is not fitting that those who are enrolled in divine ministry should beg with disgrace to their order or practice any sordid gain, and it is known that in many places very many are admitted to sacred orders with almost no discrimination, who by various arts and deceits feign that they obtain an ecclesiastical benefice or also suitable resources, the holy synod establishes that henceforth no secular cleric, however otherwise suitable in morals, knowledge, and age, be promoted to sacred orders unless it be first legitimately established that he peacefully possesses an ecclesiastical benefice which may suffice for his honest livelihood.
¶524
CANON II
Cum non deceat eos qui divino ministerio adscripti sunt cum ordinis dedecore mendicare aut sordidum aliquem quaestum exercere, compertum que sit quamplures plerisque in locis ad sacros ordines nullo fere delectu admitti, qui variis artibus ac fallaciis confingunt se beneficium ecclesiasticum aut etiam idoneas facultates obtinere, statuit sancta synodus ne quis deinceps clericus saecularis, quamvis alias sit idoneus moribus, scientia et aetate, ad sacros ordines promoveatur, nisi prius legitime constet eum beneficium ecclesiasticum, quod sibi ad victum honeste sufficiat, pacifice possidere.
But he cannot resign that benefice unless mention be made that he was promoted to the title of that benefice; nor let that resignation be admitted unless it be established that he can otherwise live conveniently, and a resignation made otherwise shall be void.
¶525
Id vero beneficium resignare non possit, nisi facta mentione quod ad illius beneficii titulum sit promotus; neque ea resignatio admittatur, nisi constiterit quod aliunde vivere commode possit, et aliter facta resignatio nulla sit.
Those obtaining patrimony or a pension, however, cannot henceforth be ordained unless those whom the bishop shall have judged to be assumed for the necessity or convenience of his churches, it having also first been considered that that patrimony or pension is truly obtained by them and is such that it suffices for sustaining them in life. And let those things be by no means alienated, extinguished, or remitted without the license of the bishop until they have obtained a sufficient ecclesiastical benefice or have elsewhere the means of living, the penalties of the ancient canons being renewed in these matters.
¶526
Patrimonium vero vel pensionem obtinentes ordinari posthac non possint, nisi illi quos episcopus iudicaverit assumendos pro necessitate vel commoditate ecclesiarum suarum, eo quoque prius perspecto patrimonium illud vel pensionem vere ab eis obtineri talia que esse quae eis ad vitam sustentandam satis sint. Atque illa deinceps sine licentia episcopi alienari aut exstingui vel remitti nullatenus possint, donec beneficium ecclesiasticum sufficiens sint adepti vel aliunde habeant unde vivere possint, antiquorum canonum poenas super his innovando.
Since benefices have been established for divine worship and for performing ecclesiastical duties, that divine worship be in no part diminished, but due service be given to it in all things, the holy synod establishes that in churches both cathedral and collegiate in which there are no daily distributions, or such small ones that they are verisimilarly neglected, a third part of the fruits and of whatsoever proceeds and obventions, both of dignities and of canonries, personates, portions, and offices, is to be separated and converted into daily distributions, which shall be divided proportionally among the dignities obtaining them and the others attending divine services, according to the division to be made by the bishop, also as a delegate of the Apostolic See, in the very first deduction of fruits, the customs, however, of those churches being preserved in which non-residents or non-serving persons receive nothing or less than a third part, any exemptions and other customs even immemorial, and appeals whatsoever, notwithstanding.
¶527
CANON III
Cum beneficia ad divinum cultum atque ecclesiastica munia obeunda sint constituta, ne qua in parte minuatur divinus cultus, sed ei debitum omnibus in rebus obsequium praestetur, statuit sancta synodus in ecclesiis tam cathedralibus quam collegiatis in quibus nullae sunt distributiones quotidianae vel ita tenues ut verisimiliter negligantur, tertiam partem fructuum et quorumcumque proventuum et obventionum, tam dignitatum quam canonicatuum, personatuum, portionum et officiorum, separari debere et in distributiones quotidianas converti, quae inter dignitates obtinentes et ceteros divinis interessentes proportionabiliter, iuxta divisionem ab episcopo, etiam tamquam apostolicae sedis delegato, in ipsa prima fructuum deductione faciendam, dividantur, salvis tamen consuetudinibus earum ecclesiarum in quibus non residentes seu non servientes nihil vel minus tertia parte percipiunt. Non obstantibus exemptionibus et aliis consuetudinibus, etiam immemorabilibus, et appellationibus quibuscumque.
Contumacy of those not serving increasing, let it be lawful to proceed against them according to the disposition of the law and of the sacred canons.
¶528
Crescente que non servientium contumacia, liceat contra eos procedere iuxta iuris ac sacrorum canonum dispositionem.
Bishops, also as delegates of the Apostolic See, in all parish or baptismal churches in which the people are so numerous that one rector cannot suffice for administering the ecclesiastical sacraments and performing divine worship, shall compel the rectors or others to whom it pertains to join to themselves as many priests for this duty as may suffice for exhibiting the sacraments and celebrating divine worship.
¶529
CANON IV
Episcopi etiam tamquam apostolicae sedis delegati in omnibus ecclesiis parochialibus vel baptismalibus in quibus populus ita numerosus sit ut unus rector non possit sufficere ecclesiasticis sacramentis ministrandis et cultui divino peragendo, cogant rectores vel alios ad quos pertinet sibi tot sacerdotes ad hoc munus adiungere quot sufficiant ad sacramenta exhibenda et cultum divinum celebrandum.
In those, however, in which on account of the distance or difficulty of places the parishioners cannot without great inconvenience approach to receive the sacraments and hear divine offices, they may establish new parishes, even against the will of the pastors, according to the form of the constitution of Alexander III which begins Ad audientiam.
¶530
In iis vero in quibus ob locorum distantiam sive difficultatem parochiani sine magno incommodo ad percipienda sacramenta et divina officia audienda accedere non possunt, novas parochias, etiam invitis pastoribus, iuxta formam constitutionis Alexandri III quae incipit "Ad audientiam," constituere possint.
Moreover, a competent portion shall be assigned to those priests who are to be placed over churches newly erected, at the discretion of the bishop, from the fruits belonging in any way to the mother church; and if it be necessary, he can compel the people to supply those things which suffice for sustaining the life of the said priests, any general or special reservation or affection concerning the said churches notwithstanding.
¶531
Illis autem sacerdotibus qui de novo erunt ecclesiis noviter rectis praeficiendi, competens assignetur portio arbitrio episcopi ex fructibus ad ecclesiam matricem quomodocumque pertinentibus; et si necesse fuerit, compellere possit populum ea subministrare quae sufficiant ad vitam dictorum sacerdotum sustentandam, quacumque reservatione generali vel speciali vel affectione super dictis ecclesiis non obstantibus.
Nor can ordinances and erections of this kind be taken away nor impeded by any provisions whatsoever, even by virtue of resignation or by any derogations or suspensions.
¶532
Neque huiusmodi ordinationes et erectiones possint tolli nec impediri ex quibuscumque provisionibus, etiam vigore resignationis aut quibusvis aliis derogationibus vel suspensionibus.
That also the state of churches where sacred offices are ministered to God may be preserved in dignity, bishops, also as delegates of the Apostolic See, according to the form of law, without prejudice however to those obtaining them, may make perpetual unions of any parish and baptismal churches and of other benefices, whether with cure or without cure, with those having cure, on account of their poverty and in other cases permitted by law, even if the said churches or benefices were generally or specially reserved or in any way affected. Which unions also cannot be revoked nor in any way infringed by virtue of any provision, even on account of resignation or by any derogation or suspension.
¶533
CANON V
Ut etiam ecclesiarum status ubi sacra deo officia ministrantur ex dignitate conservetur, possint episcopi etiam tamquam apostolicae sedis delegati, iuxta formam iuris, sine tamen praeiudicio obtinentium, facere uniones perpetuas quarumcumque ecclesiarum parochialium et baptismalium et aliorum beneficiorum curatorum vel non curatorum cum curatis, propter earum paupertatem et in ceteris casibus a iure permissis, etiam si dictae ecclesiae vel beneficia essent generaliter vel specialiter reservata aut qualitercumque affecta. Quae uniones etiam non possint revocari nec quoquo modo infringi vigore cuiuscumque provisionis, etiam ex causa resignationis aut derogationis aut suspensionis.
Because illiterate and unskillful rectors of parish churches are less suitable for sacred offices, and others on account of the baseness of their life rather destroy than build up, bishops, also as delegates of the Apostolic See, to the same illiterate and unskillful, if otherwise they be of honest life, may depute coadjutors or vicars for a time, and assign to them a portion of the fruits for their sufficient support, or otherwise provide, any appeal and exemption being removed.
¶534
CANON VI
Quia illitterati et imperiti parochialium ecclesiarum rectores sacris minus apti sunt officiis, et alii propter eorum vitae turpitudinem potius destruunt quam aedificant, episcopi etiam tamquam apostolicae sedis delegati eisdem illitteratis et imperitis, si alias honestae vitae sint, coadiutores aut vicarios pro tempore deputare, partem que fructuum eisdem pro sufficienti victu assignare, vel aliter providere possint, quacumque appellatione et exemptione remota.
But those who live basely and scandalously, after they have been warned, let them coerce and chastise, and if still incorrigible they persevere in their wickedness, let them have the faculty of depriving them of their benefices according to the institutions of the sacred canons, any exemption and appeal being removed.
¶535
Eos vero qui turpiter et scandalose vivunt, postquam praemoniti fuerint, coerceant ac castigent, et si adhuc incorrigibiles in sua nequitia perseverent, eos beneficiis iuxta sacrorum canonum institutiones, exemptione et appellatione quacumque remota, privandi facultatem habeant.
Since that also is greatly to be taken care of, that those things which are dedicated to sacred ministries do not grow old by the injury of time and fall out of the memory of men, bishops, also as delegates of the Apostolic See, may transfer simple benefices, even of the right of patronage, from churches which have collapsed through age or otherwise and cannot be repaired on account of their poverty, the parties concerned having been summoned, to mother or other churches of the same places or of the nearer places, at their own discretion, and in the same churches let them erect altars or chapels under the same invocations, or transfer them to altars or chapels already erected, with all the emoluments and burdens imposed on the prior churches.
¶536
CANON VII
Cum illud quoque valde curandum sit ne ea quae sacris ministeriis dicata sunt temporum iniuria obsolescant et ex hominum memoria excidant, episcopi etiam tamquam apostolicae sedis delegati transferre possint beneficia simplicia, etiam iuris patronatus, ex ecclesiis quae vetustate vel alias collapsae sint et ob eorum inopiam nequeant instaurari, vocatis iis quorum interest, in matrices aut alias ecclesias locorum eorundem seu viciniorum, arbitrio suo, atque in eisdem ecclesiis erigant altaria vel capellas sub eisdem invocationibus, vel in iam erecta altaria vel capellas transferant, cum omnibus emolumentis et oneribus prioribus ecclesiis impositis.
Parish churches, however, even if they be of the right of patronage, so collapsed they shall take care to be repaired and restored from the fruits and proceeds belonging in any way to the same churches; which if they be not sufficient, let them compel all patrons and others who receive any fruits coming from the said churches, or in default of them the parishioners, by all opportune remedies, to the aforesaid, any appeal, exemption, and contradiction being removed.
¶537
Parochiales vero ecclesias, etiam si iuris patronatus sint, ita collapsas refici et instaurari procurent ex fructibus et proventibus quibuscumque ad easdem ecclesias quomodocumque pertinentibus; qui si non fuerint sufficientes, omnes patronos et alios qui fructus aliquos ex dictis ecclesiis provenientes percipiunt, aut in illorum defectum parochianos, omnibus remediis opportunis ad praedicta cogant, quacumque appellatione, exemptione et contradictione remota.
But if all suffer from excessive poverty, let them be transferred to mother or nearer churches, with the faculty of converting the said parish and other ruined churches to profane but not sordid uses, a cross having been erected there.
¶538
Quodsi nimia egestate omnes laborent, ad matrices seu viciniores ecclesias transferantur, cum facultate tam dictas parochiales quam alias ecclesias dirutas in profanos usus non sordidos, erecta tamen ibi cruce, convertendi.
Most illustrious lords, most reverend fathers, does it please you that for the praise of almighty God an end be put to this sacred oecumenical synod, and that confirmation of all and each of the things which have been decreed and defined in it, both under Paul III and Julius III of happy memory and under our most holy lord Pius IV, Roman pontiffs, be sought in the name of this holy synod from the most blessed Roman pontiff through the legates of the Apostolic See and presidents?
¶539
*DECRETUM DE CLOSURA CONCILII ET DE PETITIONE CONFIRMATIONIS*
DECREE ON THE CLOSING OF THE COUNCIL AND ON PETITIONING FOR CONFIRMATION
Illustrissimi domini, reverendissimi que patres, placet ne vobis ut ad laudem dei omnipotentis huic sacrae oecumenicae synodo finis imponatur, et omnium et singulorum quae tam sub felicis recordationis Paulo III et Iulio III quam sub sanctissimo domino nostro Pio IV, Romanis pontificibus, in ea decreta et definita sunt confirmatio nomine sanctae huius synodi per apostolicae sedis legatos et praesidentes a beatissimo Romano pontifice petatur?
¶540

This completes the Council of Trent (Sessions 1-25).
