First Lateran Council

Concilium Lateranense I

1123

Editorial note

Primarily disciplinary consolidation of the Gregorian reform. Clerical celibacy, simony, lay investiture settlement following the Concordat of Worms. Limited dogmatic content but institutionally foundational.

FIRST LATERAN COUNCIL A.D. 1123
CANON I
Sanctorum patrum exempla sequentes et officii nostri debito innovantes ordinari quemquam per pecuniam in ecclesia dei vel promoveri auctoritate sedis apostolicae modis omnibus prohibemus.
Following the examples of the holy fathers and renewing by the duty of our office, we prohibit in every manner that anyone be ordained in the church of God or be promoted by money through the authority of the apostolic see.
¶1
CANON II
Si quis vero in ecclesia ordinationem vel promotionem taliter adquisierit adquisita prorsus careat dignitate.
If indeed anyone shall have acquired ordination or promotion in the church in such a manner, let him entirely lack the dignity acquired.
¶2
CANON III
A suis episcopis excommunicatos ab aliis episcopis abbatibus et clericis in communionem recipi procul dubio prohibemus.
Those excommunicated by their own bishops we without doubt prohibit from being received into communion by other bishops, abbots, and clerics.
¶3
CANON IV
Nullus in episcopum nisi canonice electum consecret.
Let no one consecrate as bishop unless he has been canonically elected.
¶4
CANON V
Quod si praesumptum fuerit et consecratus et consecrator absque recuperationis spe deponatur.
But if it shall have been presumed, both the consecrated and the consecrator shall be deposed without hope of recovery.
¶5
CANON VI
Nullus omnino archidiaconus aut archipresbyter sive praepositus vel decanus animarum curam vel praebendas ecclesiae sine iudicio vel consensu episcopi alicui tribuat.
Let no archdeacon or archpriest or provost or dean at all bestow the care of souls or the prebends of the church upon anyone without the judgment or consent of the bishop.
¶6
CANON VII
Immo sicut sanctis canonibus constitutum est animarum cura et rerum ecclesiasticarum dispensatio in episcopi iudicio et potestate permaneat.
Rather, just as has been established by the sacred canons, let the care of souls and the dispensation of ecclesiastical affairs remain in the judgment and power of the bishop.
¶7
CANON VIII
Si quis vero contra hoc facere aut potestatem quae ad episcopum pertinet sibi vendicare praesumpserit ab ecclesiae liminibus arceatur.
If indeed anyone shall have presumed to act against this or to claim for himself the power which pertains to the bishop, let him be thrust from the thresholds of the church.
¶8
CANON IX
Ordinationes quae a Burdino haeresiarcha postquam a Romana ecclesia est damnatus quaecumque etiam a pseudoepiscopis per eum postea ordinatis factae sunt nos irratas esse iudicamus.
The ordinations which have been performed by the haeresiarcha Burdinus after he was condemned by the Roman church, and any whatsoever performed by pseudo-bishops later ordained by him, we judge to be invalid.
¶9
CANON X
Nullus in praepositum nullus in archipresbyterum nullus in decanum nisi presbyter nullus in archidiaconum nisi diaconus ordinetur.
Let no one be ordained as provost, no one as archpriest, no one as dean unless a presbyter; no one as archdeacon unless a deacon.
¶10
CANON XI
Presbyteris diaconibus vel subdiaconibus concubinarum et uxorum contubernia penitus interdicimus et aliarum mulierum cohabitationem praeter quas synodus Nicaena propter solas necessitudinum causas habitare permisit videlicet matrem sororem amitam vel materteram aut alias huiusmodi de quibus nulla iuste valeat suspicio oriri.
To presbyters, deacons, or subdeacons we entirely forbid the cohabitation of concubines and wives, and the cohabitation of other women except those whom the Nicene synod permitted to dwell together on account of necessity alone—namely, mother, sister, aunt by father's side or aunt by mother's side, or other women of this kind concerning whom no suspicion can justly arise.
¶11
CANON XII
Praeterea iuxta beatissimi Stephani papae sanctionem statuimus ut laici quamvis religiosi sint nullam tamen de ecclesiasticis rebus aliquid disponendi habeant facultatem sed secundum apostolorum canones omnium negotiorum ecclesiasticorum curam episcopus habeat et ea velut deo contemplante dispenset.
Moreover, according to the sanction of the most blessed Pope Stephen, we decree that laypersons, however religious they may be, nevertheless have no faculty of disposing anything concerning ecclesiastical affairs; but, according to the canons of the apostles, let the bishop have the care of all ecclesiastical business and let him dispense these things as contemplating God.
¶12
CANON XIII
Si quis ergo principum vel aliorum laicorum dispositionem seu donationem rerum sive possessionum ecclesiasticarum sibi vendicaverit ut sacrilegus videatur.
If therefore any prince or of other laypersons shall have claimed for himself the disposition or donation of ecclesiastical affairs or possessions, let him be regarded as sacrilegious.
¶13
CANON XIV
Coniunctiones consanguineorum fieri prohibemus quoniam eas et divinae et saeculi prohibent leges.
We prohibit the unions of blood-relatives because both the divine and the secular laws prohibit them.
¶14
Leges enim divinae hoc agentes et eos qui ex eis prodeunt non solum eiciunt sed maledictos appellant.
For the divine laws not only cast out those doing this and those who spring from them, but also call them cursed.
¶15
Leges vero saeculi infames tales eos vocant et ab hereditate repellunt.
But the secular laws indeed call such persons infamous and repel them from inheritance.
¶16
Nos itaque patres nostros sequentes infamia eos notamus et infames esse censemus.
We therefore, following our fathers, mark them with infamy and we judge them to be infamous.
¶17
CANON XV
Eis qui Hierosolymam proficiscuntur et ad christianam gentem defendendam et tyrannidem infidelium debellandam efficaciter auxilium praebuerint suorum peccatorum remissionem concedimus et domos et familias atque omnia bona eorum in beati Petri et Romanae ecclesiae protectione sicut a domino nostro papa Urbano statutum fuit suscipimus.
To those who journey to Jerusalem and shall have provided effective aid for defending the Christian people and for warring down the tyranny of the unbelievers, we grant remission of their sins; and we receive their houses and families and all their goods under the protection of blessed Peter and the Roman church, just as was established by our lord Pope Urban.
¶18
Quicumque ergo ea distrahere vel auferre quamdiu in via illa morantur praesumpserint excommunicationis ultione plectantur.
Whoever therefore shall have presumed to alienate or take away these things as long as they remain on that journey, let them be punished with the vengeance of excommunication.
¶19
CANON XVI
Eos autem qui vel pro hierosolymitano vel pro Hispanico itinere cruces sibi in vestibus posuisse noscuntur et eas dimisisse cruces iterato assumere et viam ab instanti pascha usque ad sequens proximum pascha perficere apostolica auctoritate praecipimus.
Those moreover who are known to have placed crosses upon their garments either for the Jerusalem journey or for the Spanish journey, and have set them aside, we command by apostolic authority that they take up the crosses again and complete the journey from the present Easter until the next following Easter.
¶20
Alioquin eos ab ecclesiae introitu sequestramus et in omnibus terris eorum divina officia praeter infantium baptisma et morientium poenitentias interdicimus.
Otherwise, we sequester them from the entrance of the church and we forbid divine offices in all their lands except the baptism of infants and the penances of the dying.
¶21
CANON XVII
Illam vero pravam defunctorum Porticanorum consuetudinem quae hactenus ibi fuit ex fratrum nostrorum et totius curiae consilio necnon ex voluntate atque consensu praefecti removendam censemus ut Porticanorum habitatorum sine heredibus morientium bona contra morientis deliberationem minime pervadantur.
But that wicked custom of the deceased Porticani which has existed there up to now, we judge to be removed by the counsel of our brothers and of the whole curia, and also by the will and consent of the prefect—namely, that the goods of the inhabitants of Porto dying without heirs not be seized upon contrary to the deliberation of the dying person.
¶22
Ita tamen ut Porticani in Romanae ecclesiae et nostra nostrorum que successorum oboedientia et fidelitate permaneant.
Yet in such a way that the Porticani remain in the obedience and fidelity to the Roman church and to us and to our successors.
¶23
CANON XVIII
Sanctorum patrum canonibus consona sentientes oblationes de sacratissimo et reverendissimo altari beati Petri et salvatoris et sanctae Mariae rotundae et sancti Nicolai Barensis sancti Egidii aut de aliis omnium ecclesiarum altaribus sive crucibus a laicis auferri penitus interdicimus et sub anathematis districtione prohibemus.
Sensing things consonant with the canons of the holy fathers, we entirely forbid offerings to be taken away by laypersons from the most sacred and most reverend altar of blessed Peter and of the Savior and of holy Mary Rotunda and of holy Nicholas of Bari, of holy Giles, or from other altars or crosses of all churches, and we prohibit this under the constraint of anathema.
¶24
CANON XIX
Ecclesias a laicis incastellari aut in servitutem redigi auctoritate apostolica prohibemus.
Churches to be fortified into castles by laypersons or to be reduced into servitude we prohibit by apostolic authority.
¶25
CANON XX
Quicumque monetam falsam scienter fecerit aut studiose expenderit tamquam maledictus et pauperum virorum oppressor et civitatis turbator a fidelium consortio separetur.
Whoever knowingly shall have made false money or shall have deliberately expended it, as one cursed and an oppressor of poor men and a disturber of the city, let him be separated from the fellowship of the faithful.
¶26
CANON XXI
Si quis romipetas et peregrinos apostolorum limina et aliorum sanctorum oratoria visitantes capere seu rebus quas ferunt spoliare vel mercatores novis teloneorum seu pedaticorum exactionibus molestare tentaverit donec satisfecerit communione careat christiana.
If anyone shall have attempted to capture those journeying to Rome and pilgrims visiting the thresholds of the apostles and the oratories of other saints, or to plunder them of the goods which they carry, or to harass merchants with new exactions of tolls or gate-taxes, let him lack Christian communion until he has made satisfaction.
¶27
CANON XXII
Quicquid vero de pace et trevia dei vel de incendio seu de publicis stratis ab antecessoribus nostris Romanis pontificibus constitutum est nos sancti spiritus auctoritate confirmamus.
Whatever indeed concerning the peace and truce of God or concerning arson or concerning public roads was established by our predecessors the Roman pontiffs, we confirm by the authority of the Holy Spirit.
¶28
CANON XXIII
Sanctorum etiam patrum vestigiis inhaerentes generali decreto sancimus ut monachi propriis episcopis cum omni humilitate subiecti exsistant et eis uti magistris et ecclesiae dei pastoribus debitam oboedientiam et devotam in omnibus subiectionem exhibeant.
Adhering also to the footsteps of the holy fathers, we decree by general decree that monks be subject to their own bishops with all humility and that they exhibit to them as masters and pastors of the church of God due obedience and devoted subjection in all things.
¶29
Publicas missarum sollemnitates nusquam celebrent.
Let them never celebrate the public solemnities of masses.
¶30
A publicis etiam infirmorum visitationibus inunctionibus seu etiam poenitentiis quod ad illorum nullatenus officium pertinet sese omnino abstineant.
And from the public visitations of the sick, anointings, or even penances—which in no way pertain to their office—let them utterly abstain.
¶31
In ecclesiis vero quibus ministrare noscuntur presbyteros nonnisi per manum sui episcopi habeant qui ei de suscepta animarum cura respondeant.
But in the churches to which they are known to minister, let them have presbyters only through the hand of their own bishop, who may be responsible to him for the care of souls undertaken.
¶32
CANON XXIV
Si quis treguam diffregerit usque tertio ad satisfactionem ab episcopo admoneatur.
If anyone shall have broken the truce, let him be admonished by the bishop up to the third time for satisfaction.
¶33
Quod si tertio admonitus satisfacere contempserit episcopus vel cum metropolitani consilio aut cum duobus aut uno vicinorum episcoporum in rebellem anathematis sententiam dictet et per scripturam episcopis circumquaque denuntiet.
But if, having been admonished a third time, he shall have refused to make satisfaction, let the bishop either with the counsel of the metropolitan or with two or one of the neighboring bishops pronounce sentence of anathema against the rebel and let him announce it in writing to the bishops round about.
¶34
CANON XXV
Interdicimus abbatibus et monachis publicas poenitentias dare infirmos visitare et unctiones facere et missas publicas cantare.
We forbid abbots and monks to give public penances, to visit the sick, to perform anointings, and to sing public masses.
¶35
Chrisma et oleum consecrationes altarium ordinationes clericorum ab episcopis accipiant in quorum parochiis manent.
Let them receive chrism and oil, the consecrations of altars, the ordinations of clerics from the bishops in whose parishes they remain.
¶36
CANON XXVI
Ad haec sanctae Romanae ecclesiae possessiones quietas servare per dei gratiam cupientes praecipimus et sub districtione anathematis interdicimus ne aliqua militaris persona Beneventanam beati Petri civitatem praesumat invadere aut violenter retinere.
To these things, desiring to keep the possessions of the holy Roman church undisturbed through the grace of God, we command and we forbid under the constraint of anathema that any military person presume to invade or violently to retain the Beneventan city of blessed Peter.
¶37
Si quis aliter praesumpserit anathematis vinculo teneatur.
If anyone shall have presumed otherwise, let him be held by the bond of anathema.
¶38
CANON XXVII
In parochialibus ecclesiis presbyteri per episcopos constituantur qui eis respondeant de animarum cura et de iis quae ad episcopum pertinent.
In parochial churches, let presbyters be appointed by the bishops, who may be responsible to them for the care of souls and for those things which pertain to the bishop.
¶39
Decimas et ecclesias a laicis non suscipiant absque consensu et voluntate episcoporum et si aliter praesumptum fuerit canonicae ultioni subiaceant.
Let them not receive tithes and churches from laypersons without the consent and will of the bishops; and if it shall have been presumed otherwise, let them be subject to canonical vengeance.
¶40
CANON XXVIII
Servitium quod monasteria aut eorum ecclesiae a tempore Gregorii papae vii usque ad hoc tempus fecere et nos concedimus.
The service which monasteries or their churches have performed from the time of Pope Gregory VII up to the present time, we also grant.
¶41
CANON XXIX
Possessiones ecclesiarum et episcoporum tricennales abbates vel monachos habere omnimodis prohibemus.
Possessions of churches and of bishops of thirty years' standing we entirely forbid abbots or monks to hold.
¶42
CANON XXX
Paternarum traditionum exemplis commoniti pastoralis officii debitum persolventes ecclesias cum bonis suis tam personis quam possessionibus clericos videlicet ac monachos eorum que conversos oratores quoque cum suis nihilominus rebus quas ferunt tutos et sine molestia esse statuimus.
Admonished by the examples of the paternal traditions and paying the debt of the pastoral office, we decree that churches with their goods—namely, both persons and possessions, clerics indeed and monks and their conversi, and also tenant-farmers with their goods which they carry—be safe and without molestation.
¶43
Si quis autem contra hoc facere praesumpserit et postquam facinus suum recognoverit infra triginta dierum spatium competenter non emendaverit a liminibus ecclesiae arceatur et anathematis gladio feriatur.
If indeed anyone shall have presumed to act against this and after he has recognized his crime shall not have made suitable amends within the space of thirty days, let him be thrust from the thresholds of the church and let him be stricken with the sword of anathema.
¶44
CANON XXXI
Presbyteris diaconibus subdiaconibus et monachis concubinas habere seu matrimonia contrahere penitus interdicimus contracta quoque matrimonia ab huiusmodi personis disiungi et personas ad poenitentiam debere redigi iuxta sacrorum canonum diffinitionem iudicamus.
To presbyters, deacons, subdeacons, and monks we entirely forbid to have concubines or to contract marriages; and marriages already contracted by persons of this kind we judge to be dissolved and the persons to be reduced to penance according to the definition of the sacred canons.
¶45
CANON XXXII
Alienationes quae specialiter per Ottonem Hieremiam seu forte Philippum ubilibet de possessionibus Ravennatis exarchatus factae sunt damnamus.
The alienations which have been made specifically by Otto, by Jeremiah, or indeed by Philip anywhere concerning the possessions of the Ravennate exarchate, we condemn.
¶46
Generaliter autem omnium per intrusionem seu canonice electorum sub episcopi nomine aut abbatis qui secundum usum ecclesiae suae consecrandus est alienationes quocunque modo factas necnon personarum ordinationes ab eisdem sine communi consensu clericorum ecclesiae sive per simoniam itidem factas irritas iudicamus.
But generally, alienations of all persons made in any way—whether of those intruded or of those canonically elected under the name of bishop or of abbot who is to be consecrated according to the custom of his church—as well as the ordinations of persons made by the same without the common consent of the clerics of the church or likewise made through simony, we judge to be invalid.
¶47
CANON XXXIII
Illud etiam per omnia interdicimus ut nullus clericus praebendam suam seu aliquod ecclesiasticum beneficium aliquo modo alienare praesumat.
That also we entirely forbid, that any cleric presume to alienate in any way his prebend or any ecclesiastical benefice.
¶48
Quod si praesumptum olim fuit vel aliquando fuerit irritum erit et canonicae ultioni subiacebit.
But if it was presumed long ago or shall ever be presumed, it shall be invalid and shall be subject to canonical vengeance.
¶49

This completes the First Lateran Council (Concilium Lateranense I a. 1123) – all canons (I–XXXIII as presented in this manuscript tradition).
