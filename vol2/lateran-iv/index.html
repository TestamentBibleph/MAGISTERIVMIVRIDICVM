Fourth Lateran Council

Concilium Lateranum IV

1215

Editorial note

Primary contributions: Trinity and Christology (substantia terminology); transubstantiation formally defined; universal Church as one; confession and Eucharist as precepts; canon against Joachim of Fiore's Trinitarian error.

Canon 1 alone covers Trinity, Christology, Eucharist, and Church in a single synthetic statement denser than any other Latin ecumenical council. Functions as the capstone of the entire Latin doctrinal tradition up to this point, which is why Volume 1's appendix uses it as its pivot.

CONSTITUTIONS
CONSTITUTIO I
CONSTITUTION I
CAPUT I
CHAPTER I
Firmiter credimus et simpliciter confitemur quod unus solus est verus Deus æternus et immensus omnipotens incommutabilis incomprehensibilis et ineffabilis Pater et Filius et Spiritus Sanctus tres quidem personæ sed una essentia substantia seu natura simplex omnino.
Firmly we believe and simply we confess that one alone is true God, eternal and immense, omnipotent, unchangeable, incomprehensible, and ineffable, Father and Son and Holy Spirit: three persons indeed but one essence, substance, or nature, altogether simple.
¶1
Pater a nullo Filius autem a solo Patre ac Spiritus Sanctus ab utroque pariter absque initio semper et fine.
The Father from no one, the Son however from the Father alone, and the Holy Spirit from both equally, without beginning, always and without end.
¶2
Pater generans Filius nascens et Spiritus Sanctus procedens consubstantiales et coæquales coomnipotentes et coæterni unum universorum principium creator omnium invisibilium et visibilium spiritualium et corporalium qui sua omnipotenti virtute simul ab initio temporis utramque de nihilo condidit creaturam spiritualem et corporalem angelicam videlicet et mundanam ac deinde humanam quasi communem ex spiritu et corpore constitutam.
The Father begetting, the Son being born, and the Holy Spirit proceeding, consubstantial and co-equal, co-omnipotent and co-eternal: one principle of all things, creator of all invisible and visible, spiritual and corporal, who by His omnipotent virtue simultaneously from the beginning of time founded both creations from nothing, the spiritual and the corporal, namely angelic and worldly, and then human, as it were common, constituted from spirit and body.
¶3
Diabolus enim et dæmones alii a Deo quidem natura creati sunt boni sed ipsi per se facti sunt mali.
The devil indeed and demons, others by God indeed in nature were created good, but they themselves by themselves were made evil.
¶4
Homo vero diaboli suggestione peccavit.
Man however by the suggestion of the devil sinned.
¶5
Hæc sancta Trinitas secundum communem essentiam individua et secundum personales proprietates discreta per Moysen et sanctos prophetas alios que famulos suos iuxta ordinatissimam dispositionem temporum doctrinam humano generi tribuit salutarem.
This holy Trinity according to common essence undivided and according to personal properties distinct, through Moses and the holy prophets and other servants of His, according to the most ordered disposition of times, salutary doctrine to the human race bestowed.
¶6
Et tandem unigenitus Dei Filius Iesus Christus a tota Trinitate communiter incarnatus ex Maria semper virgine Spiritus Sancti cooperatione conceptus verus homo factus ex anima rationali et humana carne compositus una in duabus naturis persona viam vitæ manifestius demonstravit.
And finally the only-begotten Son of God Jesus Christ, by the whole Trinity in common incarnate, from Mary ever-virgin by the cooperation of the Holy Spirit conceived, true man made from rational soul and human flesh composed, one in two natures person, the way of life more manifestly demonstrated.
¶7
Qui cum secundum divinitatem sit immortalis et impassibilis idem ipse secundum humanitatem factus est passibilis et mortalis quin etiam pro salute humani generis in ligno crucis passus et mortuus descendit ad inferos resurrexit a mortuis et ascendit in cœlum.
Who, since according to divinity He is immortal and impassible, the same Himself according to humanity was made passible and mortal; indeed even for the salvation of the human race upon the wood of the cross suffered and died, descended to hell, rose from the dead, and ascended into heaven.
¶8
Sed descendit in anima resurrexit in carne ascendit que pariter in utroque venturus in fine sæculi iudicare vivos et mortuos et redditurus singulis secundum opera sua tam reprobis quam electis.
But He descended in soul, rose in flesh, and ascended equally in both, about to come at the end of the age to judge the living and the dead and to render to each according to his works, both to the reprobate and to the elect.
¶9
Qui omnes cum suis propriis corporibus resurgent quæ nunc gestant ut recipiant secundum merita sua sive bona fuerint sive mala illi cum diabolo pœnam perpetuam et isti cum Christo gloriam sempiternam.
Who all with their own bodies will rise, which now they bear, so that they may receive according to their merits, whether they have been good or evil: those with the devil eternal punishment and these with Christ everlasting glory.
¶10
Una vero est fidelium universalis Ecclesia extra quam nullus omnino salvatur in qua idem ipse sacerdos et sacrificium Iesus Christus cuius corpus et sanguis in sacramento altaris sub speciebus panis et vini veraciter continentur transsubstantiatis pane in corpus et vino in sanguinem potestate divina ut ad perficiendum mysterium unitatis accipiamus ipsi de suo quod accepit ipse de nostro.
One however is the universal Church of the faithful, outside which no one at all is saved, in which the same Himself is priest and sacrifice, Jesus Christ, whose body and blood in the sacrament of the altar under the species of bread and wine truly are contained, the bread being transubstantiated into the body and the wine into the blood by divine power, so that to perfect the mystery of unity we may receive from Him of His own that which He received from our own.
¶11
Et hoc utique sacramentum nemo potest conficere nisi sacerdos qui fuerit rite ordinatus secundum claves Ecclesiæ quas ipse concessit apostolis et eorum successoribus Iesus Christus.
And this sacrament indeed no one is able to confect except a priest who has been duly ordained according to the keys of the Church, which He Himself granted to the apostles and their successors, Jesus Christ.
¶12
Sacramentum vero baptismi quod ad invocationem individuæ Trinitatis videlicet patris et filii et Spiritus Sancti consecratur in aqua tam parvulis quam adultis in forma Ecclesiæ a quocunque rite collatum proficit ad salutem.
The sacrament indeed of baptism, which by the invocation of the undivided Trinity, namely of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, is consecrated in water, both to little ones and to adults, in the form of the Church by anyone duly conferred, profits unto salvation.
¶13
Et si post susceptionem baptismi quisquam prolapsus fuerit in peccatum per veram pœnitentiam semper potest reparari.
And if after the reception of baptism anyone has lapsed into sin, by true penance he is always able to be restored.
¶14
Non solum autem virgines et continentes verum etiam coniugati per fidem rectam et operationem bonam placentes Deo ad æternam merentur beatitudinem pervenire.
Not only however virgins and continent persons, but truly also the married, by right faith and good work pleasing God, to eternal beatitude deserve to arrive.
¶15
CONSTITUTIO II
CONSTITUTION II
CAPUT II
CHAPTER II
Damnamus ergo et reprobamus libellum sive tractatum quem abbas Ioachim edidit contra magistrum Petrum Lombardum de unitate seu essentia Trinitatis appellans ipsum hæreticum et insanum pro eo quod in suis dixit sententiis quoniam quædam summa res est Pater et Filius et Spiritus Sanctus et illa non est generans neque genita nec procedens unde asserit quod ille non tam Trinitatem quam quaternitatem adstruebat in Deo videlicet tres personas et illam communem essentiam quasi quartam manifeste protestans quod nulla res est quæ sit Pater et Filius et Spiritus Sanctus nec est essentia nec substantia nec natura quamvis concedat quod Pater et Filius et Spiritus Sanctus sunt una essentia una substantia una que natura.
We condemn therefore and reprove the little book or tract which the abbot Joachim published against Master Peter Lombard concerning the unity or essence of the Trinity, calling him heretic and insane for that which he said in his Sentences, because a certain supreme thing is Father and Son and Holy Spirit, and that is neither begetting nor begotten nor proceeding; whence he asserts that he not so much Trinity as quaternity established in God, namely three persons and that common essence as it were fourth, manifestly protesting that no thing is which is Father and Son and Holy Spirit, nor is there essence nor substance nor nature, although he concedes that Father and Son and Holy Spirit are one essence, one substance, and one nature.
¶16
Verum unitatem huiusmodi non veram et propriam sed quasi collectivam et similitudinariam esse fatetur quemadmodum dicuntur multi homines unus populus et multi fideles una Ecclesia iuxta illud multitudinis credentium erat cor unum et anima una et qui adhæret Deo unus spiritus est cum illo.
But unity of such a kind he confesses not true and proper, but as it were collective and analogical, just as many men are said to be one people and many faithful one Church, according to that saying: of the multitude of believers there was one heart and one soul; and he who adheres to God is one spirit with Him.
¶17
Item qui plantat et qui rigat unus sunt et omnes unum corpus sumus in Christo.
Likewise, he who plants and he who waters are one, and we are all one body in Christ.
¶18
Rursus in libro regum populus meus et populus tuus unum sunt.
Again in the book of Kings: My people and your people are one.
¶19
Ad hanc autem sententiam adstruendam illud potissimum verbum inducit quod Christus de fidelibus inquit in evangelio volo Pater ut sint unum in nobis sicut et nos unus sumus ut sint consummati in unum.
But to this sentence to be established he especially introduces that saying which Christ concerning the faithful says in the Gospel: "I will, Father, that they be one in us, just as also we are one, that they may be consummated into one."
¶20
Non enim ut ait fideles Christi sunt unum id est una quædam res quæ communis sit omnibus hic modo sunt unum id est una Ecclesia propter catholicæ fidei unitatem et tandem unum regnum propter unionem indissolubilis caritatis.
For not, as he says, the faithful of Christ are one, that is, a certain one thing which is common to all. But in this way they are one, that is, one Church on account of the unity of catholic faith, and finally one kingdom on account of the union of indissoluble charity.
¶21
Quemadmodum in canonica Ioannis epistola legitur quia tres sunt qui testimonium dant in cœlo Pater et Verbum et Spiritus Sanctus et hi tres unum sunt.
Just as in the canonical epistle of John it is read that three are who give testimony in heaven: Father and Word and Holy Spirit, and these three are one.
¶22
Statim que subiungit et tres sunt qui testimonium dant in terra spiritus aqua et sanguis et tres unum sunt sicut in codicibus quibusdam invenitur.
And immediately he subjoins: And three are who give testimony on earth: spirit, water, and blood; and three are one, just as in certain codices it is found.
¶23
Nos autem sacro et universali concilio approbante credimus et confitemur cum Petro quod una quædam summa res est incomprehensibilis quidem et ineffabilis quæ veraciter est Pater et Filius et Spiritus Sanctus tres simul personæ ac sigillatim quælibet earundem et ideo in Deo trinitas est solummodo non quaternitas quia quælibet trium personarum est illa res videlicet substantia essentia sive natura divina quæ sola est universorum principium præter quod aliud inveniri non potest et illa res non est generans neque genita nec procedens sed est Pater qui generat Filius qui gignitur et Spiritus Sanctus qui procedit ut distinctiones sint in personis et unitas in natura.
We however, with the sacred and universal council approving, believe and confess with Peter that one certain supreme thing is, incomprehensible indeed and ineffable, which truly is Father and Son and Holy Spirit, three persons together and singly each of them; and therefore in God there is Trinity only, not quaternity, because each of the three persons is that thing, namely the divine substance, essence, or nature, which alone is the principle of all things, besides which nothing else is able to be found; and that thing is neither begetting nor begotten nor proceeding, but the Father is who begets, the Son who is begotten, and the Holy Spirit who proceeds, so that distinctions are in the persons and unity in the nature.
¶24
Licet igitur alius sit Pater alius Filius alius Spiritus Sanctus non tamen aliud sed id quod est Pater est Filius et Spiritus Sanctus idem omnino ut secundum orthodoxam et catholicam fidem consubstantiales esse credantur.
Therefore although other is the Father, other the Son, other the Holy Spirit, not however other thing, but that which is the Father is the Son and the Holy Spirit, altogether the same, so that according to orthodox and catholic faith they may be believed consubstantial.
¶25
Pater enim ab æterno filium generando suam substantiam ei dedit iuxta quod ipse testatur Pater quod dedit mihi maius est omnibus ac dici non potest quod partem suæ substantiæ illi dederit et partem retinuerit ipse sibi cum substantia patris indivisibilis sit utpote simplex omnino.
For the Father from eternity by begetting the Son gave His substance to him, according to that which He Himself testifies: "The Father, that which He gave to me, is greater than all"; and it cannot be said that He gave a part of His substance to him and retained a part for Himself, since the substance of the Father is indivisible, as altogether simple.
¶26
Sed nec dici potest quod Pater in filium transtulerit suam substantiam generando quasi sic dederit eam filio quod non retinuerit ipsam sibi alio desiisset esse substantia.
But neither can it be said that the Father transferred His substance into the Son by begetting, as if He gave it to the Son in such a way that He did not retain it for Himself, otherwise He would have ceased to be substance.
¶27
Patet ergo quod sine ulla diminutione Filius nascendo substantiam patris accepit et ita Pater et Filius habent eandem substantiam et sic eadem res est Pater et Filius necnon et Spiritus Sanctus ab utroque procedens.
It is clear therefore that without any diminution the Son by being born received the substance of the Father, and thus the Father and the Son have the same substance, and so the same thing is the Father and the Son, and likewise also the Holy Spirit proceeding from both.
¶28
Cum ergo veritas pro fidelibus suis ad patrem orat volo inquiens ut ipsi sint unum in nobis sicut et nos unum sumus hoc nomen unum pro fidelibus quidem accipitur ut intelligatur unio caritatis in gratia pro personis vero divinis ut attendatur identitatis in natura unitas quemadmodum veritas alibi ait estote perfecti sicut et Pater vester cœlestis perfectus est ac si diceret manifestius estote perfecti perfectione gratiæ sicut Pater vester cœlestis perfectus est perfectione naturæ utraque videlicet suo modo quia inter creatorem et creaturam non potest tanta similitudo notari quin inter eos maior sit dissimilitudo notanda.
When therefore the Truth for His faithful ones prays to the Father, saying "I will that they be one in us, just as also we are one," this word "one" for the faithful indeed is accepted so that the union of charity in grace may be understood; for the divine persons however, so that the unity of identity in nature may be attended to, just as the Truth says elsewhere: "Be perfect, just as also your heavenly Father is perfect," as if He said more manifestly: Be perfect with the perfection of grace, just as your heavenly Father is perfect with the perfection of nature; both indeed in their own way, because between the Creator and the creature so great a similarity cannot be noted that between them a greater dissimilarity is not to be noted.
¶29
Si quis igitur sententiam sive doctrinam præfati Ioachim in hac parte defendere vel approbare præsumpserit tamquam hæreticus ab omnibus confutetur.
If therefore anyone the sentence or doctrine of the aforementioned Joachim in this part to defend or approve presumes, as a heretic by all is to be refuted.
¶30
In nullo tamen per hoc Florensi monasterio cuius ipse Ioachim exstitit institutor volumus derogari quoniam ibi et regularis institutio est et observantia salutaris maxime cum idem Ioachim omnia scripta sua nobis assignari mandaverit Apostolicæ Sedis iudicio approbanda seu etiam corrigenda dictans epistolam cui propria manu subscripsit in qua firmiter confitetur se illam fidem tenere quam Romana tenet Ecclesia quæ cunctorum fidelium disponente Domino mater est et magistra.
In nothing however through this to the Florensian monastery, of which the same Joachim was founder, do we wish derogation to be made, because there both the regular institution is and the observance salutary, especially since the same Joachim all his writings to be assigned to us commanded, by the judgment of the Apostolic See to be approved or indeed to be corrected, dictating a letter to which with his own hand he subscribed, in which firmly he confesses that he holds that faith which the Roman Church holds, which of all the faithful, the Lord disposing, is mother and teacher.
¶31
Reprobamus etiam et damnamus perversissimum dogma impii Amalrici cuius mentem sic pater mendacii excæcavit ut eius doctrina non tam hæretica censenda sit quam insana.
We reprove also and condemn the most perverse dogma of the impious Amalric, whose mind the father of lying so blinded that his doctrine not so much heretical is to be judged as insane.
¶32
CONSTITUTIO III
CONSTITUTION III
CAPUT III
CHAPTER III
Excommunicamus et anathemizamus omnem hæresim extollentem se adversus hanc sanctam orthodoxam catholicam fidem quam superius exposuimus condemnantes universos hæreticos quibuscumque nominibus censeantur facies quidem habentes diversas sed caudas adinvicem colligatas quia de vanitate conveniunt in id ipsum.
We excommunicate and anathematize every heresy lifting itself up against this holy, orthodox, catholic faith which we above have set forth, condemning all heretics by whatsoever names they may be considered, having faces indeed diverse but tails to one another tied together, because from vanity they agree in this same thing.
¶33
Damnati vero sæcularibus potestatibus præsentibus aut eorum balivis relinquantur animadversione debita puniendi clericis prius a suis ordinibus degradatis ita quod bona huiusmodi damnatorum si laici fuerint confiscentur si vero clerici applicentur ecclesiis a quibus stipendia perceperunt.
Those condemned indeed to secular powers present or to their bailiffs are to be handed over, with due punishment to be punished, clerics first from their orders degraded, so that the goods of such condemned persons, if they are laypersons, may be confiscated; if however clerics, they may be applied to the churches from which they received stipends.
¶34
Qui autem inventi fuerint sola suspicione notabiles nisi iuxta considerationes suspicionis qualitatem que personæ propriam innocentiam congrua purgatione monstraverint anathematis gladio feriantur et usque ad satisfactionem condignam ab omnibus evitentur ita quod si per annum in excommunicatione perstiterint extunc velut hæretici condemnentur.
Those however who have been found marked with suspicion alone, unless according to the consideration of the quality of the suspicion and of the person, by a suitable purgation their own innocence have shown, by the sword of anathema are to be struck and until condign satisfaction by all are to be avoided, so that if for a year in excommunication they have persisted, from then as heretics they are to be condemned.
¶35
Moneantur autem et inducantur et si necesse fuerit per censuram ecclesiasticam compellantur sæculares potestates quibuscumque fungantur officiis ut sicut reputari cupiunt et haberi fideles ita pro defensione fidei præstent publice iuramentum quod de terris suæ iurisdictioni subiectis universos hæreticos ab Ecclesia denotatos bona fide pro viribus exterminare studebunt ita quod amodo quandocumque quis fuerit in potestatem sive spiritualem sive temporalem assumptus hoc teneatur capitulum iuramento firmare.
Secular powers, whatsoever offices they may exercise, are to be admonished and induced, and if necessary through ecclesiastical censure compelled, that just as they desire to be reputed and held faithful, so for the defense of the faith they publicly present an oath that from the lands subject to their jurisdiction all heretics by the Church designated, in good faith according to their strength, they will strive to exterminate, so that from now on whenever anyone into power, whether spiritual or temporal, has been assumed, he is held to confirm this chapter with an oath.
¶36
Si vero dominus temporalis requisitus et monitus ab Ecclesia terram suam purgare neglexerit ab hac hæretica fœditate per metropolitanum et ceteros comprovinciales episcopos excommunicationis vinculo innodetur.
If however a temporal lord, required and admonished by the Church, his land to purge has neglected, by the metropolitan and the other suffragan bishops let him be bound with the bond of excommunication.
¶37
Et si satisfacere contempserit infra annum significetur hoc summo pontifici ut ex ipse vassallos ab eius fidelitate denunciet absolutos et terram exponat catholicis occupandam qui eam exterminatis hæreticis sine ulla contradictione possideant et in fidei puritate conservent salvo iure domini principalis dummodo super hoc ipse nullum præstet obstaculum nec aliquod impedimentum opponat.
And if he has despised to satisfy within the year, let this be signified to the Supreme Pontiff, so that he may declare the vassals from his fealty absolved and the land expose to catholics to be occupied, who, the heretics having been exterminated, without any contradiction may possess and in the purity of the faith preserve, saving the right of the principal lord, provided that he himself in this matter present no obstacle nor any impediment oppose.
¶38
Eadem nihilominus lege servata circa eos qui non habent dominos principales.
The same law, nevertheless, is to be observed concerning those who have no principal lords.
¶39
Catholici vero qui crucis assumpto charactere ad hæreticorum exterminium se accinxerint illa gaudeant indulgentia illo que sancto privilegio sint muniti quod accedentibus in terræ sanctæ subsidium conceditur.
Catholics indeed who, the cross having been assumed, for the extermination of heretics have girded themselves, let them rejoice in that indulgence and that holy privilege be granted which to those going in aid of the Holy Land is conceded.
¶40
Credentes vero præterea receptores defensores et fautores hæreticorum excommunicationi decernimus subiacere firmiter statuentes ut postquam quis talium fuerit excommunicatione notatus si satisfacere contempserit infra annum ex ipso iure sit factus infamis nec ad publica officia seu consilia nec ad eligendos aliquos ad huiusmodi nec ad testimonium admittatur.
Believers moreover, likewise receivers, defenders, and favorers of heretics, to excommunication we decree to be subject, firmly establishing that after anyone of such has been marked with excommunication, if he has despised to satisfy within the year, by the law itself let him be made infamous, and to public offices or councils, nor to electing any to such, nor to testimony, be admitted.
¶41
Sit etiam intestabilis ut nec testandi liberam habeat facultatem nec ad hereditatis successionem accedat.
Let him also be intestable, so that neither of making a will may he have free faculty nor to the succession of inheritance may he approach.
¶42
Nullus præterea ipsi super quocumque negocio sed ipse alii respondere cogatur.
Moreover, no one against him concerning any business, but he to another to respond may be compelled.
¶43
Quod si forte iudex exstiterit eius sententia nullam obtineat firmitatem nec causæ aliquæ ad eius audientiam perferantur.
But if perhaps he has been a judge, his sentence may obtain no firmness, nor any cases to his hearing be brought.
¶44
Si fuerit advocatus eius patrocinium nullatenus admittatur.
If he has been an advocate, his advocacy may by no means be admitted.
¶45
Si tabellio eius instrumenta confecta per ipsum nullius penitus sint momenti sed cum auctore damnato damnentur.
If a notary, his instruments by him prepared of no value at all shall be, but with the author condemned let them be condemned.
¶46
Et in similibus idem præcipimus observari.
And in similar matters the same we command to be observed.
¶47
Si vero clericus fuerit ab omni officio et beneficio deponatur ut in quo maior est culpa gravior exerceatur vindicta.
If however a cleric he has been, from every office and benefice let him be deposed, so that in which there is greater fault graver vindication may be exercised.
¶48
Si qui autem tales postquam ab Ecclesia denotati fuerint evitare contempserint excommunicationis sententia usque ad satisfactionem idoneam percellantur.
But if any such persons, after they have been by the Church designated, have despised to avoid, by the sentence of excommunication until suitable satisfaction let them be struck.
¶49
Sane clerici non exhibeant huiusmodi pestilentibus ecclesiastica sacramenta nec eos christianæ præsumant sepulturæ tradere nec eleemosynas aut oblationes eorum accipiant.
Truly, clerics shall not present to such pestilent persons ecclesiastical sacraments, nor to christian burial presume to deliver them, nor alms or oblations of them receive.
¶50
Alioquin suo priventur officio ad quod numquam restituantur absque indulto Sedis Apostolicæ speciali.
Otherwise, let them be deprived of their office, to which never may they be restored without an indult of the Apostolic See special.
¶51
Similiter quilibet regulares quibus hoc etiam infligatur ut eorum privilegia illa diœcesi non serventur in qua tales excessus præsumpserint perpetrare.
Similarly, any regulars, to whom this also is inflicted, that their privileges in that diocese be not preserved in which such excesses they have presumed to perpetrate.
¶52
Quia vero nonnulli sub specie pietatis virtutem eius iuxta quod ait apostolus abnegantes auctoritatem sibi vendicant prædicandi cum idem apostolus dicat quomodo prædicabunt nisi mittantur omnes qui prohibiti vel non missi præter auctoritatem ab Apostolica Sede vel catholico episcopo loci susceptam publice vel privatim prædicationis officium usurpare præsumpserint excommunicationis vinculo innodentur et nisi quantocius resipuerint alia competenti pœna plectantur.
Because indeed not a few under the appearance of piety, its virtue according to what the apostle says denying, authority to themselves arrogate of preaching, since the same apostle says: "How will they preach unless they are sent?" — all who, prohibited or not sent, without authority from the Apostolic See or from the catholic bishop of the place received, publicly or privately the office of preaching have presumed to usurp, by the bond of excommunication let them be bound, and unless as quickly as possible they have recovered their senses, by another suitable penalty let them be punished.
¶53
Adicimus insuper ut quilibet archiepiscopus vel episcopus per se aut per archidiaconum suum vel idoneas personas honestas bis aut saltem semel in anno propriam parochiam in qua fama fuerit hæreticos habitare circumeat et ibi tres vel plures boni testimonii viros vel etiam si expedire videbitur totam viciniam iurare compellat.
We add moreover that any archbishop or bishop by himself or through his archdeacon or suitable honest persons, twice or at least once in a year, his own parish in which fame has it that heretics dwell, let him go around, and there three or more men of good testimony or even, if it shall seem expedient, the whole neighborhood, let him compel to swear.
¶54
Quod si quis ibidem hæreticos sciverit vel aliquos occulta conventicula celebrantes seu a communi conversatione fidelium vita et moribus dissidentes eos episcopo studeat indicare.
That if anyone there heretics has known or any persons secret conventicles celebrating or from the common conversation of the faithful in life and morals dissenting, them to the bishop let him strive to indicate.
¶55
Ipse autem episcopus ad præsentiam suam convocet accusatos qui nisi se ab obiecto reatu purgaverint vel si post purgationem exhibitam in pristinam fuerint relapsi perfidiam canonice puniantur.
The bishop himself then to his presence let him summon the accused, who unless themselves from the objected guilt they have purged, or if after a purgation exhibited into their former perfidy they have relapsed, canonically let them be punished.
¶56
Si qui vero ex eis iuramenti religionem obstinatione damnabili respuentes iurare forte noluerint ex hoc ipso tamquam hæretici reputentur.
But if any of them the religion of an oath with damnable obstinacy rejecting to swear perhaps have been unwilling, from this very fact as heretics let them be considered.
¶57
Volumus igitur et mandamus et in virtute obedientiæ districte præcipimus ut ad hæc efficaciter exequenda episcopi per diœceses suas diligenter invigilent si canonicam effugere voluerint ultionem.
We will therefore and command, and in virtue of obedience strictly we command, that for these things efficaciously to be carried out, bishops through their dioceses diligently keep watch, if canonical vengeance they wish to escape.
¶58
Si quis enim episcopus super expurgando de sua diœcesi hæreticæ pravitatis fermento negligens fuerit vel remissus cum id certis indiciis apparuerit et ab episcopali officio deponatur et in loco ipsius alter substituatur idoneus qui velit et possit hæreticam confundere pravitatem.
For if any bishop concerning purging from his diocese the leaven of heretical depravity has been negligent or remiss, when this by certain indicators shall have appeared, both from the episcopal office let him be deposed, and in his place let another suitable one be substituted who is willing and able heretical depravity to confound.

CONSTITUTION IV

CHAPTER IV
Licet Græcos in diebus nostris ad obedientiam Sedis Apostolicæ revertentes fovere et honorare velimus mores ac ritus eorum quantum cum Domino possumus sustinendo in his tamen illis deferre nec volumus nec debemus quæ periculum generant animarum et ecclesiasticæ derogant honestati.
Although we wish the Greeks, who in our days are returning to the obedience of the Apostolic See, to favor and honor, their customs and rites, as much as with the Lord we are able, sustaining, in those things nevertheless to them we neither wish nor ought to defer which generate peril of souls and detract from ecclesiastical honesty.
¶60
Postquam enim Græcorum Ecclesia cum quibusdam complicibus ac fautoribus suis ab obedientia Sedis Apostolicæ se subtraxit in tantum Græci cœperunt abominari Latinos quod inter alia quæ in derogationem eorum impie committebant si quando sacerdotes Latini super eorum celebrassent altaria non prius ipsi sacrificare volebant in illis quam ea tamquam per hoc inquinata lavissent.
For after the Church of the Greeks, with certain accomplices and favorers of its own, from the obedience of the Apostolic See withdrew itself, to such an extent the Greeks began to abominate the Latins that, among other things which to the derogation of them impiously they committed, if at any time Latin priests upon their altars had celebrated, not first were they themselves willing to sacrifice upon them until they had washed them, as by this as if polluted.
¶61
Baptizatos etiam a Latinis et ipsi Græci rebaptizare ausu temerario præsumebant et adhuc sicut accepimus quidam agere hoc non verentur.
The baptized also by Latins the Greeks themselves with temerarious daring presumed to rebaptize, and still, as we have heard, certain ones to do this do not fear.
¶62
Volentes ergo tantum ab Ecclesia Dei scandalum amovere sacro suadente concilio districte præcipimus ut talia de cætero non præsumant conformantes se tamquam obedientiæ filii sacrosanctæ Romanæ Ecclesiæ matri suæ ut sit unum ovile et unus pastor.
Willing therefore so great a scandal from the Church of God to remove, with the sacred council advising, strictly we command that such things from now on they not presume, conforming themselves as sons of obedience to the sacrosanct Roman Church their mother, that there may be one fold and one shepherd.
¶63
Si quis autem quid tale præsumpserit excommunicationis mucrone percussus ab omni officio et beneficio ecclesiastico deponatur.
If anyone however anything such has presumed, by the sword of excommunication struck, from every office and ecclesiastical benefice let him be deposed.

CONSTITUTION V

Antiqua patriarchalium sedium privilegia renovantes sacra universali synodo approbante sancimus ut post Romanam Ecclesiam quæ disponente Domino super omnes alias ordinariæ potestatis obtinet principatum utpote mater universorum Christi fidelium et magistra constantinopolitana primum Alexandrina secundum Antiochena tertium hierosolymitana quartum locum obtineant servata cuilibet propria dignitate ita quod postquam eorum antistites a Romano pontifice receperint pallium quod est plenitudinis officii pontificalis insigne præstito sibi fidelitatis et obedientiæ iuramento licenter et ipsi suis suffraganeis pallium largiantur recipientes pro se professionem canonicam et pro Romana Ecclesia sponsionem obedientiæ ab eisdem.
Renewing the privileges of the patriarchal sees, with the sacred universal council approving, we sanction that after the Roman Church, which, the Lord disposing, over all others of ordinary power obtains the primacy as the mother of all the faithful of Christ and teacher, the Constantinopolitan first, the Alexandrian second, the Antiochene third, the Hierosolymitan fourth place obtain, saving to each its own dignity, so that after their prelates from the Roman pontiff have received the pallium, which is of the fullness of pontifical office the insignia, having first been given to him the oath of fidelity and obedience, freely also they themselves to their suffragans the pallium may bestow, receiving for themselves canonical profession and for the Roman Church the sponsion of obedience from the same.
¶65
Dominicæ vero crucis vexillum ante se faciant ubique deferri nisi in urbe Romana et ubicumque Summus Pontifex præsens exstiterit vel eius legatus utens insigniis apostolicæ dignitatis.
The vexillum indeed of the Lord's cross before themselves they shall cause everywhere to be carried, except in the Roman city and wherever the Supreme Pontiff is present or his legate, using the insignia of apostolic dignity.
¶66
In omnibus autem provinciis eorum iurisdictioni subiectis ad eos cum necesse fuerit provocetur salvis appellationibus ad Sedem Apostolicam interpositis quibus est ab omnibus humiliter deferendum.
In all provinces however to their jurisdiction subject, to them, when it shall be necessary, let appeal be made, saving the appeals to the Apostolic See interposed, to which by all it is to be humbly deferred.

CONSTITUTIO VI
CONSTITUTION VI
CAPUT VI
CHAPTER VI
Sicut olim a sanctis patribus noscitur institutum metropolitani singulis annis cum suis suffraganeis provincialia non omittant concilia celebrare in quibus de corrigendis excessibus et moribus reformandis præsertim in clero diligentem habeant cum Dei timore tractatum canonicas regulas et maxime quæ statuta sunt in hoc generali concilio relegentes ut eas faciant observari debitam pœnam transgressoribus infligendo.
Just as of old by the holy fathers it is known to have been instituted, metropolitans each year with their suffragans provincial councils shall not omit to celebrate, in which concerning correcting excesses and reforming morals, especially in the clergy, diligent with the fear of God treatment they shall have, the canonical rules and especially those things which have been established in this general council rereading, so that they cause them to be observed, due penalty upon transgressors inflicting.
¶68
Ut autem id valeat efficacius adimpleri per singulas diœceses statuant idoneas personas providas videlicet et honestas quæ per totum annum simpliciter et de plano absque ulla iurisdictione sollicite investigent quæ correctione vel reformatione sint digna et ea fideliter perferant ad metropolitanum et suffraganeos et alios in concilio subsequenti ut super his et aliis prout utilitati et honestati congruerit provida deliberatione procedant.
So that this may be able more efficaciously to be fulfilled, through each diocese let them establish suitable persons, provident indeed and honest, who through the whole year simply and plainly without any jurisdiction solicitously investigate which things are worthy of correction or reformation, and those things faithfully convey to the metropolitan and suffragans and others in the subsequent council, so that concerning these and other things as utility and honesty may accord, with provident deliberation they may proceed.
¶69
Et quæ statuerint faciant observari publicantes ea in episcopalibus synodis annuatim per singulas diœceses celebrandis.
And what they have established they shall cause to be observed, publishing them in episcopal synods annually through each diocese to be celebrated.
¶70
Quisquis autem hoc salutare statutum neglexerit adimplere a suis beneficiis et executione officii suspendatur donec per superioris arbitrium eius relaxetur.
Whoever however this salutary statute to fulfill has neglected, from his benefices and execution of office let him be suspended, until by the decision of a superior it may be relaxed.
¶71
CONSTITUTIO VII
CONSTITUTION VII
CAPUT VII
CHAPTER VII
Irrefragabili constitutione sancimus ut ecclesiarum prælati ad corrigendum subditorum excessus maxime clericorum et reformandum mores prudenter et diligenter intendant ne sanguis eorum de suis manibus requiratur.
With irrevocable constitution we sanction that the prelates of churches, for correcting the excesses of subjects, especially of clerics, and for reforming morals, prudently and diligently they intend, lest their blood from their hands be required.
¶72
Ut autem correctionis et reformationis officium libere valeant exercere decernimus ut executionem ipsarum nulla consuetudo vel appellatio valeat impedire nisi formam excesserint in talibus observandam.
So that however the office of correction and reformation freely they may be able to exercise, we decree that the execution of those things no custom nor appeal may be able to impede, unless they have exceeded the form in such matters to be observed.
¶73
Excessus tamen canonicorum Ecclesiæ cathedralis qui consueverunt corrigi per capitulum per ipsum in illis ecclesiis quæ talem hactenus consuetudinem habuerunt ad commonitionem et iussionem episcopi corrigantur infra terminum competentem ab episcopo præfigendum.
The excesses however of the canons of the cathedral church, which have been accustomed to be corrected by the chapter, by the chapter itself in those churches which such a custom thus far have had, at the admonition and command of the bishop, within a competent term by the bishop to be prefixed, let them be corrected.
¶74
Alioquin ex episcopus Deum habens præ oculis omni contradictione cessante ipsos prout animarum cura exegerit per censuram ecclesiasticam corrigere non postponat sed et alios eorum excessus corrigere non omittat prout animarum causa requirit debito tamen ordine in omnibus observato.
Otherwise, the bishop, having God before his eyes, with all contradiction ceasing, them just as the care of souls shall have required, through ecclesiastical censure to correct let him not delay; but also their other excesses to correct let him not omit, just as the cause of souls requires, with due order however in all things observed.
¶75
Cæterum si canonici absque manifesta et rationabili causa maxime in contemptum episcopi cessaverint a divinis episcopus nihilominus si voluerit celebret in Ecclesia cathedrali et metropolitanus ad querelam ipsius tamquam super hoc delegatus a nobis taliter eos per censuram ecclesiasticam cognita veritate castiget quod pœnæ metu talia de cetero non præsumant.
Moreover, if the canons without manifest and reasonable cause, especially in contempt of the bishop, have ceased from divine offices, the bishop nevertheless, if he shall wish, let him celebrate in the cathedral church; and the metropolitan, at the complaint of the bishop as upon this matter delegated by us, thus them through ecclesiastical censure, the truth having been known, let him chastise that by fear of punishment such things from now on they not presume.
¶76
Provideant itaque diligenter ecclesiarum prælati ut hoc salutare statutum ad quæstum pecuniæ vel gravamen aliud non convertant sed illud studiose ac fideliter exequantur si canonicam voluerint effugere ultionem quoniam super his Apostolica Sedes auctore Domino attentissime vigilabit.
Let the prelates of churches therefore diligently provide that this salutary statute into the quest of money or other burden they do not convert, but that they study it faithfully and effectively to carry out, if canonical vengeance they wish to escape, because concerning these things the Apostolic See, the Lord being author, most attentively will keep watch.
¶77
CONSTITUTIO VIII
CONSTITUTION VIII
CAPUT VIII
CHAPTER VIII
Qualiter et quomodo debeat prælatus procedere ad inquirendum et puniendum subditorum excessus ex auctoritatibus novi et veteris testamenti colligitur evidenter ex quibus postea processerunt canonicæ sanctiones sicut olim aperte distinximus et nunc sacri approbatione concilii confirmamus.
In what manner and how a prelate ought to proceed to inquiring into and punishing the excesses of subjects, from the authorities of the New and Old Testament is collected evidently, from which afterwards proceeded canonical sanctions, just as of old we have openly distinguished and now by the approval of the sacred council we confirm.
¶78
Legitur enim in evangelio quod villicus ille qui diffamatus erat apud dominum suum quasi dissipasset bona ipsius audivit ab illo quid hoc audio de te redde rationem villicationis tuæ iam enim non poteris villicare.
For it is read in the Gospel that that steward who had been accused before his lord as if he had dissipated his goods, heard from him: "What is this I hear of you? Render the account of your stewardship, for now you will not be able to steward."
¶79
Et in genesi Dominus ait descendam et videbo utrum clamorem qui venit ad me opere compleverunt.
And in Genesis the Lord says: "I will descend and see whether the cry which comes to me according to work they have completed."
¶80
Ex quibus auctoritatibus manifeste comprobatur quod non solum cum subditus verum etiam cum prælatus excedit si per clamorem et famam ad aures superioris pervenerit non quidem a malevolis et maledicis sed a providis et honestis nec semel tantum sed sæpe quod clamor innuit et diffamatio manifestat debet coram Ecclesiæ senioribus veritatem diligentius perscrutari.
From which authorities manifestly it is proven that not only when a subject but also when a prelate exceeds, if by clamor and fame to the ears of a superior it shall have come, not indeed from the malevolent and slanderous but from the provident and honest, nor only once but often, which clamor intimates and fame manifests, before the elders of the church the truth more diligently is to be scrutinized.
¶81
Et si rei poposcerit qualitas canonica districtio culpam feriat delinquentis non tamquam sit actor et iudex sed quasi deferente fama vel denunciante clamore officii sui debitum exequatur.
And if the quality of the matter shall have demanded, canonical severity let it strike the fault of the delinquent, not as if he were actor and judge, but as if fame reporting or clamor denouncing, the debt of his office let him carry out.
¶82
Licet autem hoc sit observandum in subditis diligentius tamen observandum est in prælatis qui quasi signum sunt positi ad sagittam.
Although this is to be observed in subjects, more diligently however it is to be observed in prelates, who as it were a sign are placed for the arrow.
¶83
Et quia non possunt omnibus complacere cum ex officio teneantur non solum arguere sed etiam increpare quin etiam interdum suspendere nonnunquam vero ligare frequenter odium multorum incurrunt et insidias patiuntur.
And because they are not able to please all, since by office they are held not only to argue but also to rebuke, indeed even sometimes to suspend, and sometimes truly to bind, frequently the hatred of many they incur and snares they suffer.
¶84
Ideo sancti patres provide statuerunt ut accusatio prælatorum non facile admittatur ne concussis columnis corruat ædificium nisi diligens adhibeatur cautela per quam non solum falsæ sed etiam malignæ criminationi ianua præcludatur.
Therefore the holy fathers providently established that the accusation of prelates not easily be admitted, lest the columns having been shaken the building may collapse, unless diligent caution be applied, through which not only false but also malignant accusation the door may be closed.
¶85
Verum ita voluerunt providere prælatis ne criminarentur iniuste ut tamen caverent ne delinquerent insolenter contra morbum utrumque invenientes congruam medicinam videlicet ut criminalis accusatio quæ ad diminutionem capitis id est degradationem intenditur nisi legitima præcedat inscriptio nullatenus admittatur.
But thus they wished to provide for prelates lest they be criminated unjustly, that yet they should take care lest they should offend insolently, against each disease finding suitable medicine, namely that criminal accusation, which to the diminution of the head, that is to degradation, intends, unless a legitimate inscription precedes, by no means is to be admitted.
¶86
Sed cum super excessibus suis quisquam fuerit infamatus ita ut iam clamor ascendat qui diutius sine scandalo dissimulari non possit vel sine periculo tolerari absque dubitationis scrupulo ad inquirendum et puniendum eius excessus non ex odii fomite sed caritatis procedatur affectu.
But when concerning his excesses anyone shall have been defamed, so that now clamor rises up which longer without scandal cannot be dissimulated or without peril tolerated, without the scruple of doubt, to inquiring into and punishing his excesses, not from the fuel of hatred but from the affection of charity, let it be proceeded.
¶87
Quatenus si fuerit gravis excessus etsi non degradetur ab ordine ab administratione tamen amoveatur omnino quod est secundum evangelicam sententiam a villicatione villicum amoveri qui non potest villicationis suæ dignam reddere rationem.
So that if it shall have been a grave excess, even if he is not degraded from order, from administration however let him be altogether removed, which is according to the evangelical sentence: the steward from stewardship to be removed who is not able of his stewardship a worthy account to render.
¶88
Debet igitur esse præsens is contra quem facienda est inquisitio nisi se per contumaciam absentaverit et exponenda sunt ei illa capitula de quibus fuerit inquirendum ut facultatem habeat defendendi se ipsum et non solum dicta sed etiam nomina ipsa testium sunt ei ut quid et a quo sit dictum appareat publicanda necnon exceptiones et replicationes legitimæ admittendæ ne per suppressionem nominum infamandi per exceptionum vero exclusionem deponendi falsum audacia præbeatur.
He against whom the inquisition is to be made ought therefore to be present, unless he shall have absented himself by contumacy, and those chapters concerning which inquisition is to be made are to be set forth to him, so that he may have the faculty of defending himself; and not only the sayings but also the names themselves of the witnesses are to be made public to him, so that from whom and what has been said may appear, and likewise legitimate exceptions and rejoinders are to be admitted, lest through the suppression of names the audacity of defaming, and through the exclusion of exceptions of deposing falsely, may be provided.
¶89
Ad corrigendos itaque subditorum excessus tanto diligentius debet prælatus assurgere quanto damnabilius eorum offensas desereret incorrectas contra quos ut de notoriis excessibus taceatur etsi tribus modis possit procedi per accusationem videlicet denunciationem et inquisitionem eorum ut tamen in omnibus diligens adhibeatur cautela ne forte per leve compendium ad grave dispendium veniatur sicut accusationem legitima præcedere debet inscriptio sic et denunciationem caritativa admonitio et inquisitionem clamosa insinuatio prævenire illo semper adhibito moderamine ut iuxta formam iudicii sententiæ quoque forma dictetur.
For correcting therefore the excesses of subjects, so much more diligently ought the prelate to rise up, as more damnably he would leave uncorrected the offenses of those against whom, not to mention notorious excesses, even if in three ways it may be possible to proceed, through accusation namely, denunciation, and inquisition, that yet in all things diligent caution be applied, lest perhaps through light gain to grave loss may be come; just as accusation a legitimate inscription ought to precede, thus also denunciation a charitable admonition, and inquisition a clamorous insinuation, that always applied moderation so that according to the form of the judgment the form also of the sentence may be dictated.
¶90
Hunc tamen ordinem circa regulares personas non credimus usquequaque servandum quæ cum causa requirit facilius et liberius a suis possunt administrationibus amoveri.
This order however concerning regular persons we do not believe in every respect to be to be observed, which when cause requires, more easily and more freely from their administrations can be removed.

CONSTITUTIO IX
CONSTITUTION IX
CAPUT IX
CHAPTER IX
Quoniam in plerisque partibus intra eandem civitatem atque diœcesim permixti sunt populi diversarum linguarum habentes sub una fide varios ritus et mores districte præcipimus ut pontifices huiusmodi civitatum sive diœcesum provideant viros idoneos qui secundum diversitates rituum et linguarum divina officia illis celebrent et ecclesiastica sacramenta ministrent instruendo eos verbo pariter et exemplo.
Since in very many parts within the same city and diocese mixed are peoples of diverse languages, having under one faith various rites and customs, strictly we command that the pontiffs of such cities or dioceses provide suitable men who according to the diversities of rites and languages the divine offices for them celebrate and the ecclesiastical sacraments administer, instructing them by word equally and example.
¶92
Prohibemus autem omnino ne una eadem que civitas sive diœcesis diversos pontifices habeat tanquam unum corpus diversa capita quasi monstrum.
We prohibit however altogether that one and the same city or diocese diverse pontiffs have, as if one body diverse heads, as it were a monster.
¶93
Sed si propter prædictas causas urgens necessitas postulaverit Pontifex loci catholicum præsulem nationibus illis conformem provida deliberatione constituat sibi vicarium in prædictis qui ei per omnia sit obediens et subiectus unde si quis aliter se ingesserit excommunicationis se noverit mucrone percussum et si nec sic resipuerit ab omni ecclesiastico ministerio deponatur adhibito si necesse fuerit brachio sæculari ad tantam insolentiam compescendam.
But if on account of the aforesaid causes urgent necessity shall have demanded, the pontiff of the place a catholic prelate to those nations conformed, with provident deliberation let him establish for himself a vicar in the aforesaid, who to him in all things is obedient and subject; whence if anyone otherwise shall have intruded himself, by the sword of excommunication he shall know himself to be struck; and if not thus he shall have recovered his senses, from every ecclesiastical ministry let him be deposed, the secular arm being applied if necessary for so great insolence to be repressed.

CONSTITUTIO X
CONSTITUTION X
CAPUT X
CHAPTER X
Inter cætera quæ ad salutem spectant populi christiani pabulum Verbi Dei permaxime sibi noscitur esse necessarium quia sicut corpus materiali sic anima spirituali cibo nutritur eo quod non in solo pane vivit homo sed in omni verbo quod procedit de ore Dei.
Among other things which pertain to the salvation of the Christian people, the nourishment of the Word of God is known to be especially necessary, because just as the body by material, so the soul by spiritual food is nourished, since not on bread alone does man live but on every word which proceeds from the mouth of God.
¶95
Unde cum sæpe contingat quod episcopi propter occupationes multiplices vel invaletudines corporales aut hostiles incursus seu occasiones alias ne dicamus defectum scientiæ quod in eis est reprobandum omnino nec de cætero tolerandum per se ipsos non sufficiunt ministrare populo Verbum Dei maxime per amplas diœceses et diffusas generali constitutione sancimus ut episcopi viros idoneos ad sanctæ prædicationis officium salubriter exequendum assumant potentes in opere et sermone qui plebes sibi commissas vice ipsorum cum per se idem nequiverint sollicite visitantes eas verbo ædificent et exemplo.
Whence, since it often happens that bishops on account of multiple occupations or bodily infirmities or hostile incursions or other occasions, not to say defect of knowledge, which in them is altogether to be reproved and from now on not to be tolerated, by themselves are not sufficient to minister to the people the Word of God, especially through ample and diffuse dioceses, by a general constitution we sanction that bishops suitable men for the office of holy preaching salubriously to carry out assume, powerful in work and speech, who the people to themselves committed, in their place when they themselves are not able, solicitously visiting, by word may edify and by example.
¶96
Quibus ipsi cum indiguerint congrue necessaria ministrent ne pro necessariorum defectu compellantur desistere ab incœpto.
To whom they themselves, when they shall have need, congruous necessities they minister, lest on account of the defect of necessities they may be compelled to desist from that which has been begun.
¶97
Unde præcipimus tam in cathedralibus quam in aliis conventualibus ecclesiis viros idoneos ordinari quos episcopi possint coadiutores et cooperatores habere non solum in prædicationis officio verum etiam in audiendis confessionibus et pœnitentiis iniungendis ac cæteris quæ ad salutem pertinent animarum.
Whence we command both in cathedral and in other conventual churches suitable men to be ordained, whom bishops may be able to have as coadjutors and cooperators, not only in the office of preaching but also in hearing confessions and enjoining penances and in the other things which pertain to the salvation of souls.
¶98
Si quis autem hoc neglexerit adimplere districtæ subiaceat ultioni.
If anyone however this to fulfill has neglected, to strict vengeance let him be subject.

CONSTITUTIO XI
CONSTITUTION XI
CAPUT XI
CHAPTER XI
Quia nonnullis propter inopiam et legendi studium et opportunitas proficiendi subtrahitur in Lateranensi concilio pia fuit institutione provisum ut per unamquamque cathedralem Ecclesiam magistro qui clericos eiusdem Ecclesiæ alios que scholares pauperes gratis instrueret aliquod competens beneficium præberetur quo et docentis relevaretur necessitas et via pateret discentibus ad doctrinam.
Because for not a few, on account of poverty, both the zeal for reading and the opportunity of profiting is withdrawn, in the Lateran Council by a pious institution it was provided that through each cathedral church to a master who the clerics of that church and other poor scholars freely should instruct, some competent benefice be provided, by which both the necessity of the teacher might be relieved and the way might lie open to learners for instruction.
¶100
Verum quoniam in multis ecclesiis id minime observatur nos prædictum roborantes statutum adicimus ut non solum in qualibet cathedrali Ecclesia sed etiam in aliis quarum sufficere poterunt facultates constituatur magister idoneus a prælato cum capitulo seu maiori ac saniori parte capituli eligendus qui clericos ecclesiarum ipsarum et aliarum gratis in grammaticæ facultate ac aliis instruat iuxta posse.
But since in many churches this is by no means observed, we, the aforesaid statute strengthening, add that not only in any cathedral church but also in others whose resources are able to suffice, a suitable master be appointed, by the prelate with the chapter or the greater or sounder part of the chapter to be chosen, who the clerics of those churches and others gratis in the faculty of grammar and in other things instruct according to his ability.
¶101
Sane metropolitana Ecclesia theologum nihilominus habeat qui sacerdotes et alios in sacra pagina doceat et in his præsertim informet quæ ad curam animarum spectare noscuntur.
Truly, the metropolitan church shall also have a theologian, who priests and others in sacred page may teach and in those things especially instruct which to the care of souls are known to pertain.
¶102
Assignetur autem cuilibet magistrorum a capitulo unius præbendæ proventus et pro theologo a metropolitano tantundem non quod per hoc efficiatur canonicus sed tamdiu redditus ipsius percipiat quamdiu perstiterit in docendo.
Let there be assigned moreover to each of the masters by the chapter the proceeds of one prebend, and for the theologian by the metropolitan an equal amount, not that by this he may become a canon, but for as long as he persists in teaching, for so long the income itself let him receive.
¶103
Quod si forte de duobus magistris metropolitana Ecclesia gravetur theologo iuxta modum prædictum ipsa provideat grammatico vero in alia Ecclesia suæ civitatis sive diœcesis quod sufficere valeat faciat provideri.
But if perhaps by two masters the metropolitan church should be burdened, for the theologian according to the aforesaid measure let the same church provide; for the grammarian however in another church of its city or diocese let it cause provision to be made which may be able to suffice.

CONSTITUTIO XII
CONSTITUTION XII
CAPUT XII
CHAPTER XII
In singulis regnis sive provinciis fiat de triennio in triennium salvo iure diœcesanorum pontificum commune capitulum abbatum atque priorum abbates proprios non habentium qui non consueverunt tale capitulum celebrare.
In each kingdom or province let there be done, from three years to three years, saving the right of diocesan pontiffs, a common chapter of abbots and of priors not having proper abbots, who have not been accustomed to celebrate such a chapter.
¶105
Ad quod universi conveniant præpeditionem canonicam non habentes apud unum de monasteriis ad hoc aptum hoc adhibito moderamine ut nullus eorum plus quam sex evectiones et octo personas adducat.
To which all shall convene, not having canonical hindrance, at one of the monasteries suitable for this, with this moderation applied, that none of them more than six riding horses and eight persons may bring.
¶106
Advocent autem caritative in huius novitatis primordiis duos Cisterciensis ordinis abbates vicinos ad præstandum sibi consilium et auxilium opportunum cum sint in huiusmodi capitulis celebrandis ex longa consuetudine plenius informati.
Let them invite moreover charitably, in the beginnings of this newness, two abbots of the Cistercian order, neighbors, to provide to themselves counsel and opportune aid, since they in such chapters to be celebrated by long custom are more fully informed.
¶107
Qui absque contradictione duos sibi de ipsis associent quos viderint expedire.
Who without contradiction let them associate two for themselves from those same persons whom they shall have seen to be expedient.
¶108
Ac ipsi quatuor præsint capitulo universo ita quod ex hoc nullus eorum auctoritatem prælationis assumat unde cum expedierit provida possint deliberatione mutari.
And those four let them preside over the universal chapter, in such a way that from this none of them the authority of prelation assumes; whence when it shall have been expedient, by provident deliberation they may be changed.
¶109
Huiusmodi vero capitulum aliquot certis diebus continue iuxta morem Cisterciensium celebretur in quo diligens habeatur tractatus de reformatione ordinis et observantia regulari et quod statutum fuerit illis quatuor approbantibus ab omnibus inviolabiliter observetur omni excusatione et contradictione ac appellatione remotis.
Such a chapter moreover on certain days continuously according to the custom of the Cistercians let it be celebrated, in which diligent treatment be had concerning the reformation of the order and regular observance, and what shall have been established, with those four approving, by all inviolably be observed, all excuse and contradiction and appeal being removed.
¶110
Proviso nihilominus ubi sequenti termino debeat capitulum celebrari.
Provision having been made nevertheless where at the following term the chapter ought to be celebrated.
¶111
Et qui convenerint vitam ducant communem et faciant proportionabiliter simul omnes communes expensas ita quod si non omnes potuerint in eisdem saltem plures simul in diversis domibus commorentur.
And those who shall have convened let them lead a common life and let them make proportionally together all common expenses, so that if not all have been able in the same, at least several together in diverse houses may abide.
¶112
Ordinentur etiam in eodem capitulo religiosæ ac circumspectæ personæ quæ singulas abbatias eiusdem regni sive provinciæ non solum monachorum sed etiam monialium secundum formam sibi præfixam vice nostra studeant visitare corrigentes et reformantes quæ correctionis et reformationis officio viderint indigere ita quod si rectorem loci cognoverint ab administratione penitus amovendum denuncient episcopo proprio ut illum amovere procuret.
Let there be ordained also in the same chapter religious and circumspect persons who each abbey of the same kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns, according to the form set before them, in our stead may strive to visit, correcting and reforming those things which the office of correction and reformation shall have seen to need, so that if the rector of the place they shall have known from administration altogether to be removed, let them denounce to the proper bishop, that he may cause him to be removed.
¶113
Quod si non fecerit ipsi visitatores hoc referant ad Apostolicæ Sedis examen.
But if he shall not have done it, let the visitors themselves refer this to the examination of the Apostolic See.
¶114
Hoc ipsum regulares canonicos secundum ordinem suum volumus et præcipimus observare.
This same thing we will and command that regular canons according to their order observe.
¶115
Si vero in hac novitate quicquam difficultatis emerserit quod per prædictas personas nequeat expediri ad Apostolicæ Sedis iudicium absque scandalo referatur cæteris irrefragabiliter observatis quæ concordi fuerint deliberatione provisa.
If indeed in this newness anything of difficulty shall have emerged which through the aforesaid persons cannot be expedited, to the judgment of the Apostolic See without scandal let it be referred, the other things irrevocably being observed which by concordant deliberation shall have been provided.
¶116
Porro diœcesani episcopi monasteria sibi subiecta ita studeant reformare ut cum ad ea prædicti visitatores accesserint plus in illis inveniant quod commendatione quam quod correctione sit dignum attentissime præcaventes ne per eos dicta monasteria indebitis oneribus aggraventur quia sic volumus superiorum iura servari ut inferiorum nolimus iniurias sustinere.
Moreover, diocesan bishops the monasteries subject to themselves so let them strive to reform that when to them the aforesaid visitors shall have come, more may they find in them which is worthy of commendation than which of correction, most attentively taking care lest through them the said monasteries by undue burdens may be aggravated, because thus we wish the rights of superiors to be preserved that we are unwilling injuries of inferiors to be sustained.
¶117
Ad hoc districte præcipimus tam diœcesanis episcopis quam personis que præerunt capitulis celebrandis ut per censuram ecclesiasticam appellatione remota compescant advocatos patronos vicedominos rectores et consules magnates et milites seu quoslibet alios ne monasteria præsumant offendere in personis ac rebus.
To this end we strictly command both the diocesan bishops and the persons who shall be in charge of chapters to be celebrated that through ecclesiastical censure, appeal being removed, they restrain advocates, patrons, vicedomini, rectors, and consuls, magnates and knights, or any others, lest monasteries presume to offend in persons and in things.
¶118
Et si forsitan offenderint eos ad satisfactionem compellere non omittant ut liberius et quietius omnipotenti Deo valeant famulari.
And if perhaps they shall have offended, to satisfaction to compel let them not omit, that more freely and more quietly to omnipotent God they may be able to serve.

CHAPTER XIII
Ne nimia religionum diversitas gravem in Ecclesia Dei confusionem inducat firmiter prohibemus ne quis de cætero novam religionem inveniat sed quicumque voluerit ad religionem converti unam de approbatis assumat.
Lest excessive diversity of religions grave confusion in the Church of God may induce, firmly we prohibit that anyone from now on a new religion may invent; but whoever wishes to convert to a religion, let him assume one of the approved.
¶120
Similiter qui voluerit religiosam domum fundare de novo regulam et institutionem accipiat de religionibus approbatis.
Similarly, he who wishes a religious house to found anew, a rule and institution let him receive from the approved religions.
¶121
Illud etiam prohibemus ne quis in diversis monasteriis locum monachi habere præsumat nec unus abbas pluribus monasteriis præsidere.
That also we prohibit, lest anyone in diverse monasteries the place of a monk presume to have, nor one abbot over many monasteries preside.

CONSTITUTIO XIV
CONSTITUTION XIV
CAPUT XIV
CHAPTER XIV
Ut clericorum mores et actus in melius reformentur continenter et caste vivere studeant universi præsertim in sacris ordinibus constituti ab omni libidinis vitio præcaventes maxime illo propter quod ira Dei venit de cœlo in filios diffidentiæ quatenus in conspectu Dei omnipotentis puro corde ac mundo corpore valeant ministrare.
That the morals and acts of clerics for the better may be reformed, continently and chastely let all strive to live, especially those constituted in sacred orders, from all vice of lust taking care, especially that on account of which the wrath of God comes from heaven upon sons of distrust, so that in the sight of omnipotent God with pure heart and clean body they may be able to minister.
¶123
Ne vero facilitas veniæ incentivum tribuat delinquendi statuimus ut qui deprehensi fuerint incontinentiæ vitio laborare prout magis aut minus peccaverint puniantur secundum canonicas sanctiones quas efficacius et districtius præcipimus observari ut quos divinus timor a malo non revocat temporalis saltem pœna a peccato cohibeat.
Lest however the facility of pardon an incentive may give to sinning, we establish that those who have been caught laboring under the vice of incontinence, just as more or less they shall have sinned, be punished according to the canonical sanctions, which we command more efficaciously and more strictly to be observed, so that those whom divine fear from evil does not recall, temporal at least punishment from sin may restrain.
¶124
Si quis igitur hac de causa suspensus divina celebrare præsumpserit non solum ecclesiasticis beneficiis spolietur verum etiam pro hac duplici culpa perpetuo deponatur.
If therefore anyone on this account suspended divine things to celebrate shall have presumed, not only from ecclesiastical benefices let him be despoiled, but also for this double fault perpetually let him be deposed.
¶125
Prælati vero qui tales præsumpserint in suis iniquitatibus sustinere maxime obtentu pecuniæ vel alterius commodi temporalis pari subiaceant ultioni.
Prelates indeed who such persons shall have presumed in their iniquities to sustain, especially for the sake of money or of other temporal advantage, to the same punishment let them be subject.
¶126
Qui autem secundum regionis suæ morem non abdicarunt copulam coniugalem si lapsi fuerint gravius puniantur cum legitimo matrimonio possint uti.
Those however who according to the custom of their region have not put away conjugal union, if they shall have lapsed, more gravely let them be punished, since legitimate marriage they are able to use.

CONSTITUTIO XV
CONSTITUTION XV
CAPUT XV
CHAPTER XV
A crapula et ebrietate omnes clerici diligenter abstineant unde vinum sibi temperent et se vino nec ad bibendum quispiam incitetur cum ebrietas et mentis inducat exilium et libidinis provocet incentivum.
From gluttony and drunkenness all clerics diligently shall abstain; whence wine for themselves let them temper, and to wine let no one be incited to drink, since drunkenness both the banishment of the mind induces and the incentive of lust provokes.
¶128
Unde illum abusum decernimus penitus abolendum quo in quibusdam partibus ad potus æquales suo modo se obligant potatores et ille iudicio talium plus laudatur qui plures inebriat et calices fæcundiores exhaurit.
Whence that abuse we decree altogether to be abolished whereby in certain parts to equal drinks in their own manner bind themselves the drinkers, and he by the judgment of such is more praised who more men inebriates and more fruitful cups empties.
¶129
Si quis autem super his culpabilem se exhibuerit nisi a superiore commonitus satisfecerit competenter a beneficio vel officio suspendatur.
If anyone however concerning these things culpable shall have shown himself, unless by a superior admonished he shall have satisfied competently, from benefice or office let him be suspended.
¶130
Venationem et aucupationem universis clericis interdicimus unde nec canes nec aves ad aucupandum habere præsumant.
Hunting and fowling we forbid to all clerics; whence neither dogs nor birds for fowling they shall presume to have.
¶131
CONSTITUTIO XVI
CONSTITUTION XVI
CAPUT XVI
CHAPTER XVI
Clerici officia vel commercia sæcularia non exerceant maxime inhonesta mimis ioculatoribus et histrionibus non intendant et tabernas prorsus evitent nisi forte causa necessitatis in itinere constituti.
Clerics secular offices or businesses shall not exercise, especially dishonest; to mimes, jesters, and actors let them not attend; and taverns altogether let them avoid, unless perhaps by necessity when placed on a journey.
¶132
Ad aleas vel taxillos non ludant nec huiusmodi ludis intersint.
At dice or at tables let them not play, nor to such games let them attend.
¶133
Coronam et tonsuram habeant congruentem et se in officiis divinis et aliis bonis exerceant studiis diligenter.
A crown and tonsure let them have congruent, and in divine offices and other good studies diligently let them be exercised.
¶134
Clausa deferant desuper indumenta nimia brevitate vel longitudine non notanda.
Closed garments let them wear, not to be noted for excessive brevity or length.
¶135
Pannis rubeis aut viridibus necnon manicis aut sotularibus consuticiis seu rostratis frenis sellis pectoralibus et calcaribus deauratis aut aliam superfluitatem gerentibus non utantur.
Red or green cloths, nor also sleeves or boots with stitching or beaked, reins, saddles, peytrals, and gilded spurs, or other superfluity bearing, let them not use.
¶136
Cappas manicatas ad divinum officium intra Ecclesiam non gerant sed nec alibi qui sunt in sacerdotio vel personatibus constituti nisi iusti causa timoris exegerit habitum transformari.
Hooded capes at divine office within church let them not wear, nor elsewhere who are in priesthood or in personates constituted, unless a just cause of fear shall have required the habit to be changed.
¶137
Fibulas omnino non ferant neque corrigias auri vel argenti ornatum habentes sed nec anulos nisi quibus competit ex officio dignitatis.
Brooches altogether let them not bear, nor belts having ornament of gold or silver, nor rings except those to whom it is fitting by virtue of their office of dignity.
¶138
Pontifices autem in publico et in Ecclesia superindumentis lineis omnes utantur nisi monachi fuerint quos oportet deferre habitum monachalem.
Pontiffs indeed in public and in church, overgarments of linen let all use, unless they are monks, whom it is fitting to wear a monastic habit.

CHAPTER XVII
Dolentes referimus quod non solum quidam minores clerici verum etiam aliqui ecclesiarum prælati circa comessationes superfluas et confabulationes illicitas ut de aliis taceamus fere medietatem noctis expendunt et somno residuum relinquentes vix ad diurnum concentum avium excitantur transcurrendo undique continuata syncopa matutinum.
Grieving we report that not only certain lesser clerics but also some prelates of churches concerning excessive feasting and illicit conversations, not to mention others, nearly half of the night spend, and to sleep the remainder leaving, scarcely at the diurnal assembly of birds are awakened, by running here and there with continuous syncopation Matins rushing through.
¶140
Sunt et alii qui missarum celebrant solemnia vix quater in anno et quod deterius est interesse contemnunt et si quando dum hæc celebrantur intersunt chori silentium fugientes intendunt externis collocutionibus laicorum dum que auditum ad indebitos sermones effundunt aures intentas non porrigunt ad divina.
There are also others who the solemnities of Mass celebrate scarcely four times in the year, and what is worse, to be present despise; and if at any time while these things are celebrated they are present, the silence of the choir fleeing, they intend to external conversations of laypersons; and while they pour out their hearing to indecent speeches, attentive ears they do not extend to divine things.
¶141
Hæc igitur et similia sub pœna suspensionis penitus inhibemus districte præcipientes in virtute obedientiæ ut divinum officium diurnum pariter et nocturnum quantum eis Deus dederit studiose celebrent pariter et devote.
These things therefore and similar things under penalty of suspension altogether we inhibit, strictly commanding in virtue of obedience that the divine office, both diurnal and nocturnal, as much as to them God shall have given, studiously and devoutly they celebrate equally.
¶142
CONSTITUTIO XVIII
CONSTITUTION XVIII
CAPUT XVIII
CHAPTER XVIII
Sententiam sanguinis nullus clericus dictet aut proferat sed nec sanguinis vindictam exerceat aut ubi exercetur intersit.
Sentence of blood no cleric shall dictate or pronounce, but nor blood vengeance exercise nor where it is exercised be present.
¶143
Si quis autem huiusmodi occasione statuti ecclesiis vel personis ecclesiasticis aliquod præsumpserit inferre dispendium per censuram ecclesiasticam compescatur nec quisquam clericus literas scribat aut dictet pro vindicta sanguinis destinandas unde in curiis principum hæc solicitudo non clericis sed laicis committatur.
If however anyone on occasion of this statute to churches or ecclesiastical persons any damage shall have presumed to bring, through ecclesiastical censure let him be repressed; nor let any cleric letters write or dictate for the vengeance of blood to be sent; whence in the courts of princes this solicitude not to clerics but to laypersons is to be entrusted.
¶144
Nullus quoque clericus rottariis aut balistariis aut huiusmodi viris sanguinum præponatur nec illam chirurgiæ artem subdiaconus diaconus vel sacerdos exerceant quæ ad ustionem vel incisionem inducit nec quisquam purgationi aquæ ferventis vel frigidæ seu ferri candentis ritum cuiuslibet benedictionis aut consecrationis impendat salvis nihilominus prohibitionibus de monomachiis sive duellis antea promulgatis.
Nor let any cleric be set over crossbowmen or arbalists or such men of blood, nor the art of surgery let a subdeacon, deacon, or priest exercise which leads to burning or cutting, nor for the purgation of boiling or cold water or of glowing iron the rite of any blessing or consecration let anyone bestow, saving nevertheless the prohibitions concerning duels or combats heretofore promulgated.

CONSTITUTION XIX
CAPUT XIX
CHAPTER XIX
Relinqui nolumus incorrectum quod quidam clerici sic exponunt ecclesias supellectilibus propriis et etiam alienis ut potius domus laicæ quam Dei basilicæ videantur non considerantes quod Dominus non sinebat ut vas transferretur per templum.
We are unwilling to leave uncorrected that certain clerics so expose churches with furnishings, both their own and also of others, that rather the house of a layperson than the basilica of God they appear, not considering that the Lord did not permit a vessel to be carried through the temple.
¶146
Sunt et alii qui non solum ecclesias dimittunt incultas verum etiam vasa ministerii et vestimenta ministrorum ac pallas altaris necnon et ipsa corporalia tam immunda relinquunt quod interdum aliquibus sunt horrori.
There are also others who not only churches leave uncultivated, but also the vessels of ministry and the vestments of ministers and the altar cloths, and also the corporals themselves, so unclean leave that sometimes to some they are a horror.
¶147
Quia vero zelus nos comedit domus Dei firmiter prohibemus ne huiusmodi supellectilia in ecclesias admittantur nisi propter hostiles incursus aut incendia repentina seu alias necessitates urgentes ad eas oporteat haberi refugium.
Because indeed zeal for the house of God consumes us, firmly we prohibit that such furnishings into churches be admitted unless on account of hostile incursions or sudden fires or other urgent necessities it may be necessary to have refuge to them.
¶148
Sic tamen ut necessitate cessante res in loca pristina reportentur.
Yet in such a way that when necessity ceases, the things to their former places be carried back.
¶149
Præcipimus quoque ut oratoria vasa corporalia et vestimenta prædicta munda et nitida conserventur.
We command also that oratories, vessels, corporals, and the aforesaid vestments clean and neat be preserved.
¶150
Nimis enim videtur absurdum in sacris sordes negligere quæ dedecerent etiam in prophanis.
Too absurd indeed it seems in sacred things sordidness to neglect which would disgrace even in profane things.

CONSTITUTION XX
CAPUT XX
CHAPTER XX
Statuimus ut in cunctis ecclesiis chrisma et eucharistia sub fideli custodia clavibus adhibitis conserventur ne possit ad illa temeraria manus extendi ad aliqua horribilia vel nefaria exercenda.
We establish that in all churches the chrism and eucharist under faithful custody, keys being applied, be kept, lest a rash hand be able to be extended to those things for any horrible or nefarious acts to be exercised.
¶152
Si vero is ad quem spectat custodia ea incaute reliquerit tribus mensibus ab officio suspendatur et si per eius incuriam aliquid nefandum inde contigerit graviori subiaceat ultioni.
If indeed he to whom the custody pertains shall have incautiously left them, for three months from office let him be suspended; and if through his incuria anything nefarious thence shall have happened, to a graver punishment let him be subject.

CONSTITUTIO XXI
CONSTITUTION XXI
CAPUT XXI
CHAPTER XXI
Omnis utriusque sexus fidelis postquam ad annos discretionis pervenerit omnia sua solus peccata confiteatur fideliter saltem semel in anno proprio sacerdoti et iniunctam sibi pœnitentiam studeat pro viribus adimplere suscipiens reverenter ad minus in pascha eucharistiæ sacramentum nisi forte de consilio proprii sacerdotis ob aliquam rationabilem causam ad tempus ab eius perceptione duxerit abstinendum.
Every faithful person of either sex, after he has reached the years of discretion, all his own sins alone let him faithfully confess at least once a year to his own priest, and the penance enjoined to him let him strive according to his strength to fulfill, reverently receiving at least at Easter the sacrament of the Eucharist, unless perhaps on the advice of his own priest on account of some reasonable cause for a time from its perception he has decided to abstain.
¶154
Alioquin et vivens ab ingressu Ecclesiæ arceatur et moriens christiana careat sepultura.
Otherwise, both living from the entrance of Church let him be barred, and dying let him lack christian burial.
¶155
Unde hoc salutare statutum frequenter in ecclesiis publicetur ne quisquam ignorantiæ cæcitate velamen excusationis assumat.
Whence this salutary statute frequently in churches let it be published, lest anyone the blindness of ignorance as a cloak of excuse may assume.
¶156
Si quis autem alieno sacerdoti voluerit iusta de causa sua confiteri peccata licentiam prius postulet et obtineat a proprio sacerdote cum aliter ille ipsum non possit solvere vel ligare.
If indeed anyone to another priest wishes on just cause his sins to confess, let him first request and obtain license from his own priest, since otherwise that priest is not able to loose or bind him.
¶157
Sacerdos autem sit discretus et cautus ut more periti medici superinfundat vinum et oleum vulneribus sauciati diligenter inquirens et peccatoris circumstantias et peccati per quas prudenter intelligat quale illi consilium debeat exhibere et cuiusmodi remedium adhibere diversis experimentis utendo ad sanandum ægrotum.
The priest however let him be discreet and cautious, so that after the manner of a skilled physician may pour wine and oil upon the wounds of the injured one, diligently inquiring both the circumstances of the sinner and of the sin, through which he may prudently understand what counsel he ought to give and what remedy to apply, using diverse experiments to heal the sick.
¶158
Caveat autem omnino ne verbo vel signo vel alio quovis modo prodat aliquatenus peccatorem sed si prudentiori consilio indiguerit illud absque ulla expressione personæ caute requirat quoniam qui peccatum in pœnitentiali iudicio sibi detectum præsumpserit revelare non solum a sacerdotali officio deponendum decernimus verum etiam ad agendam perpetuam pœnitentiam in arctum monasterium detrudendum.
Let him take care however altogether that neither by word nor sign nor any other way betray in any way the sinner; but if he shall have need of more prudent counsel, let him seek it cautiously without any expression of the person, since whoever a sin in penitential judgment to him detected shall have presumed to reveal, not only from the priestly office to be deposed we decree, but also to undergo perpetual penance into a strict monastery to be thrust.

CONSTITUTIO XXII
CONSTITUTION XXII
CAPUT XXII
CHAPTER XXII
Cum infirmitas corporalis nonnumquam ex peccato proveniat dicente Domino languido quem sanaverat vade et amplius noli peccare ne deterius aliquid tibi contingat decreto præsenti statuimus et districte præcipimus medicis corporum ut cum eos ad infirmos vocari contigerit ipsos ante omnia moneant et inducant quod medicos advocent animarum ut postquam infirmis fuerit de spirituali salute provisum ad corporalis medicinæ remedium salubrius procedatur cum causa cessante cesset effectus.
Since bodily infirmity not infrequently from sin proceeds, the Lord saying to the sick man whom He healed: "Go and sin no more, lest something worse happen to you," by the present decree we establish and strictly command physicians of bodies that when it shall happen that they are summoned to the sick, them before all things they admonish and induce that they call physicians of souls, so that after for the sick concerning spiritual salvation provision has been made, to the remedy of bodily medicine more salutarily it may be proceeded, since when cause ceases, the effect ceases.
¶160
Hoc quidem inter alia huic causam dedit edicto quod quidam in ægritudinis lecto iacentes cum eis a medicis suadetur ut de animarum salute disponant in desperationis articulum incidunt unde facilius mortis periculum incurrunt.
This indeed, among other things, cause gave to this edict, that certain persons lying in the bed of sickness, when by physicians they are advised that concerning the salvation of their souls they should arrange, into an articulation of desperation fall, whence more easily the peril of death they incur.
¶161
Si quis autem medicorum huius nostræ constitutionis postquam per prælatos locorum fuerit publicata transgressor exstiterit tamdiu ab ingressu Ecclesiæ arceatur donec pro transgressione huiusmodi satisfecerit competenter.
If however any of physicians of this our constitution after it shall have been published by the prelates of places a transgressor shall have been, for so long from the entrance of Church let him be barred until for a transgression of this kind he shall have satisfied competently.
¶162
Ceterum cum anima sit multo pretiosior corpore sub interminatione anathematis prohibemus ne quis medicorum pro corporali salute aliquid ægroto suadeat quod in periculum animæ convertatur.
Moreover, since the soul is much more precious than the body, under the threat of anathema we prohibit lest any of physicians for bodily salvation anything to the sick man persuade which into peril of the soul may be converted.

CONSTITUTIO XXIII
CONSTITUTION XXIII
CAPUT XXIII
CHAPTER XXIII
Ne pro defectu pastoris gregem dominicum lupus rapax invadat aut in facultatibus suis Ecclesia viduata grave dispendium patiatur volentes in hoc occurrere periculis animarum et ecclesiarum indemnitatibus providere statuimus ut ultra tres menses cathedralis vel regularis Ecclesia prælato non vacet infra quos iusto impedimento cessante si electio celebrata non fuerit qui eligere debuerant eligendi potestate careant ea vice ac ipsa eligendi potestas ad eum qui proximo præesse dignoscitur devolvatur.
Lest on account of lack of a shepherd the lordly flock a ravening wolf may invade, or in its goods the widowed Church grave damage may suffer, wishing in this to meet the perils of souls and to provide for the indemnities of churches, we establish that beyond three months a cathedral or regular church without a prelate not be vacant; within which, just impediment ceasing, if an election shall not have been celebrated, those who ought to elect the power of electing for that turn lose, and the power of electing itself to him who is known to be next in authority may be devolved.
¶164
Is vero ad quem devoluta fuerit potestas dominum habens præ oculis non differat ultra tres menses cum capituli sui consilio et aliorum virorum prudentium viduatam Ecclesiam de persona idonea ipsius quidem Ecclesiæ vel alterius si digna non reperiatur in illa canonice ordinare si canonicam voluerit effugere ultionem.
He indeed to whom the power shall have been devolved, having the Lord before his eyes, let him not delay beyond three months, with the counsel of his chapter and of other prudent men, the widowed Church with a suitable person, indeed of that Church or of another if a worthy one is not found in that one, canonically to order, if canonical vengeance he wishes to escape.

CONSTITUTION XXIV
CAPUT XXIV
CHAPTER XXIV
Quia propter electionum formas diversas quas quidam invenire conantur et multa impedimenta proveniunt et magna pericula imminent ecclesiis viduatis statuimus ut cum electio fuerit celebranda præsentibus omnibus qui debent et volunt et possunt commode interesse assumantur tres de collegio fide digni qui secreto et singulatim voces cunctorum diligenter exquirant et in scriptis redacta mox publicent in communi nullo prorsus appellationis obstaculo interiecto ut is collatione adhibita eligatur in quem omnes vel maior vel sanior pars capituli consentit.
Because on account of the diverse forms of elections, which certain persons strive to invent, both many impediments arise and great perils impend to widowed churches, we establish that when an election is to be celebrated, with all present who ought and are willing and are able conveniently to be present, let three be taken from the college, trustworthy in faith, who secretly and individually the votes of all diligently gather, and reduced into writing soon make public in common, with no obstacle of appeal whatsoever interposed, so that by comparison applied he may be elected upon whom all or the greater or the sounder part of the chapter consents.

Vel saltem eligendi potestas aliquibus viris idoneis committatur qui vice omnium Ecclesiæ viduatæ provideant de pastore.
Or at least the power of electing to certain suitable men be committed who in place of all for the widowed Church may provide concerning a pastor.
¶167
Aliter electio facta non valeat nisi forte communiter esset ab omnibus quasi per inspirationem divinam absque vitio celebrata.
Otherwise, an election made shall not be valid, unless perhaps commonly by all as if through divine inspiration without fault it would have been celebrated.
¶168
Qui vero contra prædictas formas eligere attentaverint eligendi ea vice potestate priventur.
Those who indeed against the aforesaid forms to elect shall have attempted, the power of electing for that turn let them lose.
¶169
Illud penitus interdicimus ne quis in electionis negotio procuratorem constituat nisi sit absens in eo loco de quo debeat advocari iusto que impedimento detentus venire non possit.
That altogether we forbid, lest anyone in the business of election a proxy constitute unless he is absent in that place from which he ought to be summoned and by just impediment detained is not able to come.

Super quo si fuerit opus fidem faciat iuramento et tunc si voluerit uni committat de ipso collegio vicem suam.
Concerning which, if it shall be necessary, let him make faith by oath, and then if he shall wish, let him commit to one from that same college his vote.

Electiones quoque clandestinas reprobamus statuentes ut quam cito electio fuerit celebrata solemniter publicetur.
Elections also clandestine we reprove, establishing that as soon as an election shall have been celebrated, solemnly it be published.

CHAPTER XXVI
Nihil est quod Ecclesiæ Dei magis officiat quam quod indigni assumantur prælati ad regimen animarum.
Nothing is which more may injure the Church of God than that unworthy persons are assumed as prelates to the governance of souls.
¶175
Volentes igitur huic morbo necessariam adhibere medelam irrefragabili constitutione sancimus quatenus cum quisquam fuerit ad regimen animarum assumptus is ad quem pertinet ipsius confirmatio diligenter examinet et electionis processum et personam electi ut cum omnia rite concurrerint munus ei confirmationis impendat. Quia si secus fuerit incaute præsumptum non solum deiciendus est indigne promotus verum etiam indigne promovens puniendus.
Willing therefore to this disease necessary remedy to apply, by irrevocable constitution we sanction that when anyone shall have been assumed to the governance of souls, he to whom the confirmation of the same pertains diligently examine both the process of the election and the person of the elect, so that when all things have rightly concurred, the gift of confirmation to him he may bestow. Because if otherwise it shall have been incautiously presumed, not only is he to be cast down who has been unworthily promoted, but truly also he who unworthily promotes is to be punished.
¶176
Ipsum quoque decernimus hac animadversione puniri ut cum de ipsius constiterit negligentia maxime si hominem insufficientis scientiæ vel inhonestæ vitæ aut ætatis illegitimæ approbaverit non solum confirmandi primum successorem illius careat potestate verum etiam ne aliquo casu pœnam effugiat a perceptione proprii beneficii suspendatur quousque si æquum fuerit indulgentiam valeat promereri. Si convictus fuerit in hoc per malitiam excessisse graviori subiaceat ultioni. Episcopi quoque tales ad sacros ordines et ecclesiasticas dignitates promovere procurent qui commissum sibi officium digne valeant adimplere si et ipsi canonicam cupiunt effugere ultionem.
Him also we decree by this animadversion to be punished, that when concerning his negligence it shall have been established, especially if a man of insufficient knowledge or of dishonest life or of illegitimate age he shall have approved, not only the power of confirming the first successor of that one let him lack, but also lest by any case he may escape punishment, from the perception of his own benefice let him be suspended until, if it shall be equitable, he may be able to merit indulgence. If he shall have been convicted in this thing by malice to have exceeded, to a graver punishment let him be subject. Bishops also such men to sacred orders and ecclesiastical dignities shall strive to promote who the office committed to themselves worthily are able to fulfill, if they themselves also wish canonical vengeance to escape.
¶177
Cæterum qui ad Romanum pertinent immediate pontificem ad percipiendam sui confirmationem officii eius se conspectui si commode potest fieri personaliter repræsentent vel personas transmittant idoneas per quas diligens inquisitio super electionis processu et electis possit haberi ut sic demum per ipsius circumspectionem consilii sui plenitudinem assequantur officii cum eis nihil obstiterit de canonicis institutis ita quod interim valde remoti videlicet ultra Italiam constituti si electi fuerint in concordia dispensative propter necessitates et utilitates ecclesiarum in spiritualibus et temporalibus administrent sic tamen ut de rebus ecclesiasticis nil penitus alienent. Moreover, those who pertain immediately to the Roman pontiff, to receive the confirmation of their office, to his sight, if it can be done conveniently, personally let them present themselves, or suitable persons send, through whom diligent inquisition concerning the process of the election and concerning the elect may be had, so that thus at length through his circumspection the fullness of counsel they may obtain for their office, when nothing shall have hindered them from canonical institutes, so that in the meantime the very remote, namely those constituted beyond Italy, if they shall have been elected in concord, dispensationally on account of the necessities and utilities of churches, in spiritual and temporal things may administer, yet in such a way that concerning ecclesiastical things nothing at all they alienate. The gift of consecration indeed or of benediction let them receive just as thus far they have been accustomed to receive.

By great providence it was in the Lateran Council prohibited that anyone diverse ecclesiastical dignities and multiple parish churches should receive against the institutes of the sacred canons; otherwise both the one receiving should lose what he has thus received, and the one conferring should be deprived of the power of bestowing.

Quia vero propter præsumptiones et cupiditates quorundam nullus hactenus fructus aut rarus de prædicto statuto provenit nos evidentius et expressius occurrere cupientes præsenti decreto statuimus ut quicumque receperit aliquod beneficium habens curam animarum annexam si prius tale beneficium obtinebat eo sit iure ipso privatus et si forte illud retinere contenderit alio etiam spolietur.
Because indeed on account of the presumptions and cupidities of certain persons, no fruit thus far or a rare one from the aforesaid statute has resulted, we, wishing more evidently and more expressly to meet these things, by the present decree establish that whoever shall have received any benefice having the cure of souls annexed, if he previously was obtaining such a benefice, by the law itself let him be deprived, and if perhaps he shall have contended to retain that one, from the other also let him be despoiled.

Is quoque ad quem prioris spectat donatio illud post receptionem alterius libere conferat cui merito viderit conferendum et si ultra tres menses conferre distulerit non solum ad alium secundum statutum Lateranensis concilii eius collatio devolvatur verum etiam tantum de suis cogatur proventibus in utilitatem Ecclesiæ cuius illud est beneficium assignare quantum a tempore vacationis ipsius constiterit ex eo esse perceptum.
He also to whom the donation of the prior pertains, that after the reception of the other, freely let him confer upon whom he shall have deservedly seen to be conferred; and if beyond three months to confer he shall have delayed, not only to another according to the statute of the Lateran Council his collation may be devolved, but also as much from his own proceeds in utility of the Church whose that benefice is let him be compelled to assign as much as from the time of its vacancy it shall have been established that from it was received.

This same thing in personates we decree to be observed, adding that in the same church no one multiple dignities or personates presume to have, even if they do not have cure of souls.

A vice of custom to be extirpated has grown inveterate in certain parts, that namely the patrons of parish churches and certain other persons the proceeds of the same churches altogether to themselves vindicating, to the presbyters of the same assigned to their service leave so small a portion that from it they are not able congruously to be sustained. For, as we have certainly learned, in certain regions parish presbyters for their sustenance do not obtain except a fourth of a fourth, that is, the sixteenth part of tithes.

Whence it happens that in these regions almost no parish priest is found who even moderate knowledge of letters has.

Since therefore the mouth of the ox treading out the grain ought not to be muzzled, but he who serves the altar ought to live from the altar, we establish that, any custom of bishop or patron or of any other notwithstanding, a portion sufficient to the presbyters themselves be assigned.

Quoniam contra falsam assertionem iniqui iudicis innocens litigator quandoque non potest veram negationem probare cum negantis factum per rerum naturam nulla sit directa probatio ne falsitas veritati præiudicet aut iniquitas prævaleat æquitati statuimus ut tam in ordinario iudicio quam extraordinario iudex semper adhibeat aut publicam si potest habere personam aut duos viros idoneos qui fideliter universa iudicii acta conscribant videlicet citationes dilationes recusationes et exceptiones petitiones et responsiones interrogationes confessiones testium depositiones instrumentorum productiones interlocutiones appellationes renunciationes conclusiones et cætera quæ occurrunt competenti ordine conscribenda designando loca tempora et personas et omnia sic conscripta partibus tribuantur ita quod originalia penes scriptores remaneant ut si super processu iudicis fuerit suborta contentio per hæc possit veritas declarari.
Since against the false assertion of an iniquitous judge an innocent litigator sometimes is not able a true denial to prove, since of a person denying a fact by the nature of things there is no direct proof, lest falsity may prejudice truth or iniquity may prevail over equity, we establish that both in ordinary and in extraordinary judgment the judge always either employ a public person, if he is able to have one, or two suitable men who faithfully all the acts of the judgment may write down, namely citations, delays, recusations and exceptions, petitions and responses, interrogations, confessions, depositions of witnesses, productions of instruments, interlocutory orders, appeals, renunciations, conclusions, and the other things which occur, in competent order to be written down, designating places, times, and persons; and all things thus written down let them be given to the parties, so that the originals may remain with the writers, so that if concerning the process of the judge a dispute shall have arisen, through these things the truth may be declared.
¶219
Hoc adhibito moderamine quatenus sic honestis et discretis deferatur iudicibus quod per improvidos et iniquos innocentum iustitia non lædatur.
This moderation being applied, that thus honest and discreet judges be deferred to, that by the improvident and iniquitous the justice of the innocent may not be injured.
¶220
Iudex autem qui constitutionem istam neglexerit observare si propter eius negligentiam aliquid difficultatis emerserit per superiorem iudicem animadversione debita castigetur nec pro ipsius præsumatur processu nisi quatenus in causa legitimis constiterit documentis.
A judge however who this constitution to observe shall have neglected, if on account of his negligence any difficulty shall have emerged, by a superior judge with due animadversion let him be chastised, nor concerning his process let it be presumed except insofar as in the case it shall have been established by legitimate documents.

Sæpe contingit quod spoliatus iniuste per spoliatorem in alium re translata dum adversus possessorem non subvenitur per restitutionis beneficium spoliato commodo possessionis amisso propter difficultatem probationis ius proprietatis amittit effectum.
It often happens that the one who has been unjustly despoiled, after the thing has been transferred into another by the despoiler, when against the possessor relief is not given through the benefit of restitution, the advantage of possession having been lost on account of the difficulty of proof, loses the effect of the right of property.
¶222
Unde non obstante civilis iuris rigore sancimus ut si quis de cætero scienter rem talem acceperit cum spoliatori quasi succedat in vitium eo quod non multum intersit præsertim quoad periculum animæ detinere iniuste ac invadere alienum contra possessorem huiusmodi spoliato per restitutionis beneficium succurratur.
Whence, not withstanding the rigor of civil law, we sanction that if anyone from now on knowingly a thing such has received, since he as it were succeeds the despoiler in the vice, because it does not much differ, especially with regard to the peril of the soul, to retain unjustly and to invade another's property, against a possessor of this kind to the despoiled one by the benefit of restitution relief be given.

Contingit interdum quod cum actori ob contumaciam partis adversæ adiudicatur causa rei servandæ possessio propter rei potentiam sive dolum actor infra annum rem custodiendam nancisci non potest vel nactam amittit et sic cum secundum assertionem multorum verus non efficeretur post lapsum anni possessor reportat commodum de malitia sua reus.
It happens sometimes that when on account of the contumacy of the adverse party the cause is adjudicated to the plaintiff, the possession of the thing to be preserved, on account of the power or the deceit of the defendant, within the year the plaintiff is not able to obtain the thing to be preserved, or having obtained, loses it; and thus, according to the assertion of many, he would not become the true possessor; after the lapse of the year the defendant obtains the advantage from his malice.
¶224
Ne igitur contumax melioris quam obediens conditionis exsistat de canonica æquitate sancimus ut in casu præmisso actor verus constituatur elapso anno possessor.
Lest therefore a contumacious person should be in a better condition than an obedient one, from canonical equity we sanction that in the aforesaid case the plaintiff be constituted the true possessor after the year has elapsed.

Ad hæc generaliter prohibemus ne super rebus spiritualibus compromittatur in laicum quia non decet ut laicus in talibus arbitretur.
To these things generally we prohibit lest concerning spiritual things compromise be made to a layperson, because it is not fitting that a layperson in such things should act as arbitrator.

Quoniam omne quod non est ex fide peccatum est synodali iudicio definimus ut nulla valeat absque bona fide præscriptio tam canonica quam civilis cum sit generaliter omni constitutioni atque consuetudini derogandum quæ absque mortali non potest observari peccato.
Since everything which is not of faith is sin, by synodal judgment we define that no prescription, whether canonical or civil, is valid without good faith, since it is to be derogated generally from every constitution and custom which cannot be observed without mortal sin.

Unde oportet ut qui præscribit in nulla temporis parte rei habeat conscientiam alienæ.
Whence it is fitting that he who prescribes in no part of time may have consciousness of another's property.

Sicut volumus ut iura clericorum non usurpent laici ita velle debemus ne clerici iura sibi vindicent laicorum.
Just as we wish that the rights of clerics not be usurped by laypersons, so we ought to wish that clerics not claim for themselves the rights of laypersons.
¶229
Quocirca universis clericis interdicimus ne quis prætextu ecclesiasticæ libertatis suam de cætero iurisdictionem extendat in præiudicium iustitiæ sæcularis sed contentus exsistat constitutionibus scriptis et consuetudinibus hactenus approbatis ut quæ sunt Cæsaris reddantur Cæsari et quæ sunt Dei Deo recta distributione reddantur.
Wherefore we forbid all clerics that anyone under pretext of ecclesiastical liberty should henceforth extend his jurisdiction to the prejudice of secular justice, but let him be content with the constitutions written and customs hitherto approved, that the things which are Caesar's may be rendered to Caesar and the things which are God's to God, by a just distribution being rendered.

CHAPTER XLIII
Nimis de iure divino quidam laici usurpare conantur cum viros ecclesiasticos nihil temporale detinentes ab eis ad præstandum sibi fidelitatis iuramenta compellunt.
Too much from divine law certain laypersons attempt to usurp when they compel ecclesiastical men, holding no temporal thing, to give to them oaths of fidelity.

Quia vero secundum apostolum servus suo Domino stat aut cadit sacri auctoritate concilii prohibemus ne tales clerici personis sæcularibus præstare cogantur huiusmodi iuramentum.
Because indeed according to the apostle, the servant stands or falls by his own Lord, by the authority of the sacred council we prohibit that such clerics be compelled to give such an oath to secular persons.

CONSTITUTION XLIV
CAPUT XLIV
CHAPTER XLIV
Cum laicis quamvis religiosis disponendi de rebus ecclesiasticis nulla sit attributa potestas quos subsequendi manet necessitas non auctoritas imperandi dolemus sic in quibusdam ex illis refrigescere caritatem quod immunitatem ecclesiasticæ libertatis quam nedum sancti patres sed etiam principes sæculares multis privilegiis munierunt non formidant suis constitutionibus vel potius confictionibus impugnare non solum de feudorum alienatione ac aliarum possessionum ecclesiasticarum et usurpatione iurisdictionum sed etiam de mortuariis necnon et aliis quæ spirituali iuri videntur annexa illicite præsumendo.
Since to laypersons, even religious ones, no power of disposing concerning ecclesiastical things has been attributed, for whom the necessity of following remains, not the authority of commanding, we grieve that in certain of them charity has so grown cold that the immunity of ecclesiastical liberty, which not only the holy fathers but also secular princes have fortified with many privileges, they do not fear to assail with their constitutions or rather confections, not only concerning the alienation of fiefs and of other ecclesiastical possessions and the usurpation of jurisdictions, but also concerning mortuaries and other things which seem to be annexed to spiritual right, illicitly presuming.
¶233
Volentes igitur super his ecclesiarum indemnitati consulere ac tantis gravaminibus providere constitutiones huiusmodi et vindicationes feudorum seu aliorum bonorum ecclesiasticorum sine legitimo ecclesiasticarum personarum assensu præsumptas occasione constitutionis laicæ potestatis cum non constitutio sed destitutio vel destructio dici possit necnon usurpatio iurisdictionum sacri approbatione concilii decernimus non tenere præsumptoribus per censuram ecclesiasticam compescendis.
Willing therefore concerning these things to consult the indemnity of churches and to provide for such great burdens, constitutions of this kind and vindications of fiefs or other ecclesiastical goods, without legitimate assent of ecclesiastical persons presumed, on occasion of a lay power's constitution (since it can be called not a constitution but a destitution or destruction), as well as the usurpation of jurisdictions, by the approval of the sacred council we decree not to be valid, the presumptuous ones through ecclesiastical censure to be repressed.

CONSTITUTIO XLV
CONSTITUTION XLV
CAPUT XLV
CHAPTER XLV
In quibusdam provinciis ecclesiarum patroni seu vicedomini et advocati se in tantam insolentiam erexerunt quod non solum cum vacantibus debet ecclesiis de pastoribus idoneis provideri difficultates ingerunt et malitias verum etiam de possessionibus et aliis bonis ecclesiasticis pro sua voluntate ordinare præsumunt et quod horrendum est dicere in necem prælatorum prorumpere non formidant.
In certain provinces the patrons of churches or vicedomini and advocates have risen to such great insolence that not only concerning vacant churches which ought to be provided with suitable pastors do they inject difficulties and malicious tricks, but also concerning possessions and other ecclesiastical goods according to their own will they presume to order, and, which is horrible to say, to rush forth to the slaughter of prelates they do not fear.
¶235
Cum igitur quod ad defensionis subsidium est inventum ad depressionis dispendium non debeat retorqueri prohibemus expresse ne patroni vel advocati seu vicedomini super præmissis de cætero plus usurpent quam reperiatur in iure permissum.
Since therefore that which for the subsidy of defense was invented ought not to be turned back to the loss of depression, we expressly prohibit that patrons or advocates or vicedomini concerning the aforesaid henceforth more usurp than is found permitted by law.
¶236
Et si contra præsumpserint districtissime per severitatem canonicam compescantur.
And if they shall have presumed against this, most strictly by canonical severity let them be repressed.
¶237
Sacri nihilominus concilii approbatione statuimus quatenus si patroni vel advocati aut feudatarii seu vicedomini seu alii beneficiati alicuius Ecclesiæ rectorem vel clericum alium ipsius Ecclesiæ per se vel per alios occidere vel mutilare ausu nefando præsumpserint patroni ius patronatus advocati advocatiam feudatarii feudum vicedomini vicedominatum beneficiati beneficium prorsus amittant.
By the approval of the sacred council nevertheless we establish that if patrons or advocates or feudatories or vicedomini or other beneficiaries of any church, the rector or another cleric of that same church by themselves or through others to kill or to mutilate by nefarious daring shall have presumed, patrons lose the right of patronage, advocates the advocacy, feudatories the fief, vicedomini the vicedominatus, beneficiaries the benefice altogether.
¶238
Et ne minus vindictæ quam excessus memoria prorogetur non solum de præmissis nil perveniat ad heredes sed etiam usque ad quartam generationem posteritates talium in clericorum collegium nullatenus admittantur nec in regularibus domibus alicuius prælationis assequantur honorem nisi cum eis fuerit misericorditer dispensatum.
And lest less of vengeance than of excess may the memory be prolonged, not only concerning the aforesaid things let nothing come to the heirs, but also up to the fourth generation let the posterities of such persons by no means be admitted into the college of clerics, nor in regular houses may they obtain the honor of any prelation, unless with them it shall have been mercifully dispensed.

CONSTITUTION XLVI
CAPUT XLVI
CHAPTER XLVI
Adversus consules ac rectores civitatum et alios qui ecclesias et viros ecclesiasticos talliis seu collectis et exactionibus aliis aggravare nituntur volens immunitati ecclesiasticæ Lateranense concilium providere præsumptionem huiusmodi sub anathematis districtione prohibuit transgressores et fautores eorum excommunicationi præcipiens subiacere donec satisfactionem impendant competentem.
Against consuls and rectors of cities and others who strive to burden churches and ecclesiastical men with tallages or collections and other exactions, wishing to provide for ecclesiastical immunity, the Lateran Council forbade presumption of this kind under the stricture of anathema, commanding that transgressors and their favorers be subject to excommunication until they shall have paid competent satisfaction.
¶240
Verum si quando forsan episcopus simul cum clericis tantam necessitatem vel utilitatem prospexerint ut absque ulla coactione ad relevandas utilitates vel necessitates communes ubi laicorum non suppetunt facultates subsidia per ecclesias duxerint conferenda prædicti laici humiliter et devote recipiant cum actionibus gratiarum.
But if perhaps at any time a bishop together with the clerics shall have seen so great a necessity or utility that without any coercion, for relieving common utilities or necessities where the resources of laypersons do not suffice, subsidies through churches should be judged to be contributed, let the aforesaid laypersons humbly and devoutly receive them with acts of thanksgiving.
¶241
Propter imprudentiam tamen quorundam Romanum prius consulant pontificem cuius interest communibus utilitatibus providere.
On account of the imprudence however of certain persons, let them first consult the Roman pontiff, whose interest it is to provide for common utilities.
¶242
Quoniam vero nec sic quorundam malitia contra Dei Ecclesiam conquievit adicimus ut constitutiones et sententiæ quæ ab excommunicatis huiusmodi vel de ipsorum mandato fuerint promulgatæ inanes et irritæ habeantur nullo unquam tempore valituræ.
Since indeed not even thus has the malice of certain persons rested against the Church of God, we add that constitutions and sentences which by such excommunicates or at their mandate shall have been promulgated, be held vain and invalid, at no time to be valid.
¶243
Cæterum quia fraus et dolus alicui patrocinari non debent nullus vano decipiatur errore ut infra tempus regiminis sustineat anathema quasi post illud non sit ad satisfactionem debitam compellendus.
Moreover, because fraud and deceit ought not to patronize anyone, let no one be deceived by a vain error that during the time of governance he may sustain anathema as if after that he is not to be compelled to due satisfaction.
¶244
Nam et ipsum qui satisfacere recusaverit et successorem ipsius si non satisfecerit infra mensem manere decernimus ecclesiastica censura conclusum donec satisfecerit competenter cum succedat in onere qui substituitur in honore.
For both him who refuses to satisfy and his successor, if he shall not have satisfied within a month, we decree to remain bound by ecclesiastical censure until he shall have satisfied competently, since he succeeds in the burden who is substituted in the honor.

CHAPTER XLVII
Sacro approbante concilio prohibemus ne quis in aliquem excommunicationis sententiam nisi competenti commonitione præmissa et præsentibus idoneis personis per quas si necesse fuerit possit probari monitio promulgare præsumat.
With the sacred council approving, we forbid that anyone presume to pronounce a sentence of excommunication against anyone unless a competent admonition has preceded and suitable persons are present through whom, if necessary, the admonition may be proved.
¶246
Quod si contra præsumpserit etiamsi iusta fuerit excommunicationis sententia ingressum Ecclesiæ per mensem unum sibi noverit interdictum alia nihilominus pœna mulctandus si visum fuerit expedire. Caveat etiam diligenter ne ad excommunicationem cuiusquam absque manifesta et rationabili causa procedat.
But if he shall have presumed against this, even if the sentence of excommunication was just, for one month let him know the entrance of the church to be forbidden to himself, being otherwise to be mulcted with another penalty if it shall have seemed expedient. Let him also take care diligently lest he proceed to the excommunication of anyone without manifest and reasonable cause.
¶247
Ad quam si forte taliter processerit et requisitus humiliter processum huiusmodi non curaverit absque gravamine revocare gravatus apud superiorem deponat de iniusta excommunicatione querelam. Quod si absque periculo moræ potest ad excommunicatorem illum cum suo mandato remittat infra competentem terminum absolvendum alioquin ipse per se vel per alium prout viderit expedire sufficienti cautione recepta munus ei absolutionis impendat.
But if perhaps he shall have so proceeded and, being humbly requested, has not taken care to revoke such process without burden, let the burdened party lodge a complaint concerning unjust excommunication before the superior. But if without peril of delay he is able, let him remit him to that excommunicator with his mandate, within a competent term to be absolved; otherwise, let him himself by himself or through another, as he shall have seen expedient, sufficient caution having been received, bestow upon him the gift of absolution.
¶248
Cum que adversus excommunicatorem de iniusta excommunicatione constiterit excommunicator condemnetur excommunicato ad interesse alias nihilominus si culpæ qualitas postulaverit superioris arbitrio puniendus cum non levis sit culpa tantam infligere pœnam insonti nisi forsan erraverit ex causa probabili maxime si laudabilis opinionis existat.
And when concerning unjust excommunication it shall have been established against the excommunicator, let the excommunicator be condemned to the excommunicated for interest, otherwise also, if the quality of the fault shall have demanded, to be punished by the decision of a superior, since it is no light fault to inflict so great a penalty upon an innocent person, unless perhaps he shall have erred from a probable cause, especially if he is of laudable opinion.
¶249
Verum si contra excommunicationis sententiam nihil rationabile fuerit a conquerente probatum et idem super iniusta conquestionis molestia per pœnam ad interesse vel alias secundum superioris arbitrium condemnetur nisi forsan et ipsum probabilis error excuset et super eo pro quo iusta fuerit excommunicatione ligatus per cautionem receptam satisfacere compellatur vel in pristinam reducatur sententiam usque ad satisfactionem condignam inviolabiliter observandam.
But if against the sentence of excommunication nothing reasonable shall have been proven by the complainant, and the same one concerning the annoyance of unjust complaint by a penalty for interest or otherwise according to the decision of the superior let him be condemned, unless perhaps even a probable error excuses him; and concerning that for which he was bound by just excommunication, by a caution received let him be compelled to satisfy, or let him be reduced to the former sentence inviolably to be observed until condign satisfaction.
¶250
Si vero iudex suum recognoscens errorem paratus sit talem revocare sententiam et is pro quo lata fuerit ne absque satisfactione revocet illam appellet appellationi non deferat in hac parte nisi talis sit error de quo merito possit dubitari. Et tunc sufficienti cautione recepta quod coram eo ad quem exstitit appellatum vel delegato ab ipso iuri parebit excommunicatum absolvat sic que pœnæ præscriptæ minime subiacebit cavens omnino ne voluntate perversa in alterius præiudicium mentiatur errorem si districtionis canonicæ vult effugere ultionem.
If indeed a judge, recognizing his error, is prepared to revoke such a sentence, and he for whom it was pronounced appeals lest he revoke it without satisfaction, let him not defer to the appeal in this part, unless such is the error that it may rightly be doubted concerning it. And then, sufficient caution having been received that before him to whom it has been appealed or a delegate from him he will comply with the law, let him absolve the excommunicated person, and thus to the prescribed penalty he will by no means be subject, taking care altogether lest by a perverse will he lie about error to the prejudice of another, if he wishes to escape canonical severity.

CHAPTER XLVIII
Cum speciali sit prohibitione provisum ne quis in aliquem excommunicationis sententiam nisi competenti commonitione præmissa promulgare præsumat volentes etiam providere ne forte commonitus frustratoriæ recusationis vel appellationis obtentu monentis declinare possit examen statuimus quod si allegaverit se iudicem habere suspectum coram eodem causam iustæ suspicionis assignet.
Since it has been provided by a special prohibition that no one presume to pronounce a sentence of excommunication against anyone unless a competent admonition has preceded, wishing also to provide lest perhaps, being admonished, he may be able to avoid the examination of the admonisher by the pretext of a frivolous recusation or appeal, we establish that if he shall have alleged that he holds the judge as suspect, before the same let him assign the cause of just suspicion.
¶252
Et ipse cum adversario vel si forte adversarium non habeat cum iudice arbitros communiter eligat aut si forte communiter convenire non possunt eligant absque malitia ipse unum et ille alium qui de suspicionis causa cognoscant.
And let him with the adversary, or if perhaps he does not have an adversary then with the judge, choose arbiters in common; or if perhaps they are not able to agree in common, let them choose without malice: he one, and the other another, who may take cognizance concerning the cause of suspicion.
¶253
Et si nequiverint in unam concordare sententiam advocent tertium ut quod duo ex ipsis decreverint robur obtineat firmitatis.
And if they shall not have been able to agree into one sentence, let them call a third, so that what two of them shall have decreed may obtain the force of firmness.
¶254
Sciant quoque se ad id fideliter exequendum ex iniuncto a nobis in virtute obedientiæ sub attestatione divini iudicii districto præcepto teneri.
Let them know also that they are held to fulfill this faithfully by what has been enjoined by us, in virtue of obedience, under the attestation of divine judgment, by strict command.
¶255
Causa vero suspicionis legitima coram ipsis infra competentem terminum non probata sua iurisdictione iudex utatur.
But the cause of suspicion not having been proven legitimate before them within a competent term, let the judge use his own jurisdiction.
¶256
At ipsa probata legitime de recusatoris assensu personæ idoneæ committat negocium recusatus vel ad superiorem transmittat ut in eo ipse procedat secundum quod fuerit procedendum.
But it having been proven legitimately, with the assent of the recusator, let the recused judge commit the business to suitable persons, or let him transmit it to a superior, so that in it he himself may proceed according to what is to be proceeded.
¶257
Porro commonito ad appellationem convolante si eius excessus evidentia rei vel ipsius confessione aut alio modo legitime fuerit manifestatus cum appellationis remedium non sit ad defensionem iniquitatis sed in præsidium innocentiæ institutum non est provocationi huiusmodi deferendum.
Moreover, when the admonished person flies to an appeal, if his excess has been manifest by the evidence of the thing or by his own confession or in another way legitimately, since the remedy of appeal is not instituted for the defense of iniquity but for the protection of innocence, to a provocation of this kind it is not to be deferred.
¶258
Excessu quoque dubio exsistente ne frivolæ appellationis diffugio appellans iudicis processum impediat coram eodem probabilem causam appellationis exponat talem videlicet quæ si foret probata deberet legitima reputari.
The excess also being doubtful, lest by the refuge of a frivolous appeal the appellant may impede the process of the judge, before the same judge let him set forth a probable cause of appeal, such namely that if it were proven it ought to be considered legitimate.
¶259
Et tunc si habuerit adversarium infra terminum secundum locorum distantiam et temporis qualitatem et naturam negotii ab eodem iudice moderandum appellationis causam prosequatur.
And then if he has an adversary, within a term according to the distance of places and the quality of time and the nature of the business, by the same judge to be moderated, let him pursue the cause of appeal.
¶260
Quam si prosequi non curaverit ex ipse iudex non obstante appellatione procedat.
Which if he shall not have taken care to pursue, by the same judge, notwithstanding the appeal, let him proceed.
¶261
Nullo autem adversario comparente cum ex suo iudex procedat officio appellationis causa coram superiore probata superior suæ iurisdictionis officium exequatur. Sed si appellans in eius probatione defecerit ad eum a quo ipsum malitiose appellasse constiterit remittatur.
With no adversary appearing, since the judge proceeds from his own office, let the cause of appeal be proven before the superior; the superior shall carry out the office of his jurisdiction. But if the appellant fails in its proof, let him be remitted to him from whom it shall be established that he has appealed maliciously.
¶262
Cæterum has duas constitutiones præmissas nolumus ad regulares extendi qui suas habent observantias speciales.
Moreover, these two preceding constitutions we are unwilling to be extended to regulars, who have their own special observances.

CHAPTER XLIX
Sub interminatione divini iudicii penitus interdicimus ut causa cupiditatis nullus audeat excommunicationis vinculo aliquem innodare vel absolvere innodatum in illis maxime regionibus in quibus ex consuetudine cum excommunicatus absolvitur pecuniaria pœna mulctatur.
Under the threat of divine judgment altogether we forbid that for the sake of cupidity anyone dare to bind anyone by the bond of excommunication or to absolve one already bound, in those regions especially in which by custom, when an excommunicated person is absolved, he is mulcted with a pecuniary penalty.
¶264
Statuentes ut cum excommunicationis sententiam iniustam fuisse constiterit excommunicator ad restituendam pecuniam sic extortam per censuram ecclesiasticam compellatur et nisi probabili fuerit errore deceptus tantundem iniuriam passo persolvat et si forte solvendo non fuerit animadversione alia castigetur.
Establishing that when the sentence of excommunication shall have been established to have been unjust, let the excommunicator be compelled by ecclesiastical censure to restore the money thus extorted, and unless he has been deceived by a probable error, let him pay an equal amount to the injured party; and if perhaps he shall not be able to pay, let him be chastised by another animadversion.

CONSTITUTIO L
CONSTITUTION L
CAPUT L
CHAPTER L
Non debet reprehensibile iudicari si secundum varietatem temporum statuta quandoque varientur humana præsertim cum urgens necessitas vel evidens utilitas id exposcerit quoniam ipse Deus ex his quæ in veteri testamento statuerat nonnulla mutavit in novo.
It ought not to be judged reprehensible if according to the variety of times human statutes are sometimes varied, especially when urgent necessity or evident utility demands it, since God Himself from those things which He established in the Old Testament changed some things in the New.
¶266
Cum igitur prohibitiones de coniugio in secundo et tertio affinitatis genere minime contrahendo et de sobole suscepta ex secundis nuptiis cognationi viri non copulanda prioris et difficultatem frequenter inducant et aliquando periculum pariant animarum ut cessante prohibitione cesset effectus constitutiones super hoc editas sacri approbatione concilii revocantes præsenti constitutione decernimus ut sic contrahentes de cætero libere copulentur.
Since therefore the prohibitions concerning marriage in the second and third degree of affinity not to be contracted, and concerning offspring received from second marriages not to be joined to the blood relation of the husband, frequently both induce difficulty and sometimes bring peril to souls, so that the prohibition ceasing the effect may cease, revoking the constitutions heretofore published concerning this, by the approval of the sacred council we decree by the present constitution that those thus contracting from now on freely be joined.
¶267
Prohibitio quoque copulæ coniugalis quartum consanguinitatis et affinitatis gradum de cætero non excedat quoniam in ulterioribus gradibus iam non potest absque gravi dispendio huiusmodi prohibitio generaliter observari.
The prohibition also of conjugal union the fourth degree of consanguinity and affinity for the future shall not exceed, because in further degrees such a prohibition cannot without grave loss be generally observed.
¶268
Quaternarius enim numerus bene congruit prohibitioni coniugii corporalis de quo dicit apostolus quod vir non habet potestatem sui corporis sed mulier neque mulier habet potestatem sui corporis sed vir quia quatuor sunt humores in corpore quod constat ex quatuor elementis.
For the number four well accords with the prohibition of bodily marriage, concerning which the apostle says that the husband does not have power over his own body but the wife, nor does the wife have power over her own body but the husband, because there are four humors in the body which consists of four elements.
¶269
Cum ergo iam usque ad quartum gradum prohibitio coniugalis copulæ sit restricta eam ita esse volumus perpetuam non obstantibus constitutionibus super hoc dudum editis vel ab aliis vel a nobis ut si qui contra prohibitionem huiusmodi præsumpserint copulari nulla longinquitate defendantur annorum cum diuturnitas temporum non minuat peccatum sed augeat tanto que graviora sint crimina quanto diutius infelicem detinent animam alligatam.
Since therefore now as far as the fourth degree the prohibition of conjugal union is restricted, we wish it thus to be perpetual, the constitutions heretofore published concerning this, whether by others or by us, notwithstanding, so that if any shall have presumed to be joined against such a prohibition, they may be defended by no length of years, because the duration of time does not diminish sin but increases it, and crimes are so much the graver the longer they hold the unhappy soul bound.

CONSTITUTIO LI
CONSTITUTION LI

CHAPTER LI
Cum inhibitio copulæ coniugalis sit in tribus ultimis gradibus revocata eam in aliis volumus districte observari.
Although the inhibition of conjugal union has been revoked in the three last degrees, we wish it in the others to be strictly observed.
¶271
Unde prædecessorum nostrorum inhærendo vestigiis clandestina coniugia penitus inhibemus prohibentes etiam ne quis sacerdos talibus interesse præsumat.
Whence, adhering to the footsteps of our predecessors, clandestine marriages altogether we inhibit, also forbidding that any priest presume to be present at such.
¶272
Quare specialem quorundam locorum consuetudinem ad alia generaliter prorogando statuimus ut cum matrimonia fuerint contrahenda in ecclesiis per presbyteros publice proponantur competenti termino præfinito ut infra illum qui voluerit et valuerit legitimum impedimentum opponat. Et ipsi presbyteri nihilominus investigent utrum aliquod impedimentum obsistat.
Wherefore, extending the special custom of certain places generally to others, we establish that when marriages are to be contracted, in churches by priests they be publicly announced within a competent term prefixed, so that within that term whoever wishes and is able may oppose a legitimate impediment. And the priests themselves nevertheless investigate whether any impediment stands in the way.
¶273
Cum autem probabilis apparuerit coniectura contra copulam contrahendam contractus interdicatur expresse donec quid fieri debeat super eo manifestis constiterit documentis.
When however a probable conjecture shall have appeared against the union to be contracted, let the contract be expressly forbidden until what ought to be done concerning it shall have been established by manifest documents.
¶274
Si qui vero huiusmodi clandestina vel interdicta coniugia inire præsumpserint in gradu prohibito etiam ignoranter soboles de tali coniunctione suscepta prorsus illegitima censeatur de parentum ignorantia nullum habitura subsidium cum illi taliter contrahendo non expertes scientiæ vel saltem affectatores ignorantiæ videantur. Pari modo illegitima proles censeatur si ambo parentes impedimentum scientes legitimum præter omne interdictum in conspectu Ecclesiæ contrahere præsumpserint.
But if any persons such clandestine or forbidden marriages to enter shall have presumed in a prohibited degree, even ignorantly, offspring from such a union received altogether illegitimate is to be considered, from the ignorance of the parents about to have no subsidy, since those thus contracting are seen not to be without knowledge or at least to be affecters of ignorance. In like manner, illegitimate is the offspring to be considered if both parents, knowing a legitimate impediment, against every prohibition in the sight of the Church to contract shall have presumed.
¶275
Sane parochialis sacerdos qui tales coniunctiones prohibere contempserit aut quilibet etiam regularis qui eis præsumpserit interesse per triennium ab officio suspendatur gravius puniendus si culpæ qualitas postulaverit. Sed et iis qui taliter copulari præsumpserint etiam in gradu concesso condigna pœnitentia iniungatur. Si quis autem ad impediendum legitimam copulam malitiose impedimentum obiecerit ecclesiasticam non effugiet ultionem.
Truly, a parish priest who to forbid such unions shall have despised, or any regular whatsoever who to be present at them shall have presumed, for three years from office let him be suspended, to be punished more gravely if the quality of the fault shall have demanded. But also to those who thus to be joined shall have presumed, even in a conceded degree, a condign penance is to be enjoined. If however anyone to impede a legitimate union maliciously shall have objected an impediment, he will not escape ecclesiastical vengeance.

CONSTITUTION LII

CHAPTER LII
Licet ex quadam necessitate præter communem formam alias fuerit institutum ut in consanguinitatis et affinitatis gradibus computandis valeret testimonium de auditu cum propter brevem hominum vitam testes de visu deponere non valerent usque ad gradum septimum computando quia tamen pluribus exemplis et certis experimentis didicimus ex hoc multa pericula contra legitima coniugia provenisse statuimus ne super hoc recipiantur testes de cætero de auditu cum iam quartum gradum prohibitio non excedat nisi personæ graves exstiterint quibus fides merito sit adhibenda et ante motam litem testificata didicerint ab antiquioribus quidem suis non utique uno cum non sufficeret ille si viveret sed duobus ad minus nec ab infamibus et suspectis sed a fide dignis et omni exceptione maioribus.
Although out of a certain necessity contrary to the common form it was otherwise instituted that in the degrees of consanguinity and affinity to be computed the testimony of hearsay should be valid, because on account of the short life of men witnesses of sight would not be able to depose as far as the seventh degree in computing, because however by very many examples and certain experiments we have learned that from this many perils against legitimate marriages have arisen, we establish that concerning this henceforth witnesses of hearsay are not to be received, since now the prohibition does not exceed the fourth degree, unless they are grave persons to whom faith ought deservedly to be given, and before a controversy was moved they have learned from their elders, not indeed from one (since that one, if he were living, would not suffice), but from at least two, nor from infamous and suspected persons, but from trustworthy and above all exception.
¶277
Cum satis videretur absurdum illos admitti quorum repellerentur actiones. Nec tamen si unus a pluribus vel infames ab hominibus bonæ famæ acceperint quod testentur tamquam plures et idonei testes debent admitti cum etiam secundum solitum ordinem iudiciorum non sufficiat unius testis assertio etiamsi præsidali dignitate præfulgeat et actus legitimi sint infamibus interdicti.
Since it would seem absurd enough that those would be admitted whose actions would be repelled. Not however if one from many or infamous persons from men of good fame have received what they testify, ought they to be admitted as many and suitable witnesses, since even according to the usual order of judgments the assertion of one witness does not suffice, even if he shines with presidential dignity, and legitimate acts are forbidden to infamous persons.
¶278
Testes autem huiusmodi proprio iuramento firmantes quod ad ferendum in causa ipsa testimonium odio vel timore vel amore vel commodo non procedant personas expressis nominibus vel demonstratione sive circumlocutione sufficienti designent et ab utroque latere clara computatione gradus singulos distinguant et in suo nihilominus iuramento concludant se accepisse a suis maioribus quod deponunt et credere ita esse.
Witnesses however of this kind, confirming by their own oath that to giving testimony in the case itself they do not proceed from hatred or fear or love or advantage, let them designate persons by expressed names or by demonstration or sufficient circumlocution, and from each side by clear computation let them distinguish each degree, and in their oath nevertheless let them conclude that they have received from their elders what they depose and believe it to be so.
¶279
Sed nec tales sufficiant nisi iurati deponant se vidisse personas saltem in uno prædictorum graduum constitutas pro consanguineis se habere.
But nor shall such persons suffice unless they swear that they have seen the persons themselves constituted in at least one of the aforesaid degrees to hold themselves as blood relations.
¶280
Tolerabilius est enim aliquos contra statuta hominum copulatos dimittere quam coniunctos legitime contra statuta domini separare.
For it is more tolerable that some joined contrary to human statutes to be separated than those legitimately joined contrary to the statutes of the Lord to be separated.

CONSTITUTION LIII

CHAPTER LIII
In aliquibus regionibus quædam permixtæ sunt gentes quæ secundum suos ritus decimas de more non solvunt quamvis censeantur nomine christiano.
In certain regions certain nations are mixed which according to their rites do not pay tithes according to custom, although they are considered under the Christian name.
¶282
His nonnulli domini prædiorum ea tribuunt excolenda ut decimis defraudantes ecclesias maiores inde reditus assequantur.
To these certain lords of estates give fields to be cultivated, so that defrauding churches of tithes they may obtain greater revenues from them.
¶283
Volentes igitur super his ecclesiarum indemnitatibus providere statuimus ut ipsi domini talibus personis et taliter sua prædia excolenda committant quod absque contradictione decimas ecclesiis cum integritate persolvant et ad id si necesse fuerit per censuram ecclesiasticam compellantur.
Willing therefore concerning these things to provide for the indemnity of churches, we establish that the lords themselves to such persons and in such manner commit their estates to be cultivated that without contradiction they pay tithes to churches with integrity, and to this, if necessary, through ecclesiastical censure they be compelled.
¶284
Illæ quippe decimæ necessario sunt solvendæ quæ debentur ex lege divina vel loci consuetudine approbata.
Those tithes indeed are necessarily to be paid which are owed by divine law or by approved custom of the place.
¶285
CONSTITUTIO LIV
CONSTITUTION LIV
CAPUT LIV
CHAPTER LIV
Cum non sit in homine quod semen serenti respondeat quoniam iuxta verbum apostoli neque qui plantat est aliquid neque qui rigat sed qui incrementum dat Deus ipso quidem de mortificato semine plurimum fructum afferente nimis avare in decimis quidam defraudare nituntur census et tributa quæ interdum indecimata prætereuntur de frugibus et primitiis educentes.
Since it is not in man to respond to the sower of seed, because according to the word of the apostle neither he who plants is anything nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase, He indeed from the deadened seed bringing forth very much fruit, too avariciously in tithes certain persons strive to defraud the census and tributes which sometimes, the tithes being passed over, are exacted from the fruits and firstfruits, bringing them forth.
¶286
Cum autem in signum universalis dominii quasi quodam titulo speciali sibi dominus decimas reservaverit nos et ecclesiarum dispendiis et animarum periculis obviare volentes statuimus ut in prærogativa dominii generalis exactionem tributorum et censuum præcedat solutio decimarum vel saltem hi ad quos census et tributa indecimata pervenerint quoniam res cum onere suo transit ea per censuram ecclesiasticam decimare cogantur ecclesiis quibus iure debentur.
Since however in sign of universal dominion, as if by a certain special title, the Lord has reserved tithes for Himself, we, wishing to meet both the losses of churches and the perils of souls, establish that in the prerogative of universal dominion, the exaction of tributes and census be preceded by the payment of tithes, or at least those to whom the census and tributes, the tithes having been passed over, have come, since a thing passes with its burden, through ecclesiastical censure be compelled to pay tithes to the churches to which they are owed by right.
¶287
CONSTITUTIO LV
CONSTITUTION LV
CAPUT LV
CHAPTER LV
Nuper abbates Cisterciensis ordinis in generali capitulo congregati ad commonitionem nostram provide statuerunt ne de cætero fratres ipsius ordinis emant possessiones de quibus decimæ debentur ecclesiis nisi forte pro monasteriis noviter fundandis.
Recently the abbots of the Cistercian order, gathered in general chapter at our admonition, provided that henceforth the brothers of that same order not buy possessions from which tithes are owed to churches, unless perhaps for monasteries newly to be founded.
¶288
Et si tales possessiones eis fuerint pia fidelium devotione collatæ aut emptæ pro monasteriis de novo fundandis committant excolendas aliis a quibus ecclesiis decimæ persolvantur ne occasione privilegiorum suorum Ecclesiæ ulterius prægraventur.
And if such possessions have been conferred upon them by the pious devotion of the faithful or bought for monasteries newly to be founded, let them commit them to be cultivated by others from whom tithes may be paid to churches, lest on occasion of their privileges churches may be further burdened.
¶289
Decernimus ergo ut de alienis terris et amodo acquirendis etiam si eas propriis manibus aut sumptibus deinceps excoluerint decimas persolvant ecclesiis quibus ratione prædiorum antea solvebantur nisi cum ipsis ecclesiis aliter duxerint componendum.
We decree therefore that concerning other lands and henceforth to be acquired, even if they have cultivated them thereafter with their own hands or at their own expense, they pay tithes to the churches to which by reason of the estates they were previously paid, unless with the churches themselves they have decided otherwise to compound.
¶290
Nos ergo statutum huiusmodi gratum et ratum habentes hoc ipsum ad alios regulares qui gaudent similibus privilegiis extendi volumus et mandamus ut ecclesiarum prælati proniores et efficaciores exsistant ad exhibendum eis de suis malefactoribus iustitiæ complementum eorum que privilegia diligentius et perfectius studeant observare.
We therefore, holding this statute agreeable and ratified, we will and command this same thing to be extended to other regulars who enjoy similar privileges, that the prelates of churches may be more ready and more effective in rendering to them concerning their malefactors the completion of justice, and their privileges more diligently and more perfectly strive to observe.

CONSTITUTION LVI
CAPUT LVI
CHAPTER LVI
Plerique sicut accepimus regulares et clerici seculares interdum cum vel domos locant vel feuda concedunt in præiudicium parochialium ecclesiarum pactum adiciunt ut conductores et feudatarii decimas eis solvant et apud eosdem eligant sepulturam.
Very many, as we have heard, regulars and secular clerics sometimes when they either rent out houses or concede fiefs, to the prejudice of parish churches, add a pact that the tenants and feudatories pay tithes to them and with them choose burial.
¶292
Cum autem id de avaritiæ radice procedat pactum huiusmodi penitus reprobamus statuentes ut quicquid fuerit occasione huiusmodi pacti perceptum Ecclesiæ parochiali reddatur.
Since however this proceeds from the root of avarice, a pact of this kind altogether we reprove, establishing that whatever shall have been received by occasion of such a pact be rendered to the parish church.

CONSTITUTION LVII
CAPUT LVII
CHAPTER LVII
Ut privilegia quæ quibusdam religiosis personis Romana concessit Ecclesia permaneant inconvulsa quædam in eis declaranda duximus ne minus sane intellecta pertrahant ad abusum propter quem merito possint revocari quia privilegium meretur amittere qui permissa sibi abutitur potestate.
So that privileges which the Roman Church has granted to certain religious persons may remain unshaken, we have decided that certain things in them are to be declared, lest less soundly understood they may be drawn to abuse on account of which they may deservedly be revoked, because a privilege merits to lose who uses the power permitted to him as an abuse.
¶294
Sane quibusdam regularibus sedes apostolica indulsit ut iis qui eorum fraternitatem assumpserint si forsan Ecclesiæ ad quas pertinent a divinis fuerint officiis interdictæ ipsos que mori contingat sepultura ecclesiastica non negetur nisi excommunicati vel nominatim fuerint interdicti suos que confratres quos ecclesiarum prælati apud ecclesias suas non permiserint sepeliri nisi excommunicati vel interdicti fuerint nominatim ipsi ad ecclesias suas deferant tumulandos.
Truly, to certain regulars the Apostolic See has indulged that to those who have assumed their fraternity, if perhaps the churches to which they pertain have been interdicted from divine offices, and they happen to die, ecclesiastical burial be not denied unless they have been excommunicated or nominately interdicted; and that their confreres whom the prelates of churches have not permitted to be buried at their churches, unless they have been excommunicated or nominately interdicted, they themselves may carry to their own churches to be buried.
¶295
Hoc autem de illis confratribus intelligimus qui vel adhuc manentes in sæculo eorum ordini sunt oblati mutato habitu sæculari vel qui eis inter vivos sua bona dederunt retento sibi quandiu in sæculo vixerint usufructu qui tantum sepeliantur apud ipsorum regularium vel aliorum non interdictas ecclesias in quibus elegerint sepulturam ne si de quibuslibet ipsorum fraternitatem assumentibus fuerit intellectum pro duobus aut tribus denariis annuatim sibi collatis dissolvatur pariter et vilescat ecclesiastica disciplina.
This however we understand concerning those confreres who either, still remaining in the world, are offered to their order, their secular habit having been changed, or who have given to them during their life their goods, the usufruct being retained for themselves as long as they have lived in the world, so that they be buried only at the churches of those regulars or of others not interdicted, in which they have chosen burial, lest if it were understood concerning any assuming their fraternity for two or three pence yearly paid to them, ecclesiastical discipline might be dissolved and likewise become cheap.
¶296
Certam tamen et ipsi remissionem obtineant ab Apostolica sibi Sede concessam.
Nevertheless, let them obtain the certain remission granted to them by the Apostolic See.
¶297
Illud etiam pro huiusmodi regularibus est indultum ut si qui fratrum suorum qui ab eis missi fuerint ad recipiendas fraternitates sive collectas in quamlibet civitatem castellum vel vicum advenerint si forte locus ille a divinis sit officiis interdictus in eorum iucundo adventu semel in anno aperiantur Ecclesiæ ut exclusis excommunicatis divina ibi officia celebrentur.
That also has been indulged for such regulars that if any of their brothers who have been sent by them to receive fraternities or collections into any city, castle, or village shall have come, if perhaps that place has been interdicted from divine offices, on their joyful arrival once in the year churches be opened so that, the excommunicated being excluded, divine offices may be celebrated there.
¶298
Sic intelligi volumus quod in eadem civitate aut castro vel villa una tantum Ecclesia eiusdem ordinis fratribus ut dictum est semel aperiatur in anno. Quia licet pluraliter dictum sit quod in eorum iucundo adventu aperiantur Ecclesiæ non tamen ad ecclesias eiusdem loci divisim sed prædictorum locorum coniunctim sano referendum est intellectu ne si hoc modo singulas eiusdem loci ecclesias visitarent nimium vilipendi contingeret sententiam interdicti.
Thus we wish it to be understood that in the same city or castle or town, one only church of the same order to the brothers, as has been said, once in the year be opened. Because although it has been said in the plural that on their joyful arrival churches be opened, it is to be referred not to the churches of the same place separately but to the aforesaid places conjointly, by a sound understanding, lest if they were to visit in this way each church of the same place singly, it might happen that the sentence of interdict be too much despised.
¶299
Qui vero contra declarationes præscriptas quicquam sibi præsumpserint usurpare gravi subiaceant ultioni.
Those who however against the aforesaid declarations anything for themselves shall have presumed to usurp, to a grave punishment let them be subject.

Quod nonnullis est religiosis indultum in favorem pontificalis officii ad episcopos extendentes concedimus ut cum commune terræ fuerit interdictum excommunicatis et interdictis exclusis possint quandoque ianuis clausis suppressa voce non pulsatis campanis celebrare officia divina nisi hoc ipsum eis expresse fuerit interdictum.
That which has been granted to certain religious persons, extending in favor of the pontifical office to bishops, we concede that when the land has been placed under general interdict, the excommunicated and interdicted being excluded, they may sometimes, with doors closed, with suppressed voice, with bells not rung, celebrate divine offices, unless this same thing has been expressly forbidden to them.

Verum hoc illis concedimus qui causam aliquam non præstiterint interdicto ne quicquam doli vel fraudis ingesserint tale compendium ad iniquum dispendium pertrahentes.
But this we concede to those who have not furnished any cause for the interdict, lest they may have introduced any deceit or fraud, drawing such advantage to iniquitous loss.

CONSTITUTIO LIX
CONSTITUTION LIX

CHAPTER LIX
Quod quibusdam religiosis a Sede Apostolica est prohibitum volumus et mandamus ad universos extendi ne quis videlicet religiosus absque abbatis et maioris partis sui capituli licentia pro aliquo fideiubeat vel ab alio pecuniam mutuo accipiat ultra summam communi providentia constitutam.
That which has been prohibited by the Apostolic See to certain religious persons, we will and command to be extended to all, namely that no religious person without the license of the abbot and the greater part of his chapter stand as surety for anyone or receive borrowed money from another beyond the sum established by common provision.

Alioquin non teneatur conventus pro his aliquatenus respondere nisi forte in utilitatem domus ipsius manifeste constiterit redundasse et qui contra statutum istud venire præsumpserit graviori disciplinæ subdatur.
Otherwise, let not the convent be held to respond for these things in any way, unless perhaps it shall have been manifestly established that it redounded to the utility of the house itself; and he who shall have presumed to come against this statute, to a graver discipline let him be subject.

Accendentibus ad nos de diversis mundi partibus episcoporum querelis intelleximus graves et grandes quorundam abbatum excessus qui suis finibus non contenti manus ad ea quæ sunt episcopalis dignitatis extendunt de causis matrimonialibus cognoscendo iniungendo publicas pœnitentias concedendo etiam indulgentiarum literas et similia præsumendo unde contingit interdum quod vilescat episcopalis auctoritas apud multos.
Complaints of bishops ascending to us from diverse parts of the world, we have understood the grave and great excesses of certain abbots, who not content with their own boundaries extend their hands to those things which are of episcopal dignity, by taking cognizance of matrimonial causes, by enjoining public penances, by also granting letters of indulgences, and by presuming similar things, whence it sometimes happens that episcopal authority becomes cheap in the eyes of many.
¶305
Volentes igitur in iis et episcoporum dignitati et abbatum providere saluti præsenti decreto firmiter prohibemus ne quis abbatum ad talia se præsumat extendere si proprium voluerit periculum evitare nisi forte quisquam eorum speciali concessione vel alia legitima causa super huiusmodi valeat se tueri.
Willing therefore in these matters to provide both for the dignity of bishops and for the salvation of abbots, by the present decree we firmly forbid that any abbot presume to extend himself to such things if he wishes to avoid his own peril, unless perhaps anyone of them may be able to defend himself concerning such matters by special concession or another legitimate cause.

CONSTITUTIO LXI
CONSTITUTION LXI
CAPUT LXI
CHAPTER LXI
In Lateranensi concilio noscitur fuisse prohibitum ne quilibet regulares ecclesias seu decimas sine consensu episcoporum de manu præsumant recipere laicali nec excommunicatos vel nominatim interdictos admittant aliquatenus ad divina.
In the Lateran Council it is known to have been prohibited that any regulars presume to receive churches or tithes without the consent of bishops from the hand of a layperson, nor admit the excommunicated or nominately interdicted in any way to divine things.
¶307
Nos autem id fortius inhibentes transgressores condigna curabimus animadversione puniri statuentes nihilominus quatenus in ecclesiis quæ ad ipsos pleno iure non pertinent iuxta eiusdem statuta concilii episcopis instituendos presbyteros repræsentent ut illis de plebis cura respondeant. Ipsis vero pro rebus temporalibus rationem exhibeant competentem. Institutos vero removere non audeant episcopis inconsultis.
We however, more strongly inhibiting these things, will take care that transgressors be punished by condign animadversion, establishing nevertheless that in churches which do not pertain to them by full right, according to the statutes of the same council, they present to bishops priests to be instituted, so that they may answer to them concerning the cure of the people. But to them themselves let them render a competent account concerning temporal things. But let them not dare to remove those instituted without consulting the bishops.
¶308
Sane adicimus ut illos repræsentare procurent quos vel conversatio reddit notos vel commendat probabile testimonium prælatorum.
Truly we add that they take care to present those whom either their conversation renders known or the probable testimony of prelates commends.

CONSTITUTIO LXII
CONSTITUTION LXII
CAPUT LXII
CHAPTER LXII
Cum ex eo quod quidam sanctorum reliquias exponunt venales et eas passim ostendunt christianæ religioni sit detractum sæpius ne detrahatur in posterum præsenti decreto statuimus ut antiquæ reliquiæ amodo extra capsam non ostendantur nec exponantur venales.
Because from the fact that certain persons expose the relics of saints for sale and display them here and there, it has been a detraction from the Christian religion; lest it be detracted in the future, by the present decree we establish that old relics from now on not be displayed outside a reliquary nor be exposed for sale.
¶310
Inventas autem de novo nemo publice venerari præsumat nisi prius auctoritate Romani pontificis fuerint approbatæ.
But those newly found let no one presume publicly to venerate unless first they shall have been approved by the authority of the Roman pontiff.
¶311
Prælati vero de cætero non permittant illos qui ad eorum ecclesias causa venerationis accedunt vanis figmentis aut falsis decipi documentis sicut et in plerisque locis occasione quæstus fieri consuevit.
Prelates indeed henceforth shall not permit those who come to their churches for the sake of veneration to be deceived by vain fictions or false documents, just as also in very many places on occasion of gain has been accustomed to be done.
¶312
Eleemosynarum quoque quæstores quorum quidam se alios mentiendo abusiones nonnullas in sua prædicatione proponunt admitti nisi apostolicas vel diœcesani episcopi literas veras exhibeant prohibemus. Et tunc præter id quod in ipsis continebitur literis nihil populo proponere permittantur.
Also questors of alms, of whom certain ones, lying about themselves, propose not a few abuses in their preaching, we forbid to be admitted unless they exhibit true apostolic or diocesan bishop's letters. And then, besides that which will be contained in those letters, nothing to the people may they be permitted to propose.
¶313
Formam vero quam communiter talibus Apostolica Sedes indulget duximus exprimendam ut secundum eam diœcesani episcopi suas literas moderentur.
The form indeed which the Apostolic See commonly grants to such persons we have decided to be expressed, so that according to it diocesan bishops may moderate their letters.
¶314
Ea siquidem talis est quoniam ut ait apostolus omnes stabimus ante tribunal Christi recepturi prout in corpore gessimus sive bonum sive malum fuerit oportet nos diem messionis extremæ misericordiæ operibus prævenire ac æternorum intuitu seminare in terris quod reddente Domino cum multiplicato fructu colligere debeamus in cœlis firmam spem fiduciam que tenentes quoniam qui parce seminat parce et metet et qui seminat in benedictionibus de benedictionibus et metet in vitam æternam.
Such indeed it is, that since, as the apostle says, we shall all stand before the tribunal of Christ, about to receive according to what we have done in the body, whether good or evil, it is fitting that we should prevent the day of the final harvest by works of mercy, and with a view to eternal things sow in the earth that which, the Lord rendering, with multiplied fruit we ought to gather in heaven, holding firm hope and confidence, because he who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and he who sows in blessings will also reap from blessings unto eternal life.
¶315
Cum igitur ad sustentationem fratrum et egenorum ad tale confluentium hospitale propriæ non suppetant facultates universitatem vestram monemus et exhortamur in Domino atque in remissionem vobis iniungimus peccatorum quatenus de bonis a Deo vobis collatis pias eleemosynas et grata eis caritatis subsidia erogetis ut per subventionem vestram ipsorum inopiæ consulatur et vos per hæc et per alia bona quæ Domino inspirante feceritis ad æterna possitis gaudia pervenire.
Since therefore for the sustenance of the brothers and the needy flocking to such a hospital our own resources do not suffice, we admonish and exhort your universality in the Lord and enjoin upon you for the remission of sins, that from the goods bestowed upon you by God you bestow pious alms and grateful subsidies of charity to them, so that by your subvention their need may be provided for, and you may be able through these things and through other goods which, the Lord inspiring, you shall have done, to attain to eternal joys.
¶316

Those who are sent to seek alms, let them be modest and discreet, nor in taverns or other incongruous places let them lodge, nor make useless or sumptuous expenses, taking care altogether lest they bear the habit of false religion.

To these things, because by indiscreet and superfluous indulgences, which certain prelates of churches do not fear to make, both the keys of the Church are despised and penitential satisfaction is enervated, we decree that when a basilica is dedicated, the indulgence be extended not beyond one year, whether by one bishop alone or by several the basilica is dedicated; and thereafter on the anniversary of the time of dedication, a remission by way of indulgence of forty days from enjoined penances not exceed.
¶318

This also number of days we command to be moderated in letters of indulgence which on any account are sometimes granted, since the Roman Pontiff, who obtains the fullness of power, has been accustomed to observe this moderation in such matters.

Since therefore not only from evil but also from every appearance of evil, according to the apostle, we ought to abstain, exactions of this kind altogether we forbid.

Willing therefore in this part to provide for Christians lest by Jews they be immoderately burdened, by synodal decree we establish that if in the future under any pretext Jews shall have exacted grave and immoderate usuries from Christians, let their participation with Christians be withdrawn until for the immoderate burden they shall have competently satisfied.

And by the same penalty we decree that Jews be compelled to satisfy churches for the tithes and oblations owed which they had been accustomed to receive from Christians from houses and other possessions before they had come to Jews by any title, so that churches may thus be preserved unharmed.

That however most strictly we forbid, that to the contumely of the Redeemer they may presume in any way to spring forth.

And since we ought not to dissimulate the reproach of Him who has destroyed our reproaches, we command that presumptuous persons of this kind by secular princes, by the addition of condign animadversion, be repressed, lest they may presume in any way to blaspheme the Crucified One for us.

CAPUT LXXI
CHAPTER LXXI
Ad liberandam terram sanctam de manibus impiorum ardenti desiderio adspirantes de prudentum virorum consilio qui plene noverant circunstantias temporum et locorum sacro approbante concilio diffinimus ut ita crucesignati se præparent quod in calendas iunii sequentis post proximum omnes qui disposuerunt transire per mare conveniant in regnum Siciliæ.
To liberate the Holy Land from the hands of the impious, breathing with ardent desire, by the counsel of prudent men who fully knew the circumstances of times and places, with the sacred council approving, we define that the crusaders so prepare themselves that on the calends of next June following after the next, all who have decided to cross by sea convene in the kingdom of Sicily.
¶353
Alii sicut oportuerit et decuerit apud Brundusium et alii apud Messanam et partes utrobique vicinas ubi et nos personaliter Domino annuente disposuimus tunc adesse quatenus nostro consilio et auxilio exercitus christianus salubriter ordinetur cum benedictione divina et apostolica profecturus.
Others, as shall have been fitting and proper, at Brundusium and others at Messina and the parts on both sides adjacent, where also we personally, the Lord granting, have decided then to be present, so that by our counsel and aid the Christian army may be salutarily ordered, about to set out with divine and apostolic blessing.
¶354
Ad eundem quoque terminum se studeant præparare qui proposuerunt per terram proficisci.
To the same term also let them strive to prepare themselves who have proposed to set out by land.
¶355
Significaturi hoc interim nobis ut eis ad consilium et auxilium legatum idoneum de nostro latere concedamus.
Signifying this in the meantime to us, so that to them for counsel and aid we may grant a suitable legate from our side.
¶356
Sacerdotes autem et alii clerici qui fuerint in exercitu christiano tam subditi quam prælati orationi et exhortationi diligenter insistant docentes eos verbo pariter et exemplo ut timorem et amorem semper habeant divinum ante oculos ne quid dicant aut faciant quod divinam maiestatem offendat.
Priests indeed and other clerics who shall have been in the Christian army, both subjects and prelates, to prayer and exhortation diligently let them attend, teaching them by word equally and example, so that they may always have the fear and love of God before their eyes, lest they say or do anything which may offend the divine majesty.
¶357
Et si aliquando lapsi fuerint in peccatum per veram pœnitentiam mox resurgant gerentes humilitatem cordis et corporis et tam in victu quam in vestitu mediocritatem servantes dissensiones et æmulationes omnino vitando rancore ac livore a se penitus relegatis ut sic spiritualibus et materialibus armis muniti adversus hostes fidei securius prælientur non de sua præsumantes potentia sed de divina virtute sperantes.
And if at any time they shall have lapsed into sin, by true penance let them rise again immediately, bearing humility of heart and body, and both in food and in dress observing mediocrity, discords and rivalries altogether avoiding, rancor and envy from themselves altogether being banished, so that thus, fortified by spiritual and material arms, against the enemies of the faith more securely they may fight, not presuming upon their own power but hoping in divine virtue.
¶358
Ipsis autem clericis indulgemus ut beneficia sua integre percipiant per triennium ac si essent in ecclesiis residentes et si necesse fuerit ea per idem tempus pignori valeant obligare.
To the clerics themselves we indulge that they receive their benefices in full for three years, just as if they were residing in their churches, and if necessary, for the same time they may be able to pledge them.
¶359
Ne igitur hoc sanctum propositum impediri vel retardari contingat universis ecclesiarum prælatis districte præcipimus ut singuli per loca sua illos qui signum crucis deposuerunt resumere ac tam ipsos quam alios crucesignatos et quos adhuc signari contigerit ad reddendum vota sua Domino diligenter moneant et inducant et si necesse fuerit per excommunicationis in personas et interdicti sententias in terras eorum omni tergiversatione cessante compellant illis dumtaxat exceptis quibus tale impedimentum occurrerit propter quod secundum Sedis Apostolicæ providentiam votum eorum commutari debeat merito vel differri.
Lest therefore this holy purpose may happen to be impeded or delayed, we strictly command all prelates of churches that each in his own place those who have laid down the cross to take it up again, and both them and other crusaders and those whom it shall happen to be signed thereafter, to render their vows to the Lord diligently admonish and induce, and if necessary, by excommunication against persons and sentences of interdict against their lands, all tergiversation ceasing, compel them, with those alone excepted for whom such an impediment shall have occurred that according to the provision of the Apostolic See their vow ought deservedly to be commuted or deferred.
¶360
Ad hæc ne quid in negotio Iesu Christi de contingentibus omittatur volumus et mandamus ut patriarchæ archiepiscopi episcopi abbates et alii qui curam obtinent animarum studiose proponant sibi commissis verbum crucis obsecrantes per patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum unum solum verum æternum Deum reges duces principes marchiones comites et barones alios que magnates necnon communiones civitatum villarum oppidorum ut qui personaliter non accesserint in subsidium terræ sanctæ competentem conferant numerum bellatorum cum expensis ad triennium necessariis secundum proprias facultates in remissionem peccatorum suorum prout in generalibus literis est expressum et ad maiorem cautelam etiam inferius exprimetur.
To these things, lest anything in the business of Jesus Christ from the things that may befall may be omitted, we will and command that patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, abbots, and others who hold the care of souls, studiously propose to those committed to themselves the word of the cross, beseeching them by the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one alone true eternal God, kings, dukes, princes, marquises, counts, and barons and other magnates, and also the communities of cities, towns, and villages, that those who shall not go in person to the aid of the Holy Land contribute a competent number of warriors with expenses necessary for three years according to their own resources, for the remission of their sins, just as in the general letters is expressed and for greater caution will also be expressed below.
¶361
Huius remissionis volumus esse participes non solum eos qui naves proprias exhibent sed etiam illos qui propter hoc opus naves studuerint fabricare.
We wish that of this remission not only those who provide their own ships, but also those who for this work shall have striven to build ships, be participants.
¶362
Renuentibus autem si qui forte tam ingrati fuerint Domino Deo nostro ex parte apostolica firmiter protestemur ut sciant se super hoc nobis in novissimo districti examinis die coram tremendo iudice responsuros prius tamen considerantes qua conscientia qua ve securitate confiteri poterunt coram unigenito Dei filio Iesu Christo cui omnia Pater dedit in manus si ei pro peccatoribus crucifixo servire renuerint in hoc negocio quasi proprie sibi proprio cuius munere vivunt cuius beneficio sustentantur quin etiam cuius sanguine sunt redempti.
To those refusing however, if perhaps any shall have been so ungrateful to the Lord our God, let us firmly protest on the apostolic part, that they know that concerning this they will answer to us on the last day of the strict examination before the terrible Judge; first however considering with what conscience or with what security they will be able to confess before the only-begotten Son of God Jesus Christ, to whom the Father has given all things into hands, if for Him crucified for sinners they refuse to serve in this business, as if for their own private interest, by whose gift they live, by whose benefit they are sustained, indeed even by whose blood they are redeemed.
¶363
Ne vero in humeros hominum onera gravia et importabilia imponere videamur quæ digito nostro movere nolimus similes illis qui dicunt utique sed non faciunt ecce nos de his quæ ultra necessaria et moderatas expensas potuimus reservare triginta milia librarum in hoc opus concedimus et donamus præter navigium quod crucesignatis de urbe atque vicinis partibus conferimus assignaturi nihilominus ad hoc ipsum tria millia marcarum argenti quæ apud nos de quorundam fidelium eleemosynis remanserunt aliis in necessitates et utilitates prædictæ terræ per manus felicis memoriæ abbatis hierosolymitani patriarchæ ac magistrorum templi et hospitalis fideliter distributis.
Lest however we seem to place heavy and insupportable burdens upon the shoulders of men, which we are unwilling to move with our finger, like those who say indeed but do not do, behold we, from those things which beyond necessary and moderate expenses we have been able to reserve, grant and donate thirty thousand pounds for this work, besides the shipping which we bestow upon the crusaders from the city and adjacent parts, likewise assigning for this same purpose three thousand marks of silver which have remained with us from the alms of certain faithful, other things having been faithfully distributed for the necessities and utilities of the aforesaid land through the hands of the abbot of Hierosolyma of happy memory, the patriarch, and the masters of the Temple and the Hospital.
¶364
Cupientes autem alios ecclesiarum prælatos necnon clericos universos et in merito et in præmio habere participes et consortes ex communi concilii approbatione statuimus ut omnes omnino clerici tam subditi quam prælati vigesimam partem ecclesiasticorum proventuum usque ad triennium conferant in subsidium terræ sanctæ per manus eorum qui ad hoc apostolica fuerint providentia ordinati.
Wishing however that other prelates of churches and also all clerics, both in merit and in reward, be participants and co-sharers, by the common approval of the council we establish that all clerics altogether, both subjects and prelates, the twentieth part of ecclesiastical proceeds for up to three years contribute in subsidy of the Holy Land, through the hands of those who for this shall have been ordained by apostolic providence.
¶365
Quibusdam dumtaxat religiosis exceptis ab hac prætaxatione merito eximendis illis similiter qui assumpto vel assumendo crucis signaculo sunt personaliter profecturi.
Certain religious persons alone excepted from this taxation, deservedly to be exempted, likewise those who, having assumed or about to assume the sign of the cross, are about to go in person.
¶366
Nos autem et fratres nostri sanctæ Romanæ Ecclesiæ cardinales plenarie decimam persolvemus sciant que se omnes ad hoc fideliter observandum per excommunicationis sententiam obligatos ita quod illi qui super hoc fraudem scienter commiserint sententiam excommunicationis incurrant.
We however and our brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman Church will pay in full the tenth; and let all know that they are bound to observe this faithfully by the sentence of excommunication, so that those who concerning this shall have knowingly committed fraud incur the sentence of excommunication.
¶367
Sane quia iusto iudicio cœlestis imperatoris obsequiis inhærentes speciali decet prærogativa gaudere cum tempus proficiscendi annum excedat in modico crucesignati vel a collectis vel talliis aliis que gravaminibus sint immunes quorum personas et bona post crucem assumptam sub beati Petri et nostra protectione suscipimus.
Truly, because by a just judgment of the heavenly Emperor it is fitting that those who adhere to His service enjoy a special prerogative, since the time of setting out exceeds a year by a little, let the crusaders be immune from collections or tallages or other burdens, whose persons and goods after the cross has been assumed we take under the protection of blessed Peter and ours.
¶368
Statuentes ut sub archiepiscoporum episcoporum ac omnium prælatorum Ecclesiæ defensione consistant propriis nihilominus protectoribus ad hoc specialiter deputandis ita quod donec de ipsorum obitu vel reditu certissime cognoscatur integra maneant et quieta.
Establishing that they remain under the defense of archbishops, bishops, and all prelates of the Church, likewise under their own protectors for this specially to be deputed, so that until concerning their death or return it shall be certainly known, they remain whole and undisturbed.
¶369
Et si quisquam contra præsumpserit per censuram ecclesiasticam compescatur.
And if anyone shall have presumed against this, through ecclesiastical censure let him be repressed.
¶370
Si qui vero proficiscentium illuc ad præstandas usuras iuramento tenentur adstricti creditores eorum ut remittant eis præstitum iuramentum et ab usurarum exactione desistant eadem præcipimus districtione compelli.
But if any of those setting out to that land are held bound by oath to pay usuries, we command by the same strictness that their creditors be compelled to remit to them the oath given and to desist from the exaction of usuries.
¶371
Quod si quisquam creditorum eos ad solutionem coegerit usurarum eum ad restitutionem earum simili cogi animadversione mandamus.
But if any of the creditors shall have compelled them to pay usuries, we command by a like animadversion that he be compelled to restitution of them.
¶372
Iudæos vero ad remittendas usuras per sæcularem compelli præcipimus potestatem et donec illas remiserint ab universis Christi fidelibus per excommunicationis sententiam eis omnino communio denegetur.
Jews indeed we command to be compelled by secular power to remit usuries, and until they shall have remitted them, let communion be altogether denied them by all the faithful of Christ by the sentence of excommunication.
¶373
His autem qui Iudæis debita solvere nequeunt in præsenti sic principes sæculares utili dilatione provideant quod post iter arreptum usquequo de ipsorum obitu vel reditu certissime cognoscatur usurarum incommoda non incurrant compulsis Iudæis proventus pignorum quos interim ipsi perceperint in sortem expensis deductis necessariis computare.
To those however who are not able to pay their debts to Jews at present, let secular princes provide by a useful delay, so that after the journey has been undertaken, until concerning their death or return it shall be certainly known, they may not incur the disadvantages of usuries, the Jews being compelled to count the proceeds of pledges which they have received in the meantime toward the principal, necessary expenses having been deducted.
¶374
Cum huiusmodi beneficium non multum videatur habere dispendii quod solutionem sic prorogat quod debitum non absorbet.
Since a benefit of this kind is not seen to have much of loss, which so delays payment that it does not absorb the debt.
¶375
Porro ecclesiarum prælati qui in exhibenda iustitia crucesignatis et eorum familiis negligentes exstiterint sciant se graviter puniendos.
Moreover, prelates of churches who shall have been negligent in rendering justice to crusaders and their families, know that they will be severely punished.
¶376
Cæterum quia cursarii et piratæ nimium impediunt subsidium terræ sanctæ capiendo et expoliando transeuntes ad illam et redeuntes ab ipsa nos speciales adiutores et fautores eorum excommunicationis vinculo innodamus sub interminatione anathematis inhibentes ne quis cum eis scienter communicet aliquo venditionis vel emptionis contractu et iniungentes rectoribus civitatum et locorum suorum ut eos ab hac iniquitate revocent et compescant.
Moreover, because corsairs and pirates greatly impede the subsidy of the Holy Land by capturing and plundering those going to it and returning from it, we bind their special helpers and favorers by the bond of excommunication, under the threat of anathema forbidding that anyone knowingly communicate with them by any contract of sale or purchase, and enjoining upon the rectors of cities and their places that they recall and restrain them from this iniquity.
¶377
Alioquin quia nolle perturbare perversos nihil aliud est quam fovere nec caret scrupulo societatis occultæ qui manifesto facinori desinit obviare in personas et terras eorum per ecclesiarum prælatos severitatem ecclesiasticam volumus et præcipimus exerceri.
Otherwise, because to be unwilling to disturb the perverse is nothing other than to favor, nor lacks the scruple of occult society who ceases to oppose manifest crime, we will and command by the prelates of churches that ecclesiastical severity be exercised against their persons and lands.
¶378
Excommunicamus præterea et anathematizamus illos falsos et impios christianos qui contra ipsum Christum et populum christianum Saracenis arma ferrum et lignamina deferunt galearum.
Moreover we excommunicate and anathematize those false and impious Christians who against Christ Himself and the Christian people bear to Saracens arms, iron, and timbers of galleys.
¶379
Eos etiam qui galeas eis vendunt vel naves qui que in piraticis Saracenorum navibus curam gubernationis exercent vel in machinis aut quibuslibet aliis aliquod eis impendunt consilium vel auxilium in dispendium terræ sanctæ ipsarum rerum suarum privatione mulctari et capientium servos fore censemus.
Those also who sell galleys to them or ships, and those who exercise the care of navigation in the pirate ships of Saracens, or in engines or in any other things bestow upon them any counsel or aid to the loss of the Holy Land, we decree to be mulcted by the privation of their own things and to become the slaves of capturers.
¶380
Præcipientes ut per omnes urbes maritimas diebus dominicis et festivis huiusmodi sententia innovetur et talibus gremium non aperiatur Ecclesiæ nisi totum quod ex substantia tam damnata perceperint et tantundem de sua in subsidium prædictæ terræ transmiserint ut æquo iudicio in quo deliquerint puniantur.
Commanding that through all maritime cities on the Lord's days and feast days a sentence of this kind be renewed, and to such persons the bosom of the Church be not opened unless they shall have sent as much as they have received from such damnable substance and an equal amount from their own in subsidy of the aforesaid land, so that by a just judgment they may be punished in that in which they have transgressed.
¶381
Quod si forte solvendo non fuerint alias sic reatus talium castigetur quod in pœna ipsorum aliis interdicatur audacia similia præsumendi.
But if perhaps they shall not be able to pay, then let the guilt of such persons be so chastised that by their punishment others may be forbidden from the audacity of presuming similar things.
¶382
Prohibemus insuper omnibus christianis et sub anathemate interdicimus ne in terras Saracenorum qui partes orientales inhabitant usque ad quadriennium transmittant aut transvehant naves suas ut per hoc volentibus transfretare in subsidium terræ sanctæ maior navigii copia præparetur et Saracenis prædictis subtrahatur auxilium quod eis consuevit ex hoc non modicum provenire.
Moreover we forbid all Christians and under anathema we interdict that to the lands of Saracens who inhabit the eastern parts up to four years they send or transport their ships, so that by this a greater abundance of shipping may be prepared for those wishing to cross in subsidy of the Holy Land, and aid may be withdrawn from the aforesaid Saracens, which has been accustomed to come to them from this not moderately.
¶383
Licet autem torneamenta sint in diversis conciliis sub certa pœna generaliter interdicta quia tamen hoc tempore crucis negotium per ea plurimum impeditur nos illa sub pœna excommunicationis firmiter prohibemus usque ad triennium exerceri.
Although tournaments are in diverse councils under a certain penalty generally interdicted, because however at this time the business of the cross is very much impeded by them, we firmly forbid them under penalty of excommunication to be exercised for up to three years.
¶384
Quia vero ad hoc negotium exequendum est permaxime necessarium ut principes populi christiani adinvicem pacem observent sancta universali synodo suadente statuimus ut saltem per quadriennium in toto orbe christiano servetur pax generaliter ita quod per ecclesiarum prælatos discordantes reducantur ad plenam pacem aut firmam treguam inviolabiliter observandam.
Because indeed for this business to be carried out it is especially necessary that the princes and peoples of Christians observe peace among themselves, with the holy universal council advising we establish that at least for four years in the whole Christian world peace be generally observed, so that through the prelates of churches those at variance be reduced to full peace or a firm truce inviolably to be observed.
¶385
Et qui acquiescere forte contempserint per excommunicationem in personas et interdictum in terras arctissime compellantur nisi tanta fuerit iniuriarum malitia quod ipsi tali non debeant pace gaudere.
And those who perhaps shall have despised to acquiesce, by excommunication against persons and interdict upon lands most strictly let them be compelled, unless there has been such a degree of malice of injuries that they ought not to enjoy such peace.
¶386
Quod si forte censuram ecclesiasticam vilipenderint poterunt non immerito formidare ne per auctoritatem Ecclesiæ circa eos tanquam perturbatores negocii crucifixi sæcularis potentia inducatur.
But if perhaps they shall have despised ecclesiastical censure, they may not undeservedly fear that by the authority of the Church, concerning them as disturbers of the business of the Crucified, secular power may be induced.
¶387
Nos igitur omnipotentis Dei misericordia et beatorum apostolorum Petri et Pauli auctoritate confisi ex illa quam nobis licet indigne Deus ligandi atque solvendi contulit potestate omnibus qui laborem propriis personis subierint et expensis plenam suorum peccaminum de quibus liberaliter fuerint corde contriti et ore confessi veniam indulgemus et in retributione iustorum salutis æternæ pollicemur augmentum.
We therefore, trusting in the mercy of omnipotent God and in the authority of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, from that power which, though unworthily, God has conferred upon us of binding and loosing, to all who shall have undergone the labor in their own persons and at their own expense, full remission of their sins concerning which they shall have been heartily contrite and of mouth confessed, we grant, and in the retribution of the just we promise an increase of eternal salvation.
¶388
Eis autem qui non in personis propriis illuc accesserint sed in suis dumtaxat expensis iuxta facultatem et qualitatem suam viros idoneos destinarint et illis similiter qui licet in alienis expensis in propriis tamen personis accesserint plenam suorum concedimus veniam peccatorum.
To those however who shall have gone there not in their own persons but have sent suitable men at their own expense only, according to their resources and their quality, and likewise to those who, though at another's expense, yet in their own persons shall have gone, we grant full remission of their sins.
¶389
Huius quoque remissionis volumus et concedimus esse participes iuxta qualitatem subsidii et devotionis affectum omnes qui ad subventionem ipsius terræ de bonis suis congrue ministrabunt aut consilium et auxilium impenderint opportunum.
Of this remission also we will and concede that all be participants, according to the quality of the subsidy and the affection of devotion, who shall have suitably ministered from their goods to the subvention of that land or shall have bestowed opportune counsel and aid.
¶390
Omnibus etiam pie proficiscentibus in hoc opere communi universalis synodus omnium beneficiorum suorum suffragium impartitur ut eis digne proficiat ad salutem.
To all also piously setting out in this work, the universal synod bestows the suffrage of all its benefits, that it may profit them worthily unto salvation.
¶391

This completes the Constitutions of the Fourth Lateran Council (Constitutiones Concilii Lateranensis IV) by Pope Innocent III and the Sacred Council.
