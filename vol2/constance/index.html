Council of Constance

Concilium Constantiense

1414–1418

Editorial note

Resolution of the Great Schism; deposition of three rival claimants; election of Martin V.

Haec Sancta — must be treated explicitly. The decree Haec Sancta (Session 5, 1415) asserts that a general council holds authority directly from Christ and that the pope is bound to obey it in matters of faith, schism, and reform. This is the conciliarist high-water mark and the single most significant internal vulnerability in Volume 2. The chapter cannot treat Constance as straightforwardly authoritative without addressing it.

The standard Latin resolution has three components, all of which should appear in the chapter:

  • Martin V approved the acts of Constance selectively and conditionally — his approbation did not extend to all decrees indiscriminately
  • Exsecrabilis (Pius II, 1460) formally condemned appeal to councils over popes
  • Vatican I (Pastor Aeternus) settles the question definitively by defining papal supremacy without conciliarist qualification

The honest position is that Haec Sancta reflects a genuine crisis ecclesiology whose universal binding force the Latin tradition subsequently rejected through these three stages. This should be stated directly. Constance without Haec Sancta is an apologetically incomplete chapter that will be exploited by any serious critical reader.

COUNCIL OF CONSTANCE A.D. 1414–1418
Ioannes episcopus servus servorum dei ad futuram rei memoriam.
John, bishop, servant of the servants of God, for future remembrance of the matter.
¶1
Intendentes ad executionem eorum quae per felicis recordationis Alexandrum papam v praedecessorem nostrum in concilio Pisano decreta fuerunt circa convocationem concilii generalis iterum faciendam alias praesens concilium convocavimus per nostras litteras quarum tenorem hic duximus inserendum: Ioannes episcopus ....
Intending to execute those things which were decreed by our predecessor Pope Alexander V of happy memory in the Council of Pisa concerning the convocation of a general council to be made again another time, we have convoked the present council by our letters, the tenor of which we have deemed to be inserted here: John, bishop ....
¶2
et subinde cum venerabilibus fratribus nostris sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalibus et nostra curia ad hanc civitatem Constantiensem venimus tempore constituto.
And then, with our venerable brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman church and our curia, we came to this city of Constance at the appointed time.
¶3
Et nunc per dei gratiam hic existentes cum huius sacrae synodi consilio intendimus insistere ad pacem exaltationem et reformationem ecclesiae ac tranquillitatem populi christiani.
And now, by the grace of God existing here, we intend with the counsel of this sacred synod to insist upon the peace, exaltation, and reformation of the church and the tranquillity of the Christian people.
¶4
Et quia in hac re tam ardua non est propriis viribus aliquid praesumendum sed de dei adiutorio confidendum ideo a cultu divino inchoantes hoc sacro approbante concilio ordinavimus quod hac ipsa die specialis missa ad hoc constituta diceretur.
And because in so arduous a matter nothing is to be presumed upon our own strengths but we must trust in the aid of God, therefore, beginning from divine worship, with this sacred council approving, we ordained that on this very day a special mass established for this be said.
¶5
Sicuti per dei gratiam nunc celebrata est.
Just as by the grace of God it has now been celebrated.
¶6
Quam etiam missam constituimus in hac et in singulis aliis ecclesiis collegiatis secularibus et regularibus huius civitatis semel in hebdomade videlicet feria quinta hoc sacro concilio durante collegialiter celebrari.
Which mass we also constituted to be celebrated collegially in this and in each of the other collegiate churches, secular and regular, of this city once a week, namely on the fifth feria, during this sacred council.
¶7
Et ut eo ferventius huic sacrae celebrationi fideles insistant quo ampliori gratiae munere senserint se refectos omnibus et singulis vere poenitentibus et confessis videlicet sacerdotibus dictas missas celebrantibus pro singulis missis unum annum interessentibus vero quadraginta dies de iniunctis sibi poenitentiis misericorditer in domino relaxamus.
And so that the faithful may insist upon this sacred celebration the more fervently the more they shall have felt themselves refreshed with the gift of greater grace, to all and singular truly penitent and confessed—namely, to priests celebrating the said masses, for each mass one year; to those attending, forty days—of their enjoined penances we mercifully relax in the Lord.
¶8
Ad cuius etiam missae celebrationem exhortamur venerabiles fratres nostros sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinales nec non patriarchas archiepiscopos episcopos ac dilectos filios electos abbates caeteros que in sacerdotio constitutos ut et ipsi ad impetrandum praedictum divinum auxilium cum devotione singulis septimanis praedictam missam semel celebrent quibus celebrantibus et celebrationi interessentibus similes indulgentias elargimur.
To the celebration of which mass we also exhort our venerable brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman church and also patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, and our beloved sons, elect, abbots, and others constituted in the priesthood, that they also, to obtain the aforesaid divine aid, celebrate the aforesaid mass once each week with devotion; to those celebrating and attending the celebration we bestow similar indulgences.
¶9
Exhortamur etiam in domino omnes et singulos qui christi nomine gloriantur ut ad obtinendam optatam consummationem tantae rei diligenter insistant orationibus ieiuniis eleemosynis et aliis piis operibus ut deus ex nostra et ipsorum humilitate placatus dignetur felicem exitum huic sacrae congregationi concedere.
We also exhort in the Lord all and singular who glory in the name of Christ that for the purpose of obtaining the desired consummation of so great a matter, they diligently insist upon prayers, fasts, alms, and other pious works, so that God, placated by our and their humility, may deign to grant a happy outcome to this sacred congregation.
¶10
Praeterea considerantes quod precipuum agendum in concilio secundum laudabiles observantias antiquorum conciliorum est de his quae concernunt catholicam fidem et attendentes quod talia propter suam arduitatem exigant diligentiam tempus sufficiens ac studium exhortamur omnes habentes peritiam sacrarum litterarum ut diligenter se cum et cum aliis illa cogitent atque tractent quae eis videbuntur ad hanc rem utilia et opportuna quamprimum commode poterunt illa ad nostram et huius sacrae synodi notitiam perducant ut tempore opportuno possint ea determinari quae videbuntur tenenda vel repudianda pro utilitate et incremento ipsius catholicae fidei.
Moreover, considering that the principal thing to be done in a council according to the laudable observances of ancient councils concerns those things which pertain to the catholic faith, and attending that such things on account of their arduousness require diligence, sufficient time, and study, we exhort all having skill in sacred letters that they diligently think and treat with themselves and with others those things which shall seem to them useful and opportune for this matter, and as soon as they conveniently can, bring them to the knowledge of us and of this sacred synod, so that at the opportune time those things which shall seem to be held or rejected for the utility and increase of the catholic faith itself may be determined.
¶11
Et specialiter cogitent circa nonnullos errores qui a certis temporibus dicuntur in nonnullis partibus pullulasse et maxime circa illos qui ortum dicuntur habuisse a quodam Ioanne dicto Wicleff.
And specially they should think concerning certain errors which from certain times are said to have sprung up in certain parts, and especially concerning those which are said to have had origin from a certain John called Wycliffe.
¶12
Insuper etiam exhortamur omnes catholicos hic congregatos et alios ad hanc sacram synodum venturos ut velint diligenter cogitare et prosequi et ad nos et eamdem sacram synodum perducere ea per quae possit etiam congregatio catholicorum ad debitam reformationem et optatam tranquillitatem deo iuvante perduci.
Moreover, we also exhort all catholics here gathered and others about to come to this sacred synod that they be willing diligently to think and prosecute and to bring to us and the same sacred synod those things through which the congregation of catholics may also be brought, with God helping, to due reformation and desired tranquillity.
¶13
Nostrae namque intentionis et voluntatis est ut omnes hac de causa congregati cum omnimoda libertate possint dicere consulere et facere omnia et singula quae ad praemissa putaverint pertinere.
For our intention and will is that all gathered on this account be able with all freedom to say, counsel, and do all and singular which they shall have thought to pertain to the premises.
¶14
Ut autem notus sit modus qui in huius sacrae synodi processu servandus est tam quoad ea quae dicenda et determinanda erunt quam etiam quoad gestum et morum compositionem putamus in hoc recurrendum ad observantias antiquorum patrum quae maxime colliguntur ex canone Toletani concilii cuius tenorem hic duximus inserendum: in loco benedictionis considentes domini sacerdotes nullus debet aut indiscretis vocibus perstrepere aut quibuslibet tumultibus perturbare nullus etiam fabulis vanis vel risibus et quod est deterius obstinatis disceptationibus tumultuosas debet voces effundere.
So that the mode which is to be observed in the process of this sacred synod may be known, both as to those things which are to be said and determined and also as to gesture and composition of morals, we think that in this one must have recourse to the observances of the ancient fathers, which are chiefly gathered from the canon of the Council of Toledo, the tenor of which we have deemed to be inserted here: "The lord priests sitting down in a place of blessing, no one ought to clamor with indiscreet voices or to disturb with any tumults, nor ought anyone to pour forth tumultuous voices with vain fables or laughter or—which is worse—with obstinate disputations."
¶15
Si quis enim ut ait apostolus putat se religiosum esse non refrenans linguam suam sed seducens cor suum huius vana est religio. Cultum enim suum iustitia perdit quando silentia iudicii obstrepentium turba confundit dicente propheta erit cultus iustitiae silentium.
For if anyone, as the apostle says, thinks himself to be religious, not restraining his tongue but deceiving his heart, this man's religion is vain. For justice loses its cultivation when the silence of judgment is disturbed by a crowd of clamorers, the prophet saying: "The cultivation of justice shall be silence."
¶16
Debet ergo quicquid aut considentium consultationibus agitur aut ab accusantium parte proponitur mitissima verborum relatione proferri ut nec contentiosis vocibus sensus audientium turbetur et iudicii vigorem de tumultu enervent.
Therefore whatever is done by the consultations of those sitting together or is proposed on the part of accusers ought to be set forth with the most gentle relation of words, so that neither the sense of the hearers be disturbed by contentious voices and they enervate the vigor of judgment by tumult.
¶17
Quicumque ergo in conventu concilii haec quae praemissa sunt violanda crediderit et contra haec interdicta aut tumultu aut contumeliis vel risibus concilium perturbaverit iuxta divinae legis edictum quo praecipitur eiice derisorem et exibit cum eo iurgium cum omni dedecore de consessione abstractus a communi coetu recedat et trium dierum excommunicationis sententiam ferat.
Whoever therefore in the assembly of the council shall have believed that these things which are premised are to be violated and against these interdicts shall have disturbed the council by tumult or contumelies or laughter, let him be removed from the assembly with all disgrace and depart from the common gathering, and let him bear the sentence of excommunication for three days, according to the edict of the divine law which commands: "Cast out the scorner, and contention shall go out with him."
¶18
Et quoniam contingere potest quod aliqui ex considentibus non erunt in sedibus debitis collocati eodem sacro approbante concilio decernimus quod ex sessione huiusmodi nulli ecclesiae vel personae praeiudicium generetur.
And because it can happen that some of those sitting together will not be placed in their due seats, with the same sacred council approving, we decree that from such session no prejudice be generated to any church or person.
¶19
Quia vero ad prosecutionem huius concilii requiruntur certi ministri et officiales ideo hoc sacro approbante concilio deputamus infra scriptos videlicet dilectos filios ....
Because, moreover, for the prosecution of this council certain ministers and officials are required, therefore with this sacred council approving, we depute the under-written, namely our beloved sons ....
¶20
SESSION V (APRIL 6, 1415): DECREE HAEC SANCTA ON THE AUTHORITY OF THE COUNCIL
Ad honorem laudem et gloriam sanctissimae trinitatis patris et filii et spiritus sancti pacem que in terris hominibus bonae voluntatis divinitus promissam in dei ecclesia consequendum haec sancta synodus sacrum generale concilium Constantiense nuncupata pro unione et reformatione dictae ecclesiae in capite et in membris fienda in spiritu sancto hic debite congregata decernit declarat diffinit et ordinat ut sequitur.
To the honor, praise, and glory of the most holy Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, and for obtaining the peace divinely promised on earth to men of good will in the church of God, this holy synod, called the sacred general Council of Constance, for the union and reformation of the said church to be done in head and members, being duly gathered here in the Holy Spirit, decrees, declares, defines, and ordains as follows.
¶21
Et primo quod ipsa synodus fuit et est recte et rite convocata ad hunc locum civitatis Constantiensis et similiter recte et rite initiata et celebrata.
And first: that this synod has been and is rightly and duly convoked to this place of the city of Constance, and likewise rightly and duly initiated and celebrated.
¶22
Item quod per recessum domini nostri papae de hoc loco Constantiensi vel etiam per recessum aliorum praelatorum seu aliorum quorumcumque non est dissolutum hoc sacrum concilium sed remanet in sua integritate et auctoritate etiam si quae ordinationes factae essent in contrarium vel fierent in futurum.
Likewise: that by the departure of our lord the pope from this place of Constance or also by the departure of other prelates or any others, this sacred council is not dissolved but remains in its integrity and authority, even if any ordinations should have been made to the contrary or should be made in the future.
¶23
Item quod istud sacrum concilium non debet dissolvi nec dissolvatur usque ad perfectam exstirpationem praesentis schismatis et quousque ecclesia sit reformata in fide et in moribus in capite et in membris.
Likewise: that this sacred council ought not to be dissolved nor be dissolved until the perfect extirpation of the present schism and until the church be reformed in faith and in morals in head and members.
¶24
Item quod ipsum sacrum concilium non transferatur ad alium locum nisi ex causa rationabili et de consilio huius sacri concilii deliberanda et concludenda.
Likewise: that this sacred council be not transferred to another place except for a reasonable cause to be deliberated and concluded upon by the counsel of this sacred council.
¶25
Item quod praelati et alii qui debent interesse huic concilio non recedant de hoc loco ante finitum concilium nisi ex causa rationabili examinanda per deputatos seu deputandos ab hoc sacro concilio. Qua causa examinata et approbata possint recedere cum licentia eius vel illorum qui habebit vel habebunt auctoritatem: et tunc recedens teneatur dimittere potestatem suam aliis remanentibus sub poenis iuris et aliis per hoc sacrum concilium indicendis et contra eos exsequendis.
Likewise: that prelates and others who ought to be present at this council do not depart from this place before the council is finished except for a reasonable cause to be examined by those deputed or about to be deputed by this sacred council. Which cause having been examined and approved, they may be able to depart with the license of him or those who shall have or shall have authority; and then the one departing shall be held to leave his power to others remaining, under the penalties of law and other [penalties] to be imposed by this sacred council and to be executed against them.
¶26
DECREE HAEC SANCTA (SESSION V, APRIL 6, 1415)
In nomine sanctae et individuae trinitatis patris et filii et spiritus sancti Amen.
In the name of the holy and individual Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Amen.
¶27
haec sancta synodus Constantiensis generale concilium faciens pro exstirpatione praesentis schismatis et unione ac reformatione ecclesiae dei in capite et in membris fienda ad laudem omnipotentis dei in spiritu sancto legitime congregata ad consequendum facilius securius uberius et liberius unionem et reformationem ecclesiae dei ordinat diffinit statuit decernit et declarat ut sequitur.
This holy synod of Constance, making a general council, for the extirpation of the present schism and for the union and reformation of the church of God to be done in head and members, to the praise of omnipotent God, being legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, in order to obtain more easily, more securely, more abundantly, and more freely the union and reformation of the church of God, ordains, defines, decrees, and declares as follows.
¶28
Et primo quod ipsa synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata generale concilium faciens ecclesiam catholicam militantem repraesentans potestatem a christo immediate habeat cui quilibet cuiuscumque status vel dignitatis etiam si papalis exsistat obedire tenetur in his quae pertinent ad fidem et exstirpationem dicti schismatis.
And first: that this synod, being legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, making a general council and representing the catholic militant church, has power immediately from Christ, to which any person, of whatever state or dignity, even if papal, is bound to obey in those things which pertain to faith and the extirpation of the said schism.
¶29
Item quod sanctissimus dominus noster papa Ioannes xxiii Romanam curiam et officia publica illius vel illorum officiarios de hac civitate ad alium locum non mutet aut transferat seu personas dictorum officiorum ad sequendum eum directe vel indirecte cogat sine deliberatione et consensu ipsius sanctae synodi et hoc quoad illos officiarios vel illa officia per quorum absentiam concilium verisimiliter dissolveretur vel laederetur.
Likewise: that our most holy lord Pope John XXIII shall not change or transfer the Roman curia and the public offices of it or their officers from this city to another place, nor compel the persons of the said officers to follow him directly or indirectly, without the deliberation and consent of this sacred synod—and this as to those officers or those offices by whose absence the council would be likely to be dissolved or harmed.
¶30
Et si contrarium fecisset seu faceret in futurum aut aliquos processus seu mandata contra dictos officiarios vel alios quoscumque huic concilio adhaerentes etiam censuras ecclesiasticas aut alias poenas quascumque continentes ut ipsum sequantur fulminasset fulminaret seu fulminaverit totum sit irritum et inane nec eisdem processibus censuris et poenis tamquam irritis et inanibus quomodolibet obediendum fore et ea et eos irritat.
And if he shall have done the contrary or shall do in the future, or shall have fulminated, shall fulminate, or shall fulminate any processes or mandates against the said officers or any others adhering to this council, even containing ecclesiastical censures or any other penalties whatsoever, that they follow him, let the whole be invalid and void, nor is it to be obeyed in any way in the same processes, censures, and penalties as if they were invalid and void; and he makes them void.
¶31
Quin immo dicti officiarii in dicta civitate Constantiensi suis officiis utantur et illa exerceant libere ut prius quamdiu ipsa sancta synodus in eadem civitate celebrabitur.
Nay rather, let the said officers use their offices in the said city of Constance and freely exercise them as before, as long as this sacred synod shall be celebrated in the same city.
¶32
Item quod omnes et singulae translationes praelatorum nec non privationes eorumdem aut aliorum beneficiatorum quorumcumque commendarum ac donationum revocationes monitiones censurae ecclesiasticae processus sententiae acta et gesta gerenda agenda et fienda per praefatum dominum nostrum ac suos officiarios seu commissarios in laesionem concilii seu adhaerentium eidem a tempore sui recessus contra adhaerentes huic sacro concilio vel exsistentes in eodem aut in ipsorum praeiudicium seu alicuius eorumdem quomodolibet ipsis invitis facta vel fienda factae vel facti faciendae vel faciendi ipso iure sint nulla cassa irrita et inania cassae irritae et inanes ac nullius roboris vel momenti et sua auctoritate cassat irritat et annullat.
Likewise: that all and singular translations of prelates, and also the privations of them or of other beneficiaries, and revocations of commendams and donations, monitions, ecclesiastical censures, processes, sentences, acts, deeds, and things to be done, to be carried out, and to be made by the aforesaid our lord and his officers or commissaries in injury of the council or of those adhering to it from the time of his departure against those adhering to this sacred council or existing in it, or in prejudice of them or any of them in any way against their will, made or to be made, be ipso iure null, void, invalid, and empty, of no strength or moment, and by his authority he annuls, makes void, and invalidates them.
¶33
[Item fuit declaratum et conclusum quod de qualibet natione eligantur tres qui cognoscant de causis recedere volentium et poenis recedentium sine licentia infligendis].
[Likewise it was declared and concluded that from each nation three be chosen who may know concerning the causes of those wishing to depart and concerning the penalties to be inflicted upon those departing without license.]
¶34
Item quod pro bono unionis non creentur novi cardinales.
Likewise: that for the good of union, no new cardinals be created.
¶35
Et ne fraude vel dolo dicantur facti dudum cardinales aliqui declarat sacrum concilium pro non cardinalibus haberi qui non erant cardinales publice reputati et tenti tempore recessus eiusdem domini nostri papae a civitate Constantiensi.
And lest any cardinals be said to have been made by fraud or deceit, the sacred council declares that those are to be held as not cardinals who were not publicly reputed and held as cardinals at the time of the departure of the same our lord the pope from the city of Constance.
¶36
DECREE HAEC SANCTA (SESSION V, APRIL 6, 1415 – SECOND VERSION)
In nomine sanctae et individuae trinitatis patris et filii et spiritus sancti Amen.
In the name of the holy and individual Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Amen.
¶37
haec sancta synodus Constantiensis generale concilium faciens pro exstirpatione praesentis schismatis et unione ac reformatione ecclesiae dei in capite et in membris fienda ad laudem omnipotentis dei in spiritu sancto legitime congregata ad consequendum facilius securius uberius et liberius unionem ac reformationem ecclesiae dei ordinat diffinit statuit decernit et declarat ut sequitur.
This holy synod of Constance, making a general council, for the extirpation of the present schism and for the union and reformation of the church of God to be done in head and members, to the praise of omnipotent God, being legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, in order to obtain more easily, more securely, more abundantly, and more freely the union and reformation of the church of God, ordains, defines, decrees, and declares as follows.
¶38
Et primo declarat quod ipsa in spiritu sancto legitime congregata generale concilium faciens et ecclesiam catholicam militantem repraesentans potestatem a christo immediate habet cui quilibet cuiuscumque status vel dignitatis etiam si papalis exsistat obedire tenetur in his quae pertinent ad fidem et exstirpationem dicti schismatis ac generalem reformationem dictae ecclesiae dei in capite et in membris.
And first, it declares that this synod, being legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, making a general council and representing the catholic militant church, has power immediately from Christ, to which any person, of whatever state or dignity, even if papal, is bound to obey in those things which pertain to faith and the extirpation of the said schism and the general reformation of the said church of God in head and members.
¶39
Item declarat quod quicumque cuiuscumque conditionis status dignitatis etiam si papalis exsistat qui mandatis statutis seu ordinationibus aut praeceptis huius sacrae synodi et cuiuscumque alterius concilii generalis legitime congregati super praemissis seu ad ea pertinentibus factis vel faciendis obedire contumaciter contempserit nisi resipuerit condignae poenitentiae subiiciatur et debite puniatur etiam ad alia iuris subsidia si opus fuerit recurrendo.
Likewise, it declares that whoever, of whatever condition, state, dignity, even if papal, who shall have contumaciously despised to obey the mandates, statutes, or ordinations or precepts of this sacred synod and of any other general council legitimately gathered concerning the premises or pertaining to them, made or to be made, unless he shall have repented, be subjected to condign penance and be duly punished, even having recourse to other aids of law if necessary.
¶40
Item diffinit et ordinat dicta sancta synodus quod dominus Ioannes papa xxiii Romanam curiam et officia publica illius seu illorum officiarios de hac civitate Constantiensi ad alium locum non mutet aut transferat seu personas dictorum officiariorum ad sequendum eum directe vel indirecte cogat sine deliberatione et consensu ipsius sanctae synodi.
Likewise, the said sacred synod defines and ordains that the lord Pope John XXIII shall not change or transfer the Roman curia and the public offices of it or their officers from this city of Constance to another place, nor compel the persons of the said officers to follow him directly or indirectly, without the deliberation and consent of this sacred synod.
¶41
Et si contrarium fecisset aut faceret in futurum aut aliquos processus seu mandata contra dictos officiarios aut alios quoscumque huic sacro concilio adhaerentes etiam censuras ecclesiasticas vel alias poenas quascumque continentes ut ipsum sequantur fulminasset fulminaret seu fulminaverit totum sit irritum et inane nec eisdem processibus censuris et poenis tamquam irritis et inanibus quomodolibet obediendum fore.
And if he shall have done the contrary or shall do in the future, or shall have fulminated, shall fulminate, or shall fulminate any processes or mandates against the said officers or any others adhering to this sacred council, even containing ecclesiastical censures or any other penalties whatsoever, that they follow him, let the whole be invalid and void, nor is it to be obeyed in any way in the same processes, censures, and penalties as if they were invalid and void.
¶42
Quin immo dicti officiarii in dicta civitate Constantiensi suis officiis utantur et illa exerceant libere ut prius quamdiu ipsa sancta synodus in eadem civitate celebrabitur.
Nay rather, let the said officers use their offices in the said city of Constance and freely exercise them as before, as long as this sacred synod shall be celebrated in the same city.
¶43
Item ordinat et diffinit quod omnes et singulae translationes praelatorum nec non privationes eorundem aut aliorum beneficiatorum officialium administratorum quarumcumque commendarum ac donationum revocationes monitiones censurae ecclesiasticae processus sententiae et quaecumque acta gesta gerenda agenda aut fienda per praefatum dominum Ioannem papam aut suos officiarios vel commissarios in laesionem dicti concilii seu adhaerentium eidem a tempore inchoationis eiusdem concilii et contra adhaerentes huic sacro concilio vel exsistentes in eodem aut in ipsorum seu alicuius eorumdem praeiudicium quomodolibet ipsis invitis facta seu facienda factae aut facti faciendae vel faciendi auctoritate huius sacri concilii ipso facto sint nulla cassa irrita et inania cassae irritae et inanes ac nullius roboris vel momenti et eadem auctoritate cassat irritat et annullat.
Likewise, it ordains and defines that all and singular translations of prelates, and also the privations of them or of other beneficiaries, officials, administrators, and revocations of commendams and donations, monitions, ecclesiastical censures, processes, sentences, and any acts, deeds, things to be done, carried out, or to be made by the aforesaid lord John XXIII or his officers or commissaries in injury of the said council or of those adhering to it from the time of the beginning of the same council and against those adhering to this sacred council or existing in it, or in prejudice of them or any of them in any way against their will, made or to be made, be ipso facto by the authority of this sacred council null, void, invalid, and empty, of no strength or moment, and by the same authority he annuls, makes void, and invalidates them.
¶44
Item declarat quod dominus Ioannes papa xxiii et omnes praelati et alii ad hoc sacrum concilium vocati et alii in eodem concilio exsistentes in plenaria libertate fuerunt et exsistunt ut visum est dicto sacro concilio nec ad notitiam dictorum vocatorum seu dicti concilii contrarium deductum est et hoc testificatur dictum sacrum concilium coram deo et hominibus.
Likewise, it declares that the lord Pope John XXIII and all prelates and others called to this sacred council and others existing in the same council have been and are in full liberty, as has seemed to the said sacred council, nor has anything to the contrary been brought to the knowledge of the said called persons or of the said council, and this the said sacred council testifies before God and men.
¶45
CONDEMNATION OF JOHN WYCLIFFE (SESSION VIII, MAY 4, 1415)
Sacrosancta Constantiensis synodus generale concilium faciens et ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans ad exstirpationem praesentis schismatis errorum que et haeresium sub eius umbra pullulantium eliminationem et reformationem ecclesiae in spiritu sancto legitime congregata ad perpetuam rei memoriam.
The sacrosanct synod of Constance, making a general council and representing the catholic church, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit for the extirpation of the present schism and the elimination of errors and heresies springing up under its shadow and for the reformation of the church, for perpetual remembrance of the matter.
¶46
Fidem catholicam sine qua ut ait apostolus impossibile est placere deo a falsis eiusdem fidei cultoribus immo perversis impugnatoribus et superba curiositate intendentibus plus sapere quam oportet mundi gloriam cupientibus oppugnatam saepius et contra illos per fideles ecclesiae milites spirituales opposito scuto fidei defensatam fuisse sanctorum patrum scripturis atque gestis instruimur. Haec quippe bellorum genera in bellis carnalibus Israelitici populi adversus gentes idololatras praesignata fuerunt.
We are instructed by the scriptures and deeds of the holy fathers that the catholic faith, without which—as the apostle says—it is impossible to please God, has been frequently attacked by false worshipers of the same faith, nay rather by perverse impugners, and by those intending through proud curiosity to know more than is fitting, desiring the glory of the world, and that it has been defended against them by the faithful soldiers of the church, the shield of faith having been opposed spiritually. For these kinds of wars were prefigured in the carnal wars of the Israelite people against the idolatrous nations.
¶47
In his itaque spiritualibus bellis sancta ecclesia catholica in fidei veritate superni luminis radiis illustrata domino providente et sanctorum patrocinio opem ferente semper immaculata permanens erroris tenebris velut hostibus profligatis gloriosissime triumphavit.
In these spiritual wars, the holy catholic church, illustrated by the rays of supernal light in the truth of faith, the Lord providing and the patronage of the saints bringing aid, always remaining immaculate, having routed the darkness of errors as if enemies, has most gloriously triumphed.
¶48
Nostris vero temporibus vetus ille et invidus hostis nova certamina ut probati temporis huius manifesti fiant suscitavit quorum dux et princeps exstitit quondam Ioannes Wicleff pseudochristianus: qui dum viveret adversus religionem christianam et fidem catholicam pertinaciter asseruit et dogmatizavit plures articulos quorum quadragintaquinque huic paginae duximus inserendos qui sequuntur.
But in our times, that old and envious enemy has raised up new battles, so that the tested ones of this time might become manifest, the leader and prince of which was a certain John Wycliffe, a pseudo-Christian. For while he lived, he asserted and dogmatized many articles against the Christian religion and the catholic faith, forty-five of which we have deemed to be inserted in this page, which follow.
¶49
THE FORTY-FIVE ARTICLES OF JOHN WYCLIFFE
Substantia panis materialis et similiter substantia vini materialis manent in sacramento altaris.
- The substance of material bread and likewise the substance of material wine remain in the sacrament of the altar.
¶50
Accidentia panis non manent sine subiecto in eodem sacramento.
- The accidents of bread do not remain without a subject in the same sacrament.
¶51
Christus non est in eodem sacramento identice et realiter in propria persona corporali.
- Christ is not in the same sacrament identically and really in His own bodily person.
¶52
Si episcopus vel sacerdos est in peccato mortali non ordinat non conficit non consecrat nec baptizat.
- If a bishop or priest is in mortal sin, he does not ordain, does not consecrate, does not baptize.
¶53
Non est fondatum in evangelio quod christus missam ordinarit.
- It is not founded in the gospel that Christ ordained the mass.
¶54
Deus debet obedire diabolo.
- God ought to obey the devil.
¶55
Si homo debite fuerit contritus omnis confessio exterior est sibi superflua et inutilis.
- If a man be duly contrite, all exterior confession is superfluous and useless to him.
¶56
Si papa sit praescitus et malus et per consequens membrum diaboli non habet potestatem super fideles ab aliquo sibi datam nisi forte a Caesare.
- If the pope be foreknown and evil, and consequently a member of the devil, he does not have power over the faithful given to him by anyone, unless perhaps by Caesar.
¶57
Post Urbanum vi non est aliquis recipiendus in papam sed vivendum est more Graecorum sub legibus propriis.
- After Urban VI, no one is to be received as pope, but one must live in the manner of the Greeks under their own laws.
¶58
Contra scripturam sacram est quod viri ecclesiastici habeant possessiones.
- It is against sacred scripture that ecclesiastical men have possessions.
¶59
Nullus praelatus debet aliquem excommunicare nisi prius sciat eum esse excommunicatum a deo: et qui sic excommunicat fit haereticus ex hoc vel excommunicatus.
- No prelate ought to excommunicate anyone unless he first knows him to be excommunicated by God; and he who thus excommunicates becomes a heretic or excommunicated by this.
¶60
Praelatus excommunicans clericum qui appellavit ad regem vel ad concilium regni eo ipso traditor est regis et regni.
- A prelate excommunicating a cleric who has appealed to the king or to the council of the kingdom is thereby a traitor to the king and kingdom.
¶61
Illi qui dimittunt praedicare sive verbum dei audire propter excommunicationem hominum sunt excommunicati et in die iudicii traditores christi habebuntur.
- Those who cease to preach or to hear the word of God on account of the excommunication of men are excommunicated and shall be held traitors of Christ on the day of judgment.
¶62
Licet alicui diacono vel presbytero praedicare verbum dei absque auctoritate sedis apostolicae vel episcopi catholici.
- It is lawful for any deacon or presbyter to preach the word of God without the authority of the apostolic see or of a catholic bishop.
¶63
Nullus est dominus civilis nullus est praelatus nullus est episcopus dum est in peccato mortali.
- No one is a civil lord, no one is a prelate, no one is a bishop while he is in mortal sin.
¶64
Domini temporales possunt ad arbitrium suum auferre bona temporalia ab ecclesia possessionatis habitualiter delinquentibus id est ex habitu non solo actu delinquentibus.
- Temporal lords can at their own will take away temporal goods from the church habitually delinquent—that is, those delinquent from habit, not only from act.
¶65
Populares possunt ad suum arbitrium dominos delinquentes corrigere.
- The people can at their own will correct delinquent lords.
¶66
Decimae sunt purae eleemosynae et parochiani possunt propter peccata suorum praelatorum ad libitum suum eas auferre.
- Tithes are pure alms, and parishioners can, on account of the sins of their prelates, take them away at their pleasure.
¶67
Speciales orationes applicatae uni personae per praelatos vel religiosos non plus prosunt eidem quam generales caeteris paribus.
- Special prayers applied to one person by prelates or religious profit that person no more than general prayers, other things being equal.
¶68
Conferens eleemosynam fratribus est excommunicatus eo facto.
- He who confers alms upon brothers is excommunicated by that very fact.
¶69
Si quis ingreditur religionem privatam qualemcumque tam possessionatorum quam mendicantium redditur ineptior et inhabilior ad observantiam mandatorum dei.
- If anyone enters any private religion, whether of the possessors or of the mendicants, he is rendered more inept and more incapable for the observance of the commandments of God.
¶70
Sancti instituentes religiones privatas sic instituendo peccaverunt.
- The saints who instituted private religions, by so instituting, sinned.
¶71
Religiosi viventes in religionibus privatis non sunt de religione christiana.
- Religious persons living in private religions are not of the Christian religion.
¶72
Fratres tenentur per labores manuum victum acquirere et non per mendicitatem.
- The brothers are held to acquire their livelihood by the labors of their hands, and not by begging.
¶73
Omnes sunt simoniaci qui se obligant orare pro aliis eis in temporalibus subvenientibus.
- All are simoniacs who bind themselves to pray for others who give them temporal assistance.
¶74
Oratio praesciti nulli valet.
- The prayer of a foreknown person profits no one.
¶75
Omnia de necessitate absoluta eveniunt.
- All things happen by absolute necessity.
¶76
Confirmatio iuvenum clericorum ordinatio locorum consecratio reservantur papae et episcopis propter cupiditatem lucri temporalis et honoris.
- The confirmation of youths, the ordination of clerics, the consecration of places are reserved to the pope and bishops on account of the desire for temporal gain and honor.
¶77
Universitates studia collegia graduationes et magisteria in eisdem sunt vana gentilitate introducta et tantum prosunt ecclesiae sicut diabolus.
- Universities, studies, colleges, graduations, and masteries in them are introduced by vain heathenism, and they profit the church as much as the devil.
¶78
Excommunicatio papae vel cuiuscumque praelati non est timenda quia est censura antichristi.
- The excommunication of the pope or of any prelate is not to be feared, because it is the censure of Antichrist.
¶79
Peccant fundantes claustra et ingredientes sunt viri diabolici.
- They sin who build cloisters, and those who enter them are devilish men.
¶80
Ditare clerum est contra christi mandatum.
- To enrich the clergy is against the mandate of Christ.
¶81
Silvester papa et Constantinus imperator erraverunt ecclesiam dotando.
- Pope Sylvester and Emperor Constantine erred in endowing the church.
¶82
Omnes de ordine mendicantium sunt haeretici et dantes eis eleemosynam sunt excommunicati.
- All of the mendicant order are heretics, and those giving alms to them are excommunicated.
¶83
Ingredientes religionem aut aliquem ordinem eo ipso inhabiles sunt ad observandum divina praecepta et per consequens perveniendi ad regna caelorum nisi apostataverint ab eisdem.
- Those entering a religion or any order are thereby rendered incapable of observing the divine precepts, and consequently of attaining the kingdoms of heaven, unless they apostatize from them.
¶84
Papa cum omnibus clericis suis possessionem habentibus sunt haeretici eo quod possessionem habent et omnes consentientes eis omnes scilicet domini saeculares et caeteri laici.
- The pope with all his clerics having possessions are heretics, because they have possessions, and all consenting to them—namely all secular lords and other laypersons.
¶85
Ecclesia Romana est synagoga satanae nec papa est immediatus et proximus vicarius christi et apostolorum.
- The Roman church is the synagogue of Satan, nor is the pope the immediate and proximate vicar of Christ and the apostles.
¶86
Decretales epistolae sunt apocryphae et seducunt a christi fide et clerici sunt stulti qui student eas.
- The decretal epistles are apocryphal and seduce from the faith of Christ, and the clerics who study them are foolish.
¶87
Imperator et domini saeculares seducti sunt a diabolo ut ecclesiam dotarent de bonis temporalibus.
- The emperor and secular lords were seduced by the devil that they endowed the church with temporal goods.
¶88
Electio papae a cardinalibus per diabolum est introducta.
- The election of the pope by the cardinals was introduced by the devil.
¶89
Non est de necessitate salutis credere Romanam ecclesiam esse supremam inter alias ecclesias.
- It is not of necessity for salvation to believe that the Roman church is supreme among other churches.
¶90
Fatuum est credere indulgentiis papae et episcoporum.
- It is foolish to believe in the indulgences of the pope and bishops.
¶91
Iuramenta illicita sunt quae fiunt ad roborandum humanos contractus et commercia civilia.
- Oaths which are made to strengthen human contracts and civil transactions are illicit.
¶92
Augustinus Benedictus Bernardus damnati sunt nisi poenituerint de hoc quod habuerunt possessiones et instituerunt et intraverunt religiones et sic a papa usque ad infimum religiosum omnes sunt haeretici.
- Augustine, Benedict, Bernard are damned unless they repented of having had possessions and instituted and entered religions, and thus from pope down to the lowest religious, all are heretics.
¶93
Omnes religiones indifferenter introductae sunt a diabolo.
- All religions without distinction were introduced by the devil.
¶94
Idem que Ioannes Wicleff libros dialogum et trialogum per ipsum nominatos et plures alios tractatus volumina et opuscula composuit in quibus praescriptos et plures alios damnabiles inseruit et dogmatizavit articulos. Quos libros ad sui perversi dogmatis publicationem publice legendos exposuit ex quibus insuper multa scandala damna animarum que pericula in diversis regionibus praesertim Angliae et Bohemiae regnis secuta sunt.
The same John Wycliffe also composed books called by him the Dialogus and Trialogus and many other treatises, volumes, and small works, in which he inserted and dogmatized the aforementioned and many other damnable articles. These books he set forth to be publicly read for the publication of his perverse dogma, from which moreover many scandals, losses of souls, and perils followed in various regions, especially in the kingdoms of England and Bohemia.
¶95
Adversus quos articulos atque libros in dei virtute exsurgentes magistri et doctores universitatum et studiorum Oxoniensis et Pragensis articulos praedictos scholastice diu post reprobaverunt. Reverendissimi insuper patres archiepiscopi et episcopi pro tempore Cantuariensis et Eboracensis sedis apostolicae legati in Angliae et Pragensis in Bohemiae regnis condemnarunt. Libros etiam eiusdem Ioannis Wicleff comburendos fore dictus archiepiscopus Pragensis sedis apostolicae commissarius in hac parte sententialiter iudicavit et eorum qui superessent prohibuit lectionem.
Against which articles and books, rising up in the strength of God, the masters and doctors of the universities and studies of Oxford and Prague scholastically reproved the aforesaid articles for a long time afterwards. Moreover, the most reverend fathers, the archbishops and bishops for the time of Canterbury and York, legates of the apostolic see in the kingdom of England, and of Prague in the kingdom of Bohemia, condemned them. Also, the said archbishop of Prague, commissary of the apostolic see in this matter, judged by sentence that the books of the same John Wycliffe were to be burned, and forbade the reading of those that survived.
¶96
Rursus his ad notitiam sedis apostolicae et generalis concilii deductis Romanus pontifex in concilio Romano ultimo celebrato dictos libros tractatus et opuscula condemnavit iubens illos publice concremari districtius inhibendo ne quis christi nomine insignitus auderet aliquem vel aliquos seu aliqua ex dictis libellis voluminibus et tractatibus ac opusculis legere exponere vel tenere aut illis quomodolibet uti vel illos nisi in ipsorum reprobationem allegare publice vel occulte.
Again, these things having been brought to the knowledge of the apostolic see and of the general council, the Roman pontiff in the last Roman council celebrated condemned the said books, treatises, and small works, ordering them to be publicly burned, more strictly inhibiting that anyone marked with the name of Christ dare to read, expound, or hold any or any of the said books, volumes, treatises, and small works, or to use them in any way, or to cite them except for their reprobation, publicly or secretly.
¶97
Et ut de medio ecclesiae illa periculosa spurcissima que doctrina eliminaretur iussit omnes per locorum ordinarios libros tractatus volumina et opuscula huiusmodi auctoritate apostolica per censuram ecclesiasticam etiam si opus esset cum adiectione quod contra non parentes procederetur tanquam contra fautores haeresis diligenter inquiri et repertos ac reperta ignibus publice concremari.
And so that that dangerous and most filthy doctrine might be eliminated from the midst of the church, he ordered that all such books, treatises, volumes, and small works be diligently searched for by the ordinaries of the places by apostolic authority, through ecclesiastical censure, even if it should be necessary with the addition that it be proceeded against those not obeying as against favorers of heresy, and that those found be publicly burned with fire.
¶98
Haec autem sancta synodus praefatos articulos quadragintaquinque examinari fecit et saepius recenseri per plures reverendissimos patres ecclesiae Romanae cardinales episcopos abbates magistros in theologia doctores iuris utriusque et plures notabiles in multitudine copiosa.
This holy synod caused the aforesaid forty-five articles to be examined and to be frequently reviewed by many most reverend fathers of the Roman church, cardinals, bishops, abbots, masters in theology, doctors of both laws, and many notable persons in a copious multitude.
¶99
Quibus articulis examinatis fuit repertum prout in veritate est aliquos et plures ex ipsis fuisse et esse notorios haereticos et a sanctis patribus dudum reprobatos alios non catholicos sed erroneos alios scandalosos et blasphemos quosdam piarum aurium offensivos nonnullos eorum temerarios et seditiosos.
These articles having been examined, it was found—as is in truth—that some and many of them have been and are notorious heretics, and were long ago reproved by the holy fathers; others, not catholic but erroneous; others, scandalous and blasphemous; some, offensive to pious ears; several of them, temerarious and seditious.
¶100
Compertum est etiam libros eius plures alios articulos continere similium qualitatum doctrinam que in dei ecclesia vesanam et fidei ac moribus inimicam inducere.
It was also found that his books contain many other articles of similar qualities and doctrine, introducing insanity into the church of God and inimical to faith and morals.
¶101
Propterea in nomine domini nostri Iesu christi haec sancta synodus sententias praedictorum archiepiscoporum ac concilii Romani ratificans et approbans praedictos articulos et eorum quemlibet libros eiusdem dialogum et trialogum per eumdem Ioannem Wicleff nominatos et alios eiusdem auctoris libros volumina tractatus et opuscula quocumque nomine censeantur quos hic haberi vult pro sufficienter expressis hoc perpetuo decreto reprobat et condemnat eorumdem librorum et cuiuslibet ipsorum lectionem doctrinam expositionem et allegationem nisi ad eorum reprobationem omnibus christi fidelibus prohibendo: inhibens omnibus et singulis catholicis sub anathematis interminatione ne de cetero dictos articulos aut ipsorum aliquem audeat publice praedicare dogmatizare vel tenere sive libros ipsos docere approbare et tenere vel quomodolibet nisi ad eorum reprobationem ut dictum est allegare: iubens illos libros et tractatus volumina et opuscula praelibata publice concremari prout decretum fuerat in synoda romana sicut superius est expressum.
Therefore, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, this holy synod, ratifying and approving the sentences of the aforesaid archbishops and of the Roman council, reproves and condemns by this perpetual decree the aforesaid articles and each of them, the books of the same, the Dialogus and Trialogus named by the same John Wycliffe, and the other books, volumes, treatises, and small works of the same author, by whatever name they are called—which it wishes to be held as sufficiently expressed here—prohibiting the reading, doctrine, exposition, and citation of the same books and of each of them to all Christ's faithful, except for their reprobation.
¶102
Super quibus et exsequendis et debite conservandis mandat praedicta sancta synodus ordinariis locorum vigilanter intendere prout ad quemlibet pertinet secundum iura et canonicas sanctiones.
Concerning which things, and for executing and duly preserving them, the aforesaid holy synod mandates the ordinaries of the places to attend vigilantly, as pertains to each according to the laws and canonical sanctions.
¶103
THE TWO HUNDRED SIXTY ARTICLES OF WYCLIFFE
Quia vero libris praedictis diligenter examinatis per doctores et magistros universitatis studii Oxoniensis ultra dictos quadragintaquinque articulos ducentos sexaginta extra pendentes collegerunt quorum aliqui cum supradictis in sententia coincidunt licet non in eadem forma verborum: et sicut de aliis superius dictum est quidam ipsorum erant et sunt haeretici quidam seditiosi quidam erronei alii temerarii nonnulli scandalosi alii insani nec non omnes pene contra bonos mores et catholicam veritatem fuerunt propterea per dictam universitatem scholastice et debite reprobati.
Because, moreover, with the aforesaid books diligently examined by the doctors and masters of the University of Oxford, beyond the said forty-five articles, they collected two hundred sixty others standing apart, some of which coincide with the above in sentence, although not in the same form of words, and as was said above concerning the others, some of them were and are heretical, some seditious, some erroneous, others temerarious, some scandalous, others insane, and almost all were against good morals and catholic truth; therefore by the said university they were scholastically and duly reproved.
¶104
Haec igitur synodus sacrosancta cum deliberatione qua supra praedictos articulos et eorum quemlibet reprobat et condemnat inhibens mandans et decernens prout de aliis xlv superius est expressum.
Therefore this sacrosanct synod, with the deliberation stated above, reproves and condemns the aforesaid articles and each of them, inhibiting, mandating, and decreeing as is expressed above concerning the other forty-five.
¶105
Eorumdem ducentorum sexaginta articulorum tenores duximus inferius inserendos.
The tenors of the same two hundred sixty articles we have deemed to be inserted below.
¶106
CONDEMNATION OF WYCLIFFE AS A HERETIC
Insuper quia auctoritate sive decreto Romani concilii mandato que ecclesiae et sedis apostolicae datis dilationibus debitis processum fuit super condemnatione dicti Wicleff et suae memoriae edictis propositis denunciationibus que ad vocandum eos qui eumdem sive eius memoriam defendere vellent si qui penitus exsisterent nullus vero comparuit qui eumdem vel suam memoriam defensaret.
Moreover, because by the authority or decree of the Roman council and the mandate of the church and apostolic see, due delays having been given, process was had concerning the condemnation of the said Wycliffe and his memory, with edicts and denunciations having been proposed for summoning those who would wish to defend the same or his memory, if any absolutely existed; and no one appeared who would defend him or his memory.
¶107
Examinatis insuper testibus super impoenitentia finali pertinacia que dicti Wicleff per commissarios deputatos per dominum Ioannem papam modernum et hoc sacrum concilium servatis que servandis prout in tali negotio postulat ordo iuris de eius impoenitentia ac finali pertinacia per evidentia signa testibus legitimis comprobata fuit legitime facta fides.
Moreover, witnesses having been examined concerning the final impenitence and pertinacity of the said Wycliffe by commissaries deputed by the lord Pope John the current pope and by this sacred council, and the things to be observed having been observed as the order of law demands in such a matter, faith was legitimately made concerning his impenitence and final pertinacity, having been proven by evident signs and legitimate witnesses.
¶108
Propterea instante procuratore fiscali edicto que proposito ad audiendam sententiam ad hunc diem haec sancta synodus declarat definit et sententiat eumdem Ioannem Wicleff fuisse notorium haereticum pertinacem ac in haeresi decessisse anathematizando ipsum pariter et suam memoriam condemnando.
Therefore, the fiscal procurator pressing and the edict having been proposed for hearing the sentence on this day, this holy synod declares, defines, and sentences that the same John Wycliffe was a notorious and pertinacious heretic and died in heresy, anathematizing him and likewise condemning his memory.
¶109
Decernit que et ordinat corpus et eius ossa si ab aliis fidelium corporibus discerni possint exhumari et procul ab ecclesiastica sepultura iactari secundum canonicas et legitimas sanctiones.
It decrees and ordains that his body and bones, if they can be discerned from the bodies of other faithful persons, be exhumed and cast far away from ecclesiastical burial according to the canonical and legitimate sanctions.
¶110
CONCERNING THE ELECTION OF A FUTURE POPE DURING THE VACANCY OF THE APOSTOLIC SEE
Sacrosancta generalis Constantiensis synodus ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans ad exstirpationem praesentis schismatis et errorum ecclesiae que reformationem in capite et in membris faciendam in spiritu sancto legitime congregata ad facilius celerius liberius et utilius unionem ecclesiae consequendam pronunciat decernit statuit et ordinat quod si contingat sedem apostolicam quovis modo vacare in prima illius vacatione ad electionem futuri summi pontificis nullo modo procedatur sine deliberatione et consensu huius sacri generalis concilii.
The sacrosanct general synod of Constance, representing the catholic church, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit for the extirpation of the present schism and the errors of the church and the reformation to be done in head and members, in order to obtain the union of the church more easily, more quickly, more freely, and more usefully, pronounces, decrees, ordains, and orders that if it happen that the apostolic see become vacant in any way, in that first vacation it shall by no means be proceeded to the election of a future supreme pontiff without the deliberation and consent of this sacred general council.
¶111
Et si contrarium fieret illud sit ipso facto auctoritate dicti sacri concilii irritum et inane.
And if the contrary be done, let it be ipso facto by the authority of the said sacred council invalid and void.
¶112
Nullus que electum contra hoc decretum in papam recipiat nec illi ut papae quomodolibet adhaereat vel oboediat sub poena fautoriae dicti schismatis et maledictionis aeternae: puniantur que hoc casu eligentes et electus si consentiat ac illi adhaerentes poenis per hoc sacrum concilium ordinandis.
Let no one receive as pope one elected against this decree, nor adhere to nor obey him as pope in any way, under penalty of favoring the said schism and of eternal malediction; and in this case let those electing, and the elected if he consents, and those adhering to him be punished with the penalties to be ordained by this sacred council.
¶113
Suspendit que dicta sancta synodus pro bono unionis ecclesiae omnia iura positiva etiam in conciliis generalibus edita et ipsorum statuta ordinationes consuetudines que et privilegia quibuscumque concessa et poenas contra quoscumque editas in quantum effectum dicti decreti impedire possent quoquo modo.
The said sacred synod suspends, for the good of the union of the church, all positive laws even issued in general councils and their statutes, ordinances, customs, and privileges granted to anyone whatsoever, and penalties enacted against anyone whatsoever, insofar as they could impede the effect of the said decree in any way.

SENTENCE OF DEPOSITION AGAINST POPE JOHN XXIII (BALDASSARE COSSA) – SESSION XIV (MAY 29, 1415)
In nomine sanctae et individuae trinitatis patris et filii et spiritus sancti Amen.
In the name of the holy and individual Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Amen.
¶115
Sacrosancta generalis Constantiensis synodus in spiritu sancto legitime congregata invocato christi nomine solum deum prae oculis habens visis articulis in hac causa contra dominum Ioannem papam xxiii formatis et oblatis ac probationibus eorumdem factis eiusdem submissione spontanea una cum toto processu huius causae habita matura deliberatione super eisdem per hanc sententiam definitivam quam profert in scriptis pronunciat decernit et declarat recessum per praefatum dominum Ioannem papam xxiii ab hac civitate Constantiensi et dicto sacro generali concilio clandestine de nocte hora suspecta in habitu dissimulato et indecenti factum fuisse et esse illicitum ecclesiae dei et dicto concilio notorie scandalosum pacis et unionis ipsius ecclesiae turbativum et impeditivum schismatis inveterati nutritivum a voto promissione et iuramento per ipsum dominum Ioannem papam deo et ecclesiae et huic sacro concilio praestitis deviativum ipsum que dominum Ioannem papam simoniacum notorium bonorum que et iurium nedum Romanae sed aliarum ecclesiarum et plurium aliorum locorum piorum dilapidatorem notorium malum que spiritualium et temporalium ecclesiae administratorem et dispensatorem fuisse et esse suis detestabilibus inhonestis que vita et moribus ecclesiam dei et populum christianum notorie scandalizantem ante eius assumptionem ad papatum et post usque ad ista tempora: ipsum que per praemissa ecclesiam dei et populum christianum notorie scandalizasse et scandalizare post que monitiones debitas et charitativas iteratis et crebris vicibus eidem factas in praemissis malis pertinaciter perseverasse se que ex hoc notorie incorrigibilem reddidisse ipsum que propter praemissa et alia crimina in processu dictae causae contra eum deducta et contenta tamquam indignum inutilem et damnosum a papatu et omni eius administratione spirituali et temporali amovendum privandum et deponendum fore.
The sacrosanct general synod of Constance, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, the name of Christ having been invoked, having only God before its eyes, having seen the articles formed and offered in this cause against the lord Pope John XXIII and the proofs of them made, and his spontaneous submission together with the whole process of this cause, having held mature deliberation concerning them, by this definitive sentence which it pronounces in writing, pronounces, decrees, and declares that the departure of the aforesaid lord Pope John XXIII from this city of Constance and from the said sacred general council—clandestinely, at night, at a suspect hour, in a dissimulated and indecent habit—was and is illicit, notoriously scandalous to the church of God and the said council, disturbing and impeding the peace and union of the same church, nourishing the inveterate schism, deviating from the vow, promise, and oath made by the same lord Pope John to God, to the church, and to this sacred council; and that the same lord Pope John was and is a notorious simoniac, a notorious dilapidator of the goods and rights not only of the Roman see but of other churches and of very many other pious places, an evil administrator and dispenser of the spiritual and temporal goods of the church, notoriously scandalizing the church of God and the Christian people by his detestable and dishonest life and morals before his assumption to the papacy and after up to the present times; and that by the premises he has notoriously scandalized and does scandalize the church of God and the Christian people, and after due and charitable admonitions made to him repeatedly and frequently, he has pertinaciously persisted in the aforesaid evils, and by this he has rendered himself notoriously incorrigible; and that on account of the premises and other crimes deduced and contained in the process of the said cause against him, he is to be removed, deprived, and deposed from the papacy and from all his spiritual and temporal administration, as unworthy, useless, and harmful.
¶116
Et eum dicta sancta synodus amovet privat et deponit universos et singulos christicolas cuiuscumque status dignitatis vel conditionis exsistant ab eius oboedientia fidelitate et iuramento absolutos declarando.
And the said sacred synod removes, deprives, and deposes him, declaring absolved from his obedience, fidelity, and oath all and singular Christians, of whatever state, dignity, or condition they may exist.
¶117
Inhibendo insuper universis christifidelibus ne eumdem a papatu ut praemittitur sic depositum de cetero in papam recipiant seu eum papam nominent aut illi tamquam papae adhaereant seu quoquo modo oboediant.
Moreover, inhibiting all Christ's faithful that they do not henceforth receive as pope the same thus deposed from the papacy as premised, nor name him pope, nor adhere to him as pope, nor obey him in any way.
¶118
Et nihilominus ex certa scientia et de plenitudine potestatis supplet dicta sancta synodus omnes et singulos defectus si qui forsan in praemissis vel aliquo praemissorum intervenerint.
And nonetheless, from certain knowledge and from the plenitude of power, the said sacred synod supplies all and singular defects, if any perchance have intervened in the premises or in any of the premises.
¶119
Eum que ad standum et morandum in aliquo bono loco et honesto sub custodia tuta serenissimi principis domini Sigismundi Romanorum et Hungariae et caetera regis et universalis ecclesiae advocati et defensoris devotissimi nomine dicti sacri concilii generalis quamdiu dicto sacro concilio generali pro bono unionis ecclesiae dei videbitur condemnandum fore et eadem sententia condemnat.
And it condemns him to stand and to remain in some good and honest place under safe custody of the most serene prince, the lord Sigismund, king of the Romans and Hungary, etc., and most devoted advocate and defender of the universal church, in the name of the said general sacred council, as long as it shall seem to the said sacred general council for the good of the union of the church of God; and by the same sentence it condemns him.
¶120
Alias vero poenas quae pro dictis criminibus et excessibus inferri deberent iuxta canonicas sanctiones dictum concilium arbitrio suo reservat declarandas et infligendas secundum quod rigor iustitiae vel misericordiae ratio suadebit.
Other penalties, however, which ought to be inflicted for the said crimes and excesses according to the canonical sanctions, the said council reserves to its own arbitration to be declared and inflicted according as the rigor of justice or the reason of mercy shall persuade.
¶121
DECREE CONCERNING THE DEPOSED POPES
Item decernit statuit et ordinat dicta sancta synodus pro bono unionis ecclesiae dei quod nullo umquam tempore reeligantur in papam dominus Balthasar de Cossa nuper Ioannes xxiii nec Angelus de Corario Gregorius xii nec Petrus de Luna Benedictus xiii in suis oboedientiis nuncupati.
Likewise, the said sacred synod decrees, ordains, and ordains for the good of the union of the church of God that at no time may the lord Baldassare Cossa, lately called John XXIII, nor Angelus of Corario, called Gregory XII, nor Peter of Luna, called Benedict XIII in their obediences, be re-elected as pope.
¶122
Et si contrarium fieret id sit ipso facto irritum et inane.
And if the contrary be done, let it be ipso facto invalid and void.
¶123
Nullus que cuiuscumque dignitatis vel praeminentiae etiam si imperiali regali cardinalatus vel pontificali dignitate praefulgeat eis vel eorum alteri contra hoc decretum ullo umquam tempore oboediat seu adhaereat sub poena fautoriae dicti schismatis et maledictionis aeternae ad quas contra praesumptores si qui in posterum fuerint etiam cum invocatione brachii saecularis et alias rigide procedatur
Let no one, of whatever dignity or pre-eminence, even if he shine with imperial, royal, cardinalatial, or pontifical dignity, obey or adhere to them or any of them against this decree, at any time, under penalty of favoring the said schism and of eternal malediction, against which, against presumptors, if there shall be any in the future, it be strictly proceeded even with the invocation of the secular arm and otherwise.
¶124
DECREE CONCERNING COMMUNION UNDER BOTH KINDS (SESSION XIII, JUNE 15, 1415)
In nomine sanctae et individuae trinitatis patris et filii et spiritus sancti Amen.
In the name of the holy and individual Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Amen.
¶125
Cum in nonnullis mundi partibus quidam temerarie asserere praesumant populum christianum debere sacrum eucharistiae sacramentum sub utraque panis et vini specie suscipere et non solum sub specie panis sed etiam sub specie vini populum laicum passim communicent etiam post coenam vel alias non ieiunum et communicandum esse pertinaciter asserant contra laudabilem ecclesiae consuetudinem rationabiliter comprobatam quam tamquam sacrilegam damnabiliter reprobare conantur a capite incipiendo: hinc est quod hoc praesens Constantiense concilium generale in spiritu sancto legitime congregatum adversus hunc errorem saluti fidelium providere satagens matura plurium doctorum tam divini quam humani iuris deliberatione praehabita declarat decernit et definit quod licet christus post coenam instituerit et suis apostolis ministraverit sub utraque specie panis et vini hoc venerabile sacramentum tamen hoc non obstante sacrorum canonum auctoritas laudabilis et approbata consuetudo ecclesiae servavit et servat quod huiusmodi sacramentum non debet confici post coenam neque a fidelibus recipi non ieiunis nisi in casu infirmitatis aut alius necessitatis a iure vel ab ecclesia concesso vel admisso.
Since in some parts of the world certain persons rashly presume to assert that the Christian people ought to receive the sacred sacrament of the Eucharist under both the species of bread and wine, and that they communicate the lay people indiscriminately not only under the species of bread but also under the species of wine, even after supper or otherwise not fasting, and pertinaciously assert that it is to be communicated against the laudable and reasonably approved custom of the church—which they damnably attempt to reprobate as sacrilegious, beginning from the head—hence it is that this present general Council of Constance, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, wishing to provide for the salvation of the faithful against this error, a mature deliberation of many doctors of both divine and human law having been held beforehand, declares, decrees, and defines that although Christ after supper instituted and administered to His apostles this venerable sacrament under both species of bread and wine, nevertheless this not standing in the way, the authority of the sacred canons and the laudable and approved custom of the church has kept and keeps that such a sacrament ought not to be confected after supper nor to be received by the faithful not fasting, except in case of infirmity or other necessity conceded or admitted by law or by the church.
¶126
Et sicut haec consuetudo ad vitanda aliqua pericula et scandala rationabiliter introducta est sic potuit simili aut maiori ratione introduci et rationabiliter observari quod licet in primitiva ecclesia huiusmodi sacramentum reciperetur a fidelibus sub utraque specie tamen postea a conficientibus sub utraque et a laicis tantummodo sub specie panis suscipiatur cum firmissime credendum sit et nullatenus dubitandum integrum christi corpus et sanguinem tam sub specie panis quam sub specie vini veraciter contineri.
And just as this custom was reasonably introduced to avoid certain perils and scandals, so for a similar or greater reason it could be introduced and reasonably observed that although in the primitive church this sacrament was received by the faithful under both species, nevertheless later it is received by those confecting under both and by laypersons only under the species of bread, since it is to be most firmly believed and by no means doubted that the whole body and blood of Christ are truly contained both under the species of bread and under the species of wine.
¶127
Unde cum huiusmodi consuetudo ab ecclesia et sanctis patribus rationabiliter introducta et diutissime observata sit habenda est pro lege quam non licet reprobare aut sine ecclesiae auctoritate pro libito innovare.
Hence, since such a custom, having been reasonably introduced by the church and the holy fathers and observed for a very long time, is to be held as a law which it is not lawful to reprobate or to innovate at pleasure without the authority of the church.
¶128
Quapropter dicere quod hanc consuetudinem aut legem observare sit sacrilegum aut illicitum censeri debet erroneum: et pertinaciter asserentes oppositum praemissarum tamquam haeretici arcendi sunt et graviter puniendi per dioecesanos locorum seu officiales eorum aut inquisitores haereticae pravitatis in regnis seu provinciis in quibus contra hoc decretum aliquid fuerit forsan attentatum aut praesumptum iuxta canonicas et legitimas sanctiones in favorem catholicae fidei contra haereticos et eorum fautores salubriter adinventas.
Wherefore, to say that to observe this custom or law is sacrilegious or illicit ought to be judged erroneous; and those pertinaciously asserting the opposite of the premises are to be restrained as heretics and gravely punished by the diocesans of the places or their officials or the inquisitors of heretical depravity in the kingdoms or provinces in which anything shall perhaps have been attempted or presumed against this decree, according to the canonical and legitimate sanctions salubriously devised in favor of the catholic faith against heretics and their favorers.
¶129
Item ipsa sancta synodus decernit et declarat super ista materia reverendissimis in christo patribus et dominis patriarchis primatibus archiepiscopis episcopis et eorum in spiritualibus vicariis ubilibet constitutis processus esse dirigendos in quibus eis committatur et mandetur auctoritate huius sacri concilii sub poena excommunicationis ut effectualiter puniant eos contra hoc decretum excedentes.
Likewise, the same sacred synod decrees and declares that concerning this matter processes are to be sent to the most reverend fathers in Christ and lords, patriarchs, primates, archbishops, bishops, and their vicars in spirituals constituted anywhere, in which it be committed and mandated to them by the authority of this sacred council under penalty of excommunication that they effectually punish those exceeding this decree.
¶130
Eos vero qui in communicando populum sub utraque specie panis et vini exorbitaverunt et sic faciendum esse docuerunt si ad poenitentiam redeant ad gremium ecclesiae suscipiant iniuncta eis pro modo culpae poenitentia salutari.
But those who have deviated in communicating the people under both species of bread and wine and have taught that it ought to be so done, if they return to penance, let them be received into the bosom of the church, salutary penance having been enjoined on them according to the measure of the fault.
¶131
Qui vero ex illis ad poenitentiam redire non curaverint animo indurato per censuras ecclesiasticas per eos ut haeretici sunt coercendi invocato etiam ad hoc si opus fuerit auxilio brachii saecularis.
But those among them who have not taken care to return to penance with hardened heart are to be coerced by ecclesiastical censures as heretics by them, the aid of the secular arm having also been invoked for this if necessary.

SENTENCE OF CONDEMNATION OF JAN HUS (SESSION XV, JUNE 6, 1415)
CONCERNING THE CONDEMNATION OF THE ARTICLES OF JOHN HUS
Cum principium cuiuslibet rei potissima pars sit ut principium decens et amabile deo et possibilis ecclesiae reintegratio fiat sacrosancta generalis synodus Constantiensis in spiritu sancto congregata legitime ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans ut istae duae oboedientiae videlicet illa quae profitetur dominum Ioannem xxiii olim fuisse papam et illa quae profitetur dominum Gregorium xii esse papam unanimiter una alteri et altera alteri coniungantur sub capite christo dictas convocationem auctorizationem approbationem et confirmationem nomine illius domini qui in sua oboedientia dicitur Gregorius xii nunc factas quantum ad ipsum spectare videtur quia abundans ad certitudinem pro bona cautela nemini nocet sed omnibus prodest in omnibus et per omnia admittit et aliquo modo has supradictas duas oboedientias coniunctas et unitas esse decernit et declarat in uno corpore domini nostri Iesu christi et huius sacri universalis concilii generalis in nomine patris et filii et spiritus sancti.
Since the principle of any matter is the most powerful part, so that a becoming beginning may be made, lovable to God, and a possible reintegration of the church may be achieved, the sacrosanct general synod of Constance, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, representing the catholic church, in order that these two obediences—namely that which professes that the lord John XXIII was formerly pope and that which professes that the lord Gregory XII is pope—may be united unanimously, one to the other and the other to the other, under Christ the head, admits and in some manner admits the convocation, authorization, approval, and confirmation made now in the name of that lord who in his obedience is called Gregory XII, insofar as it seems to pertain to him, because abundance for certainty for the sake of good caution harms no one but profits all, in all and through all, and decrees and declares that these aforesaid two obediences are conjoined and united into one body of our Lord Jesus Christ and of this sacred universal general council, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

DECREE CONCERNING THE FUTURE ELECTION OF THE ROMAN PONTIFF
Sacrosancta generalis synodus Constantiensis et caetera ut ecclesiae sanctae dei eo melius sincerius et securius provideatur statuit pronunciat ordinat et decernit quod proxima electio futuri Romani pontificis fiat modo forma loco tempore et materia per sacrum concilium ordinandis.
The sacrosanct general synod of Constance, etc., so that the holy church of God may be provided for better, more sincerely, and more securely, ordains, pronounces, orders, and decrees that the next election of the future Roman pontiff take place in the mode, form, place, time, and matter to be ordained by the sacred council.
¶134
Et quod idem concilium possit et valeat in futurum quascumque personas cuiuscumque status vel oboedientiae sint aut fuerint ad electionem huiusmodi active et passive et alios quoscumque actus ecclesiasticos et omnia alia opportuna non obstantibus quibuscumque processibus poenis aut sententiis habilitare recipere et deputare modo et forma quibus tunc videbitur expedire.
And that the same council can and may in the future habilitate, receive, and depute any persons, of whatever state or obedience they are or may be, for such election actively and passively and for any other ecclesiastical acts and all other opportune things, in the mode and form which shall then seem expedient, notwithstanding any processes, penalties, or sentences whatsoever.
¶135
Quod que sacrum concilium non dissolvatur donec electio huiusmodi fuerit celebrata.
And that the sacred council shall not be dissolved until such election shall have been celebrated.
¶136
Exhortans dicta sancta synodus invictissimum principem dominum Sigismundum Romanorum et Hungarorum regem pariter et requirens quatenus tamquam pius ecclesiae advocatus et sacri concilii defensor et protector omnem ad hoc operam efficaciter impendat et verbo regio velle facere hoc promittat ac litteras suae maiestatis desuper fieri decernat.
The said sacred synod exhorts and likewise requests the most invincible prince, the lord Sigismund, king of the Romans and Hungarians, that as a pious advocate of the church and defender and protector of the sacred council, he efficaciously expend all effort for this and promise by his royal word to wish to do this, and decree that letters of his majesty be made concerning it.

Sacrosancta generalis synodus Constantiensis in spiritu sancto legitime congregata universalem ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans cessionem renunciationem et resignationem pro parte illius domini qui in sua oboedientia dicebatur Gregorius xii iuris tituli et possessionis quod quem et quam habuit in papatu vel si quod quem vel quam habuit in papatu nunc per magnificum et potentem dominum Carolum de Malatestis hic praesentem eius ad hoc procuratorem irrevocabilem pro eodem domino qui dicebatur Gregorius xii factas admittit approbat et collaudat in nomine patris et filii et spiritus sancti.

CONDEMNATION OF THE ARTICLES OF JOHN HUS
Sacrosancta Constantiensis synodus generalis ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans ad exstirpationem schismatis errorum que et haeresium in spiritu sancto legitime congregata auditis diligenter et examinatis libris et opusculis damnatae memoriae Ioannis Wicleff per doctores et magistros studii generalis Oxoniensis qui de eisdem libris et opusculis ducentos et sexaginta reprobos excerpendo collegerunt et scholastice reprobarunt articulos quos omnes haec sacra synodus fecit examinari et saepius recenseri per plures reverendissimos patres ecclesiae Romanae cardinales episcopos abbates magistros in theologia doctores utriusque iuris et alios plures notabiles diversorum generalium studiorum numero copioso.
The sacrosanct general synod of Constance, representing the catholic church, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit for the extirpation of schisms, errors, and heresies, having diligently heard and examined the books and small works of John Wycliffe of damned memory through the doctors and masters of the general study of Oxford, who collected and scholastically reproved two hundred and sixty reprobate articles, excerpting them from the same books and small works—all of which this sacred synod caused to be examined and to be frequently reviewed by many most reverend fathers of the Roman church, cardinals, bishops, abbots, masters in theology, doctors of both laws, and many other notable persons of various general studies in a numerous quantity.
¶139
Quibus articulis sic examinatis fuit et est repertum aliquos et plures ex ipsis fuisse et esse notorie haereticos et dudum a sanctis patribus reprobatos quosdam piarum aurium offensivos nonnullos eorumdem temerarios et seditiosos.
These articles having been thus examined, it was and is found that some and many of them have been and are notoriously heretical and were long ago reproved by the holy fathers; some, offensive to pious ears; several of them, temerarious and seditious.
¶140
Propterea in nomine domini nostri Iesu christi haec sancta synodus praedictos articulos et eorum quemlibet hoc perpetuo decreto reprobat et condemnat: inhibens omnibus et singulis catholicis sub anathematis interminatione ne de cetero dictos articulos aut ipsorum aliquem audeant praedicare dogmatizare offerre vel tenere.
Therefore, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, this holy synod reproves and condemns by this perpetual decree the aforesaid articles and each of them, inhibiting all and singular catholics under the intermination of anathema that they henceforth dare to preach, dogmatize, set forth, or hold the said articles or any of them.
¶141
Super quibus exsequendis et debite conservandis mandat praedicta sancta synodus ordinariis locorum ac inquisitoribus haereticae pravitatis vigilanter intendere prout ad quemlibet pertinet secundum iura et canonicas sanctiones.
Concerning which things to be executed and duly preserved, the aforesaid sacred synod mandates the ordinaries of the places and the inquisitors of heretical depravity to attend vigilantly, as pertains to each according to the laws and canonical sanctions.
¶142
Si quis vero praemissorum huius sacri concilii decretorum et sententiarum temerarius violator exstiterit animadversione debita per locorum ordinarios quocumque privilegio non obstante sacri huius auctoritate concilii puniatur.
But if anyone shall exist as a rash violator of the decrees and sentences of this sacred council, let him be punished with due animadversion by the ordinaries of the places, any privilege notwithstanding, by the authority of this sacred council.
¶143
THE THIRTY ARTICLES OF JOHN HUS
Sicut christus est simul deus et homo sic hostia consecrata est simul corpus christi et verus panis.
Just as Christ is at once God and man, so the consecrated host is at once the body of Christ and true bread.
¶144
Quia est corpus christi ad minimum in figura et panis verus in natura vel quod idem sonat est verus panis naturaliter et corpus christi figuraliter.
Because it is the body of Christ at least in figure, and true bread in nature, or what sounds the same, it is true bread naturally and the body of Christ figuratively.
¶145
Cum mendacium haereticum de hostia consecrata inter haereses singulas teneat principatum ut ipsa ab ecclesia exstirpetur secure denuncio modernis haereticis quod non possunt declarare nec intelligere accidens sine subiecto.
Since heretical falsehood concerning the consecrated host holds the principality among the individual heresies, so that it may be securely extirpated by the church, I announce to modern heretics that they cannot declare nor understand an accident without a subject.
¶146
Et ideo omnes istae sectae haereticae in capitulo ignorantium Ioannis quartum: nos adoramus quod scimus.
And therefore all these heretical sects are in the chapter of the ignorant: John 4: "We adore what we know."
¶147
Audacter praenostico omnibus istis sectis et suis complicibus quod non defendant fidelibus quod sacramentum erit accidens sine subiecto antequam christus et tota triumphans ecclesia venerit in finali iudicio equitans super flatum angeli Gabrielis.
Boldly I foretell to all these sects and their accomplices that they will not defend to the faithful that the sacrament will be an accident without a subject before Christ and the whole triumphant church shall have come in the final judgment riding upon the breath of the angel Gabriel.
¶148
Sicut Ioannes figuraliter fuit Elias et non personaliter sic panis in altari figuraliter est corpus christi.
Just as John was Elijah figuratively and not personally, so the bread on the altar is figuratively the body of Christ.
¶149
Et absque omni ambiguitate haec est figurativa locutio: hoc est corpus meum sicut ista locutio: Ioannes est Elias.
And without all ambiguity this is a figurative locution: "This is my body," just as this locution: "John is Elijah."
¶150
Fructus istius dementiae qua fingitur accidens sine subiecto foret blasphemare in deum scandalizare sanctos et illudere ecclesiae per mendacia accidentis.
The fruit of that insanity by which an accident without a subject is feigned would be to blaspheme God, scandalize the saints, and mock the church through the falsehoods of an accident.
¶151
Definientes parvulos fidelium sine sacramentali baptismo decedentes non fore salvandos sunt in hoc stolidi et praesumptuosi.
Those defining that the children of the faithful dying without sacramental baptism are not to be saved are in this foolish and presumptuous.
¶152
Levis et brevis confirmatio episcoporum cum additis ritibus tantum solemnizatis est ex motione diaboli introducta ut populus in fide ecclesiae illudatur et episcoporum solemnitas aut necessitas plus credatur.
The light and brief confirmation of bishops, with the added rites only solemnized, was introduced by the motion of the devil, that the people might be deceived in the faith of the church and the solemnity or necessity of bishops be more believed.
¶153
Quantum ad oleum quo episcopi ungunt pueros et peplum lineum quod complexum est capiti videtur quod sit ritus levis infundabilis ex scriptura.
As to the oil with which bishops anoint children and the linen cloth which is wrapped around the head, it seems that it is a light rite not able to be founded in scripture.
¶154
Et quod ista confirmatio introducta super apostolos blasphemat in deum.
And that this confirmation introduced upon the apostles blasphemes God.
¶155
Confessio vocalis facta sacerdoti introducta per Innocentium non est tam necessaria homini ut definit.
Vocal confession made to a priest was introduced by Innocent, nor is it as necessary to man as he defines.
¶156
Quia si quis solum cogitatu verbo vel opere offenderet fratrem suum solo cogitatu verbo opere sufficit poenitere.
Because if anyone should have offended his brother only in thought, word, or deed, it suffices to repent in thought, word, deed alone.
¶157
Grave est et infundabile presbyterum audire confessionem populi modo quo Latini utuntur.
It is grave and unsubstantiable for a presbyter to hear the confession of the people in the manner which the Latins use.
¶158
In his verbis vos mundi estis sed non omnes posuit diabolus pedicam infidelium qua pedem caperet christiani.
In these words "You are clean, but not all," the devil placed a snare of the unbelieving by which he might catch the foot of the Christian.
¶159
Introduxit enim confessionem privatam et infundabilem.
For he introduced private and unsubstantiable confession.
¶160
Et postquam illa confessori nota fuit ut legem statuit quod non prodatur populo malitia sic confessi.
And after it was known to the confessor, he established as law that the malice of the one thus confessed not be betrayed to the people.
¶161
Coniectura probabilis est quod talis qui rite vivit est diaconus vel sacerdos.
It is probable conjecture that such a person who lives rightly is a deacon or priest.
¶162
Sicut enim coniicio quod iste est Ioannes sic probabili coniectura cognosco quod iste sancte vivendo constitutus est a deo in tali officio sive statu.
For just as I conjecture that this is John, so by probable conjecture I know that this person, living holy, is constituted by God in such an office or state.
¶163
Non ex testificatione hominis ordinantis sed ex iustificatione operis capienda est probabilis evidentia talis status.
The probable evidence of such a state is to be taken not from the testimony of the ordaining man but from the justification of the work.
¶164
Deus enim potest sine tali instrumento digno vel indigno personam aliam in tali statu constituere.
For God can without such a worthy or unworthy instrument constitute another person in such a state.
¶165
Nec est probabilior evidentia quam ex vita.
Nor is there more probable evidence than from life.
¶166
Ideo habita vita sancta et doctrina catholica satis est ecclesiae militanti.
Therefore, holy life and catholic doctrine being had, it suffices for the militant church.
¶167
Error in principio et fine.
Error in the beginning and end.
¶168
Conversatio mala praelati subtrahit acceptationem ordinum et aliorum sacramentorum a subditis.
The evil conversation of a prelate subtracts the acceptance of orders and other sacraments from subjects.
¶169
Qui tamen necessitate urgente possent hoc ab eis capere supplicando pie quod deus suppleat per ministros suos diabolos opus vel finem officii ad quod iurant.
Who, however, in necessity pressing, could receive these things from them, piously supplicating that God may supply through His ministers—devils—the work or end of the office to which they swear.
¶170
Antiqui ex cupiditate temporalium ex spe mutuorum iuvaminum aut ex causa excusandae libidinis licet desperent de prole copulentur ad invicem nam vere matrimonialiter copulantur.
The ancients, from cupidity of temporal things, from hope of mutual aids, or from the cause of excusing lust, although they despair of offspring, are joined together; for truly they are joined matrimonially.
¶171
Haec verba: accipiam te in uxorem eligibiliora sunt in contractu matrimoniali quam ista: ego te accipio in uxorem.
These words "I will take you as wife" are more eligible in a matrimonial contract than these: "I take you as wife."
¶172
Et quod contrahendo cum una per haec verba de futuro et post cum alia per haec verba de praesenti non debent frustrari verba prima per verba secundaria de praesenti.
And that contracting with one by these words of the future, and after with another by these words of the present, the first words ought not to be frustrated by the second words of the present.
¶173
Papa qui se falso nominat servum servorum dei sub nullo gradu est in opere evangelii sed mundano.
The pope who falsely names himself servant of the servants of God is under no grade in the work of the gospel but worldly.
¶174
Et si sit in ordine aliquo est in ordine daemonum deo plus culpabiliter servientium.
And if he be in any order, he is in the order of demons serving God more culpably.
¶175
Papa non dispensat cum simonia vel voto temerario cum ipse sit capitalis simoniacus vovens temerarie servare statum suum damnabiliter hic in via.
The pope does not dispense with simony or a rash vow, since he himself is a capital simoniac rashly vowing to keep his state damnably here in the way.

Error in fine.
Error in the end.
¶177
Quod papa sit summus pontifex est ridiculum.
That the pope is supreme pontiff is ridiculous.
¶178
Et christus nec in Petro nec in alio talem approbavit dignitatem.
And Christ approved such dignity neither in Peter nor in another.
¶179
Papa est patulus antichristus.
The pope is a manifest Antichrist.
¶180
Non solum illa persona simplex sed multitudo paparum a tempore donationis ecclesiae cardinalium episcoporum et suorum complicum aliorum est antichristi persona composita monstruosa.
Not only that single person but the multitude of popes from the time of the donation of the church, cardinals, bishops, and their accomplices and others, is the monstrous composite person of Antichrist.
¶181
Non tamen repugnat quin Gregorius et alii papae qui in vita sua fecerunt multa bona de genere fructuoso finaliter poenitebant.
It does not, however, oppose that Gregory and other popes who in their life did many good deeds, from the fruitful kind, finally repented.
¶182
Petrus et Clemens cum ceteris adiutoribus in fide non fuerunt papae sed dei adiutores ad aedificandam ecclesiam domini nostri Iesu christi.
Peter and Clement with the other helpers in the faith were not popes but helpers of God for building the church of our Lord Jesus Christ.
¶183
Quod ex fide evangelii ista papalis praeminentia coepit ortum est aeque falsum sicut quod ex prima veritate error quilibet exortus.
That this papal pre-eminence began from the faith of the gospel is as false as that any error has arisen from the first truth.
¶184
Duodecim sunt procuratores et discipuli antichristi: papa cardinales patriarchae archiepiscopi episcopi archidiaconi officiales decani monachi bifurcati canonici pseudofratres introducti iam ultimo et quaestores.
There are twelve procurators and disciples of Antichrist: the pope, cardinals, patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, archdeacons, officials, deans, bifurcated monks, canons, pseudo-brothers introduced last of all, and questors.
¶185
Patet luce clarius quod quicumque est humilior ecclesiae servicinior et in amore christi quoad suam ecclesiam amacinior est in ecclesia militante maior et proximus christi vicarius reputandus.
It is clearer than light that whoever is humbler in the service of the church and more loving in the love of Christ toward his church is greater in the militant church and is to be reputed the nearest vicar of Christ.
¶186
Omnis iniuste occupans quodcumque bonum dei capit rapina furto vel latrocinio aliena.
Everyone unjustly occupying any good of God takes the goods of others by rapine, theft, or brigandage.
¶187
Nec testium depositio nec iudicis sententiatio nec corporalis possessio sicut nec descensus haereditarius nec humana commutatio sive donatio confert homini sine gratia dominium vel ius ad aliquid vel omnia ista simul.
Neither the deposition of witnesses nor the sentencing of a judge nor corporal possession, just as neither hereditary descent nor human exchange or donation, confers upon a man without grace dominion or right to anything, nor all these together.
¶188
Error si intelligatur de gratia gratum faciente.
Error if understood of grace making pleasing.
¶189
Nisi adsit lex charitatis intrinsecus nemo propter chartas vel bullas habet habilitatem vel iustitiam plus vel minus.
Unless the law of charity be present intrinsically, no one on account of charters or bulls has ability or justice more or less.
¶190
Nos non debemus praestare aut donare aliquid peccatori dum cognoscimus ipsum esse talem. Quia sic foveremus proditorem dei nostri.
We ought not to lend or give anything to a sinner while we know him to be such. Because thus we would foster the betrayer of our God.
¶191
Sicut princeps vel dominus tempore quo est in peccato mortali non sortitur nomen illius officii nisi nomine tenus et satis aequivoce sic nec papa episcopus vel sacerdos dum lapsus fuerit in mortali.
Just as a prince or lord at the time when he is in mortal sin does not obtain the name of that office except in name and very equivocally, so neither the pope nor bishop nor priest while he has lapsed into mortal [sin].
¶192
Omnis habituatus in peccato mortali caret quocumque dominio et usu licito operis etiam boni de genere.
Everyone habituated in mortal sin lacks any dominion and lawful use of the work, even of the good in kind.
¶193
Ex principiis fidei est per se notum quod quidquid homo in mortali peccato fecerit peccat mortaliter.
From the principles of faith it is per se known that whatever a man in mortal sin does, he sins mortally.
¶194
Ad verum saeculare dominium requiritur iustitia dominantis hic quod nullus exsistens in peccato mortali est dominus alicuius rei.
For true secular dominion, the justice of the dominus is required here; that no one existing in mortal sin is lord of anything.
¶195
Omnes religiosi moderni se ipsos necessitant ut hypocrisi maculentur. Ad hoc enim sonat sua professio ut sic ieiunent ut sic induant et ut sic faciant quidquid differenter ab aliis observant.
All modern religious persons necessitate themselves to be stained with hypocrisy. For their profession sounds to this: that they thus fast, that they thus dress, and that they thus do whatever they observe differently from others.
¶196
Omnis privata religio sapit ut sic imperfectionem et peccatum quo homo indisponitur ad deo libere serviendum.
Every private religion savors thus of imperfection and sin by which a man is indisposed to serve God freely.
¶197
Religio sive regula privata sapit praesumptionem blasphemam et arrogantem supra deum.
A private religion or rule savors of blasphemous presumption and arrogance above God.
¶198
Et religiosi talium ordinum per hypocrisin defensionis suae religionis praesumunt se supra apostolos exaltare.
And the religious persons of such orders, through the hypocrisy of the defense of their religion, presume to exalt themselves above the apostles.
¶199
Christus non docet in scriptura aliquam speciem ordinis de capitulo antichristi. Et ideo non est de suo beneplacito quod sint tales.
Christ does not teach in scripture any species of order from the chapter of Antichrist. And therefore it is not of His good pleasure that there be such.
¶200
Capitulum autem istud in istis speciebus duodecim continetur quae sunt papa cardinales patriarchae archiepiscopi episcopi archidiaconi officiales decani monachi canonici fratres de quatuor ordinibus et quaestores.
That chapter, moreover, is contained in these twelve species: pope, cardinals, patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, archdeacons, officials, deans, monks, canons, brothers of the four orders, and questors.
¶201
Ex fide et operibus quatuor sectarum quae sunt clerus caesareus varius monachus varius canonicus atque fratres evidenter elicio quod nulla persona istarum est membrum christi in sanctorum catalogo nisi in fine dierum deseruerit acceptatam stolide sectam suam.
From the faith and works of the four sects—which are the various Caesarean clergy, the various monk, the canon, and the brothers—I clearly elicit that no person of these is a member of Christ in the catalogue of saints, unless at the end of days he shall have deserted his foolishly accepted sect.
¶202
Paulus quondam pharisaeus propter meliorem sectam christi de eius licentia sectam illam dereliquit.
Paul, once a Pharisee, on account of the better sect of Christ, with His license, left that sect.
¶203
Et haec ratio quare claustrales cuiuscumque sectae fuerint vel obligationis aut quocumque iuramento stulto astricti debent libere ex mandato christi exuere ista vincula et induere libere sectam christi.
And this is the reason why cloistered persons, of whatever sect they may be or obligation, or bound by any foolish oath, ought freely, by the mandate of Christ, to cast off these bonds and freely to put on the sect of Christ.
¶204
Sufficit laicis quod quandoque dant servis dei decimas suorum proventuum. Et cum istis paribus semper dant ecclesiae licet non semper clero caesareo a papa vel suis subditis assignato.
It suffices for laypersons that they sometimes give tithes of their proceeds to the servants of God. And with these equals they always give to the church, although not always to the Caesarean clergy assigned by the pope or his subjects.
¶205
Potestas quae fingitur a papa et aliis quatuor novis sectis sunt fictae et ad seducendum subditos diabolice introductae ut praelatorum caesareorum excommunicatio citatio incarceratio et redditus pecuniarum venditio.
The power which is feigned by the pope and the other four new sects is feigned and diabolically introduced for seducing subjects, such as the excommunication, citation, imprisonment of Caesarean prelates, and the selling of rents of money.
¶206
Multi sacerdotes simplices superant praelatos in huiusmodi potestate. Immo videtur fidelibus quod magnitudo potestatis spiritualis plus consequitur filium imitatorium christi in moribus quam praelatum qui per cardinales et tales apostatas est electus.
Many simple priests surpass prelates in such power. Nay rather, it seems to the faithful that the magnitude of spiritual power follows a son imitating Christ in morals more than a prelate who has been elected by cardinals and such apostates.
¶207
Subtrahat populus decimas oblationes et alias privatas eleemosynas ab indignis antichristi discipulis cum hoc facere debeat de lege dei. Nec est timenda sed gaudenter acceptanda maledictio vel censura quam inferunt discipuli antichristi.
Let the people withdraw tithes, oblations, and other private alms from the unworthy disciples of Antichrist, since they ought to do this by the law of God. Nor is the malediction or censure which the disciples of Antichrist bring to be feared but joyfully to be accepted.
¶208
Dominus papa episcopi omnes religiosi vel puri clerici titulo perpetuae possessionis dotati debent renunciare illis in manibus brachii saecularis. Quod si pertinaciter noluerint per saeculares dominos debent cogi.
The lord pope, bishops, all religious or pure clerics endowed with the title of perpetual possession, ought to renounce them in the hands of the secular arm. And if they shall have been unwilling pertinaciously, they ought to be compelled by secular lords.
¶209
Non est maior haereticus vel antichristus quam ille clericus qui docet quod licitum est sacerdotibus et levitis legis gratiae dotari in possessionibus temporalibus et si sunt aliqui haeretici vel blasphemi sunt illi clerici qui hoc docent.
There is no greater heretic or Antichrist than that cleric who teaches that it is lawful for priests and levites of the law of grace to be endowed with temporal possessions; and if there are any heretics or blasphemers, they are those clerics who teach this.
¶210
Non solum possunt domini temporales auferre bona fortunae ab ecclesia habitualiter delinquente nec solum hoc eis licet sed debent hoc facere sub poena damnationis aeternae.
Not only can temporal lords take away the goods of fortune from a habitually delinquent church, nor is this only lawful for them, but they ought to do this under penalty of eternal damnation.
¶211
Deus non approbat quemquam damnari civiliter vel civiliter iudicari.
God does not approve anyone to be damned civilly or to be judged civilly.
¶212
Si fiat obiectio contra impugnantes dotationem ecclesiae de Benedicto Gregorio et Bernardo qui pauca temporalia in pauperie possidebant dicitur quod illi finaliter poenitebant.
If objection be made against those impugning the endowment of the church concerning Benedict, Gregory, and Bernard, who possessed few temporal goods in poverty, it is said that they finally repented.
¶213
Si iterum obiicias quod fingo sanctos istos de ista declinatione a lege domini finaliter poenitere doce tu quod sint sancti et ego docebo quod finaliter poenitebant.
If you object again that I feign that these saints finally repented of this declination from the law of the Lord, do you teach that they are saints and I will teach that they finally repented.
¶214
Si scripturae sacrae et rationi debemus credere patet quod discipuli christi non habent potestatem coacte exigendi temporalia per censuras sed hoc tentantes sunt filii Heli filii Belial.
If we ought to believe sacred scripture and reason, it is clear that the disciples of Christ do not have the power to exact temporalities coercively through censures, but those attempting this are sons of Eli, sons of Belial.
¶215
Quaelibet essentia habet unum suppositum secundum quod producitur aliud suppositum par priori. Et ista est actio immanens perfectissima possibilis naturae.
Every essence has one supposit according to which another supposit is produced as equal to the prior. And this is the most perfect immanent action possible to nature.
¶216
Quaelibet essentia sive corporea sive incorporea est communis tribus suppositis et omnibus illis insunt communiter proprietates accidentia et operationes.
Every essence, whether corporeal or incorporeal, is common to three supposits, and to all of them there belong commonly properties, accidents, and operations.
¶217
Deus nihil potest annihilare nec mundum maiorare vel minorare sed animas usque ad certum numerum creare et non ultra.
God cannot annihilate anything, nor increase or diminish the world, but He can create souls up to a certain number and not beyond.
¶218
Impossibile est duas substantias corporeas coextendi unam continue quiescentem localiter et aliam corpus christi quiescens continue penetrantem.
It is impossible for two corporeal substances to be coextended, one continuously resting locally and the other continuously penetrating the resting body of Christ.
¶219
Linea aliqua mathematica continua componitur ex duobus tribus vel quatuor punctis immediatis aut solum ex punctis simpliciter finitis vel tempus est fuit vel erit compositum ex instantibus immediatis.
Any continuous mathematical line is composed of two, three, or four immediate points, or only of simply finite points, or time is, was, or will be composed of immediate instants.
¶220

Likewise, it is not possible that time and line, if they are such, be composed.
¶221
Prima pars est error in philosophia sed ultima errat circa divinam potentiam.
The first part is error in philosophy, but the last errs concerning divine power.
¶222
Imaginandum est unam substantiam corpoream in principio suo ductam esse ex indivisibilibus compositam et occupare omnem locum possibilem.
It must be imagined that one corporeal substance, drawn from its beginning, has been composed of indivisibles and occupies every possible place.

Everyone is God.

Every creature is God.

Ubique omne ens est cum omne ens sit deus.
Everywhere every being is, since every being is God.

Omnia quae eveniunt absolute necessario eveniunt.
All things which happen happen by absolute necessity.

Infans praescitus et baptizatus necessario vivet diutius et peccabit in spiritum sanctum ratione cuius merebitur ut perpetuo condemnetur. Et ita nullus ignis ipsum potest comburere pro hoc tempore vel instanti.
A foreknown and baptized infant will necessarily live longer and will sin against the Holy Spirit by reason of which he will merit to be perpetually condemned. And so no fire can burn him at this time or instant.

That I may assert as faith that all things which will happen will happen by necessity. Thus a foreknown Paul cannot truly repent—that is, by contrition delete the sin of final impenitence, nor ought he not to have it.
¶229
SENTENCE OF CONDEMNATION OF JAN HUS
Sacrosanctum generale concilium Constantiense divinitus congregatum et ecclesiam catholicam repraesentans ad perpetuam rei memoriam.
The sacrosanct general council of Constance, divinely gathered and representing the catholic church, for perpetual remembrance of the matter.
¶230
Quia teste veritate arbor mala fructus malos facere consuevit hinc est quod vir damnatae memoriae Ioannes Wicleff sua mortifera doctrina non in christo Iesu per evangelium ut olim sancti patres fideles filios genuere sed contra christi salutarem fidem velut radix virulenta plures genuit filios pestiferos quos sui perversi dogmatis reliquit successores. Adversus quos haec sancta synodus Constantiensis tamquam contra spurios et illegitimos filios cogitur insurgere et eorum errores ab agro dominico tamquam vepres nocivas cura pervigili et cultro auctoritatis ecclesiasticae resecare ne ut cancer serpant in perniciem aliorum.
Because, as truth testifies, a bad tree is accustomed to make bad fruit, hence it is that the man of damned memory, John Wycliffe, by his death-bearing doctrine—not in Christ Jesus through the gospel, as long ago the holy fathers begot faithful sons, but against the saving faith of Christ, as if a virulent root—begot many pestiferous sons, whom he left as successors of his perverse dogma. Against whom this holy synod of Constance is compelled to rise up as against spurious and illegitimate sons, and to cut off their errors from the Lord's field as harmful briers with watchful care and the knife of ecclesiastical authority, lest they creep as cancer to the ruin of others.
¶231
Cum itaque in sacro generali concilio nuper Romae celebrato decretum fuerit doctrinam damnatae memoriae Ioannis Wicleff damnandam esse et libros eius huiusmodi doctrinam continentes fore tamquam haereticos comburendos et doctrinam ipsam damnatam et libros eius tamquam doctrinam insanam et pestiferam includentes combustos fuisse cum effectu et huiusmodi decretum huius sacri auctoritate concilii fuerit approbatum nihilominus tamen quidam Ioannes Huss in hoc sacro concilio hic personaliter constitutus non christi sed potius Ioannis Wicleff haeresiarchae discipulus post et contra damnationem et decretum huiusmodi ausu temerario contraveniens errores eius plures et haereses tam ab ecclesia dei quam etiam a ceteris olim reverendis in christo patribus dominis archiepiscopis et episcopis diversorum regnorum et magistris in theologia plurium studiorum condemnatos dogmatizavit asseruit et praedicavit praesertim condemnationi scholasticae ipsorum articulorum Ioannis Wicleff factae pluries in universitate Pragensi cum suis complicibus in scholis et in praedicationibus publice resistendo ac ipsum Ioannem Wicleff virum catholicum et doctorem evangelicum in favorem eius doctrinae coram multitudine cleri et populi declaravit: certos etiam articulos infrascriptos et plures alios merito damnabiles tamquam catholicos asseruit et publicavit qui in libris ipsius Ioannis Huss et opusculis notorie continentur.
Since therefore in the sacred general council recently celebrated at Rome it was decreed that the doctrine of John Wycliffe of damned memory is to be condemned and that his books containing such doctrine be burned as heretical, and that the doctrine itself was condemned and his books, as containing insane and pestiferous doctrine, were burned with effect, and such a decree was approved by the authority of this sacred council; nevertheless, a certain John Hus, personally constituted in this sacred council—not a disciple of Christ but rather of the haeresiarch John Wycliffe—after and against such condemnation and decree, contravening with temerarious audacity, dogmatized, asserted, and preached many of his errors and heresies, both those condemned by the church of God and also formerly by other reverend fathers in Christ, the lords archbishops and bishops of various kingdoms and masters in theology of many studies, especially by scholastic condemnation of the same articles of John Wycliffe made many times at the University of Prague, publicly resisting with his accomplices in the schools and in preachings, and declared the same John Wycliffe, in favor of his doctrine, before a multitude of clergy and people, to be a catholic man and evangelical doctor; and he asserted and published certain articles written below and many others justly damnable as catholic, which are notoriously contained in the books and small works of the same John Hus.
¶232
Quapropter habita prius plenaria informatione de praemissis et diligenti deliberatione reverendissimorum in christo patrum dominorum sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalium patriarcharum archiepiscoporum episcoporum et ceterorum praelatorum et doctorum sacrae paginae et iuris utriusque in multitudine copiosa haec sacrosancta synodus Constantiensis declarat et diffinit articulos infrascriptos qui habita collatione per plures magistros in sacra pagina reperti fuerunt contineri in eius libris et opusculis manu propria scriptis et quos etiam idem Ioannes Huss in publica audientia coram patribus et praelatis huius sacri concilii confessus fuit in suis libris et opusculis contineri non esse catholicos nec tamquam tales esse dogmatizandos sed ex eis plures esse erroneos alios scandalosos aliquos piarum aurium offensivos plures que eorum esse temerarios et seditiosos et nonnullos eorumdem esse notorie haereticos et iam dudum a sanctis patribus et generalibus conciliis reprobatos et damnatos ipsos que praedicare dogmatizare aut quomodolibet approbare districte inhibendo.
Wherefore, plenary information concerning the premises having been first had and diligent deliberation of the most reverend fathers in Christ, the lords cardinals of the holy Roman church, patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, and other prelates and doctors of sacred page and of both laws in a copious multitude, this sacrosanct synod of Constance declares and defines that the articles written below—which, upon comparison by many masters in sacred page, were found to be contained in his books and small works written by his own hand, and which the same John Hus also confessed in public audience before the fathers and prelates of this sacred council to be contained in his books and small works—are not catholic, nor are to be dogmatized as such, but that many of them are erroneous, others scandalous, some offensive to pious ears, many of them temerarious and seditious, and several of them notoriously heretical and long ago reproved and condemned by the holy fathers and general councils; strictly inhibiting that they be preached, dogmatized, or approved in any way.
¶233
Sed cum infrascripti articuli expresse contineantur in libris seu tractatibus suis videlicet in libro quem intitulavit de ecclesia et aliis suis opusculis ideo libros praedictos et doctrinam suam et singulos alios tractatus et opuscula in latino sive vulgari Bohemico per ipsum editos aut in quocumque alio idiomate per alium vel alios translatos haec sacrosancta synodus reprobat atque damnat ipsos comburendos publice et solemniter in praesentia cleri et populi in civitate Constantiensi et alibi decernit et diffinit adiiciens propter praemissa omnem eius doctrinam merito esse et fore suspectam de fide et ab omnibus christi fidelibus evitandam.
But since the under-written articles are expressly contained in his books or treatises, namely in the book which he entitled On the Church and in his other small works, therefore the aforesaid books and his doctrine and each of his other treatises and small works, edited by him in Latin or in the Bohemian vernacular, or translated into any other idiom by another or others, this sacrosanct synod reproves and condemns, decreeing and defining that they be publicly and solemnly burned in the presence of the clergy and people in the city of Constance and elsewhere; adding that on account of the premises all his doctrine is deservedly and will be suspect of faith and to be avoided by all Christ's faithful.
¶234
Et ut de medio ecclesiae illa perniciosa doctrina eliminetur haec sancta synodus prorsus iubet per locorum ordinarios tractatus et opuscula huiusmodi per censuram ecclesiasticam etiam si sit opus cum adiectione poenae et fautoriae haereseos diligenter inquiri et repertos ignibus publice concremari.
And so that that pernicious doctrine may be eliminated from the midst of the church, this holy synod utterly orders that such treatises and small works be diligently searched for by the ordinaries of the places through ecclesiastical censure, even if it be necessary with the addition of the penalty of favoring heresy, and that those found be publicly burned with fire.
¶235
Si quis autem huius sententiae ac decreti violator aut contemptor exstiterit statuit eadem sancta synodus per locorum ordinarios et inquisitores haereticae pravitatis contra talem vel tales veluti suspectum vel suspectos de haeresi procedendum.
But if anyone shall exist as a violator or contemner of this sentence and decree, the same holy synod decrees that it be proceeded against such person or persons by the ordinaries of the places and inquisitors of heretical depravity as suspected of heresy.
¶236
Visis insuper actis et actitatis in causa inquisitionis de et super haeresi contra praedictum Ioannem Huss habitis et factis et habita prius fideli et plenaria commissariorum in huiusmodi causa deputatorum relatione aliorum que magistrorum in theologia et iuris utriusque doctorum in de et super actis actitatis et dictis testium fide dignorum et plurimorum quae dicta ipsi Ioannis Huss palam et publice lecta fuerunt coram patribus et praelatis huius sacri concilii per quorum testium dicta apertissime constat eumdem Ioannem multa mala scandalosa et seditiosa et periculosas haereses dogmatizasse et publice per multa annorum curricula praedicasse christi nomine invocato haec sacrosancta synodus Constantiensis solum deum prae oculis habens per hanc definitivam sententiam quam profert in his scriptis pronunciat decernit et declarat dictum Ioannem Huss fuisse et esse verum et manifestum haereticum ipsum que errores et haereses ab ecclesia dei dudum damnatos et damnatas ac plurima scandalosa piarum aurium offensiva temeraria et seditiosa dogmatizasse et publice praedicasse in non modicam offensam divinae maiestatis ac universalis ecclesiae scandalum et fidei catholicae detrimentum claves etiam ecclesiae et censuras ecclesiasticas vilipendisse in quibus per annos multos perstitit animo indurato christi fideles per suam pertinaciam nimium scandalizans cum appellationem ad dominum nostrum Iesum christum tamquam ad supremum iudicem omissis ecclesiasticis mediis interposuerit in qua multa falsa et iniuriosa posuit et scandalosa ipsius sedis apostolicae censurarum ecclesiasticarum et clavium in contemptum.
Moreover, the acts and proceedings in the cause of inquisition of and concerning heresy against the aforesaid John Hus having been seen, and a faithful and plenary report of the commissaries deputed in such cause first had, and also of other masters in theology and doctors of both laws concerning the acts, proceedings, and statements of trustworthy and very many witnesses—which statements were read publicly and plainly before the fathers and prelates of this sacred council, by whose statements it most openly appears that the same John Hus dogmatized and publicly preached for many years many evil, scandalous, and seditious things and perilous heresies, the name of Christ having been invoked—this sacrosanct synod of Constance, having only God before its eyes, by this definitive sentence which it pronounces in these writings, pronounces, decrees, and declares that the said John Hus has been and is a true and manifest heretic, and that he has dogmatized and publicly preached errors and heresies long ago condemned and damned by the church of God, and very many scandalous things offensive to pious ears, temerarious and seditious, to no small offense of the divine majesty, scandal of the universal church, and detriment of the catholic faith, and that he has held in contempt the keys of the church and ecclesiastical censures, in which he persisted with hardened spirit for many years, scandalizing Christ's faithful greatly by his pertinacity, since he interposed an appeal to our Lord Jesus Christ as to the supreme judge, the ecclesiastical means having been omitted, in which he placed many false and injurious and scandalous things in contempt of the apostolic see, ecclesiastical censures, and the keys.
¶237
Idcirco propter praemissa et alia plura praefatum Ioannem Huss haereticum fuisse haec sancta synodus pronunciat et tamquam haereticum iudicandum et condemnandum fore iudicat et condemnat per praesentes: dictam que appellationem tamquam iniuriosam et scandalosam et illusoriam iurisdictioni ecclesiasticae reprobando ipsum que Ioannem Huss populum christianum maxime in regno Bohemiae in praedicationibus suis publicis et scripturis per eum compilatis seduxisse ac eiusdem populi christiani non veracem praedicatorem evangelii christi secundum expositionem sanctorum doctorum sed fuisse verius seductorem.
Therefore, on account of the premises and many other things, this holy synod pronounces that the aforesaid John Hus has been a heretic, and judges and condemns him as a heretic to be adjudged and condemned by the presents; and it reprobates the said appeal as injurious, scandalous, and illusory of ecclesiastical jurisdiction; and that John Hus seduced the Christian people, especially in the kingdom of Bohemia, in his public preachings and writings compiled by him, and was not a true preacher of the gospel of Christ according to the exposition of the holy doctors, but was rather truly a seducer.
¶238
Verum quia per ea quae haec sacrosancta synodus vidit et audivit cognovit eumdem Ioannem Huss pertinacem et incorrigibilem et adeo talem quod non cupiebat ad gremium sanctae matris ecclesiae redire neque haereses et errores per eum publice defensatos et praedicatos velle abiurare idcirco haec sancta synodus Constantiensis eumdem Ioannem Huss ab ordine sacerdotali et aliis ordinibus quibus exsistit insignitus deponendum et degradandum fore declarat et decernit: committens nihilominus reverendis in christo patribus archiepiscopo Mediolanensi Feltrensi Astensi Alexandrino Bangorensi et Vaurensi episcopis ut in praesentia huius sacrosanctae synodi dictam degradationem secundum quod ordo iuris requirit debite exsequantur.
But because by those things which this sacrosanct synod saw and heard, it recognized the same John Hus to be pertinacious and incorrigible and such that he did not wish to return to the bosom of holy mother church nor to wish to abjure the heresies and errors publicly defended and preached by him, therefore this holy synod of Constance declares and decrees that the same John Hus be deposed and degraded from the priestly order and from the other orders with which he is marked; nonetheless committing to the reverend fathers in Christ, the archbishop of Milan, the bishops of Feltre, Asti, Alexandria, Bangor, and Vauré, that they duly execute the said degradation in the presence of this sacrosanct synod according as the order of law requires.
¶239
Haec sancta synodus constantiensis Ioannem Huss attento quod ecclesia dei non habeat ultra quod agere valeat iudicio saeculari relinquit et ipsum curiae saeculari relinquendum fore decernit.
This holy synod of Constance, having considered that the church of God has nothing more that it can do, leaves John Hus to the secular judgment and decrees that he is to be left to the secular court.
¶240
ADDITIONAL ARTICLES OF JOHN HUS (CONTINUED FROM THE THIRTY)
Unica est sancta universalis ecclesia quae est praedestinatorum universitas.
There is one holy universal church, which is the totality of the predestined.
¶241
Et infra sequitur universalis sancta ecclesia tantum est una sicut tantum unus est numerus omnium praedestinatorum.
And it follows: the universal holy church is only one, just as there is only one number of all the predestined.
¶242
Paulus numquam fuit membrum diaboli licet fecerit quosdam actus actibus ecclesiae malignantium consimiles.
Paul was never a member of the devil, although he performed certain acts similar to the acts of the malignant church.
¶243
Praesciti non sunt partes ecclesiae cum nulla pars eius ab ea finaliter excidat eo quod praedestinationis charitas quae ipsam ligat non excidit.
The foreknown are not parts of the church, since no part of it finally falls away from it, because the charity of predestination which binds it does not fall away.
¶244
Duae naturae divinitas et humanitas sunt unus christus.
The two natures, divinity and humanity, are one Christ.
¶245
Praescitus etsi aliquis sit in gratia secundum praesentem iustitiam tamen numquam est pars sanctae ecclesiae: et praedestinatus semper manet membrum ecclesiae licet aliquando excidat a gratia adventitia sed non a gratia praedestinationis.
A foreknown person, even if he be in grace according to present justice, is nevertheless never a part of the holy church; and a predestined person always remains a member of the church, although he sometimes falls away from adventitious grace but not from the grace of predestination.
¶246
Sumendo ecclesiam pro convocatione praedestinatorum sive sit in gratia sive non secundum praesentem iustitiam isto modo ecclesia est articulus fidei.
Taking the church as the convocation of the predestined, whether they be in grace or not according to present justice, in that way the church is an article of faith.
¶247
Petrus non fuit nec est caput sanctae ecclesiae catholicae.
Peter was not nor is the head of the holy catholic church.
¶248
Sacerdotes quomodolibet criminose viventes sacerdotii polluunt potestatem et sicut filii infideles sentiunt infideliter de septem sacramentis ecclesiae de clavibus officiis censuris moribus caeremoniis et sacris rebus ecclesiae veneratione reliquiarum indulgentiis et ordinibus.
Priests living wickedly in any way pollute the power of the priesthood, and as unfaithful sons they think unfaithfully concerning the seven sacraments of the church, the keys, offices, censures, morals, ceremonies, and sacred things of the church, the veneration of relics, indulgences, and orders.
¶249
Papalis dignitas a Caesare inolevit et papae praefectio et institutio a Caesaris potentia emanavit.
The papal dignity grew up from Caesar, and the prefecture and institution of popes emanated from the power of Caesar.
¶250
Nullus sine revelatione assereret rationabiliter de se vel de alio quod esset caput ecclesiae particularis sanctae: nec Romanus pontifex est caput Romanae ecclesiae.
No one without revelation would reasonably assert of himself or of another that he would be the head of any particular holy church; nor is the Roman pontiff the head of the Roman church.
¶251
Non oportet credere quod iste quicumque particularis Romanus pontifex sit caput cuiuscumque particularis ecclesiae sanctae nisi deus eum praedestinaverit.
It is not necessary to believe that this particular Roman pontiff, whoever he is, is the head of any particular holy church unless God has predestined him.
¶252
Nemo gerit vicem christi vel Petri nisi sequatur eum in moribus cum nulla alia sequela sit pertinentior nec aliter recipiat a deo procuratoriam potestatem: quia ad illud officium vicarii requiritur et morum conformitas et instituentis auctoritas.
No one bears the place of Christ or of Peter unless he follows Him in morals, since no other following is more fitting, nor otherwise does he receive procuratorial power from God; because for that office of vicar both conformity of morals and the authority of the institutor are required.
¶253
Papa non est manifestus et verus successor principis apostolorum Petri si vivit moribus contrariis Petro: et si quaerit avaritiam tunc est vicarius Iudae Iscarioth.
The pope is not the manifest and true successor of the prince of apostles Peter if he lives with morals contrary to Peter; and if he seeks avarice, then he is the vicar of Judas Iscariot.
¶254
Et pari evidentia cardinales non sunt manifesti et veri successores collegii aliorum apostolorum christi nisi vixerint more apostolorum servantes mandata et consilia domini nostri Iesu christi.
And by equal evidence, the cardinals are not the manifest and true successors of the college of the other apostles of Christ unless they have lived in the manner of the apostles, keeping the mandates and counsels of our Lord Jesus Christ.
¶255
Doctores ponentes quod aliquis per censuram ecclesiasticam emendandus si corrigi noluerit saeculari iudicio est tradendus pro certo sequuntur in hoc pontifices scribas et phariseos qui christum nolentem eis oboedire in omnibus dicentes nobis non licet interficere quemquam ipsum saeculari iudicio tradiderunt eo quod tales sunt homicidae graviores quam Pilatus.
The doctors who posit that anyone who is to be corrected by ecclesiastical censure, if he has been unwilling to be corrected, is to be handed over to secular judgment, certainly in this follow the pontiffs, scribes, and Pharisees who, saying "It is not lawful for us to kill anyone," handed over Christ, unwilling to obey them in all things, to secular judgment, because such persons are more grievous homicides than Pilate.
¶256
Oboedientia ecclesiastica est oboedientia secundum adinventionem sacerdotum ecclesiae praeter expressam auctoritatem scripturae.
Ecclesiastical obedience is obedience according to the invention of the priests of the church beyond the express authority of scripture.
¶257
Divisio immediata humanorum operum est quod sint vel virtuosa vel vitiosa: quia si homo est vitiosus et agit quicquam tunc agit vitiose et si est virtuosus et agit quicquam tunc agit virtuose.
The immediate division of human works is that they are either virtuous or vicious; because if a man is vicious and does anything, then he does it viciously, and if he is virtuous and does anything, then he does it virtuously.
¶258
Quia sicut vitium quod crimen dicitur sive mortale peccatum inficit universaliter actus hominis vitiosi sic virtus vivificat omnes actus hominis virtuosi.
Because just as vice, which is called crime or mortal sin, universally infects the acts of a vicious man, so virtue vivifies all the acts of a virtuous man.
¶259
Sacerdos christi vivens secundum legem eius et habens scripturae notitiam et affectum ad aedificandum populum debet praedicare non obstante praetensa excommunicatione.
A priest of Christ living according to His law and having knowledge of scripture and an affection for building up the people ought to preach, notwithstanding a pretended excommunication.
¶260
Et infra: quod si papa vel aliquis praepositus mandet sacerdoti sic disposito non praedicare non debet subditus oboedire.
And below: that if the pope or any prelate mandate to a priest so disposed not to preach, the subject ought not to obey.
¶261
Quilibet praedicantis officium de mandato accipit qui ad sacerdotium accedit: et illud mandatum debet exsequi praetensa excommunicatione non obstante.
Every person who approaches the priesthood receives the office of preaching by mandate, and he ought to execute that mandate, a pretended excommunication notwithstanding.
¶262
Per censuras ecclesiasticas excommunicationis suspensionis et interdicti ad suam exaltationem clerus populum laicalem sibi suppeditat avaritiam multiplicat malitiam protegit et viam praeparat antichristo.
Through ecclesiastical censures of excommunication, suspension, and interdict, the clergy, for their own exaltation, supplies the lay people to themselves, multiplies avarice, protects malice, and prepares the way for Antichrist.
¶263
Signum autem evidens est quod ab antichristo tales procedant censurae quas vocant in suis processibus fulminationes quibus clerus principalissime procedit contra illos qui denudant nequitiam antichristi quam clerus pro se maxime usurpavit.
But the evident sign is that such censures proceed from Antichrist, which they call in their processes fulminations, by which the clergy principally proceeds against those who lay bare the wickedness of Antichrist which the clergy has especially usurped for themselves.
¶264
Si papa est malus et praesertim si est praescitus tunc ut Iudas apostolus est diabolus fur et filius perditionis et non est caput sanctae militantis ecclesiae cum nec sit membrum eius.
If the pope is evil and especially if he is foreknown, then like Judas the apostle he is a devil, a thief, and a son of perdition, and he is not the head of the holy militant church, since he is not even a member of it.
¶265
Gratia praedestinationis est vinculum quo corpus ecclesiae et quodlibet eius membrum iungitur ipso capiti insolubiliter.
The grace of predestination is the bond by which the body of the church and each of its members is joined inseparably to the head itself.
¶266
Papa vel praelatus malus et praescitus est aequivoce pastor et vere fur et latro.
An evil and foreknown pope or prelate is equivocally a pastor, and truly a thief and a robber.
¶267
Papa non debet dici sanctissimus etiam secundum officium quia alias rex etiam deberet dici sanctissimus secundum officium et tortores et praecones dicerentur sancti: immo etiam diabolus deberet vocari sanctus cum sit officiarius dei.
The pope ought not to be called most holy even according to office, because otherwise a king also ought to be called most holy according to office, and torturers and heralds would be called holy; indeed, even the devil ought to be called holy, since he is an officer of God.
¶268
Si papa vivit contrarie christo etiamsi ascenderet per ritam et legitimam electionem secundum constitutionem humanam vulgatam tamen aliunde ascenderet quam per christum dato etiam quod intraret per electionem a deo principaliter factam. Nam Iudas Iscarioth rite et legitime est electus a deo Iesu christo ad apostolatum et tamen ascendit aliunde in ovile ovium.
If the pope lives contrary to Christ, even if he should have ascended through the rite and legitimate election according to vulgar human constitution, nevertheless he would ascend from elsewhere than through Christ, even if he should enter through election principally made by God. For Judas Iscariot was duly and legitimately elected by God Jesus Christ to the apostleship, and nevertheless he ascended from elsewhere into the sheepfold of the sheep.
¶269
Condemnatio quadragintaquinque articulorum Ioannis Wicleff per doctores facta est irrationabilis et iniqua et male facta et ficta est causa per eos allegata videlicet quod nullus ex eis sit catholicus sed quilibet eorum aut est haereticus aut erroneus aut scandalosus.
The condemnation of the forty-five articles of John Wycliffe made by the doctors was irrational and iniquitous and badly made, and the cause alleged by them—namely, that none of them is catholic but each of them is either heretical or erroneous or scandalous—is feigned.
¶270
Non eo ipso quo electores vel maior pars eorum consenserunt viva voce secundum ritus hominum in personam aliquam eo ipso illa persona est legitime electa vel quod eo ipso est verus et manifestus successor vel vicarius Petri apostoli vel alterius apostoli in officio ecclesiastico.
Not by this very fact that the electors or the greater part of them have consented by living voice according to the rites of men to any person, by this very fact is that person legitimately elected, or by this very fact is he the true and manifest successor or vicar of Peter the apostle or of another apostle in ecclesiastical office.
¶271
Unde sive electores bene vel male elegerunt operibus electi debemus credere.
Hence, whether the electors have elected well or badly, we ought to believe in the works of the elected.
¶272
Nam eo ipso quo quis copiosius operatur meritorie ad profectum ecclesiae habet a deo ad hoc copiosius potestatem.
For by this very fact that anyone works more copiously meritoriously for the profit of the church, he has from God more copious power for this.
¶273
Non est scintilla apparentiae quod oporteat esse unum caput in spiritualibus regens ecclesiam quod semper cum ipsa militante ecclesia conversetur.
There is not a scintilla of appearance that there ought to be one head in spirituals ruling the church who always converses with the militant church itself.
¶274
Christus sine talibus monstruosis capitibus per suos veraces discipulos sparsos per orbem terrarum melius suam ecclesiam regularet.
Christ would better rule His church without such monstrous heads through His truthful disciples scattered throughout the orb of the earth.
¶275
Apostoli et fideles sacerdotes domini strenue in necessariis ad salutem regularunt ecclesiam antequam papae officium foret introductum sic facerent deficiente per summe possibile papa usque ad diem iudicii.
The apostles and faithful priests of the Lord strenuously ruled the church in the things necessary for salvation before the office of pope was introduced; they would do so if the pope were lacking, as is supremely possible, until the day of judgment.
¶276
Nullus est dominus civilis nullus est praelatus nullus est episcopus dum est in peccato mortali.
No one is a civil lord, no one is a prelate, no one is a bishop while he is in mortal sin.
¶277
DECREE AGAINST THE TYRANNICIDE ERROR (SESSION XV, JUNE 6, 1415)
Praecipua sollicitudine volens haec sacrosancta synodus ad exstirpationem errorum et haeresium in diversis mundi partibus invalescentium procedere sicut tenetur et ad hoc collecta est nuper accepit quod nonnullae assertiones erroneae in fide et bonis moribus ac multipliciter scandalosae totius que reipublicae statum et ordinem subvertere molientes dogmatizatae sunt inter quas haec assertio delata est: quilibet tyrannus potest et debet licite et meritorie occidi per quemcumque vassalum suum vel subditum etiam per insidias et blanditias vel adulationes non obstante quocumque praestito iuramento seu confoederatione facta cum eo non expectata sententia vel mandato iudicis cuiuscumque.
Wishing to proceed with principal solicitude to the extirpation of errors and heresies growing strong in various parts of the world as it is bound, and having been gathered for this, the sacrosanct synod recently learned that certain erroneous assertions in faith and good morals, and manifoldly scandalous, and striving to subvert the state and order of the whole republic, have been dogmatized, among which this assertion has been reported: that any tyrant can and ought licitly and meritoriously to be killed by any of his vassals or subjects, even by ambushes and blandishments or adulations, notwithstanding any oath made or confederation entered into with him, no sentence or mandate of any judge having been awaited.
¶278
Adversus hunc errorem satagens haec sancta synodus insurgere et ipsum funditus tollere praehabita deliberatione matura declarat decernit et definit huiusmodi doctrinam erroneam esse in fide et in moribus ipsam que tamquam haereticam scandalosam seditiosam et ad fraudes deceptiones mendacia proditiones periuria vias dantem reprobat et condemnat.
Striving to rise up against this error and to remove it root and branch, this holy synod, mature deliberation having been held beforehand, declares, decrees, and defines that such a doctrine is erroneous in faith and in morals, and as heretical, scandalous, seditious, and giving a way to frauds, deceptions, falsehoods, betrayals, perjuries, it reproves and condemns it.
¶279
Declarat insuper decernit et definit quod pertinaciter doctrinam hanc perniciosissimam asserentes sunt haeretici et tamquam tales iuxta canonicas et legitimas sanctiones puniendi.
It declares, moreover, decrees, and defines that those pertinaciously asserting this most pernicious doctrine are heretics, and as such are to be punished according to the canonical and legitimate sanctions.
¶280
SENTENCE OF CONDEMNATION OF JEROME OF PRAGUE (SESSION XVI, JULY 6, 1415)
In nomine domini Amen.
In the name of the Lord. Amen.
¶281
Christus deus et salvator noster vitis vera cuius pater agricola est discipulos ceteros que fideles suos in illis instituens inquit: si quis in me non manserit mittetur foras sicut palmes et arescet. Cuius summi doctoris et magistri doctrinam sequens et praecepta exsequens haec sancta synodus Constantiensis in causa inquisitionis haereticae pravitatis per eamdem sanctam synodum mota fama publica referente et clamorosa insinuatione ministrante contra magistrum Hieronymum dictum de Praga magistrum in artibus laicum ex cuius causae actis et processibus constat eumdem Hieronymum tenuisse asseruisse et dogmatizasse nonnullos articulos haereticos et erroneos dudum a sanctis patribus reprobatos quosdam vero blasphemos alios scandalosos alios piarum aurium offensivos temerarios et seditiosos dudum per damnatae memoriae viros Ioannem Wicleff et Ioannem Huss assertos praedicatos et dogmatizatos ac in nonnullis eorum libris et opusculis insertos.
Christ God and our Savior, the true vine whose father is the husbandman, instituting His disciples and other His faithful in those things, says: "If anyone shall not abide in me, he shall be cast forth as a branch and shall wither." Following the doctrine of this highest teacher and master and executing His precepts, this holy synod of Constance, in the cause of inquisition of heretical depravity moved by the same holy synod, public fame reporting and clamorous insinuation furnishing, against Master Jerome called of Prague, master in arts, a layman—from whose cause's acts and processes it appears that the same Jerome has held, asserted, and dogmatized several heretical and erroneous articles long ago reproved by the holy fathers, some indeed blasphemous, others scandalous, others offensive to pious ears, temerarious and seditious, long ago asserted, preached, and dogmatized by the men of damned memory, John Wycliffe and John Hus, and inserted in some of their books and small works.
¶282
Qui que articuli doctrina et libri praefatorum Ioannis Wicleff et Ioannis Huss ac Wicleff memoria denique Hussi persona per eamdem sanctam synodum et eius sententiam de haeresi condemnati et damnatae fuerunt.
Which articles, doctrine, and books of the aforesaid John Wycliffe and John Hus, and the memory of Wycliffe and finally the person of Hus, were condemned and damned as heretical by the same holy synod and its sentence.
¶283
Quam que damnationis sententiam idem Hieronymus postmodum pendente huius inquisitionis causa in eadem sancta synodo cognoscens et profitens veram catholicam et apostolicam fidem approbavit et eidem consensit: anathematizavit que omnem haeresim praecipue eam de qua erat infamatus et se confitebatur infamatum et quam praeteritis temporibus dogmatizaverunt et tenuerunt Ioannes Wicleff et Ioannes Huss in suis opusculis sermonibus et libellis: et propter quam seu quas dicti Wicleff et Huss cum suis dogmatibus et erroribus ab eadem sancta synodo damnati fuerant tamquam haeretici et eorum doctrina similiter damnata praemissorum que omnem damnationem professus est et iuravit se in hac fidei veritate permansurum et si ipse aliquando quicquam contra sentire vel praedicare praesumpserit canonum severitati subiacere et aeternae poenae voluit obligari.
Which sentence of condemnation, the same Jerome afterwards, this cause of inquisition still pending, knowing in the same holy synod and professing the true catholic and apostolic faith, approved and consented to it; and he anathematized all heresy, especially that of which he was defamed and which he confessed himself defamed, and which in past times John Wycliffe and John Hus dogmatized and held in their small works, sermons, and booklets, and on account of which or which the said Wycliffe and Hus with their dogmas and errors were condemned by the same holy synod as heretics and their doctrine likewise condemned; and he professed and swore that he would remain in this truth of faith, and that if he should ever presume to think or preach anything against it, he wished to be subject to the severity of the canons and bound to eternal penalty.
¶284
Illam que suam professionem manu sua conscriptam eidem sanctae synodo obtulit atque dedit.
He offered and gave that his profession, written by his own hand, to the same holy synod.
¶285
Post quas professionem et abiurationem multis transactis diebus sicut canis ad vomitum rediens ut virus pestilentissimum quod in suo latebat pectore publice evomeret audientiam publicam sibi dari in eadem sancta synodo postulavit. Qua sibi concessa in eadem synodo publice congregata asseruit dixit et professus est in effectu quod praefatae sententiae damnationis dictorum Wicleff et Ioannis Huss inique consenserat et illam sententiam approbando mentitus fuerat. Nec verebatur se confiteri mentitum: quin immo confessionem approbationem et professionem suas super illorum damnatione revocabat nunc et in aeternum: asserens se in libris dictorum Wicleff et Ioannis Huss nullam umquam haeresim vel errorem legisse licet ante sententiam fuerit professus et probatum sit evidenter ipsum libros eorum diligenter studuisse legisse et dogmatizasse et in quibus constat plures errores et haereses contineri.
After which profession and abjuration, many days having passed, as a dog returning to his vomit so that he might publicly vomit forth the most pestilent poison which lay hidden in his breast, he sought that public audience be given to him in the same holy synod. Which having been granted to him, in the same synod publicly gathered, he asserted, said, and professed in effect that he had iniquitously consented to the aforesaid sentence of condemnation of the said Wycliffe and John Hus, and that in approving that sentence he had lied. Nor did he fear to confess that he had lied; nay rather, he revoked his confession, approval, and profession concerning their condemnation now and forever, asserting that he had never read any heresy or error in the books of the said Wycliffe and John Hus, although before the sentence he had professed and it was evidently proven that he had diligently studied, read, and dogmatized their books, and in which it is established that many errors and heresies are contained.
¶286
Verum idem Hieronymus de sacramento altaris et transubstantiatione panis in corpus christi professus est se tenere et credere quod ecclesia credit et tenet dicens se plus credere Augustino et ceteris ecclesiae doctoribus quam Wicleff et Huss.
Moreover, the same Jerome professed concerning the sacrament of the altar and the transubstantiation of bread into the body of Christ that he holds and believes what the church believes and holds, saying that he believes Augustine and the other doctors of the church more than Wycliffe and Hus.
¶287
Constat insuper ex praemissis eumdem Hieronymum praedictis Wicleff et Huss damnatis et eorum erroribus adhaerere illorum que fautorem fuisse et esse: propter quae eadem sancta synodus eumdem Hieronymum palmitem putridum aridum in vite non manentem foras mittendum decrevit et decernit: ipsum que haereticum et in haeresim relapsum excommunicatum anathematizatum pronunciat declarat et damnat.
Moreover, it appears from the premises that the same Jerome adheres to the aforesaid Wycliffe and Hus, condemned, and to their errors, and has been and is their favorer. On account of which, the same holy synod decrees and has decreed that the same Jerome, a rotten and dry branch not remaining in the vine, is to be cast forth; and it pronounces, declares, and condemns him as a heretic and relapsed into heresy, excommunicated and anathematized.
¶288
SENTENCE OF CONDEMNATION OF BENEDICT XIII (PETER OF LUNA) – SESSION XXXVII (JULY 26, 1417)
De vultu eius hoc iudicium prodeat qui sedet in throno et ex eius ore procedit gladius bis acutus cuius statera iusta est et aequa sunt pondera qui venturus est iudicare vivos et mortuos domini nostri Iesu christi amen.
Let this judgment go forth from his face who sits upon the throne, and from his mouth proceeds a two-edged sword, whose balance is just and whose weights are equal, who is coming to judge the living and the dead, of our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
¶289
Iustus est dominus et iustitias dilexit aequitatem vidit vultus eius. Vultus quidem domini super facientes mala ut perdat de terra memoriam eorum.
The Lord is just and He has loved justices; His face has seen equity. Indeed, the face of the Lord is upon those doing evil, that He may destroy from the earth the memory of them.
¶290
Pereat inquit sanctus propheta memoria illius qui non est recordatus facere misericordiam et qui persecutus est hominem inopem et mendicum.
Let the memory of him perish, says the holy prophet, who was not mindful to show mercy, and who persecuted the poor and needy man.
¶291
Quanto magis pereat illius qui omnes homines et ecclesiam universalem persecutus est et turbavit Petri de Luna Benedicti XIII a nonnullis nuncupati memoria.
How much more let the memory of him perish who has persecuted and troubled all men and the universal church—the memory of Peter of Luna, called by some Benedict XIII.
¶292
Qui quantum in ecclesiam dei et universum populum christianum peccaverit schisma et divisionem ecclesiae dei fovens nutriens atque continuans: quantis quam que frequentibus devotis et humilibus regum principum et praelatorum precibus exhortationibus et requisitionibus charitative iuxta doctrinam evangelicam admonitus fuerit ut pacem daret ecclesiae et illius sanaret vulnera et eius partes divisas in unam compaginem et corpus unum reficeret quemadmodum ipse iuraverat erat que et diu fuit in sua potestate: quos tamen charitative corripientes nullatenus voluit exaudire quot sint postmodum testes adhibiti quibus etiam minime exauditis necesse fuit secundum praedictam christi evangelicam doctrinam dicere ecclesiae quam quia etiam non audivit habendus sit tamquam ethnicus et publicanus: capitula in causa inquisitionis fidei et schismatis coram praesenti sancta synodo generali super praemissis et aliis contra eum edita ac illorum veritas et notorietas declaravit manifeste.
How greatly he has sinned against the church of God and the whole Christian people, fostering, nourishing, and continuing schism and division in the church of God; how many, how frequent, how devoted and humble prayers, exhortations, and requests of kings, princes, and prelates—by which he was charitably admonished according to evangelical doctrine—that he should give peace to the church and heal its wounds and restore its divided parts into one whole and one body, just as he himself had sworn, and it was and long had been in his power; yet he was altogether unwilling to hear those charitably correcting him; how many witnesses were thereafter added, whom also not having heard, it was necessary according to the said evangelical doctrine of Christ to tell the church, which also because he did not hear, he is to be held as a heathen and a publican—the chapters in the cause of inquisition of faith and schism before the present holy general synod issued concerning the premises and other things against him, and their truth and notoriety have manifested clearly.
¶293
Super quibus rite et canonice processo ac omnibus rite actis ac diligenter inspectis habita que super ipsis deliberatione matura eadem sancta generalis synodus universalem ecclesiam repraesentans in dicta inquisitionis causa pro tribunali sedens pronunciat decernit et declarat per hanc definitivam sententiam in his scriptis eumdem Petrum de Luna Benedictum XIII ut praemittitur nuncupatum fuisse et esse periurum universalis ecclesiae scandalizatorem fautorem et nutritorem inveterati schismatis inveteratae scissurae et divisionis ecclesiae sanctae dei pacis et unionis eiusdem ecclesiae impeditorem et turbatorem schismaticum et haereticum a fide devium et articuli fidei unam sanctam catholicam ecclesiam violatorem pertinacem cum scandalo ecclesiae dei incorrigibilem notorium et manifestum atque omni titulo gradu honore et dignitate se reddidisse indignum a deo eiectum et praecisum et omni iure eidem in papatu et Romano pontifici ac Romanae ecclesiae quomodolibet competente ipso iure privatum et ab ecclesia catholica tamquam membrum aridum praecisum.
All things having been duly and canonically proceeded upon, and all things duly done and diligently inspected, and mature deliberation having been had concerning them, the same holy general synod, representing the universal church, sitting in judgment in the said cause of inquisition, pronounces, decrees, and declares by this definitive sentence in these writings that the same Peter of Luna, called Benedict XIII as premised, has been and is perjured, a scandalizer of the universal church, a favorer and nourisher of the inveterate schism, of the inveterate splitting and division of the holy church of God, an impeder and disturber of the peace and union of the same church, a schismatic and heretic, deviating from the faith, a pertinacious violator of the article of faith "one holy catholic church," with scandal to the church of God, notoriously and manifestly incorrigible, and that he has rendered himself unworthy of every title, grade, honor, and dignity, cast out and cut off by God, and deprived ipso jure of every right that pertained to him in any way to the papacy and Roman pontificate and Roman church, and cut off from the catholic church as a dry member.
¶294
Ipsum que Petrum quatenus de facto papatum secundum se tenet eadem sancta synodus papatu et summo ecclesiae Romanae pontificio omni que titulo gradu honore dignitate beneficiis et officiis quibuscumque ad omnem cautelam privat et deponit et abiicit. Eidem que inhibet ne deinceps pro papa aut Romano et summo pontifice se gerat omnes que christicolas ab eius oboedientia et omni debito oboedientiae ipsius atque iuramentis et obligationibus eidem quomodolibet praestitis absolvit et absolutos fore declarat ac omnibus et singulis christi fidelibus inhibet sub poena fautoriae schismatis et haeresis atque privationis omnium beneficiorum dignitatum et honorum ecclesiasticorum et mundanorum et aliis poenis iuris etiam si episcopalis et patriarchalis cardinalatus regalis sit dignitas ac imperialis quibus si contra hanc inhibitionem fecerint sint auctoritate huius decreti et sententiae ipso facto privati et alias iuris incurrant poenas ne eidem Petro de Luna schismatico et haeretico incorrigibili notorio declarato et deposito tamquam papae oboediant pareant vel intendant aut eum quovis modo contra praemissa sustineant vel receptent sibi que praestent auxilium consilium vel favorem.
The same holy synod deprives, deposes, and casts off the same Peter, insofar as he holds the papacy in fact, from the papacy and the supreme pontificate of the Roman church and from every title, grade, honor, dignity, benefices, and offices whatsoever, to all caution, and inhibits him that he henceforth conduct himself as pope or Roman and supreme pontiff, and it absolves all Christians from his obedience and from all duty of obedience to him and from oaths and obligations made to him in any way, and declares them to be absolved, and it inhibits all and singular Christ's faithful under penalty of favoring schism and heresy and of deprivation of all benefices, dignities, and honors, ecclesiastical and worldly, and other penalties of law—even if the dignity be episcopal, patriarchal, cardinalatial, royal, or imperial, from which if they act against this inhibition they are to be deprived ipso facto by the authority of this decree and sentence and incur the other penalties of law—that they do not obey, obey, or attend to the same Peter of Luna, schismatic and heretic, declared incorrigible and deposed, as pope, nor support nor receive him in any way against the premises, nor give him aid, counsel, or favor.
¶295
Declarat insuper et decernit omnes et singulas inhibitiones omnes que processus sententias constitutiones et censuras et alia quaecumque per eum factos factas et facta quae possent praemissis obviare irritos irritas irrita atque irritat revocat et annullat: ceteris poenis quas in praemissis casibus iura statuunt semper salvis.
It declares, moreover, and decrees that all and singular inhibitions, all processes, sentences, constitutions, and censures, and any other things whatsoever made, done, and issued by him which could oppose the premises, are void, and it makes them void, revokes, and annuls; other penalties which the laws establish in the aforesaid cases always being saved.
¶296
DECREE FREQUENS CONCERNING THE FREQUENT CELEBRATION OF GENERAL COUNCILS (SESSION XXXIX, OCTOBER 9, 1417)
Frequens generalium conciliorum celebratio agri dominici praecipua cultura est quae vepres spinas et tribulos haeresium errorum et schismatum exstirpat excessus corrigit deformata reformat et vineam domini ad frugem uberrimae fertilitatis adducit illorum vero neglectus praemissa disseminat atque fovet.
The frequent celebration of general councils is the principal cultivation of the Lord's field, which extirpates thorns, thistles, and briers of heresies, errors, and schisms, corrects excesses, reforms the deformed, and brings the Lord's vineyard to the fruit of most abundant fertility; but the neglect of these things disseminates and fosters the premises.
¶297
Haec praeteritorum temporum recordatio et consideratio praesentium ante oculos nostros ponunt.
The recollection of past times and the consideration of the present place these before our eyes.
¶298
Propter hoc edicto perpetuo sancimus statuimus decernimus atque ordinamus ut amodo concilia generalia celebrentur: ita quod primum a fine huius concilii in quinquennium immediate sequens secundum vero a fine illius immediate sequentis concilii in septennium et deinceps de decennio in decennium perpetuo celebrentur in locis quae summus pontifex per mensem ante finem cuiuslibet concilii approbante et consentiente concilio vel in eius defectu ipsum concilium deputare et assignare teneatur.
Therefore, by perpetual edict we sanction, establish, decree, and ordain that from now on general councils be celebrated: thus, that the first from the end of this council in the five years immediately following, the second from the end of that immediately following council in seven years, and thereafter from ten years to ten years perpetually, in places which the supreme pontiff, with the council approving and consenting, shall be held to appoint and assign within a month before the end of each council; or in his default, the council itself [shall appoint and assign them].
¶299
Ut sic per quamdam continuationem semper aut concilium vigeat aut per termini pendentiam exspectetur: quem terminum liceat summo pontifici de fratrum suorum sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalium consilio ob emergentes forte casus abbreviare sed nullatenus prorogetur.
Thus, by a certain continuity, either a council always thrives or it is awaited for the pendency of a term. Which term it shall be lawful for the supreme pontiff, with the counsel of his brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman church, to shorten on account of emerging cases, but let it by no means be prorogued.
¶300
Locum autem pro futuro concilio celebrando deputatum absque evidenti necessitate non mutet. Sed si forte casus aliquis occurreret quo necessarium videretur ipsum locum mutari puta obsidionis guerrae pestis aut similis tunc liceat summo pontifici de praedictorum fratrum suorum aut duarum partium ipsorum consensu atque subscriptione alium locum prius deputato loco viciniorem et aptum sub eadem tamen natione subrogare nisi idem vel simile impedimemtum per totam illam nationem vigeret. Et tunc ad aliquem alium viciniorem alterius nationis locum aptum huiusmodi concilium poterit convocare: ad quem praelati et alii qui ad concilium solent convocari accedere teneantur ac si a principio locus ille fuisset deputatus.
And let him not change the place deputed for celebrating the future council without evident necessity. But if perchance some case should occur in which it would seem necessary that the place itself be changed—for example, siege, war, pestilence, or the like—then it shall be lawful for the supreme pontiff, with the consent and subscription of his aforesaid brothers or two parts of them, to subrogate another place, nearer and suitable to the place first deputed, yet within the same nation, unless the same or a similar impediment prevails throughout that whole nation. And then he shall be able to convoke the council to some other nearer suitable place of another nation, to which prelates and others who are accustomed to be convoked to a council shall be held to go just as if that place had been deputed from the beginning.
¶301
Quam tamen loci mutationem vel termini abbreviationem teneatur summus pontifex legitime et solenniter per annum ante praefixum terminum publicare et intimare ut ad ipsum concilium celebrandum possint praedicti statuto termino convenire.
However, the supreme pontiff shall be held legitimately and solemnly to publish and intimate such change of place or abbreviation of term a year before the fixed term, so that the aforesaid [persons] may be able to convene at the fixed term to celebrate the council.
¶302
Si vero quod absit in futurum schisma oriri contingeret ita quod duo vel plures pro summis pontificibus se gererent a die quo ipsi duo vel plures insignia pontificatus publice assumpserint seu administrare coeperint intelligatur ipso iure terminus concilii tunc forte ultra annum pendens ad annum proximum breviatus.
But if—which may be absent—it should happen that a schism arise in the future, so that two or more conduct themselves as supreme pontiffs, from the day on which those two or more shall have publicly assumed the insignia of the pontificate or shall have begun to administer, the term of the council then perhaps pending beyond a year shall be understood ipso jure to be shortened to the next year.
¶303
Ad quod omnes praelati et ceteri qui ad concilium ire tenentur sub poenis iuris et aliis per concilium imponendis absque alia convocatione conveniant necnon imperator ceteri que reges et principes vel personaliter aut per solemnes nuncios tamquam ad commune incendium exstinguendum per viscera misericordiae domini nostri Iesu christi ex nunc exorati concurrant.
To which all prelates and others who are held to go to the council under the penalties of law and others to be imposed by the council, without any other convocation, shall convene; and also the emperor and other kings and princes shall concur either personally or through solemn nuncios, as to a common conflagration to be extinguished, being now entreated by the bowels of the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ.
¶304
Et quilibet ipsorum pro Romano pontifice gerentium infra mensem a die qua scientiam habere potuit alium vel alios assumpsisse papatus insignia vel in papatu administrasse teneatur sub intimatione maledictionis aeternae et amissione iuris si quod forte sibi quaesitum esset in papatu quam ipso facto incurrat et ultra hoc ad quaslibet dignitates active et passive sit inhabilis concilium ipsum ad terminum anni praedictum in loco prius deputato indicere et publicare et per suas litteras competitori vel competitoribus ipsum vel ipsos provocando ad causam et ceteris praelatis ac principibus quantum in eo fuerit intimare nec non termino praefixo sub poenis praedictis ad concilii locum personaliter se transferre nec inde discedere donec per concilium causa schismatis plenarie sit finita hoc adiuncto quod nullus ipsorum contendentium de papatu in ipso concilio ut papa praesideat.
And each of those conducting themselves as Roman pontiff shall be held, within a month from the day on which he could have had knowledge that another or others have assumed the insignia of the papacy or have administered in the papacy, under threat of eternal malediction and loss of the right which perhaps he had acquired to the papacy—which he incurs ipso facto, and beyond this he is incapable actively and passively for any dignities whatsoever—to indicate, publish, and intimate the council itself at the aforesaid term of one year in the place previously deputed, and to cite his competitor or competitors to the cause by his letters, and to intimate to other prelates and princes as far as in him lies, and at the fixed term under the aforesaid penalties to transfer himself personally to the place of the council, nor to depart from there until the cause of schism be fully ended by the council; with this added, that none of those contending about the papacy preside in that council as pope.
¶305
Quin immo ut tanto liberius et citius ecclesia unico et indubitato pastore gaudeat sint ipsi omnes de papatu contendentes postquam dictum concilium inceptum fuerit auctoritate huius sacrae synodi ipso iure ab omni administratione suspensi nec eis aut eorum alteri donec causa ipsa per concilium terminata fuerit a quoquam sub poena fautoriae schismatis quomodolibet oboediatur.
Nay rather, so that the church may more freely and more quickly rejoice in one and undoubted shepherd, all those contending about the papacy, after the said council shall have been begun, are ipso jure suspended from all administration by the authority of this sacred synod, nor is anyone to obey them or any of them in any way under penalty of favoring schism, until the cause itself be terminated by the council.
¶306
CONCERNING AN ELECTION MADE THROUGH FEAR OR PRESSURE
Quod si forte electionem Romani pontificis per metum qui caderet in constantem seu impressionem de cetero fieri contingat ipsam nullius decernimus efficaciae vel momenti nec posse per sequentem consensum etiam metu praedicto cessante ratificari vel approbari.
But if perchance it should happen that the election of the Roman pontiff be made in the future through fear which would fall upon a constant man or through pressure, we decree that it be of no efficacy or moment, nor can it be ratified or approved by subsequent consent, even if the aforesaid fear cease.
¶307
Non tamen liceat cardinalibus ad aliam electionem procedere nisi ille qui fuit electus forte renunciet vel decedat donec per generale concilium de electione illa fuerit iudicatum.
Nevertheless, it shall not be lawful for the cardinals to proceed to another election unless the one who was elected perchance renounce or die, until it be judged by a general council concerning that election.
¶308
Et si procedant nulla sit electio ipso iure sint que sic secundo eligentes et electus si se papatui ingesserit omni dignitate honore et statu etiam cardinalatus et pontificali ipso iure privati et inhabiles de cetero ad easdem ac etiam ad papatum nec aliquis eidem secundo electo ut papae sub poena fautoriae schismatis oboediat quoquo modo.
And if they proceed, let the election be null ipso jure, and let those thus electing a second time and the elected, if he thrusts himself into the papacy, be deprived ipso jure of all dignity, honor, and state, even cardinalatial and pontifical, and be henceforth incapable for the same and also for the papacy, nor is anyone to obey the same second elected one as pope in any way under penalty of favoring schism.
¶309
Et eo casu concilium de electione papae provideat illa vice
And in that case, the council shall provide concerning the election of the pope for that turn.
¶310
Sed liceat immo et teneantur electores omnes aut saltem maior pars eorum quam cito sine periculo personarum poterunt etiam si periculum omnium bonorum immineat se transferre ad locum tutum et metum praedictum allegare coram notariis publicis et notabilibus personis ac multitudine populi in loco insigni: ita tamen quod allegantes metum huiusmodi habeant in illius metus allegationem exprimere speciem et qualitatem dicti metus et iurare solemniter quod metus taliter allegatus sit verus et quod credant se ipsum posse probare et quod per malitiam vel per calumniam huiusmodi metum non proponant.
But it shall be lawful, indeed the electors shall be held—all of them or at least the greater part of them—as soon as they can without peril of persons, even if peril of all goods impend, to transfer themselves to a safe place and to allege the aforesaid fear before public notaries and notable persons and a multitude of the people in a notable place; yet in such a way that those alleging such fear shall be held to express in the allegation of that fear the species and quality of the said fear and to swear solemnly that the fear thus alleged is true and that they believe they can prove it, and that they do not propose such fear through malice or calumny.
¶311
Nec ultra proximum futurum concilium ullo modo differri possit allegatio dicti metus.
Nor can the allegation of the said fear be deferred beyond the next future council in any way.
¶312
Teneantur insuper postquam se transtulerunt et metum allegaverunt modo praedicto provocare sic electum ad concilium.
Moreover, after they have transferred themselves and alleged fear in the aforesaid manner, they shall be held to provoke the one thus elected to the council.
¶313
Quod concilium si ultra annum pendeat a die provocationis huiusmodi intelligatur ad annum ut supra ipso iure terminus abbreviatus.
Which council, if it be pending beyond a year from the day of such provocation, the term shall be understood ipso jure to be shortened to the year as above.
¶314
Et nihilominus teneatur electus ipse sub poenis praedictis et cardinales sub poena amissionis cardinalatus et omnium beneficiorum suorum quam ipso facto incurrant infra mensem a die provocationis concilium ipsum indicere et publicare ut supra dicitur et quam citius poterunt intimare: ac cardinales ipsi ceteri que electores ad locum concilii tempore convenienti personaliter se transferre et usque ad finem causae exspectare teneantur.
And nevertheless the elected himself, under the aforesaid penalties, and the cardinals, under penalty of loss of their cardinalate and of all their benefices—which they incur ipso facto—shall be held within a month from the day of the provocation to indicate and publish the council itself as said above, and to intimate it as soon as they can; and the cardinals themselves and the other electors shall be held to transfer themselves personally to the place of the council at the convenient time and to wait until the end of the cause.
¶315
Teneantur que praelati ceteri ut supra ad convocationem cardinalium tantum si forte electus convocare cessaret accedere: qui sic electus in concilio ipso non praesideat quin immo sit a termino initiandi concilii ipso iure ab omni administratione papatus suspensus nec sibi a quoquam sub poena fautoriae schismatis quomodolibet oboediatur.
Moreover, other prelates and others as above shall be held to go to the convocation of the cardinals alone, if perchance the elected should cease to convoke. The one thus elected shall not preside in the council itself; nay rather, from the term of beginning the council he is ipso jure suspended from all administration of the papacy, nor is he to be obeyed by anyone in any way under penalty of favoring schism.
¶316
Quod si infra annum ante diem indicti concilii contingant supradicti casus videlicet quod plures se gerant pro papa vel quod unus per metum seu per impressionem eligatur censeantur ipso iure tam gerentes se pro papa quam electus per metum seu impressionem et cardinales ad dictum concilium provocati: teneantur que in ipso concilio comparere personaliter causam exponere et iudicium concilii exspectare.
If within a year before the day of the indicated council the aforesaid cases should occur—namely, that several conduct themselves as pope or that one be elected through fear or through pressure—let those conducting themselves as pope and the one elected through fear or pressure be held ipso jure to have been provoked to the said council; and let them be held to appear personally in that council, to set forth the cause, and to await the judgment of the council.
¶317
Sed si dictis casibus occurrentibus continget forte casus aliquis quo necessarium sit locum concilii mutare ut obsidionis vel guerrae aut pestis aut similis teneantur nihilominus tam omnes supradicti quam omnes praelati ceteri que qui ad concilium ire tenentur ad locum proximiorem ut praemittitur qui sit habilis ad concilium convenire possit que maior pars praelatorum qui infra mensem ad locum certum declinaverint illum sibi et aliis pro loco concilii deputare ad quem ceteri convenire teneantur ac si a principio fuisset deputatus.
But if, the said cases occurring, it should perchance happen that some case occur in which it be necessary to change the place of the council, such as siege, war, pestilence, or the like, nevertheless all the aforesaid and all other prelates who are held to go to the council shall be held to go to the nearest place as premised which is suitable for the council to convene; and the greater part of the prelates who within a month shall have turned aside to a certain place shall be able to depute that place for themselves and others for the place of the council, to which the others shall be held to convene as if it had been deputed from the beginning.
¶318
Concilium autem ut praefertur convocatum et congregatum de huiusmodi schismatis causa cognoscens et in contumacia electorum seu gerentium se pro papa vel cardinalium si forte venire neglexerint litem dirimat causam que definiat et culpabiles in schismate procurando seu nutriendo vel in administrando aut oboediendo vel administrantibus favendo seu contra interdictum superius eligendo vel calumniosos in allegando metum etiam ultra praedictas poenas cuiuscumque status gradus seu praeeminentiae exsistant ecclesiasticae vel mundanae personae sic puniat ut vindictae rigor transeat ceteris in exemplum.
A council thus convoked and gathered, taking cognizance of the cause of such schism, if the elected or those conducting themselves as pope or the cardinals perchance neglect to come, shall decide the litigation, define the cause, and punish the guilty in procuring or nourishing schism or in administering or obeying or favoring those administering or against the above interdict electing or calumniously alleging fear, even beyond the aforesaid penalties—persons of whatever state, grade, or pre-eminence, ecclesiastical or worldly—so that the rigor of vengeance may pass over to others as an example.
¶319
CONCERNING FEAR OR PRESSURE IN THE ELECTION OF A POPE
Ut autem metus seu impressionis molestia in electione papae eo formidolosius evitetur quo toti christianitati lamentabilius eorum incussio seu factio perpetratur: ultra praedicta specialiter duximus statuendum quod si quis huiusmodi metum vel impressionem aut violentiam electoribus ipsis vel alicui eorum in electione papae intulerit seu fecerit aut fieri procuraverit vel factum ratum habuerit aut in hoc consilium dederit vel favorem facientem ve scienter receptaverit vel defensaverit aut negligens in exsecutione poenarum inferius memoratarum exstiterit cuiuscumque status gradus aut praeeminentiae fuerit etiamsi imperiali regali pontificali aut alia quavis ecclesiastica aut saeculari praefulgeat dignitate illas poenas ipso facto incurrat quae in constitutione felicis recordationis Bonifacii papae VIII quae incipit felicis continentur illis que effectualiter puniatur.
So that the molestation of fear or pressure in the election of a pope may be avoided the more fearfully the more lamentably their incursion or faction is perpetrated against the whole of Christendom, beyond the aforesaid we have deemed specially to be decreed that if anyone shall have inflicted such fear or pressure or violence upon the electors themselves or any of them in the election of a pope, or shall have done or caused it to be done, or shall have ratified the deed, or shall have given counsel or favor in this, or shall have knowingly received or defended the one doing it, or shall have been negligent in the execution of the penalties mentioned below—of whatever state, grade, or pre-eminence he may be, even if he shine with imperial, royal, pontifical, or any other ecclesiastical or secular dignity—he incurs ipso facto those penalties which are contained in the constitution of Pope Boniface VIII of happy memory which begins "Felicis," and let him be effectually punished by them.
¶320
Civitas vero etiam si quod absit urbs Romana fuerit seu alia quaevis universitas quae talia facienti auxilium vel consilium dederit aut favorem vel infra mensem saltem taliter delinquentem prout tanti facinoris enormitas exegerit et facultas ei affuerit non duxerit puniendum eo ipso subiaceat ecclesiastico interdicto.
But a city, even if—which may be absent—it be the Roman city, or any other community whatsoever, which shall have given aid or counsel or favor to one doing such things, or shall not have deemed to punish the one thus delinquent within at least a month according as the enormity of so great a crime demands and faculty is available to it, shall be subject ipso facto to ecclesiastical interdict.
¶321
Et nihilominus praeter dictam urbem pontificali ut supra fit mentio eo ipso sit dignitate privata non obstantibus privilegiis quibuscumque.
And nonetheless, besides the said city, as mention is made above of the pontifical [city], it is deprived ipso facto of its dignity, notwithstanding any privileges whatsoever.
¶322

We wish, moreover, that at the end of each general council this decree be solemnly published, and also whenever and wherever the election of a Roman pontiff is impending to be made, before the entry of the conclave it be read and publicly announced.

DECREE CONCERNING THE PROFESSION OF FAITH TO BE MADE BY THE POPE (SESSION XXXIX, OCTOBER 9, 1417)
Quanto Romanus pontifex eminentiori inter mortales fungitur potestate tanto clarioribus ipsum decet fidei vinculis et sacramentorum ecclesiasticorum observandis ritibus alligari.
Inasmuch as the Roman pontiff holds power more eminent among mortals, so it is fitting that he be bound by clearer bonds of faith and by the observance of the rites of ecclesiastical sacraments.

Wherefore, so that full faith may shine with singular splendor in the future Roman pontiff at the beginning of his creation, we decree and ordain that henceforth anyone to be elected as Roman pontiff, before his election be published, shall publicly make before his electors the confession and profession written below.

DECREE CONCERNING THE TRANSLATION OF PRELATES (SESSION XL, OCTOBER 30, 1417)
Cum ex praelatorum translationibus ecclesiae de quibus transferuntur plerumque gravibus in spiritualibus et temporalibus subiaceant dispendiis et iacturis praelati quoque nonnumquam iura et libertates ecclesiarum translationis formidine non adeo solerter ut alias prosequantur ac ad importunitatem quorumdam quae sua et non quae Iesu christi sunt quaerentium Romanus pontifex forsan ut homo facti nescius in huiusmodi circumveniatur ut alias leviter inclinetur: praesentibus statuimus et ordinamus invitorum episcoporum et superiorum translationes absque magna et rationabili causa quae vocata parte cognita fuerit et decisa de consilio sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalium vel maioris partis et cum subscriptione eorumdem de cetero fieri non debere.
Since from the translations of prelates the churches from which they are transferred often lie under grave expenses and losses in spirituals and temporals, and also the prelates sometimes do not prosecute the rights and liberties of the churches with as much skill as otherwise on account of the fear of translation, and the Roman pontiff, perhaps as a man ignorant of the deed, may be circumvented by the importunity of certain persons seeking their own things and not the things of Jesus Christ, so that he is lightly inclined otherwise, we decree and ordain by the presents that from now on translations of unwilling bishops and superiors ought not to be made without a great and reasonable cause which, the party having been called, shall have been known and decided, with the counsel of the cardinals of the holy Roman church or the greater part and with their subscription.

But inferiors, such as abbots and other perpetual beneficiaries, ought not to be changed, removed, or deprived without a just and reasonable cause having been known.
¶332

Adding that in the changes of abbots the subscription of the cardinals shall intervene just as is premised concerning bishops, saving the constitutions and privileges of churches, monasteries, and any orders.

DECREE CONCERNING PROCURATIONS AND SPOILS (SESSION XLI, NOVEMBER 9, 1417)

Since the reservation made by the pope, and the exaction and perception of procurations due to ordinaries and other inferior prelates by reason of visitation, and also of the spoils of deceased prelates and other clerics, bring grave losses to churches, monasteries, and other ecclesiastical benefices and persons, we declare by the present edict that it is consonant with reason and accommodated to the republic that such reservations by the pope and such exactions or perceptions by collectors and others deputed or to be deputed by apostolic authority be by no means done or attempted hereafter; nay rather, such procurations and the spoils or goods of any prelates whatsoever—even of cardinals or of the familiars or officials of the pope himself or of any other clerics, dying in the Roman curia or elsewhere, wherever and whenever—found at the time of death, plainly and freely pertain to those and be received by those to whom they would pertain and ought to pertain, the aforesaid reservations, mandates, and exactions ceasing.

We also forbid that exactions of spoils be made from inferior prelates and others such against and beyond the form of common law; the constitution of Pope Boniface VIII of happy memory, which begins "Praesenti," specially issued concerning this, nonetheless remaining in its force.

DECREE CONCERNING THE CARDINALS OF THE OBEDIENCE OF BENEDICT XIII (SESSION XLIII, MARCH 21, 1418)

The sacrosanct general synod of Constance, attending to those things which were long ago agreed upon at Narbonne for the union of the church concerning the admission into the same synod of the cardinals of the obedience of Peter of Luna, formerly called Benedict XIII in his obedience, and that after the notorious ejection of the said Peter of Luna the aforesaid cardinals, long ago called according to the same agreements before such ejection, having been awaited for three months and more from the day of the aforesaid ejection, did not come, decrees and ordains that it be proceeded to the election of the Roman pontiff according to what is to be decreed by the same synod, by the authority of the same synod, their absence notwithstanding. Which [cardinals], if they shall have come before the election of the future supreme pontiff be consummated and shall have adhered to the same council, it declares that they are to be admitted to the aforesaid election together with others according to the disposition of law and what is to be decreed by the council.
¶356
FORM OF ELECTION OF MARTIN V (SESSION XLIV, APRIL 19, 1418)
Ad laudem gloriam et honorem omnipotentis dei ad pacem et unitatem universalis ecclesiae ac totius populi christiani.
To the praise, glory, and honor of omnipotent God, to the peace and unity of the universal church and of the whole Christian people.
¶357
Ut electio futuri romani et summi pontificis proxime celebranda firmiori auctoritate et plurium roboretur assensu et ne attento statu ecclesiae super dicta electione in posterum ulla retractatio ullus scrupulus in mentibus hominum resideat sed ex illa sequatur unio certa vera plenissima et perfecta fidelium sacrosancta generalis synodus Constantiensis communi utilitate pensata de speciali et expresso consensu et voluntate concordi sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalium in eadem synodo exsistentium et collegii eorumdem ac omnium nationum praesentis concilii statuit ordinat et decernit quod hac vice dumtaxat ad eligendum Romanum et summum pontificem una cum cardinalibus sex praelati vel aliae honorabiles personae ecclesiasticae in sacris ordinibus constitutae de qualibet natione in eadem synodo pro nunc exsistente et nominata quos seu quas quaelibet ipsarum nationum pro se ad hoc infra decem dies duxerit eligendos seu eligendas ipsis cardinalibus adiungantur.
So that the election of the future Roman and supreme pontiff, about to be celebrated soon, may be strengthened by firmer authority and the assent of more persons, and lest, the state of the church having been attended to concerning the said election, any retraction or any scruple remain in the minds of men in the future, but rather from it there may follow a certain, true, most full, and perfect union of the faithful, the sacrosanct general synod of Constance, common utility having been weighed, with the special and express consent and concordant will of the cardinals of the holy Roman church existing in the same synod and of their college, and of all the nations of the present council, decrees, ordains, and decides that for this turn only, for electing the Roman and supreme pontiff, six prelates or other honorable ecclesiastical persons constituted in sacred orders from each nation now existing and named in the same synod—whom each of those nations for itself shall have deemed to be elected or elected for this within ten days—be joined to the cardinals.
¶358
Quibus omnibus eadem sancta synodus eligendi Romanum pontificem secundum formam hic expressam quatenus opus est tribuit potestatem: videlicet quod ille absque ulla exceptione ab universali ecclesia Romanus pontifex habeatur qui a duabus partibus cardinalium in conclavi exsistentium et a cuiuslibet nationis duabus partibus eisdem cardinalibus adiungendorum et tunc adiunctorum electus fuerit et receptus.
To all of these the same holy synod grants power to elect the Roman pontiff according to the form here expressed, insofar as it is necessary: namely, that he shall be held as Roman pontiff by the universal church without any exception who shall have been elected and received by two parts of the cardinals existing in the conclave and by two parts of each nation to be joined to the same cardinals and then joined.
¶359

And that the election shall not be valid nor shall the elected be held as supreme pontiff unless two parts of the cardinals existing in the conclave and two parts of each nation to be joined with the cardinals for electing and then joined consent and shall have consented in electing the Roman pontiff.
¶360

Moreover, it decrees, ordains, and decides that the votes of any persons whatever to be emitted in such election be null unless, as premised, two parts of the cardinals and two parts of each nation to be joined and then joined aforesaid concur in one, either principally or through accession.
¶361

This also being added, that the prelates and others joined with the cardinals themselves for such election and then joined are bound to observe all and singular the apostolic constitutions, even penal, issued concerning the election of the Roman pontiff, and the customs accustomed to be observed, just as the cardinals themselves are held to observe them, and they are bound to their observance.
¶362
Teneantur insuper iurare et iurent dicti electores et cardinales et alii antequam ad electionem procedant quod in huiusmodi electionis negotio attendentes quid eis imminebit cum de creatione agitur vicarii Iesu christi successoris beati Petri universalis ecclesiae rectoris gregis dominici directoris puris et sinceris mentibus et quantum credent publicae utilitati universalis ecclesiae proficere omni cuiuscumque nationis personae vel alio inordinato affectu odio gratia vel favore abiectis procedere: ut eorum ministerio de utili et idoneo universalis ecclesiae provideatur pastore.
Moreover, the said electors, cardinals and others, shall be held to swear and they shall swear, before they proceed to the election, that in the business of such election, attending to what will be incumbent upon them since it is a matter of the creation of the vicar of Jesus Christ, the successor of blessed Peter, the rector of the universal church, the director of the Lord's flock, they will proceed with pure and sincere minds and as they believe will profit the public utility of the universal church, all inordinate affection of any nation, person, or other, hatred, favor, or grace having been cast aside, so that by their ministry the universal church may be provided with a useful and suitable pastor.
¶363
Ordinat insuper statuit et decernit eadem sancta synodus quod infra decem dies ex tunc continue numerandos quos decem dies omnibus et singulis sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalibus praesentibus et absentibus ac ceteris electoribus supradictis attenta Romanae ecclesiae notoria vacatione ad intrandum conclave in hac civitate Constantiensi in maiori domo communitatis eiusdem civitatis ad hoc iam ordinata praefigit et assignat praefati electores cardinales et alii supradicti intrent ipsum conclave ad electionem huiusmodi celebrandam cetera que faciendum et prosequendum quemadmodum in ceteris praeter praemissa de cardinalibus et aliis electoribus in electione Romani pontificis iura statuunt et decernunt.
Moreover, the same holy synod ordains, decrees, and decides that within ten days thereafter to be counted continuously—which ten days it fixes and assigns to all and singular the cardinals of the holy Roman church present and absent and to the other electors aforesaid, the notorious vacation of the Roman church having been attended to, for entering the conclave in this city of Constance, in the greater house of the community of the same city already ordained for this—the aforesaid electors, cardinals and others aforesaid, shall enter that conclave to celebrate such election and to do and prosecute the other things just as the laws decree and ordain concerning cardinals and other electors in the election of the Roman pontiff, other than the premises.
¶364
Quae omnia praemissis observatis vult eadem sancta synodus in suo robore permanere.
Which all things, the premises having been observed, the same holy synod wishes to remain in their force.
¶365
Hanc autem formam et hunc modum electionis approbat ordinat statuit et decernit pro hac vice: et ad omnem scrupulum submovendum eadem sancta synodus et singulos in eadem synodo praesentes et venturos qui eidem synodo adhaerebunt ad omnes actus legitimos in eadem synodo agendos active et passive quatenus opus est habilitat et habiles esse declarat ceteris eiusdem sacri concilii decretis semper salvis supplens omnes defectus si qui forsan intervenerint in praemissis apostolicis etiam et in generalibus conciliis editis constitutionibus et aliis in contrarium facientibus non obstantibus quibuscumque.
This form and this mode of election the same holy synod approves, ordains, decrees, and decides for this turn; and to remove every scruple, the same holy synod habilitates, insofar as it is necessary, and declares to be habile, each and every person present in the same synod and about to come who will adhere to the same synod, for all legitimate acts to be done in the same synod actively and passively, the other decrees of the same sacred council always being saved, supplying all defects if any perchance have intervened in the premises, notwithstanding apostolic constitutions and those issued in general councils and any other things to the contrary.

DECREE CONCERNING EXEMPTIONS MADE DURING THE SCHISM (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)

Martin, bishop, servant of the servants of God, attending that from the time of the death of our predecessor Pope Gregory XI of happy memory, several Roman pontiffs—or those conducting themselves as Roman pontiffs and reputed as such in their diverse obediences—according to their own will or on account of the importunity of petitioners, newly exempted from the jurisdictions of ordinaries certain churches, monasteries, chapters, convents, priories, benefices, places, and persons which were by no means exempt or not exempt at the time of the said Gregory, to the grave prejudice of the same ordinaries. We, wishing to meet such prejudice, by the sacred council approving, revoke all exemptions of cathedral churches, monasteries, chapters, convents, provostships, benefices, places, and any persons whatsoever—even if any monastery from the aforesaid has been exempted and afterwards subjected to a monastery of a different habit or color—made anew from the time of the death of the said Gregory XI by any who conducted themselves as Roman pontiffs, even if they have perhaps been approved by us or renewed from certain knowledge, the party not having been called, which before such exemption enjoyed no exemption but were simply subject to ordinary jurisdiction and had no beginning before that time—except also exemptions which pertain to a whole order and which were made or granted to churches, monasteries, chapters, convents, benefices, or places from the aforesaid time, founded under the mode or condition of exemption or by reason of a new foundation, or to universities of general studies or colleges of scholars, or which were made or granted by way of confirmation, increase, or addition, or concerning which it was ordained by competent authority with those whose interest it was present and heard, or in which the ordinaries consented, and all perpetual exemptions made by inferiors from the pope.
¶367
ecclesias monasteria et alia loca praedicta in pristinam ordinariorum iurisdictionem reducimus. Ceteris autem exemptionibus ante obitum dicti Gregorii habitis vel concessis nullum volumus per hoc praeiudicium generari. Insuper non intendimus exemptiones de cetero facere nisi causa cognita et vocatis quorum interest.
And we reduce the said churches, monasteries, and other places to the pristine jurisdiction of ordinaries; concerning other exemptions had or granted before the death of the said Gregory, we wish no prejudice to be generated by this. Moreover, we do not intend to make exemptions hereafter unless the cause has been known and those whose interest it is have been called.

DECREE CONCERNING UNIONS AND INCORPORATIONS (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)

Martin, etc. Unions and incorporations made or granted from the time of the death of Gregory XI, since a certain rule cannot be given, upon the complaints of those whose interest it is—unless they have been made for reasonable and true causes, even if the authority of the apostolic see intervened—we shall revoke with justice mediating, except concerning benefices thus united if the petitioners, if they have obtained possession, have been heard.
¶369
DECREE CONCERNING THE FRUITS OF VACANT BENEFICES (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Item fructus et proventus ecclesiarum monasteriorum beneficiorum vacationis tempore obvenientes iuris et consuetudinis vel privilegii dispositioni relinquimus illos que nobis vel apostolicae camerae prohibemus applicari.
Martin, etc. Likewise, the fruits and proceeds of churches, monasteries, benefices accruing at the time of vacation we leave to the disposition of law, custom, or privilege, and we forbid them to be applied to us or to the apostolic camera.
¶370
DECREE AGAINST SIMONY (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Multae contra simoniacam pravitatem ab olim factae sunt constitutiones quibus morbus ille non potuit exstirpari. Nos volentes de cetero ut possumus attentius providere sacro approbante concilio declaramus quod ordinati simoniace ab exsecutione suorum ordinum sint eo ipso suspensi.
Martin, etc. Many constitutions against simoniacal depravity have long ago been made, by which that disease could not be extirpated. Wishing to provide more attentively hereafter as we can, with the sacred council approving, we declare that those ordained simoniacally are ipso facto suspended from the execution of their orders.
¶371
Electiones autem postulationes confirmationes et quaevis provisiones simoniacae ecclesiarum monasteriorum dignitatum personatuum officiorum et beneficiorum ecclesiasticorum quorumcumque deinceps factae nullae sint ipso iure nullum que per illas ius cuiquam acquiratur nec promoti confirmati aut provisi faciant fructus suos sed ad illorum restitutionem tamquam inique ablatorum percipientes teneantur.
Moreover, simoniacal elections, postulations, confirmations, and any provisions whatsoever of churches, monasteries, dignities, personates, offices, and any ecclesiastical benefices, made henceforth, shall be null ipso jure, nor shall any right be acquired through them to anyone, nor shall those promoted, confirmed, or provided receive their fruits, but those receiving them shall be held to their restitution as things iniquitously taken away.
¶372
Statuentes insuper quod dantes et recipientes eo ipso facto sententiam excommunicationis incurrant etiam si pontificali aut cardinalatus praefulgeant dignitate.
Moreover, we decree that the givers and receivers incur the sentence of excommunication ipso facto, even if they shine with pontifical or cardinalatial dignity.
¶373
DECREE CONCERNING DISPENSATIONS FOR ORDERS (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Quoniam beneficia propter officia conceduntur reputamus absurdum ut qui beneficia obtinent recusent aut negligant officium exercere. Nos igitur sacro approbante concilio omnes dispensationes a quibuscumque pro romanis pontificibus se gerentibus concessas quibuscumque electis confirmatis seu provisis ad ecclesias monasteria prioratus conventuales decanatus archidiaconatus et alia quaecumque beneficia quibus certus ordo debitus est vel annexus aut de illis ne munus consecrationis episcopi seu benedictiones abbatis aut ceteros debitos aut annexos ordines suscipiant praeter illas quae secundum formam constitutionis Bonifacii VIII quae incipit cum ex eo factae sunt revocamus.
Martin, etc. Since benefices are granted on account of offices, we regard it as absurd that those who obtain benefices refuse or neglect to exercise the office. We therefore, with the sacred council approving, revoke all dispensations granted by any who conducted themselves as Roman pontiffs to any elected, confirmed, or provided to churches, monasteries, conventual priories, deaneries, archdeaconries, and any other benefices whatsoever to which a certain order is due or annexed, or concerning them that they not receive the office of episcopal consecration or abbatial benediction or the other due or annexed orders, except those which were made according to the form of the constitution of Boniface VIII which begins "Cum ex eo."
¶374
Statuentes ut qui de praesenti illos vel illa obtinent infra sex menses a die publicationis huiusmodi constitutionis nostrae et qui in posterum obtinebunt infra terminum iuris se faciant consecrari aut benedici sive ad alium debitum ordinem promoveri.
We decree that those who presently obtain those things or them shall cause themselves to be consecrated, blessed, or promoted to the other due order within six months from the day of publication of this our constitution, and those who shall obtain them in the future within the term of law.
¶375
Alioquin sint illi ipsis ecclesiis monasteriis dignitatibus personatibus officiis et beneficiis praedictis ipso iure privati et aliis libere conferantur aut provideatur de illis ceteris constitutionibus circa hoc editis in suo robore duraturis.
Otherwise, let them be deprived ipso jure of the same churches, monasteries, dignities, personates, offices, and benefices aforesaid, and let them be freely conferred upon others or provision be made concerning them, other constitutions issued concerning this remaining in their force.
¶376
DECREE CONCERNING THE IMPOSITION OF TITHES (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Praecipimus et mandamus iura quae prohibent inferioribus a papa decimas et alia onera ecclesiis et personis ecclesiasticis imponi districtius observari.
Martin, etc. We command and enjoin that the laws which prohibit the imposition of tithes and other burdens upon churches and ecclesiastical persons by inferiors from the pope be observed more strictly.
¶377
Per nos autem nullatenus imponentur generaliter super totum clerum nisi ex magna et ardua causa et utilitate universalem ecclesiam concernente et de consilio consensu et subscriptione fratrum nostrorum sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinalium et praelatorum quorum consilium commode haberi poterit.
They shall by no means be imposed by us generally upon the whole clergy except for a great and arduous cause and utility concerning the universal church, and with the counsel, consent, and subscription of our brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman church and of prelates whose counsel can conveniently be had.
¶378
Nec specialiter in aliquo regno vel provincia inconsultis praelatis illius regni vel provinciae et ipsis non consentientibus vel eorum maiori parte et eo casu per personas ecclesiasticas et auctoritate apostolica dumtaxat leventur.
Nor shall they be imposed specially in any kingdom or province, the prelates of that kingdom or province not having been consulted and not consenting, or the greater part of them; and in that case they shall be levied only by ecclesiastical persons and by apostolic authority.
¶379
DECREE CONCERNING THE CLOTHING OF CLERICS (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Inter ceteros clericorum et praelatorum excessus hoc maxime inolevit quod spreta in vestibus forma ecclesiasticae honestatis plurimi delectantur esse deformes et cupiunt laicis conformari et quidquid mente gerunt habitu profitentur.
Martin, etc. Among other excesses of clerics and prelates, this has especially grown up, that, the form of ecclesiastical honesty in garments having been despised, very many delight to be deformed and desire to be conformed to laypersons, and what they carry in their mind they profess by their habit.
¶380
Unde praeter cetera quae circa vestes tonsuram et habitus clericorum tam in formis quam in coloribus atque comam seu capillos vitam que et honestatem clericorum iura statuunt et quae nimium collapsa sunt tam in saecularibus quam in regularibus sacro approbante concilio innovamus et praecipimus diligentius observari.
Hence, beyond the other things which the laws establish concerning the garments, tonsure, and habits of clerics both in forms and colors, and the hair or locks, and the life and honesty of clerics—things which have greatly collapsed both in seculars and in regulars—with the sacred council approving, we renew and command them to be more diligently observed.
¶381
Illum specialiter abusum eodem approbante concilio decernimus penitus abolendum quod in quibusdam partibus nonnulli clerici et personae ecclesiasticae saeculares et regulares etiam quod magis exsecramur praelati ecclesiarum manicas ad cubitum pendentes et longas cum magna et sumptuosa superfluitate vestes etiam scissas retro et in lateribus cum foderaturis ultra oram excedentibus etiam in scissuris deferunt et cum talibus in ecclesiis cum suppelliciis ac aliis vestibus ad cultum et officium ecclesiasticum ordinatis etiam intra ecclesias ipsas in quibus beneficiati exsistunt non verentur divinis officiis interesse.
That special abuse we decree to be altogether abolished, with the same council approving, that in certain parts some clerics and ecclesiastical persons, secular and regular—even, which we more execrate, prelates of churches—bear sleeves hanging to the elbow and long garments with great and sumptuous superfluity, even slit in the back and at the sides, with furs exceeding the hem, even in the slits; and with such garments, with surplices and other vestments ordained for the cultivation and ecclesiastical office, even within those churches in which they are beneficiaries, they do not fear to be present at divine offices.
¶382
Hanc vestium deformitatem in quibuscumque personis ecclesiasticis reprobamus ac usum talium inhibemus contrarium autem facientes ut transgressores canonum puniantur: specialiter statuentes ut quicumque beneficiatus aut officium in ecclesia gerens in habitu huiusmodi divinis officiis praesumpserit interesse pro qualibet vice a perceptione proventuum ecclesiasticorum per mensem noverit se suspensum fructus que illi fabricae illius ecclesiae applicentur.
We reprove this deformity of garments in any ecclesiastical persons whatsoever, and we forbid the use of such; but those doing the contrary, as transgressors of the canons, let them be punished: specially decreeing that whoever, a beneficiary or bearing an office in the church, shall have presumed to be present at divine offices in such a habit, for each such occurrence let him know that he is suspended from the perception of ecclesiastical proceeds for a month, and let the fruits be applied to the fabric of that church.
¶383
DECREE CONFIRMING THE REFORMATORY DECREES (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Decernimus et declaramus sacro approbante concilio per decreta statuta et ordinata tam lecta in praesenti sessione quam concordata cum singulis nationibus eiusdem concilii et quae in nostra cancellaria poni volumus et litteras in forma publica sub sigillo vicecancellarii nostri volentibus habere confici atque tradi huic sacro concilio super articulis contentis in decreto super fienda reformatione die sabbati 30 mensis octobris proxime praeteriti promulgato fuisse et esse iam satisfactum.
Martin, etc. We decree and declare, with the sacred council approving, that by the decrees, statutes, and ordinances read in the present session as well as those agreed upon with the individual nations of the same council—which we wish to be placed in our chancery and to be drawn up and handed over in public form under the seal of our vice-chancellor to those wishing to have them—that this sacred council has already been and is satisfied concerning the articles contained in the decree concerning the reformation to be done, promulgated on Saturday the 30th of the month of October just past.
¶384
DECREE DEPUTING THE NEXT COUNCIL AT PAVIA (SESSION XLV, APRIL 20, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Cupientes et etiam volentes decreto huius generalis concilii satisfacere inter alias disponenti quod omnimode generalia concilia celebrentur in loco quem summus pontifex per mensem ante finem huius concilii approbante et consentiente concilio deputare et assignare teneatur pro loco dicti proxime futuri concilii celebrandi a fine praesentis concilii supradicti: eodem consentiente et approbante concilio civitatem Papiensem tenore praesentium deputamus et etiam assignamus statuentes et decernentes quod praelati et alii qui ad generalia concilia debent convocari tempore praedicto civitatem ipsam Papiensem accedere teneantur.
Martin, etc. Desiring and also wishing to satisfy the decree of this general council which among other things disposes that general councils be altogether celebrated in the place which the supreme pontiff, with the council approving and consenting, shall be held to depute and assign within a month before the end of this council, for the place of the said next future council to be celebrated from the end of the present council as aforesaid—with the same council consenting and approving, we depute and also assign the city of Pavia by the tenor of the presents, decreeing and deciding that prelates and others who ought to be convoked to general councils at the aforesaid time shall be held to go to that city of Pavia.

¶387
ABSOLUTION OF THE COUNCIL AND PERMISSION TO RETURN HOME (SESSION XLVII, APRIL 22, 1418)
Martinus et cetera Hoc etiam requirente praesente sacro concilio ex certis et rationabilibus et iustis causis ipsum concilium absolvimus omnibus que et singulis in eo existentibus ad propria remeandi ipso approbante concilio licentiam impertimur.
Martin, etc. This also the present sacred council requiring, for certain, reasonable, and just causes we absolve the council itself, and with the council approving we impart license to all and singular existing in it to return to their own homes.
¶388
Item auctoritate dei omnipotentis ac beatorum Petri et Pauli apostolorum eius et nostra concedimus omnibus et singulis qui in hoc sacro concilio et causa ipsius interfuerunt absolutionem plenariam in vita omnium peccatorum semel ita quod quilibet de praedictis infra duos menses postquam ad eius notitiam huiusmodi concessio pervenit possit dictum beneficium absolutionis assequi in forma.
Likewise, by the authority of omnipotent God and of His blessed apostles Peter and Paul and our own, we grant to all and singular who have been present in this sacred council and on its behalf a plenary absolution in life of all their sins once, such that each of the aforesaid, within two months after this concession shall have come to his knowledge, may be able to obtain the said benefit of absolution in due form.
¶389
Similem concessionem facimus in mortis articulo.
We make a similar concession at the article of death.
¶390
Et intelligimus tam de dominis quam de familiaribus eorumdem sic tamen quod a die notitiae praesentium pro absolutione in vita ieiunent per annum feria sexta et pro absolutione in mortis articulo per alium annum nisi legitime fuerint impediti quo casu habeant alia opera pietatis peragere.
And we understand both of the lords and of their familiars, yet in such a way that from the day of knowledge of the presents for the absolution in life they fast for a year on the sixth feria, and for the absolution at the article of death for another year, unless they be legitimately hindered, in which case they have to perform other works of piety.
¶391
Post Secundum vero annum debeant etiam usque ad finem vitae feria sexta ieiunare vel aliqua opera pietatis peragere.
But after the second year, they ought also to fast on the sixth feria until the end of life or to perform some works of piety.
