First Council of Lyon

Concilium Lugdunense I

1245

Editorial note

Deposition of Frederick II. Primarily political-ecclesiological. Useful as a datum for the assertion of papal authority over temporal rulers in the high medieval framework, but not a doctrinal council in the strict sense.

FIRST COUNCIL OF LYONS A.D. 1245
Innocentius episcopus servus servorum Dei sacro praesente concilio ad rei memoriam sempiternam.
Innocent, bishop, servant of the servants of God, in the presence of the sacred council, for perpetual remembrance of the matter.
¶1
Ad apostolicae dignitatis apicem licet indigni dignatione divinae maiestatis assumpti, omnium christianorum curam vigili sedula que solertia gerere ac intimae considerationis oculo singulorum discernere merita et providae deliberationis statera librare debemus, ut quos iusti vigor examinis dignos ostenderit, congruis attollamus favoribus, quos autem reos poenis debitis deprimamus, appendentes semper meritum et praemium aequa lance, retribuendo cuique iuxta qualitatem operis, poenae vel gratiae quantitatem.
To the apex of apostolic dignity, although unworthily assumed by the condescension of the divine majesty, we ought to bear the care of all Christians with watchful and sedulous diligence and to discern the merits of individuals with the eye of intimate consideration and to weigh with the balance of providential deliberation, so that those whom the strength of just examination shall have shown worthy, we may lift up with fitting favors, and those whom guilty, we may depress with due penalties, always appending merit and reward with an equal scale, rendering to each according to the quality of his work the measure of penalty or grace.
¶2
Sane cum dira guerrarum commotio nonnullas professionis christianae provincias diutius afflixisset, nos toto cupientes mentis affectu tranquillitatem et pacem ecclesiae sanctae Dei ac generaliter cuncto populo christiano, ad praecipuum principem saecularem, huiusmodi dissensionis et tribulationis actorem, a felicis recordationis Gregorio papa praedecessore nostro pro suis excessibus anathematis vinculo innodatum, speciales nuntios, magnae auctoritatis viros, videlicet venerabiles fratres Petrum Albanensem, tunc Rothomagensem archiepiscopum, et Guillelmum Sabinensem, tunc quondam Mutinensem, episcopos ac dilectum filium nostrum Guillelmum basilicae Duodecim apostolorum presbyterum cardinalem, tunc abbatem sancti Facundi, qui salutem zelabantur ipsius, duximus destinandos, facientes sibi proponi per ipsos, quod nos et fratres nostri quantum in nobis erat pacem per omnia se cum habere necnon cum omnibus hominibus optabamus, parati sibi pacem et tranquillitatem dare ac mundo etiam universo.
Now, since the dire commotion of wars had long afflicted several provinces of the Christian profession, we, desiring with all the affection of our mind tranquillity and peace for the holy church of God and generally for the whole Christian people, decided to send to the foremost secular prince—the author of this dissension and tribulation, who had been bound with the bond of anathema by our predecessor Pope Gregory of happy memory on account of his excesses—special nuncios, men of great authority: namely, our venerable brothers Peter of Albano, then archbishop of Rouen, and William of Sabina, then formerly bishop of Modena, and our beloved son William, priest cardinal of the basilica of the Twelve Apostles, then abbot of Saint Facundus, who were zealous for his salvation, causing [it] to be proposed to him through them that we and our brothers, as much as was in us, desired peace with him in all things as well as with all men, being prepared to give peace and tranquillity to him and to the whole world as well.
¶3
Et quia praelatorum, clericorum omnium que aliorum, quos detinebat captivos, et omnium tam clericorum quam laicorum, quos coeperat in galeis, restitutio poterat esse pacis plurimum inductiva, eum ut illos restitueret, cum hoc idem tam ipse quam sui nuntii, antequam ad apostolatus vocati essemus officium, promisissent, rogari et peti ab ipso fecimus per eosdem ac proponi insuper, quod iidem parati pro nobis erant audire et tractare pacem ac etiam audire satisfactionem, quam facere vellet princeps de omnibus, pro quibus vinculo erat excommunicationis astrictus, et offerri praeterea, quod si ecclesia eum in aliquo contra debitum laeserat, quod non credebat, parata erat corrigere ac in statum debitum reformare.
And because the restoration of the prelates, clerics, and all others whom he was detaining as captives, and of all both clerics and laypersons whom he had captured in galleys, could be very much conducive to peace, we caused him to be asked and requested through them that he restore them, since both he himself and his nuncios had promised this same thing before we were called to the office of apostle, and moreover to be proposed to him that the same [nuncios] were prepared on our behalf to hear and to treat about peace and also to hear the satisfaction which the prince wished to make concerning all things for which he was bound by the bond of excommunication, and furthermore to be offered that if the church had injured him in anything against what was due—which it did not believe—it was prepared to correct [it] and to reform [it] to the state due.
¶4
Et si diceret ipse, quod in nullo contra iustitiam laeserat ecclesiam vel quod nos eum contra iustitiam laesissemus, parati eramus vocare reges, praelatos et principes tam ecclesiasticos quam saeculares ad aliquem tutum locum, ubi per se vel solemnes nuntios convenirent, erat que parata ecclesia de consilio concilii sibi satisfacere, si eum laesisset in aliquo, ac revocare sententiam, si quam contra ipsum iniuste tulisset, et cum omni mansuetudine ac misericordia, quantum cum Deo et honore suo fieri poterat, recipere de iniuriis et offensis ipsi ecclesiae suis que per eum irrogatis satisfactionem ab ipso.
And if he himself should say that he had injured the church in nothing against justice or that we had injured him against justice, we were prepared to call kings, prelates, and princes both ecclesiastical and secular to some safe place where they might convene in person or through solemn nuncios, and the church was prepared by the counsel of the council to make satisfaction to him if it had injured him in anything, and to revoke the sentence if it had unjustly pronounced any against him, and with all meekness and mercy, as much as could be done with God and with its own honor, to receive satisfaction from him for the injuries and offenses inflicted upon the church itself and its [members] by him.
¶5
Volebat etiam ecclesia omnes amicos suos sibi que adhaerentes in pace ponere plena que securitate gaudere, ut numquam hac occasione possent aliquod subire discrimen.
The church also wished to place all its friends and those adhering to it in peace and to enjoy full security, so that they might never be able to undergo any danger on this occasion.
¶6
Sed licet sic apud eum pro pace paternis monitis et precum insistere curaverimus lenitate, idem tamen Pharaonis imitatus duritiam et obturans more aspidis aures suas, huiusmodi preces et monita elata obstinatione ac obstinata elatione despexit.
But although we thus took care to press upon him for peace with paternal admonitions and the gentleness of prayers, he, nevertheless, imitating the hardness of Pharaoh and stopping his ears after the manner of the asp, despised prayers and admonitions of this kind with elated obstinacy and obstinate elation.
¶7
Et licet processu temporis in die coenae Domini proximo nuper praeterita praecedente coram nobis et fratribus nostris, praesentibus carissimo in Christo filio nostro Constantinopolitano imperatore illustri, coetu quoque non modico praelatorum, senatoribus populo que Romano et maxima multitudine aliorum, qui eodem die propter solemnitatem ipsius de diversis mundi partibus ad apostolicam sedem convenerant, quod staret nostris et ecclesiae mandatis per nobilem virum Raymundum comitem Tholosanum ac magistros Petrum de Vinea et Tadeum de Suessa curiae suae iudices, nuntios et procuratores suos speciale super hoc ab ipso mandatum habentes, praestiterit iuramentum, postmodum tamen quod iuraverat non implevit.
And although in the process of time, on the day of the Lord's Supper recently past, the preceding [day], before us and our brothers, in the presence of our dearest son in Christ the illustrious emperor of Constantinople, also of no small assembly of prelates, of the senators and the Roman people, and of a very great multitude of others who on the same day on account of its solemnity had convened at the apostolic see from various parts of the world, through the noble man Raymond, count of Toulouse, and masters Peter de Vinea and Thaddeus of Suessa, judges of his curia, having a special mandate from him concerning this, he swore that he would stand by the mandates of us and the church, yet afterwards he did not fulfill what he had sworn.
¶8
Quin immo ea intentione ipsum praestitisse probabiliter creditur, sicut ex factis sequentibus colligitur evidenter, ut eidem ecclesiae ac nobis illuderet potius quam pareret, cum anno et amplius iam elapso nec ad ipsius ecclesiae graemium revocari potuerit nec sibi de illatis ei damnis et iniuriis curaverit satisfacere, licet super hoc exstiterit requisitus.
Nay rather, it is probably believed that he swore it with that intention—just as is clearly gathered from the subsequent facts—that he might rather mock the same church and us than obey, since now more than a year has elapsed and neither could he be recalled to the bosom of the church itself nor has he taken care to make satisfaction concerning the damages and injuries inflicted upon it, although he has been requested concerning this.
¶9
Propter quod non valentes absque gravi Christi offensa eius iniquitates amplius tolerare, cogimur urgente nos conscientia iuste animadvertere in eundem.
On account of which, not being able to tolerate his iniquities any longer without grave offense to Christ, we are compelled by our conscience urging us to animadvert justly against him.
¶10
Et ut ad praesens de ceteris eius sceleribus taceamus, quatuor gravissima, quae nulla possunt celari tergiversatione, commisit.
And to remain silent at present concerning his other crimes, he has committed four most grave [crimes], which can be concealed by no tergiversation.
¶11
Deieravit enim multotiens; pacem quondam inter ecclesiam et imperium reformatam temere violavit; perpetravit etiam sacrilegium, capi faciens cardinales sanctae Romanae ecclesiae ac aliarum ecclesiarum praelatos et clericos, religiosos et saeculares, venientes ad concilium quod idem praedecessor duxerat convocandum; de haeresi quoque non dubiis et levibus sed difficilibus et evidentibus argumentis suspectus habetur.
For he has perjured himself many times; he rashly violated peace once reformed between the church and the empire; he also perpetrated sacrilege, causing to be captured cardinals of the holy Roman church and prelates and clerics of other churches, religious and secular, coming to the council which the same predecessor had caused to be convoked; he is also held under suspicion of heresy by no doubtful and light but by difficult and evident arguments.
¶12
CONCERNING PERJURY
Plura siquidem eum commisisse periuria satis patet.
That he has committed many perjuries is quite clear.
¶13
Nam olim cum in Siciliae partibus morabatur, priusquam esset ad imperii dignitatem electus, coram bonae memoriae Gregorium sancti Theodori diacono cardinali, apostolicae sedis legato, felicis recordationis Innocentio papae praedecessori nostro et successoribus eius ecclesiae que Romanae, pro concessione regni Siciliae ab eadem ecclesia sibi facta, fidelitatis praestitit iuramentum et, sicut dicitur, illud idem, postquam ad eandem dignitatem electus exstitit et venit ad Urbem, coram eodem Innocentio suis que fratribus, aliis multis praesentibus, ligium hominium in eius faciens manibus, innovavit.
For long ago, when he was staying in the parts of Sicily, before he was elected to the dignity of the empire, before Gregory of good memory, deacon cardinal of Saint Theodore, legate of the apostolic see, he swore fidelity to our predecessor Pope Innocent of happy memory and to his successors and to the Roman church, for the concession of the kingdom of Sicily made to him by the same church, and, as is said, after he was elected to the same dignity and came to the City, before the same Innocent and his brothers, many others being present, he renewed liege homage, making it in his hands.
¶14
Deinde cum in Alamannia esset, eidem Innocentio et, ipso defuncto, bonae memoriae Honorio papae praedecessori nostro et eius successoribus ac ipsi ecclesiae Romanae, praesentibus imperii principibus atque nobilibus, iuravit honores, iura et possessiones Romanae ecclesiae pro posse suo servare ac protegere bona fide, et quod quaecumque ad manus suas devenirent, sine difficultate restituere procuraret, nominatis expresse dictis possessionibus in huiusmodi iuramento; quod postmodum confirmavit, coronam imperii iam adeptus.
Next, when he was in Germany, he swore to the same Innocent and, after he [Innocent] died, to our predecessor Pope Honorius of good memory and to his successors and to the Roman church itself, in the presence of the princes and nobles of the empire, that he would preserve and protect the honors, rights, and possessions of the Roman church according to his power in good faith, and that whatever things should come into his hands, he would take care to restore without difficulty, the said possessions being expressly named in such an oath; which afterwards, having already obtained the crown of the empire, he confirmed.
¶15
Sed horum trium iuramentorum temerarius exstitit violator non sine proditionis nota et laesae crimine maiestatis.
But of these three oaths he has been a rash violator, not without the mark of treason and the crime of lese-majesty.
¶16
Nam contra praefatum praedecessorem Gregorium et fratres suos comminatorias litteras eisdem fratribus destinare ac dictum Gregorium apud fratres ipsos, sicut apparet per litteras ab eo tunc directas eisdem, et etiam, prout fertur, per universum fere orbem terrarum multipliciter diffamare praesumpsit.
For against the aforesaid predecessor Gregory and his brothers he presumed to send threatening letters to the same brothers and to defame the said Gregory among the brothers themselves, as appears through letters sent by him then to the same, and also, as is reported, throughout almost the whole world in many ways.
¶17
Ac venerabilem fratrem nostrum Ottonem Portuensem, tunc sancti Nicolai in Carcere Tulliano diaconum cardinalem, et bonae memoriae Iacobum Praenestinum episcopos, apostolicae sedis legatos, nobilia et magna ecclesiae Romanae membra, personaliter capi fecit et bonis omnibus spoliatos ac per diversa loca non semel ignominiose deductos carceribus mancipari.
And he caused our venerable brother Otto of Porto, then deacon cardinal of Saint Nicholas in Carcere Tulliano, and James of Praeneste of good memory, bishops, legates of the apostolic see, noble and great members of the Roman church, to be personally captured, and, despoiled of all goods and ignominiously led about through diverse places not once, to be consigned to prisons.
¶18
CONCERNING THE VIOLATION OF PEACE AND SACRILEGE
Privilegium insuper, quod beato Petro et successoribus eius in ipso tradidit dominus Iesus Christus, videlicet: quodcumque ligaveris super terram, erit ligatum et in coelis, et quodcumque solveris super terram, erit solutum et in coelis, in quo utique auctoritas et potestas ecclesiae Romanae consistit, pro viribus diminuere vel ipsi ecclesiae auferre sategit, scribens se praefati Gregorii sententias non vereri, latam ab eo excommunicationem in ipsum non solum contemptis ecclesiae clavibus non servando, verum etiam per se ac officiales suos et illam et alias excommunicationis vel interdicti sententias, quas idem omnino contempsit, cogendo alios non servare.
Moreover, the privilege which the Lord Jesus Christ handed down in person to blessed Peter and his successors—namely, "Whatsoever you shall have bound on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatsoever you shall have loosed on earth shall be loosed in heaven," in which indeed the authority and power of the Roman church consist—he has endeavored to diminish according to his strength or to take away from the church itself, writing that he does not fear the sentences of the said Gregory, not only by not observing the excommunication pronounced against him, despising the keys of the church, but also by compelling through himself and his officials that both that [sentence] and other sentences of excommunication or interdict—which he altogether despised—be not observed by others.
¶19
Possessiones quoque praefatae Romanae ecclesiae, videlicet Marchiam, Ducatum, Beneventum, cuius muros et turres dirui fecit, ac alias quas in Tusciae ac Lombardiae partibus et quibusdam aliis obtinebat locis, paucis exceptis, occupare non metuens, eas adhuc detinet occupatas.
Also the possessions of the aforesaid Roman church—namely, the March, the Duchy, Benevento, whose walls and towers he caused to be torn down, and others which he obtained in the parts of Tuscany and Lombardy and in certain other places, not fearing to occupy, he still detains occupied, a few excepted.
¶20
Et tamquam ei non sufficeret, quod manifeste contra iuramenta praemissa praesumendo talia veniebat, per se vel suos officiales earumdem possessionum homines deierare compulit, ipsos a iuramentis fidelitatis, quibus Romanae tenebantur ecclesiae, de facto, cum de iure non posset, absolvens et faciens eosdem fidelitatem nihilominus abiurare praedictam sibi que fidelitatis huiusmodi iuramenta praestare.
And as if it did not suffice for him that he came against the aforesaid oaths by presuming such things, he compelled through himself or his officials the men of the same possessions to perjure themselves, actually—since he could not de jure—absolving them from the oaths of fidelity by which they were held to the Roman church, and causing them likewise to abjure the aforesaid fidelity and to make oaths of this kind of fidelity to himself.
¶21
CONCERNING THE VIOLATION OF PEACE
Pacis vero ipsum violatorem exsistere plene constat, quia cum olim tempore pacis inter ipsum et ecclesiam reformatae iurasset coram bonae memoriae Ioanni de Abbatis Villa episcopo Sabinensi et magistro Thomasio tituli sanctae Sabinae presbytero cardinali, praesentibus multis praelatis, principibus et baronibus, quod staret et pareret praecise absque ulla conditione omnibus mandatis ecclesiae super iis, pro quibus erat vinculo excommunicationis astrictus, causis excommunicationis eiusdem expressis per ordinem coram eo, tunc omnibus Theotonicis, hominibus regni Siciliae ac quibuslibet aliis, qui ecclesiae contra ipsum adhaeserant, omnem remittens offensam et poenam, et quod nullo tempore offenderet vel offendi faceret ipsos pro eo quod ecclesiae adstiterant, praestari in anima sua per Thomasium comitem Acerrarum faciens iuramentum, postmodum pacem et iuramenta huiusmodi, nequaquam erubescens irretiri periuriis, non servavit.
That he is a violator of peace is fully established, because when long ago, in the time of peace reformed between him and the church, he had sworn before John of Abbot's Villa of good memory, bishop of Sabina, and Master Thomasius, priest cardinal of Saint Sabina, many prelates, princes, and barons being present, that he would stand by and obey precisely without any condition all the mandates of the church concerning those things for which he was bound by the bond of excommunication—the causes of the same excommunication having been expressed in order before him—then remitting all offense and penalty to all Germans, men of the kingdom of Sicily, and any others who had adhered to the church against him, and that he would at no time offend or cause to be offended on account of the fact that they had stood by the church, causing the oath to be made on his soul by Thomasius, count of Acerra, afterwards he, not at all blushing to be entangled with perjuries, did not keep the peace and oaths of this kind.
¶22
Nonnullos enim ex ipsis hominibus tam nobiles quam alios postea capi fecit et, eis bonis suis omnibus spoliatis, uxores eorum et filios captivari ac terras ecclesiae contra promissionem, quam eisdem Ioanni Sabinensi episcopo et Thomasio cardinali fecerat, irreverenter invasit, licet ipsi extunc in eum praesentem, si contraveniret, excommunicationis sententiam promulgarint.
For he caused many of those same men, both nobles and others, later to be captured and, their goods having been despoiled, their wives and sons to be captured, and irreverently invaded the lands of the church against the promise which he had made to the same John, bishop of Sabina, and Thomasius cardinal, although they themselves from then on pronounced sentence of excommunication against him, if he should contravene.
¶23
Et cum iidem apostolica sibi auctoritate mandassent, ut nec per se nec per alium impediret, quin postulationes, electiones et confirmationes ecclesiarum et monasteriorum in regno praefato libere de cetero fierent secundum statuta concilii generalis, et quod nullus deinceps in eodem regno viris ecclesiasticis ac rebus eorum imponeret tallias vel collectas, quod que nullus ibidem clericus vel persona ecclesiastica de cetero in civili vel criminali causa conveniretur coram iudice saeculari, nisi super feudis quaestio civiliter haberetur, ac Templariis, Hospitalariis et aliis personis ecclesiasticis de damnis et iniuriis irrogatis eisdem satisfaceret competenter, ipse mandatum huiusmodi adimplere contempsit.
And when the same [legates] had mandated to him by apostolic authority that he not impede through himself or through another that postulations, elections, and confirmations of churches and monasteries in the aforesaid kingdom be freely made hereafter according to the statutes of the general council, and that no one henceforth in the same kingdom impose tolls or collections upon ecclesiastical men and their affairs, and that no cleric or ecclesiastical person there be sued hereafter in a civil or criminal cause before a secular judge, unless concerning fiefs a question be held civilly, and that he make suitable satisfaction to the Templars, Hospitallers, and other ecclesiastical persons for the damages and injuries inflicted upon them, he despised to fulfill the mandate of this kind.
¶24
Liquet namque undecim aut plures archiepiscopales et multas episcopalem sedes, abbatias quoque ac alias ecclesias vacare ad praesens in regno praedicto eas que procurante ipso, sicut aperte patet, fuisse diutius praelatorum regimine destitutas in grave ipsarum praeiudicium et periculum animarum.
For it is clear that eleven or more archiepiscopal sees and many episcopal sees, abbeys also and other churches are vacant at present in the aforesaid kingdom, and that by his procurement, as plainly appears, they have been for a long time destitute of the governance of prelates, to their grave prejudice and the peril of souls.
¶25
Et licet forte in aliquibus eiusdem regni ecclesiis electiones sint a capitulis celebratae, quia tamen per illas eiusdem familiares clerici sunt electi, probabili potest argumento concludi, quod facultatem non habuerunt liberam eligendi.
And although perhaps in some churches of the same kingdom elections have been celebrated by the chapters, yet because through them clerics of his household have been elected, it can be concluded by probable argument that they have not had free faculty of electing.
¶26
Ecclesiarum autem ipsius regni non solum facultates et bona fecit prout voluit occupari, sed etiam cruces, turibula, calices et alios sacros earum thesauros et pannos sericos velut cultus divini contemptor auferri, licet ut dicitur ipsis ecclesiis, exacto tamen prius pro eis certo pretio, in parte fuerint restituti.
Moreover, he not only caused the faculties and goods of the churches of the same kingdom to be occupied as he wished, but also crosses, thuribles, chalices, and other sacred treasures of them and silk cloths, as a despiser of divine worship, to be taken away, although, as is said, they were in part restored to the churches themselves, yet a certain price having been exacted for them beforehand.
¶27
Clerici quippe collectis et talliis multipliciter affliguntur, nec solum trahuntur ad iudicium saeculare, sed ut asseritur coguntur subire duella, incarcerantur, occiduntur et patibulis cruciantur in confusionem et opprobrium ordinis clericalis.
For clerics are afflicted manifoldly with collections and tolls, and not only are drawn to secular judgment, but, as is asserted, are compelled to undergo duels, are imprisoned, are killed, and are tortured on gibbets, to the confusion and opprobrium of the clerical order.
¶28
Praefatis autem Templariis, Hospitalariis et personis ecclesiasticis non est de damnis illatis eisdem et iniuriis satisfactum.
Moreover, to the aforesaid Templars, Hospitallers, and ecclesiastical persons, satisfaction has not been made for the damages and injuries inflicted upon them.
¶29
CONCERNING SACRILEGE
Eum quoque certum est fore sacrilegii patratorem.
It is also certain that he is a perpetrator of sacrilege.
¶30
Nam cum praefati Portuensis et Praenestinus episcopi et quam plures ecclesiarum praelati et clerici tam religiosi quam saeculares ad apostolicam sedem pro celebrando concilio, quod prius ipse petiverat, convocati per mare venirent, viis terrae ipsis de mandato eius omnino praeclusis, idem destinato Ensio filio suo cum multitudine galearum et per alias quamplures longe antea serio praeparatas in partibus Tusciae maritimis insidiis positis contra eos, ut gravius posset virus vomere praeconceptum, ipsos ausu sacrilego capi fecit, quibusdam praelatorum ipsorum et aliis in huiusmodi captione submersis, nonnullis etiam interemptis et aliquibus hostili insecutione fugatis, reliquis autem bonis spoliatis omnibus et de loco ad locum in regnum Siciliae opprobriose deductis ac ibidem diris carceribus mancipatis.
For when the aforesaid bishops of Porto and Praeneste and very many prelates of churches and clerics both religious and secular, convoked to the apostolic see for celebrating the council which he himself had previously sought, came by sea, the ways of the land having been altogether closed to them by his mandate, he, having sent his son Enzio with a multitude of galleys and by many others prepared long before earnestly in the maritime parts of Tuscany, with ambushes placed against them, so that he might vomit forth the conceived venom more gravely, caused them to be captured with sacrilegious audacity, some of the same prelates and others in such a capture having been drowned, several also having been killed, and some having been put to flight by hostile pursuit, the rest having been despoiled of all goods and led from place to place in the kingdom of Sicily disgracefully and there consigned to dire prisons.
¶31
Quorum aliqui macerati squaloribus et inedia pressi miserabiliter defecerunt.
Some of whom, wasted by squalors and pressed by hunger, have miserably perished.
¶32
CONCERNING THE SUSPICION OF HERESY
Merito insuper contra eum de haeretica pravitate suspicio est exorta, cum postquam excommunicationis sententiam a praefatis Ioanne episcopo Sabinensi et Thomasio cardinali prolatam incurrit et dictus Gregorius papa ipsum anathematis vinculo innodavit, ac post ecclesiae Romanae cardinalium, praelatorum et clericorum ac aliorum etiam diversis temporibus ad sedem apostolicam venientium captionem, claves ecclesiae contempserit et contemnat, sibi faciens celebrari vel potius quantum in eo est prophanari divina, et constanter asseveraverit, ut superius est narratum, se praefati Gregorii papae sententias non vereri.
Moreover, suspicion of heretical depravity has justly arisen against him, since after he incurred the sentence of excommunication pronounced by the aforesaid John, bishop of Sabina, and Thomasius cardinal, and the said Pope Gregory bound him with the bond of anathema, and after the capture of the cardinals of the Roman church, prelates and clerics and others also coming at various times to the apostolic see, he has despised and does despise the keys of the church, causing divine [offices] to be celebrated—or rather, as much as in him lies, to be profaned—for himself, and has constantly asserted, as is narrated above, that he does not fear the sentences of the said Pope Gregory.
¶33
Praeterea coniunctus amicitia detestabili Sarracenis, nuntios et munera pluries destinavit eisdem et ab eis vicissim cum honorificentia et ilaritate recepit ipsorum que ritus amplectitur, illos in cotidianis eius obsequiis notabiliter se cum tenens, eorumdem etiam more uxoribus quas habuit de stirpe regia descendentibus, eunuchos, praecipue quos, ut dicitur serio, castrari fecerat, non erubuit deputare custodes.
Moreover, he has been joined in detestable friendship with Saracens, has sent nuncios and gifts many times to them and has received from them in turn with honorificence and cheerfulness, and embraces their rites, notably keeping them in his daily services, and also, in the manner of them, for his wives who have descended from royal stock, he has not been ashamed to appoint eunuchs as guardians—especially those whom, as is said earnestly, he had caused to be castrated.
¶34
Et quod execrabilius est, olim exsistens in partibus transmarinis, facta compositione quadam, immo collusione verius cum soldano, Machometi nomen in templo Domini diebus et noctibus publice proclamari permisit.
And—which is more execrable—long ago, being in the overseas parts, a certain composition having been made, or rather collusion, with the sultan, he permitted the name of Mahomet to be publicly proclaimed in the temple of the Lord day and night.
¶35
Et nuper nuntios soldani Babiloniae, postquam idem soldanus Terrae sanctae ac christianis habitatoribus eius per se ac suos damna gravissima et inaestimabiles iniurias irrogarat, fecit per regnum Siciliae cum laudibus ad eiusdem soldani extollentiam, sicut fertur, honorifice suscipi et magnifice procurari.
And recently, after the same sultan of Babylon had inflicted very grave damages and inestimable injuries upon the Holy Land and the Christian inhabitants of it through himself and his [men], he caused the nuncios of the sultan to be honorably received and magnificently provided for through the kingdom of Sicily with praises to the extolling of the same sultan, as is reported.
¶36
Aliorum quoque infidelium perniciosis et horrendis obsequiis contra fideles abutens et illis qui damnabiliter vilipendentes apostolicam sedem ab unitate ecclesiae discesserunt, procurans affinitate ac amicitia copulari, clarae memoriae Ludovicum ducem Bavariae, specialem ecclesiae Romanae devotum, fecit, sicut pro certo asseritur, christiana religione despecta per assasinos occidi, et Batatio, Dei et ecclesiae inimico a communione fidelium per excommunicationis sententiam cum adiutoribus, consiliatoribus et fautoribus suis solemniter separato, filiam suam tradidit in uxorem.
Also, abusing the pernicious and horrendous services of other unbelievers against the faithful, and seeking to be joined with those who, damnably despising the apostolic see, have departed from the unity of the church by affinity and friendship, he caused Louis, duke of Bavaria, of clear memory, a special devotee of the Roman church, as is asserted for certain, the Christian religion having been despised, to be killed by assassins, and gave his daughter in marriage to Batatius, an enemy of God and the church, who had been solemnly separated from the communion of the faithful by sentence of excommunication along with his helpers, counselors, and supporters.
¶37
Catholicorum vero principum actus et mores respuens, neglector salutis et famae, pietatis operibus non intendit.
But rejecting the acts and morals of catholic princes, a neglecter of salvation and fame, he does not attend to works of piety.
¶38
Quin immo, ut de suis nefariis dissolutionibus sileamus, cum didicerit opprimere, non curat oppressos misericorditer relevare, manu eius, ut decet principem, ad eleemosynas non extenta, cum destructioni ecclesiarum institerit et religiosas ac alias ecclesiasticas iugi attriverit afflictione personas; nec ecclesias nec monasteria nec hospitalia seu alia pia loca cernitur construxisse.
Nay rather, to remain silent concerning his nefarious dissolutions, since he has learned to oppress, he does not care mercifully to relieve the oppressed, his hand not being extended to alms as befits a prince, whereas he has insisted on the destruction of churches and has worn down religious and other ecclesiastical persons with continual affliction; nor is he seen to have built churches, nor monasteries, nor hospitals, nor other pious places.
¶39
Nonne igitur haec non levia, sed efficacia sunt argumenta de suspicione haeresis contra eum? cum tamen haereticorum vocabulo illos ius civile contineri asserat et latis adversus eos sententiis debere succumbere, qui vel levi argumento a iudicio catholicae religionis et tramite detecti fuerint deviare.
Are not these therefore not light but efficacious arguments of suspicion of heresy against him? since nevertheless the civil law asserts that those are contained under the name of heretics and ought to succumb under sentences pronounced against them, who are detected to have deviated by even a light argument from the judgment of the catholic religion and the path.
¶40
CONCERNING THE KINGDOM OF SICILY
Praeter haec regnum Siciliae, quod est speciale patrimonium beati Petri et idem princeps ab apostolica sede tenebat in feudum, iam ad tantam in clericis et laicis exinanitionem servitutem que redegit, quod eis paene penitus nihil habentibus et omnibus exinde fere probis eiectis, illos qui remanserunt ibidem sub servili quasi conditione vivere ac Romanam ecclesiam, cuius principaliter sunt homines et vassalli, offendere multipliciter et hostiliter impugnare compellit.
Besides these things, the kingdom of Sicily, which is the special patrimony of blessed Peter and the same prince held in fief from the apostolic see, he has already reduced to such great exhaustion and servitude among clerics and laypersons that, with them having almost nothing at all and nearly all the upright men having been cast out from there, those who have remained there he compels to live under a condition as if servile and to offend the Roman church—whose men and vassals they principally are—manifoldly and to attack it in a hostile manner.
¶41
Posset etiam merito reprehendi, quod mille squifatorum annuam pensionem, in qua pro eodem regno ipsi ecclesiae Romanae tenetur, per novem annos et amplius solvere praetermisit.
It could also be justly reproached that he has omitted to pay the annual pension of one thousand squifatorum, which he is held to pay to the Roman church itself for the same kingdom, for nine years and more.
¶42
THE SENTENCE OF DEPOSITION
Nos itaque super praemissis et quam pluribus aliis eius nefandis excessibus cum fratribus nostris et sacro concilio deliberatione praehabita diligenti, cum Iesu Christi vices licet immeriti teneamus in terris nobis que in beati Petri apostoli persona sit dictum: quodcumque ligaveris super terram et cetera, memoratum principem, qui se imperio et regnis omni que honore ac dignitate reddidit tam indignum, qui que propter suas iniquitates a Deo ne regnet vel imperet est abiectus, suis ligatum peccatis et abiectum omni que honore ac dignitate privatum a Domino ostendimus, denuntiamus ac nihilominus sententiando privamus, omnes qui ei iuramento fidelitatis tenentur adstricti, a iuramento huiusmodi perpetuo absolventes, auctoritate apostolica firmiter inhibendo, ne quisquam de cetero sibi tamquam imperatori vel regi pareat vel intendat, et decernendo quoslibet, qui deinceps ei velut imperatori aut regi consilium vel auxilium praestiterint seu favorem, ipso facto excommunicationis vinculo subiacere.
We therefore, concerning the premises and very many other nefarious excesses of him, having held a diligent deliberation with our brothers and the sacred council, since we hold the place of Jesus Christ on earth, although unworthily, and it has been said to us in the person of blessed Peter the apostle, "Whatsoever you shall have bound on earth," etc., do show, denounce, and also by sentencing deprive the aforesaid prince—who has rendered himself so unworthy of the empire and kingdoms and all honor and dignity, and who, on account of his iniquities, has been cast out by God so that he may not reign or rule—as bound by his sins and cast out and deprived of all honor and dignity, [as made evident] by the Lord; and do perpetually absolve all who are held bound to him by an oath of fidelity from an oath of this kind, apostolic authority firmly inhibiting that anyone henceforth obey or attend upon him as emperor or king, and decreeing that any persons who henceforth shall have given counsel or aid or favor to him as emperor or king shall be subject ipso facto to the bond of excommunication.
¶43
Illi autem, ad quos in eodem imperio imperatoris spectat electio, eligant libere successorem.
Those moreover to whom in the same empire the election of the emperor pertains, let them freely elect a successor.
¶44
De praefato vero Siciliae regno providere curabimus cum eorumdem fratrum nostrorum consilio, sicut viderimus expedire.
Concerning the aforesaid kingdom of Sicily, we shall take care to provide with the counsel of the same our brothers as we shall have seen expedient.
¶45
Da(tum) Lugduni, XVI. Kal(endas) augusti, pontificatus nostri anno tertio.
Given at Lyons, the 16th of the Kalends of August [July 17], in the third year of our pontificate.
¶46
CONSTITUTION I
Cum in multis iuris articulis infinitas reprobetur provide duximus statuendum ut per generalem clausulam quidam alii quae frequenter in litteris nostris inseritur ultra tres vel quatuor in iudicium non trahantur.
Since in many articles of law infinity is disapproved, we have providently decreed to be established that through a general clause—which is frequently inserted in our letters—certain others be not drawn into judgment beyond three or four.
¶47
Quorum nomina in primo citatorio exprimat impetrator ne fraudi locus forsitan relinquatur si circa ea possit libere variare.
Let the impetrator express their names in the first citation, lest perhaps a place for fraud be left if he may be able freely to vary concerning those things.
¶48
CONSTITUTION II
Praesenti decreto duximus providendum ne a sede apostolica vel legatis ipsius causae aliquibus committantur nisi personis quae vel dignitate sint praeditae vel in ecclesiis cathedralibus seu aliis collegiatis honorabilibus institutae nec alibi quam in civitatibus vel locis magnis et insignibus ubi haberi valeat iuris copia peritorum causae huiusmodi agitentur.
By the present decree we have provided to be taken care that causes be not committed by the apostolic see or its legates to anyone except to persons who are either endowed with dignity or established in cathedral or other honorable collegiate churches, nor let causes of this kind be agitated elsewhere than in cities or great and notable places where a supply of learned men may be able to be had.
¶49
Iudicibus vero qui contra hoc statutum ad alia loca alterutram partium citaverint vel utramque non pareatur impune nisi citatio de communi utriusque partis processerit voluntate.
But let judges who against this statute shall have cited one or both of the parties to other places not be obeyed with impunity, unless the citation shall have proceeded from the common will of each party.
¶50
CONSTITUTION III
Dispendia litium aequitatis compendio volentes qua possumus industria coarctare statutum felicis recordationis Innocentii papae tertii super hoc editum ampliantes decrevimus ut si quis contra alium plures personales movere voluerit quaestiones non ad diversos iudices sed ad eosdem super omnibus huiusmodi quaestionibus litteras studeat impetrare.
Wishing to coerce the expenses of lawsuits with the benefit of equity by whatever industry we can, amplifying the statute published concerning this by Pope Innocent III of happy memory, we have decreed that if anyone wishes to move several personal actions against another, he shall strive to obtain letters concerning all such actions not to different judges but to the same judges.
¶51
Qui vero contrarium fecerit omni commodo careat litterarum nec processus valeat habitus per easdem alias si eum per ipsas fatigaverit in expensis legitimis condemnetur.
But whoever shall have done the contrary, let him lack every benefit of the letters, nor let the process had through them be valid; if he shall have harassed him through them, let him be condemned in legitimate expenses.
¶52
Reus quoque si eodem durante iudicio actorem sibi obnoxium dixerit reconventionis beneficio vel conventionis si litteras contra eum impetrare maluerit de iure suo debet apud eosdem iudices experiri nisi eos ut suspectos poterit recusare simili poena si contrafecerit puniendus.
Likewise, if the defendant during the same pending trial shall have said that the actor is liable to him by way of reconvention or convention if he shall have preferred to obtain letters against him, he ought to sue concerning his right before the same judges, unless he may be able to recuse them as suspected, being punished by a similar penalty if he shall have acted against [the statute].
¶53
CONSTITUTION IV
Statuimus ut si quis electionem postulationem vel provisionem factam impugnat in formam obiciens aliquid vel personam et propter hoc ad nos appellari contingat tam is qui opponit quam qui defendit et generaliter omnes quorum interest et quos causa contingit per se vel per procuratores ad causam instructos ad sedem apostolicam a die obiectionis iter arripiant infra mensem.
We decree that if anyone impugns an election, postulation, or provision made, objecting something concerning the form or concerning the person, and on account of this it happens that appeal be made to us, both he who objects and he who defends, and generally all whose interest [it is] and whom the cause touches, shall take up the journey to the apostolic see within a month from the day of objection, through themselves or through proctors instructed for the cause.
¶54
Sed si pars aliqua non venerit post viginti dies post adventum alterius partis exspectata in electionis negotio non obstante cuiusquam absentia sicut de iure fuerit procedetur.
But if any party shall not have come, twenty days after the arrival of the other party having been awaited, in the matter of election, notwithstanding the absence of anyone, it shall be proceeded as according to law.
¶55
Haec autem in dignitatibus personatibus et canonicis observari volumus et mandamus.
We wish and command these things to be observed in dignities, personates, and canonries.
¶56
Adicimus etiam ut qui non plene probaverit quod in forma opponit ad expensas quas propter hoc pars altera fecisse docuerit condemnetur.
We also add that he who shall not have fully proved what he objects concerning the form, let him be condemned for the expenses which the other party shall have shown that he made on account of this.
¶57
Qui vero in probatione defecerit eius quod in personam obicit a beneficiis ecclesiasticis triennio noverit se suspensum.
But whoever shall have failed in the proof of what he objects concerning the person, let him know that he is suspended from ecclesiastical benefices for three years.
¶58
Atque si infra illud tempus propria se temeritate ingesserit tunc illis ipso iure perpetuo sit privatus nullam super hoc de misericordia spem aut fiduciam habiturus nisi manifestissimis constiterit documentis quod ipsum a calumniae vitio causa probabilis et sufficiens excuset.
And if within that time he shall have thrust himself in by his own rashness, then let him be deprived of them ipso iure perpetually, about to have no hope or confidence of mercy concerning this, unless it shall have been established by very manifest documents that a probable and sufficient cause excuses him from the vice of calumny.
¶59
CONSTITUTION V
In electionibus et postulationibus ac scrutiniis ex quibus ius oritur eligendi vota conditionalia alternativa et incerta penitus reprobamus statuentes ut huiusmodi votis pro non adiectis habitis ex puris consensibus celebretur electio voce illorum qui non pure consenserint ea vice in alios recidente.
In elections, postulations, and scrutinies from which the right of electing arises, we utterly reject conditional, alternative, and uncertain votes, decreeing that, such votes having been held as not added, the election be celebrated from the pure consents, the voice of those who have not purely consented falling upon others for that turn.
¶60
CONSTITUTION VI
Statuimus ut conservatores quos plerumque concedimus a manifestis iniuriis et violentiis defendere possint quos eis committimus defendendos nec ad alia quae iudicialem indaginem exigunt suam valeant extendere potestatem.
We decree that conservators—whom we often grant—may be able to defend from manifest injuries and violences those whom we entrust to them to be defended, nor may they be able to extend their power to other things which require judicial investigation.
¶61
CONSTITUTION VII
Officii nostri debitum remediis invigilat subiectorum quia dum eorum excutimus onera dum scandala removemus nos in ipsorum quiete quiescimus et fovemur in pace.
The debt of our office keeps watch over the remedies of the subjects, because while we shake off their burdens, while we remove scandals, we rest in their quiet and are fostered in peace.
¶62
Proinde praesenti decreto statuimus ut ecclesiae Romanae legati quantumcumque plenam legationem obtineant sive a nobis missi fuerint sive suarum ecclesiarum praetextu legationis sibi vendicent dignitatem ex ipsius legationis munere conferendi beneficia nullam habeant potestatem nisi hoc alicui specialiter duxerimus indulgendum.
Hence, by the present decree we decree that the legates of the Roman church, however full legation they may obtain, whether they have been sent by us or claim the dignity of legation for themselves under the pretext of their own churches, have no power of conferring benefices from the gift of the same legation, unless we shall have judged specially to grant this to anyone.
¶63
Quod tamen in fratribus nostris legatione fungentibus nolumus observari quia sicut honoris praerogativa laetantur sic eos auctoritate fungi volumus ampliori.
Which, however, we are unwilling to be observed concerning our brothers performing a legation, because just as they rejoice in the prerogative of honor, so we wish them to be endowed with ampler authority.
¶64
CONSTITUTION VIII
Iuris esse ambiguum non videtur iudicem delegatum qui a sede apostolica ad hoc mandatum non receperit speciale iubere non posse alterutram partium coram se personaliter in iudicio comparere nisi causa fuerit criminalis vel nisi pro veritate dicenda vel iuramento calumniae faciendo iuris necessitas partes coram eo personaliter exegerit praesentari.
It does not seem ambiguous of law that a delegated judge who has not received a special mandate from the apostolic see for this cannot order one or the other of the parties to appear in person in judgment, unless the cause be criminal or unless the necessity of law shall have required the parties to be presented in person before him for telling the truth or for making the oath of calumny.
¶65
Exceptionis peremptoriae seu defensionis cuiuslibet principalis cognitionem negotii continentis ante litem contestatam obiectus nisi de re iudicata vel transacta seu finita excipiat litigator litis contestationem fieri non impediat nec retardet licet dicat obiector non fuisse rescriptum obtentum si quae sunt impetranti opposita fuissent exposita deleganti.
The objection of a peremptory exception or of any defense containing the principal cognition of the matter, interposed before the litis contestation—unless the litigator excepts concerning a matter adjudged or transacted or ended—let it not impede nor retard the litis contestation, although he may say that the rescript would not have been obtained if the things opposed to the impetrant had been exposed to the delegant.
¶66
CONSTITUTION IX
Frequens et assidua nos querela circumstrepit quod spoliationis exceptio nonnumquam in iudiciis calumniose proposita causas ecclesiasticas impedit et perturbat.
Frequent and incessant complaint surrounds us that the exception of spoliation, sometimes proposed calumniously in judgments, impedes and disturbs ecclesiastical causes.
¶67
Dum enim exceptioni insistitur appellationes interponi contingit. Et sic intermittitur et plerumque perimitur causae cognitio principalis.
For while the exception is insisted upon, it happens that appeals are interposed. And thus the principal cognition of the cause is interrupted and often destroyed.
¶68
Et propterea nos qui voluntarios labores appetimus ut quietem aliis praeparemus finem litibus cupientes imponi et calumniae materiam amputari statuimus ut in civilibus negotiis spoliationis obiectione quae ab alio quam ab actore facta proponitur iudex in principali procedere non postponat.
And therefore we, who willingly undertake labors so that we may prepare quiet for others, wishing an end to be imposed upon lawsuits and the material of calumny to be cut off, decree that in civil matters, the objection of spoliation which is proposed as having been made by someone other than the actor, the judge not postpone proceeding in the principal matter.
¶69
Sed si in civilibus ab actore in criminalibus autem se spoliatum reus asserat a quocumque infra quindecim dierum spatium post diem in quo proponitur quod asseruit comprobabit alioquin in expensis quas actor interim ob hoc fecerit iudiciali taxatione praehabita condemnetur.
But if in civil matters, the actor, or in criminal matters, the defendant, asserts that he has been despoiled by anyone, within a space of fifteen days after the day in which what he asserts is proposed, he shall prove it; otherwise, let him be condemned in the expenses which the actor in the meantime shall have made on account of this, a judicial taxation having been held beforehand.
¶70
Alias si aequum iudici visum fuerit puniendus.
Otherwise, if it shall have seemed equitable to the judge, he is to be punished.
¶71
Illum autem spoliatum intelligi volumus in hoc casu cum criminaliter accusatur qui tota substantia sua vel maiori parte ipsius per violentiam se destitutum affirmat.
Moreover, we wish him to be understood as despoiled in this case when he who is criminally accused affirms that he has been deprived by violence of his whole substance or of the greater part of it.
¶72
Et secundum hoc loqui canones sano credendum est intellectu quia nec nudi contendere nec inermes inimicis opponere non debemus.
And it is to be believed that the canons speak according to sound understanding concerning this, because we ought neither to contend nakedly nor to oppose the unarmed to enemies.
¶73
Habet enim spoliatus privilegium ut non possit exui iam nudatus.
For a despoiled person has a privilege that he cannot be stripped, already having been stripped.
¶74
Solet autem inter scholasticos dubitari si spoliatus a tertio de spoliatione contra suum accusatorem excipiat an ei tempus a iudice debeat indulgeri infra quod restitutionem imploret.
It is, however, accustomed to be doubted among scholars whether a despoiled person by a third party excepts concerning spoliation against his own accuser, whether a time ought to be granted to him by the judge within which he may implore restitution.
¶75
Ne forte sic velit exsistere ut omnem accusatorem eludat quod satis aequitati consonum aestimamus.
Lest perhaps he might thus wish to exist so that he may elude every accuser—which we consider sufficiently consonant with equity.
¶76
Et si infra tempus indultum restitutionem non petierit et causam cum potuerit non perduxerit ad finem non obstante spoliationis exceptione deinceps poterit accusari.
And if within the granted time he shall not have sought restitution and shall not have brought the cause to an end when he could, the exception of spoliation notwithstanding, he can thereafter be accused.
¶77
CONSTITUTION X
Ad haec sancimus ut rerum privatarum spoliatio agenti super ecclesiasticis vel e nullatenus apponatur.
To these things we sanction that the spoliation of private goods be by no means appended to one acting concerning ecclesiastical [goods].
¶78
Actor qui venire ad terminum ad quem citari adversarium fecerat non curavit venienti reo in expensis propter hoc factis legitime condemnetur ad citationem aliam nisi sufficienter caveat quod in termino compareat minime admittendus.
An actor who has not taken care to come to the term for which he had caused his adversary to be cited, let him be legitimately condemned in the expenses made on account of this to the defendant coming, not at all to be admitted to another citation unless he shall have sufficiently provided that he will appear at the term.
¶79
CONSTITUTION XI
Eum qui super dignitate personatu vel beneficio ecclesiastico obtinendis cum alio litigat possessore ob partis adversae contumaciam causa rei servandae in ipsorum possessionem statuimus non mittendum ne per hoc ad ea ingressus patere valeat vitiosus sed liceat in hoc casu contumacis absentiam divina replente praesentia etiam lite non contestata diligenter examinato negotio ipsum fine debito terminare.
Concerning him who litigates with another concerning obtaining a dignity, personate, or ecclesiastical benefice, by reason of the contumacy of the adverse party, we decree that he is not to be sent into possession of them for the sake of preserving the matter, lest through this a vicious entry be able to lie open to them; but let it be permissible in this case, the divine presence filling the absence of the contumacious person, even with the litis contestation not having been held, the matter having been diligently examined, to terminate it with the due end.
¶80
Statuimus ut positiones negativas quae probari non possunt nisi per confessionem adversarii iudices admittere possint si aequitate suadente viderint expedire.
We decree that judges may be able to admit negative positions which cannot be proved except through the confession of the adversary, if they shall have seen [it] expedient, equity persuading.
¶81
CONSTITUTION XII
Pia consideratione statuit mater ecclesia quod maioris excommunicationis exceptio in quacumque parte iudiciorum opposita lites differat et repellat agentes ut ex hoc magis censura ecclesiastica timeatur et communionis periculum evitetur contumaciae vitium reprimatur et excommunicati dum a communibus actibus excluduntur rubore suffusi ad humilitatis gratiam et reconciliationis effectum facilius inclinantur.
The mother church, with pious consideration, decreed that the exception of major excommunication, interposed in any part of judgments, defer lawsuits and repel actors, so that from this ecclesiastical censure be more feared, and the peril of communion be avoided, the vice of contumacy be repressed, and excommunicated persons, while they are excluded from common acts, suffused with shame, may be more easily inclined to the grace of humility and the effect of reconciliation.
¶82
Sed hominum succrescente malitia quod provisum est ad remedium transit ad noxas.
But with the malice of men growing, what was provided for a remedy passes into injuries.
¶83
Dum enim in causis ecclesiasticis frequentius haec exceptio per malitiam opponatur contingit differri negotia et partes fatigari laboribus et expensis.
For while in ecclesiastical causes this exception is interposed more frequently through malice, it happens that negotiations are deferred and parties are fatigued with labors and expenses.
¶84
Proinde quia morbus iste quasi communis irrepsit dignum duximus communem adhibere medelam.
Hence, because this disease has crept in as if common, we have judged it worthy to apply a common remedy.
¶85
Si quis igitur excommunicationem opponat speciem ipsius et nomen excommunicatoris exponat sciturus eam rem se deferre in publicam notionem quam infra octo dierum spatium die in quo proponitur minime computato probare valeat apertissimis documentis.
If therefore anyone shall oppose an excommunication, let him set forth the species of it and the name of the excommunicator, about to know that he brings a thing into public notice which he may be able to prove by most open documents within the space of eight days, the day on which it is proposed not at all being counted.
¶86
Quod si non probaverit iudex in causa procedere non omittat reum in expensis quas actor ob hoc diebus illis se fecisse docuerit praehabita taxatione condemnans.
But if he shall not have proved [it], let the judge not omit to proceed in the cause, condemning the defendant in the expenses which the actor shall have shown that he made on account of this in those days, a taxation having been held beforehand.
¶87
Si vero postmodum instantia durante iudicii et probandi copia succedente de eadem excommunicatione vel alia excipiatur iterum et probetur actor in sequentibus excludatur donec meruerit absolutionis gratiam obtinere his quae praecesserunt nihilominus in suo robore duraturis.
But if afterwards, the duration of the trial continuing and the opportunity of proving coming, it be excepted again concerning the same excommunication or another, and it be proved, let the actor be excluded in the subsequent [proceedings] until he shall have deserved to obtain the grace of absolution, the things which preceded nonetheless remaining in their force.
¶88
Proviso ut ultra duas vices non haec proponatur exceptio praeterquam si excommunicatio nova emerserit vel evidens et prompta probatio supervenerit de antiqua.
It is provided that this exception be not proposed beyond two turns, unless a new excommunication shall have emerged or evident and prompt proof shall have come concerning an old one.
¶89
Sed si post rem iudicatam talis exceptio proponatur executionem impediet sed sententia quae praecessit non minus robur debitum obtinebit eo tamen salvo ut si actor excommunicatus sit publice et hoc iudex noverit quandocumque etsi de hoc reus non excipiat iudex actorem ex suo officio repellere non postponat.
But if after a matter adjudged such an exception shall be proposed, it will impede execution, but the sentence which preceded will no less obtain its due force—this however being saved, that if the actor is publicly excommunicated and the judge knows this, whenever, even if the defendant does not except concerning this, let the judge not postpone to repel the actor from his office.
¶90
CONSTITUTION XIII
Cum aeterni principis tribunal illum reum non habeat quem iniuste iudex condemnat testante propheta nec damnabit eum cum iudicabitur illi caveant ecclesiastici iudices et prudenter attendant ut in causarum processibus nihil vendicet odium nihil vel favor usurpet timor exsulet praemium aut expectatio praemii iustitiam non evertat.
Since the tribunal of the eternal prince does not hold him guilty whom the judge unjustly condemns, the prophet testifying, "Neither will he condemn him when he shall be judged," let ecclesiastical judges be careful and prudently attend that in the processes of causes hatred claim nothing for itself, favor usurp nothing, fear be absent, reward or the expectation of reward not overthrow justice.
¶91
Sed stateram gestent in manibus lances appendant aequo libramine ut in omnibus quae in causis agenda fuerint praesertim in concipiendis sententiis et ferendis prae oculis habeant solum deum illius imitantes exemplum qui querelas populi tabernaculum ingressus ad dominum referebat ut secundum eius imperium iudicaret.
But let them carry a balance in their hands, let them weigh the scales with an equal measure, so that in all things which are to be done in causes, especially in conceiving and issuing sentences, they have only God before their eyes, imitating the example of him who, having entered the tabernacle, referred the complaints of the people to the Lord so that he might judge according to his command.
¶92
Si quis autem iudex ecclesiasticus ordinarius aut delegatus famae prodigus et proprii persecutor honoris contra conscientiam et contra iustitiam in gravamen partis alterius in iudicio quicquam fecerit per gratiam vel per sordes ab executione officii per annum noverit se suspensum ad aestimationem litis parti quam laeserit nihilominus condemnandus sciturus quod si suspensione durante damnabiliter ingesserit se divinis irregularitatis laqueo se involvet secundum canonicas sanctiones a quo nonnisi per sedem poterit apostolicam liberari salvis aliis constitutionibus quae iudicibus male iudicantibus poenas gerunt et infligunt.
But if any ecclesiastical judge, ordinary or delegated, prodigal of fame and persecutor of his own honor, against conscience and against justice, to the burden of the other party, shall have done anything in judgment through favor or through sordidness, let him know that he is suspended from the execution of office for one year, nonetheless to be condemned to the estimation of the lawsuit to the party whom he injured, about to know that if during the suspension he shall have thrust himself in damnably, he will involve himself in the snare of irregularity according to the canonical sanctions, from which he cannot be freed except through the apostolic see, other constitutions which carry and inflict penalties upon judges judging badly being saved.
¶93
Dignum est etenim ut qui in tot praesumit offendere poena multiplici castigetur.
For it is worthy that he who presumes to offend in so many things be chastised with multiple penalty.
¶94
CONSTITUTION XIV
Cordi nobis est lites minuere et a laboribus relevare subiectos.
It is upon our heart to diminish lawsuits and to relieve the subjects from labors.
¶95
Sancimus igitur ut si quis in iudicio vel extra super interlocutoria vel gravamine ad nos duxerit appellandum causam appellationis in scriptis assignare deproperet petens apostolos quos ei praecipimus exhiberi.
We sanction therefore that if anyone in or out of judgment shall have judged that he ought to appeal to us concerning an interlocutory [matter] or a grievance, let him hasten to assign the cause of the appeal in writing, seeking apostles—which we command to be exhibited to him.
¶96
In quibus appellationis causam iudex exprimat et cur appellatio non sit admissa vel si appellationi forsitan ex superioris reverentia sit delatum.
In which [apostles] let the judge express the cause of the appeal and why the appeal was not admitted, or if perhaps the appeal has been deferred out of reverence for a superior.
¶97
Post haec appellatori secundum locorum distantiam personarum et negotii qualitatem tempore prosecutionis indulto si appellatus voluerit et principales petierint per se vel per procuratores instructos cum mandato ad agendum rationibus et munimentis ad causam spectantibus accedant ad sedem apostolicam sic parati ut si nobis visum fuerit expedire finito appellationis articulo vel partium voluntati commisso procedatur in negotio principali quantum poterit et de iure debebit his quae in appellationibus a definitivis sententiis interpositis antiquitas statuit non mutatis.
After this, a time having been granted to the appellant according to the distance of places, the quality of the persons and the negotiation, if the appellee shall have wished and the principal parties shall have sought, let them come to the apostolic see through themselves or through proctors instructed with a mandate for acting, with the accounts and muniments pertaining to the cause, thus prepared so that if it shall have seemed expedient to us, the article of the appeal having been finished or committed to the will of the parties, it may proceed in the principal negotiation as much as it will be able and according to law it ought, those things which antiquity established concerning appeals interposed from definitive sentences not being changed.
¶98
Quod si appellator quae praemissa sunt non observet reputabitur non appellans et ad prioris iudicis redibit examen in expensis legitimis condemnandus.
But if the appellant shall not observe the things which are premised, he shall be reputed as not appealing and shall return to the examination of the prior judge, to be condemned in legitimate expenses.
¶99
Si autem appellatus contempserit hoc statutum in eum tamquam contumacem tam in expensis quam in causa quantum a iure permittitur procedatur.
But if the appellee shall have despised this statute, let it be proceeded against him as contumacious, both in expenses and in the cause, as much as is permitted by law.
¶100
Iustum est equidem ut in eum iura consurgant qui ius iudicem et partem eludit.
It is indeed just that the laws rise up against him who eludes the law, the judge, and the party.
¶101
CONSTITUTION XV
Legitima suspicionis causa contra iudicem assignata et arbitris a partibus secundum formam iuris electis qui de ipsa cognoscant saepe contingit quod ipsis in idem convenire nolentibus nec tertium advocantibus cum quo ambo vel alter eorum procedant ad decisionem ipsius negotii ut tenentur iudex proferat excommunicationis sententiam contra eos quam ipsi tum propter odium tum propter favorem diutius vilipendunt.
A legitimate cause of suspicion against a judge having been assigned, and arbiters having been elected by the parties according to the form of law who may know concerning that [suspicion], it often happens that, they being unwilling to agree upon the same [arbiter], nor calling a third party with whom both or one of them may proceed to the decision of that negotiation as they are held [to do], the judge pronounces a sentence of excommunication against them, which they themselves, sometimes on account of hatred, sometimes on account of favor, despise for a long time.
¶102
Quare causa ipsa plus debito prorogata non proceditur ad cognitionem negotii principalis.
Wherefore, the cause itself having been delayed more than due, it is not proceeded to the cognition of the principal negotiation.
¶103
Volentes igitur morbo huiusmodi necessariam adhibere medelam statuimus ut ipsis arbitris per iudicem competens terminus praefigatur infra quem in idem conveniant vel tertium concorditer advocent cum quo ambo vel alter ipsorum eiusdem suspicionis negotium terminare procurent.
Willing therefore to apply a necessary remedy to a disease of this kind, we decree that a suitable term be fixed for the same arbiters by the judge, within which they shall agree upon the same [arbiter] or shall harmoniously call a third party with whom both or one of them may take care to terminate the matter of the same suspicion.
¶104
Alioquin iudex extunc in principali negotio procedere non omittat.
Otherwise, let the judge from then on not omit to proceed in the principal negotiation.
¶105
CONSTITUTION XVI
Pro humani redemptione generis de summis coelorum ad ima mundi descendens et mortem tandem subiens temporalem dei filius Iesus Christus ne gregem sui pretio sanguinis gloriosi redemptum ascensurus post resurrectionem ad patrem absque pastore desereret ipsius curam beato Petro apostolo ut suae stabilitate fidei ceteros in christiana religione firmaret eorum que mentes ad salutis suae opera accenderet devotionis ardore commisit.
For the redemption of the human race, descending from the highest heavens to the lowest parts of the world, and at length undergoing temporal death, Jesus Christ the Son of God, lest after the resurrection, about to ascend to the Father, he should leave the flock redeemed by the price of his glorious blood without a shepherd, entrusted the care of it to blessed Peter the apostle, so that by the stability of his faith he might strengthen the others in the Christian religion and inflame their minds to the works of his salvation with the ardor of devotion.
¶106
Unde nos eiusdem apostoli effecti disponente domino licet immeriti successores et ipsius redemptoris locum in terris quamquam indigne tenentes circa gregis eiusdem custodiam sollicitis excitari vigiliis et animarum saluti iugis accensione cogitationis intendere submovendo noxia et agendo profutura debemus ut excusso a nobis negligentiae somno nostri que cordis oculis diligentia sedula vigilantibus animas ipsas deo lucrifacere sua nobis cooperante gratia valeamus.
Whence we, made by the Lord's disposal, although unworthily, successors of the same apostle and holding the place of the same Redeemer on earth, although undeservedly, ought to be excited with solicitous vigils concerning the custody of the same flock and to intend to the salvation of souls with the unceasing kindling of thought, removing harmful things and doing profitable things, so that, the sleep of negligence having been shaken off from us and the eyes of our heart being watchful with sedulous diligence, we may be able to gain those same souls for God with his grace cooperating with us.
¶107
CONSTITUTION XVII (Against Assassins)
Cum igitur illi qui sic horrenda inhumanitate detestanda que saevitia mortem sitiunt aliorum ut ipsos faciant per assasinos occidi non solum corporum sed mortem procurent etiam animarum nisi eas exuberans gratia divina praevenerit ut sint armis spiritualibus praemuniti ac omnis potestas tribuatur a domino ad iustitiam rectum que iudicium exercendum nos tanto periculo volentes occurrere animarum et tam nefarios praesumptores ecclesiasticae animadversionis mucrone ferire ut metus poenae meta huiusmodi praesumptionis exsistat praesertim cum nonnulli magnates taliter perimi formidantes coacti fuerint securitatem ab eorumdem assasinorum domino impetrare sic que. Ab eo non absque christianae dignitatis opprobrio redimere quodammodo vitam suam sacri concilii approbatione statuimus ut quicumque princeps praelatus seu quaelibet alia ecclesiastica seu saecularis persona quempiam christianorum per praedictos assasinos interfici fecerit vel etiam mandaverit quamquam mors ex hoc forsitan non prosequatur aut eos receptaverit vel defenderit seu occultaverit excommunicationis et depositionis a dignitate honore ordine officio et beneficio incurrat sententias ipso facto et ista libere aliis per illos ad quos illorum collatio pertinet conferantur.
Since therefore those who thus thirst for the death of others with such horrendous inhumanity and detestable savagery that they cause them to be killed by assassins, procure the death not only of bodies but also of souls, unless exuberant divine grace shall have prevented them so that they be pre-armed with spiritual arms, and all power is granted by the Lord for exercising justice and right judgment, we, wishing to meet so great a peril of souls and to strike such nefarious presumptors with the sword of ecclesiastical animadversion so that the fear of penalty may be a limit of this kind of presumption—especially since not a few magnates, fearing to be thus killed, have been compelled to obtain security from the lord of the same assassins and thus, not without the opprobrium of Christian dignity, to redeem their life in a certain way from him—by the approval of the sacred council decree that whatever prince, prelate, or any ecclesiastical or secular person shall have caused any Christian to be killed by the aforesaid assassins or shall have even ordered [it], although death may not perhaps result from this, or shall have received or defended or concealed them, incur the sentences of excommunication and deposition from dignity, honor, order, office, and benefice ipso facto, and those [things] be freely conferred upon others by those to whom their conferral pertains.
¶108
Sit etiam cum suis bonis mundanis omnibus tamquam christianae religionis aemulus a toto christiano populo perpetuo diffidatus et postquam probabilibus constiterit documentis aliquod scelus tam execrabile commisisse nullatenus alia excommunicationis vel depositionis seu diffidationis adversus eum sententia requiratur.
Let him also, as an emulator of the Christian religion, be perpetually diffidated from the whole Christian people with all his worldly goods, and after it shall have been established by probable documents that he has committed any so execrable crime, let no other sentence of excommunication or deposition or diffidation be sought against him.
¶109
CONSTITUTION XVIII (Concerning Excommunication to be Pronounced with a Cause)
Cum medicinalis sit excommunicatio non mortalis disciplinans non eradicans dum tamen is in quem lata fuerit non contemnat caute provideat iudex ecclesiasticus ut in ea ferenda ostendat se prosequi quod corrigentis est et medentis.
Since excommunication is medicinal, not mortal, disciplining, not eradicating—provided nevertheless that he upon whom it is pronounced does not despise [it]—let the ecclesiastical judge carefully provide that in pronouncing it he show that he is pursuing what belongs to one correcting and healing.
¶110
Quisquis ergo excommunicat in scriptis proferat et causam expresse conscribat propter quam excommunicatio proferatur.
Whoever therefore excommunicates, let him set forth in writing and expressly write the cause on account of which the excommunication is pronounced.
¶111
Exemplum vero scripturae huiusmodi teneatur excommunicato tradere infra mensem post diem sententiae si fuerit requisitus.
Let a copy of this writing be held to be handed over to the excommunicated person within a month after the day of the sentence if he shall have been requested.
¶112
Super qua requisitione fieri volumus publicum instrumentum vel literas testimoniales confici sigillo authentico consignatas.
Concerning which request we wish a public instrument or testimonial letters sealed with an authentic seal to be made.
¶113
Si quis autem iudicum huiusmodi constitutionis temerarius violator exsistat per mensem unum ab ingressu ecclesiae et divinis noverit se suspensum.
But if any judge shall exist as a rash violator of this constitution, let him know that he is suspended from the entrance of the church and from divine [offices] for one month.
¶114
Superior vero ad quem recurritur sententiam ipsam sine difficultate relaxans latorem excommunicationis ad expensas et omne interesse condemnet vel alias puniat animadversione condigna ut poena docente discant iudices quam grave sit excommunicationis sententias sine maturitate debita fulminare.
Moreover, the superior to whom recourse is had, relaxing the sentence itself without difficulty, let him condemn the lator of the excommunication in the expenses and all interest, or otherwise punish with condign animadversion, so that, penalty teaching, judges may learn how grave it is to fulminate sentences of excommunication without due maturity.
¶115
Et haec eadem in suspensionis et interdicti sententiis volumus observari.
And we wish these same things to be observed in sentences of suspension and interdict.
¶116
Caveant autem ecclesiarum praelati et iudices universi ne praedictam suspensionis poenam incurrant.
Let all prelates of churches and judges be careful lest they incur the aforesaid penalty of suspension.
¶117
Quod si contigerit eos divina officia prosequi sicut prius irregularitatem non effugiant iuxta canonicas sanctiones super qua nonnisi per summum pontificem poterit dispensari.
But if it shall happen that they pursue divine offices just as before, let them not escape irregularity according to the canonical sanctions, concerning which only the supreme pontiff will be able to dispense.
¶118
CONSTITUTION XIX
Solet a nonnullis in dubium revocari an cum aliquis per superiorem absolvi postulat ad cautelam dum in se latam excommunicationis sententiam asserit esse nullam sine contradictionis obstaculo munus ei debeat absolutionis impendi et an ante absolutionem huiusmodi qui se offert in iudicio probaturum se post appellationem legitimam excommunicatione innodatum vel intolerabilem errorem in sententia fuisse patenter expressum sit in ceteris excepto probationis illius articulo evitandus.
It is accustomed to be called into doubt by some whether, when someone seeks to be absolved by a superior to be on the safe side, while he asserts the sentence of excommunication pronounced against him to be null, the office of absolution ought to be bestowed upon him without the obstacle of contradiction, and whether, before absolution of this kind, he who offers himself in judgment about to prove that after a legitimate appeal he was bound by a sentence of excommunication or that an intolerable error was patently expressed in the sentence, is to be avoided in other matters except the article of that proof.
¶119
In prima igitur dubitatione sic statuimus observandum ut petenti absolutio non negetur quamvis in hoc excommunicator vel adversarius se opponat nisi eum excommunicatum pro manifesta dicat offensa in quo casu terminus octo dierum indulgebitur sic dicenti. Ut si probaverit quod opponit nec relaxetur sententia nisi prius sufficiens praestetur emenda vel competens cautio de parendo si offensa dubia proponatur.
In the first doubt therefore we decree to be observed thus: let absolution not be denied to the one seeking it, although the excommunicator or an adversary oppose himself, unless he says that he is excommunicated for a manifest offense, in which case a term of eight days shall be granted to the one saying thus, so that if he shall have proved what he opposes, the sentence not be relaxed unless first sufficient amends be provided or suitable caution of obeying if the offense is proposed as doubtful.
¶120
In secunda vero quaestione statuimus ut is qui ad probandum admittitur pendente probationis articulo in ceteris quae ut actor in iudiciis acceptaverit interim evitetur extra iudicium vero in officiis postulationibus et electionibus et aliis legitimis actibus nihilominus admittatur.
In the second question, we decree that he who is admitted to prove, pending the article of proof, in other matters which he as actor shall have accepted in judgments, be avoided in the meantime; but outside of judgment, in offices, postulations, elections, and other legitimate acts, let him nonetheless be admitted.
¶121
CONSTITUTION XX
Statuimus ut nullus iudicum participantes cum excommunicatis ab eo in locutione et aliis quibus ligatur participans excommunicatione minori ante commonitionem canonicam excommunicare maiori praesumat salvis constitutionibus contra illos legitime promulgatis qui in crimine praesumunt participare damnato.
We decree that none of the judges, [when] participating with excommunicated persons, presume to excommunicate with a greater [excommunication] the person participating with him in speech and other things by which he is bound with a lesser excommunication, before canonical admonition, saving the constitutions legitimately promulgated against those who presume to participate in a condemned crime.
¶122
Quod si ex locutione et aliis quibus participans labitur in minorem excommunicaturus fortius indurescat poterit iudex post commonitionem canonicam huiusmodi participantes consimili damnare censura.
But if, from speech and other things by which the participant slips into a lesser [excommunication], he who is about to excommunicate shall have become harder, the judge after canonical admonition may be able to condemn participants of this kind with a similar censure.
¶123
Aliter autem in participantes excommunicatio prolata non teneat et proferentes poenam legitimam poterunt formidare.
Otherwise, let an excommunication pronounced against participants not hold, and let those pronouncing be able to fear the legitimate penalty.
¶124
CONSTITUTION XXI
Quia periculosum est episcopis et eorum superioribus propter executionem pontificalis officii quod frequenter incumbit ut in aliquo casu interdicti vel suspensionis incurrant sententiam ipso facto nos deliberatione provida duximus statuendum ut episcopi et alii superiores praelati nullius constitutionis occasione sententiae seu mandati praedictam incurrant sententiam nullatenus ipso iure nisi in ipsis de episcopis et superioribus expressim mentio habeatur.
Because it is perilous for bishops and their superiors that on account of the execution of the pontifical office which frequently lies upon them they incur in any case the sentence of interdict or suspension ipso facto, we, with provident deliberation, decreed to be established that bishops and other superior prelates do not at all incur the aforesaid sentence ipso iure on the occasion of any constitution, sentence, or mandate, unless in the same [constitution] mention be expressly made of bishops and superiors.
¶125
Huic etiam adicimus sanctioni ut illud quod in constitutione solet a nonnullis a nobis hactenus promulgata fuerat constitutum ut cum aliquis se offert in iudicio probaturum post appellationem legitimam excommunicationis se sententia innodatum pendente probationis articulo in iis quae extra iudicium agitantur electionibus postulationibus officiis ac aliis actibus legitimis non debeat evitari ad episcoporum et archiepiscoporum sententias nullatenus extendatur sed illud obtineat in futurum quod olim in talibus actibus exstitit observatum.
To this sanction we also add that what in a constitution was accustomed to be established by some from us hitherto promulgated—that when someone offers himself in judgment about to prove that after a legitimate appeal he was bound by a sentence of excommunication, pending the article of proof, in those things which are agitated outside of judgment—elections, postulations, offices, and other legitimate acts—he ought not to be avoided, [this] be by no means extended to the sentences of bishops and archbishops, but let that obtain in the future which long ago was observed in such acts.
¶126
CONSTITUTION XXII (Concerning the Administration of Prelates and the Alleviation of Debts of Churches)
Cura nos pastoralis sollicitat et hortatur ut lapsis consulamus ecclesiis et ne labantur in posterum provideamus constitutione salubri.
Pastoral care solicits and exhorts us to consult for the fallen churches and to provide lest they fall in the future with a healthful constitution.
¶127
Cum igitur usurarum vorago multas ecclesias paene destruxerit et nonnulli praelati circa solutionem debitorum praesertim a suis praedecessoribus contractorum negligentes inveniantur admodum et remissi ac ad contrahenda maiora debita et obligandas res ecclesiae nimis proni desides etiam in custodiendis rebus inventis malentes in propriam laudem modicum novi facere quam bona custodire dimissa recuperare deperdita restaurare ac resarcire ruinas nos ne de cetero se de administratione minus utili excusare ac in praedecessores sive alios fundere valeant culpam suam praesentis concilii approbatione sancimus ut pontifices abbates decani ceteri que legitimam et communem administrationem gerentes infra unum mensem postquam administrationem adierint intimato prius proximo superiori ut per se. Vel per aliquam personam ecclesiasticam idoneam et fidelem intersit praesentibus que capitulo vel conventu propter hoc specialiter evocatis inventarium rerum administrationis susceptae confici faciant in quo mobilia et immobilia libri chartae instrumenta privilegia ornamenta seu paramenta ecclesiastica et cuncta quae ad instructionem urbani fundi seu rustici pertinent necnon debita ac credita diligentissime conscribantur ut in quo statu ecclesiam vel administrationem susceperint et procedente tempore gubernarint ac in morte vel cessione dimiserint per superiorem si necesse fuerit et eos qui sunt ecclesiarum deputati servitiis liquido cognoscatur.
Since therefore the whirlpool of usury has almost destroyed many churches, and some prelates are found very negligent and remiss concerning the payment of debts—especially those contracted by their predecessors—and too prone to contracting greater debts and obligating the goods of the church, and also slothful in guarding the goods found, preferring to do a little new for their own praise than to keep the goods left, to recover lost things, to restore, and to repair ruins—lest henceforth they be able to excuse themselves from a less useful administration and pour out their fault upon predecessors or others, by the approval of the present council we sanction that pontiffs, abbots, deans, and others bearing legitimate and common administration, within one month after they shall have entered upon the administration, having first notified the nearest superior so that he may be present through himself or through some suitable and faithful ecclesiastical person, and with the chapter or convent specially convoked for this being present, cause an inventory of the goods of the administration undertaken to be made, in which let mobiles and immobiles, books, charters, instruments, privileges, ornaments or ecclesiastical paraments, and all things which pertain to the furnishing of urban or rustic estate, as well as debts and credits, be most diligently written down, so that in what state they received the church or administration, and as time proceeds governed it, and at death or cession left it, may be clearly known by the superior, if necessary, and by those who are deputed for the services of the churches.
¶128
Archiepiscopi vero qui praeter Romanum pontificem superiorem non habent aliquem ex suffraganeis ut personaliter vel per alium ut est expressum superius ac abbates et alii praelati minores exempti unum vicinum episcopum qui nihil iuris in exempta ecclesia sibi vindicet ad id studeant evocare dictum que inventarium tam substituti praelati quam sui collegii necnon et superioris suffraganei seu vicini episcopi ad hoc vocatorum muniatur sigillis in archivis ecclesiae cum cautela debita conservandum et nihilominus inventarii eiusdem transcriptum tam idem institutus quam praelatus ad hoc vocatus penes se habeat simile sigillatum.
Archbishops indeed who have no superior except the Roman pontiff, [shall cause] some of their suffragans so that personally or through another as is expressed above; and abbots and other lesser exempt prelates [shall cause] one neighboring bishop who claims no right for himself in the exempt church; let them take care to call [them], and let the said inventory be fortified with the seals both of the substitute prelates and of their college and also of the superior suffragan or neighboring bishop called for this, to be preserved with due caution in the archives of the church; and nonetheless let both the instituted [prelate] himself and the prelate called for this have a similar sealed transcript of the same inventory.
¶129
Inventa quoque custodiantur fideliter et de ipsis administratio digna geratur et comperta debita de mobilibus ecclesiae si fieri potest cum celeritate solvantur.
Let the found goods also be faithfully guarded and worthy administration be carried on concerning them, and the debts found be paid with celerity from the mobiles of the church, if it can be done.
¶130
Si vero mobilia non sufficiant ad solutionem celerem faciendam omnes proventus in solutionem convertantur debitorum quae usuraria fuerint vel etiam onerosa deductis de ipsis proventibus expensis dumtaxat necessariis praelato collegio que rationabiliter computandis.
But if the mobiles do not suffice for making speedy payment, let all the proceeds be converted into the payment of debts which are usurious or also burdensome, deducted from the same proceeds only the expenses necessarily to be reasonably calculated for the prelate and college.
¶131
Si autem debita non fuerint onerosa vel usuraria tertia pars eorundem proventuum vel maior cum illorum consilio quos ad conficiendum inventarium vocandos diximus pro satisfactione huiusmodi deputentur.
But if the debts are not usurious or burdensome, let a third part of the same proceeds or greater, with the counsel of those whom we said are to be called for making the inventory, be deputed for such satisfaction.
¶132
Porro eiusdem concilii auctoritate firmiter inhibemus ne praedicti personas suas vel ecclesias sibi commissas pro aliis obligent nec pro se vel ipsis ecclesiis contrahant debita quibus possit imminere gravamen.
Moreover, by the authority of the same council we firmly forbid that the aforesaid obligate their persons or the churches entrusted to them for others, nor contract debts for themselves or for the same churches by which a burden may be able to impend.
¶133
Si vero evidens urgeat necessitas vel ecclesiarum rationabilis suadeat utilitas praelati cum superiorum archiepiscopi et abbates exempti cum praedictorum collegiorum que suorum concilio et consensu debita non usuraria si fieri potest nunquam tamen in nundinis vel mercatis publicis contrahant et contractuum litteris debitorum et creditorum nomina et causas quare contrahatur debitum etiam si in utilitatem ecclesiae sit conversum et ad id personas ecclesiasticas vel ecclesias nullatenus volumus obligari.
But if evident necessity urges or reasonable utility of the churches persuades, let prelates contract non-usurious debts with the counsel and consent of their superiors—archbishops, and exempt abbots with the counsel of the aforesaid their colleges—if it can be done, never however at fairs or public markets; and in the letters of the contracts we wish the names of the debtors and creditors and the causes why the debt is contracted, even if it has been converted to the utility of the church, to be expressed; and we wish ecclesiastical persons or churches by no means to be obligated for them.
¶134
Privilegia siquidem ecclesiarum quae securo loco fideliter custodiri mandamus nequaquam pignori obligentur nec etiam res aliae nisi forte pro necessariis et utilibus debitis cum praedicta solemnitate contractis.
The privileges indeed of churches, which we command to be faithfully guarded in a safe place, be by no means pledged, nor other things, unless perhaps for necessary and useful debts contracted with the aforesaid solemnity.
¶135
Ut autem haec salubris constitutio inviolabiliter observetur et fructus appareat quem ex ipsa provenire speramus ordinandum duximus et irrefragabiliter statuendum quod omnes abbates et priores necnon et decani vel praepositi cathedralium seu aliarum ecclesiarum semel saltem in anno in ipsorum collegiis districtam suae administrationis faciant rationem et coram superiore visitante conscripta et consignata huiusmodi ratio fideliter recitetur.
So that this healthful constitution may be inviolably observed and the fruit which we hope will come forth from it may appear, we decreed to be ordered and to be established irrefragably that all abbots and priors and also deans or provosts of cathedral or other churches, at least once a year, make a strict account of their administration in their own colleges, and before a visiting superior let an account of this kind, written and sealed, be faithfully recited.
¶136
Archiepiscopi vero et episcopi statum administrationis bonorum ad mensam propriam pertinentium similiter singulis annis capitulis suis et nihilominus episcopi metropolitanis et metropolitani legatis apostolicae sedis vel aliis quibus fuerit ab eadem sede suarum ecclesiarum visitatio delegata insinuare debita fidelitate procurent.
Archbishops and bishops, likewise, let them take care with due fidelity to intimate the state of the administration of goods pertaining to their own table to their chapters each year, and likewise bishops to metropolitans, and metropolitans to legates of the apostolic see or to others to whom the visitation of their churches shall have been delegated by the same see.
¶137
Computationes vero conscriptae semper in thesauro ecclesiae ad memoriam reserventur ut in computatione annorum sequentium praeteriti temporis et instantis diligens habeatur collatio ex qua superior administrantis diligentiam vel negligentiam comprehendat quam siquidem negligentiam solum deum habens prae oculis hominis amore odio vel timore postpositis tanta et tali animadversione castiget quod nec a deo nec a suo superiori vel sede apostolica mereatur propter hoc recipere ultionem.
Let the written computations always be reserved in the treasury of the church for remembrance, so that in the computation of following years a diligent comparison of the past time and the present may be had, from which the superior may comprehend the diligence or negligence of the administrator; which negligence indeed, having only God before his eyes, the love, hatred, or fear of man having been set aside, let him chastise with such and so great animadversion that he may not deserve on account of this to receive vengeance either from God or from his superior or from the apostolic see.
¶138
Non solum autem a futuris praelatis sed etiam a iam promotis praesentem constitutionem praecipimus observari.
We command the present constitution to be observed not only by future prelates but also by those already promoted.
¶139
CONCERNING THE AID FOR THE EMPIRE OF CONSTANTINOPLE
Arduis mens nostra occupata negotiis curis que distracta diversis inter cetera circa quae attentionis invigilat oculo ad constantinopolitani liberationem imperii suae considerationis aciem specialiter dirigit hanc ardenti desiderio concupiscit erga eam iugi cogitatione versatur et licet apostolica sedes pro ipsa grandis diligentiae studio et multiplicis subventionis remedio ferventer institerit ac diu catholici non sine gravibus laboribus et onerosis sumptibus anxiis que sudoribus et deflenda sanguinis effusione certarint nec tanti auxilii dextera imperium ipsum totaliter de inimicorum iugo potuerit impedientibus peccatis eripere propter quod non immerito dolore turbamur. Quia tamen ecclesiae corpus ex membri causa cari videlicet imperii praefati carentia notam probrosae deformitatis incurreret et sustineret debilitatis dolendae iacturam posset que digne nostrae ac ipsius ecclesiae desidiae imputari si fidelium destitueretur suffragio et relinqueretur hostibus libere opprimendum firma intentione proponimus eidem imperio efficaci et celeri subsidio subvenire ut ecclesia ferventi ad illius exurgente succursum manum que porrigente munitam imperium ipsum de adversariorum dominio erui valeat et reduci auctore domino ad eiusdem corporis unitatem sentiat que post conterentem inimicorum malleum dexteram matris ecclesiae consolantem et post assertionis erroneae caecitatem visum catholicae fidei possessione resumat.
Our mind, occupied with arduous negotiations and distracted by diverse cares, among the other things upon which the eye of attention keeps watch, specially directs the point of its consideration to the liberation of the empire of Constantinople; it desires this with ardent longing, it is turned about it with unceasing thought; and although the apostolic see has fervently insisted for it with the study of great diligence and the remedy of multiple assistance, and catholics have long struggled for it not without grave labors and burdensome expenses and anxious sweats and lamentable effusion of blood, nor has the hand of so great aid been able, sins impeding, to snatch the empire itself totally from the yoke of enemies—on account of which we are not undeservedly disturbed with sorrow—nevertheless, because the body of the church would incur the mark of shameful deformity from the lack of the dear member, namely the aforesaid empire, and would sustain the loss of lamentable debility, and it could be justly imputed to our negligence and to that of the church itself if it should be destitute of the aid of the faithful and left to be freely crushed by enemies, with firm intention we propose to succor the same empire with efficacious and swift aid, so that the church, rising up for its fervent relief and extending its hand strengthened, the empire itself may be able to be rescued from the dominion of adversaries and, the Lord being the author, to be reduced to the unity of the same body, and may feel after the hammer of the enemy crushing [it] the consoling right hand of mother church, and after the blindness of erroneous assertion may resume the sight of catholic faith with possession.
¶140
Ad liberationem autem ipsius eo magis ecclesiarum praelatos alios que viros ecclesiasticos vigiles et intentos exsistere ac opem et operam convenit exhibere quo amplius eiusdem fidei et ecclesiasticae libertatis augmentum quod per liberationem huiusmodi principaliter proveniret procurare tenentur.
For the liberation of it, the more ought prelates of churches and other ecclesiastical men to be watchful and intent and to exhibit aid and work, the more they are held to procure the increase of the same faith and ecclesiastical liberty, which would principally come about through a liberation of this kind.
¶141
Maxime quia dum praedicto subvenitur imperio consequenter subsidium impenditur terrae sanctae.
Especially because while aid is given to the aforesaid empire, aid is consequently imparted to the Holy Land.
¶142
CONCERNING THE SUBSIDY FOR THE EMPIRE
Sane ut festina fiat et utilis imperio praefato subventio ex communi concilii approbatione statuimus ut medietas omnium proventuum tam dignitatum et personatuum quam praebendarum ecclesiasticarum aliorum que beneficiorum ecclesiasticarum personarum illarum quae in ipsis residentiam non faciunt personalem per sex menses ad minus sive unum habeant sive plura eis qui nostris et fratrum nostrorum ac suorum praelatorum immorantur obsequiis aut sunt in peregrinatione vel scholis seu ecclesiarum suarum negotia de ipsorum mandato procurant aut assumpserunt vel assument crucis signaculum in praedictae terrae vel personaliter in eiusdem imperii proficiscentur succursum exceptis et si. Aliqui eorundem exceptorum praeter huiusmodi crucesignatos et proficiscentes de reditibus ecclesiasticis ultra valentiam centum marcarum argenti percipiunt annuatim tertia pars residui ipsius imperii subsidio colligenda per eos qui ad hoc apostolica fuerint ordinati providentia usque ad triennium integre deputentur non obstantibus quibuscumque consuetudinibus vel statutis ecclesiarum seu quibuslibet indulgentiis ipsis ecclesiis vel personis ab apostolica sede concessis iuramento aut quacunque firmitate alia roboratis.
So that swift and useful aid may be made for the aforesaid empire, from the common approval of the council we decree that one half of all the proceeds of dignities and personates and ecclesiastical prebends and other ecclesiastical benefices of those persons who do not make personal residence in them for at least six months—whether they have one or many—[half] of those who remain in the services of us and our brothers and their prelates, or are in pilgrimage or in schools or procure the negotiations of their churches by their mandate, or have assumed or will assume the sign of the cross for the aid of the aforesaid land or will set out personally for the succor of the same empire, excepted, and if any of the same excepted persons—apart from such cross-signed and those setting out—receive annually from ecclesiastical revenues beyond the value of one hundred marks of silver, let a third part of the residue be entirely deputed for the aid of the same empire, to be collected by those who shall have been ordained for this by apostolic providence, for three full years, notwithstanding any customs or statutes of churches or any indulgences granted to those churches or persons by the apostolic see, fortified by oath or any other firmness whatsoever.
¶143
Et si forte super hoc fraudem scienter commiserint sententiam excommunicationis incurrant.
And if perhaps they shall have knowingly committed fraud concerning this, let them incur the sentence of excommunication.
¶144
Nos vero de obventionibus ecclesiae Romanae deducta prius ex eis decima succursu terrae deputanda praedictae decimam pro dicti subventione imperii plenarie tribuemus.
We indeed, from the obventions of the Roman church, a tenth having been first deducted from them to be deputed for the aid of the aforesaid land, will fully grant a tenth for the aid of the said empire.
¶145
CONCERNING THE INDULGENCE FOR THOSE AIDING THE EMPIRE
Porro cum idem iuvatur imperium auxilium praestatur potissime ipsi terrae ac ad recuperationem eius praecipue insistitur dum ad ipsius liberationem imperii laboratur de omnipotentis dei misericordia et beatorum Petri et Pauli apostolorum eius auctoritate confisi ex illa quam nobis licet indignis ligandi atque solvendi contulit potestate omnibus eidem imperio succurrentibus illam suorum peccaminum veniam indulgemus ipsos que illo privilegio ea que volumus immunitate gaudere quae praedictae terrae subvenientibus conceduntur.
Moreover, since while the same empire is aided, aid is principally imparted to the land itself, and it is especially insisted upon for its recovery while labor is undertaken for the liberation of the empire, trusting in the mercy of almighty God and the authority of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, from that power of binding and loosing which he conferred upon us, although unworthy, we grant to all succoring the same empire that pardon of their sins, and we wish them to enjoy that privilege and that immunity, which are granted to those succoring the aforesaid land.
¶146
CONCERNING TESTAMENTS FOR THE HOLY LAND
Perennis obtentu patriae a longis retro temporibus pro redimenda terra quam dei filius aspersione sui sanguinis consecravit universitas filiorum ecclesiae non solum expensas innumeras sed inaestimabilem cruoris affluentiam noscitur effudisse sicut ex eo moesti corde colligimus quod pridem contra fideles pugnantibus impiis accidit in partibus transmarinis.
For the sake of gaining the everlasting fatherland, from long past times for redeeming the land which the Son of God consecrated with the sprinkling of his blood, the whole assembly of the children of the church is known to have poured forth not only innumerable expenses but also inestimable abundance of blood, as we gather with sorrowful heart from the fact that recently in the overseas parts, the impious fighting against the faithful, it has happened.
¶147
Verum cum propter hoc sit in sedis apostolicae voto potissimum ut de ipsius redemptione terrae communis desiderii cito deo propitio proveniat complementum digne providimus ut ad procurandum dei favorem negotio vos nostris litteris excitemus.
But since for this reason it is especially in the vow of the apostolic see that the complement of the common desire concerning the redemption of the same land may soon, with God propitious, come forth, we have worthily provided that we may excite you to the negotiation with our letters for procuring the favor of God.
¶148
Rogamus itaque universos vos et obsecramus in domino Iesu Christo mandantes quatenus singuli vestrum fideles populos vestrae curae commissos in vestris praedicationibus vel quando poenitentiam ipsis iniungitis piis monitis inducatis concessa super hoc prout expedire videritis indulgentia speciali ut in testamentis quae pro tempore fecerint aliquid in terrae sanctae vel imperii Romaniae subsidium pro suorum peccaminum remissione relinquant attentius provisuri ut quod ipsi ad crucifixi reverentiam habendo respectum in pecunia pro huiusmodi subventione dederint in certis locis sub sigillis vestris conservari fideliter et illa quae in rebus aliis ad hoc legata fuerint diligenter in scriptis redigi faciatis.
We therefore ask and beseech all of you in the Lord Jesus Christ, commanding, that each of you, in your preachings or when you enjoin penance upon them, induce the faithful people entrusted to your care with pious admonitions, a special indulgence having been granted concerning this as you shall have seen expedient, that in the testaments which they make for the time they leave something for the aid of the Holy Land or the empire of Romania for the remission of their sins, carefully providing that what they, having regard to the reverence of the Crucified, shall have given in money for such aid, be faithfully preserved in certain places under your seals, and that those things which have been bequeathed for this in other goods you cause to be diligently written down.
¶149
Hoc autem pietatis opus in quo sola causa dei quaeritur et salus fidelium procuratur sic vestra sinceritas promptis prosequatur affectibus ut tandem securi de manu superni iudicis caelestis gloriae praemium expectetis.
Let your sincerity pursue this work of piety, in which only the cause of God is sought and the salvation of the faithful is procured, with such prompt affections that at length, secure, you may expect the reward of heavenly glory from the hand of the supreme Judge.
¶150
CONCERNING THE TARTARS
Christianae religionis cultum longius latius que per orbem diffundi super omnia cupientes inaestimabilis doloris telo transfodimur si quando aliqui sic nostro in hac parte obviant desiderio affectu contrario et effectu quod ipsum cultum delere penitus de terrae superficie omni studio tota que potentia moliuntur.
Desiring above all things that the cultivation of the Christian religion be spread farther and wider through the world, we are transfixed with the dart of inestimable sorrow if sometimes anyone thus opposes our desire in this matter with contrary affection and effect, that they strive with all study and all power utterly to destroy that cultivation from the face of the earth.
¶151
Sane Tartarorum gens impia christianum populum subiugare sibi vel potius perimere appetens collectis iam dudum suarum viribus nationum Poloniam Rusciam Ungariam alias que christianorum regiones ingressa sic in eas depopulatrix insaevit ut gladio eius nec aetati parcente nec sexui sed in omnes indifferenter crudelitate horribili debacchante inaudito ipsas exterminio devastarit ac aliorum regna continuato progressu illa sibi eodem in vagina otiari gladio nesciente incessabili persecutione substernit ut subsequenter in robore fortiores exercitus christianos invadens suam plenius in ipsos possit saevitiam exercere sic que orbato quod absit fidelibus orbe fides exorbitet dum sublatos sibi gemuerit ipsius gentis feritate cultores.
Now the impious race of the Tartars, seeking to subjugate or rather to destroy the Christian people, having already long ago collected the forces of their nations, have entered Poland, Russia, Hungary, and other Christian regions, and have thus raged as a depopulator into them that, the sword of them sparing neither age nor sex but raging indiscriminately against all with horrible cruelty, they have devastated them with unheard-of extermination; and with uninterrupted progress they are subduing to themselves the kingdoms of others with unceasing persecution—the same sword not knowing how to rest—so that afterwards, invading Christian armies stronger in strength, they may more fully exercise their savagery upon them, and thus, the faithful having been bereaved, may faith—which God forbid—stray from the world, when it shall have groaned that the worshipers have been taken away by the savagery of the same race.
¶152
Ne igitur tam detestanda gentis eiusdem intentio proficere valeat sed deficiat auctore deo potius et contrario concludatur eventu ab universis christicolis attenta est consideratione pensandum et procurandum studio diligenti ut sic illius impediatur processus quod nequeat ad ipsos ulterius quantumcumque potenti armato brachio pertransire.
Lest therefore the so detestable intention of the same race be able to profit but rather, with God as author, fail and be concluded with contrary outcome, it must be pondered by all Christians with attentive consideration and procured with diligent study, so that its progress be thus impeded that it may not be able to pass further to them [the Christians] with however powerful armed hand.
¶153
Ideo que sacro suadente concilio universos vos monemus rogamus et hortamur attente mandantes quatenus viam et aditus unde in terram nostram gens ipsa posset ingredi solertissime perscrutantes illos fossatis et muris seu aliis aedificiis aut artificiis prout expedire videritis taliter praemunire curetis quod eiusdem gentis ad vos ingressus patere de facili nequeat.
Therefore, the sacred council persuading, we admonish, ask, and exhort all of you attentively, commanding, that most diligently searching out the way and entrances by which the same race might be able to enter our land, you take care so to fortify them with ditches and walls or other buildings or contrivances as you shall have seen expedient, that the entrance of the same race to you may not be able to lie open easily.
¶154
Sed prius apostolicae sedi suus denuntiari possit adventus ut ea vobis fidelium destinante succursum contra conatus et insultus gentis ipsius tuti esse adiutore domino valeatis.
But let his arrival be able to be announced first to the apostolic see so that, she sending to you the succor of the faithful, you may be able, the Lord as helper, to be safe against the attempts and onsets of the same race.
¶155
Nos enim in tam necessariis et utilibus expensis quas ob id feceritis contribuemus magnifice ac ab omnibus christianorum regionibus cum per hoc occurratur communibus periculis proportionaliter contribui faciemus et nihilominus super his aliis christifidelibus per quorum partes habere posset aditum gens praedicta litteras praesentibus similes destinamus.
For we will contribute magnificently in the so necessary and useful expenses which you shall have made on account of this, and we will cause contribution to be made proportionally from all Christian regions, since by this common perils are met; and nonetheless we send similar letters concerning these things to other Christian faithful through whose parts the aforesaid race might be able to have entrance.
¶156
CONCERNING THE AID FOR THE HOLY LAND
Afflicti corde pro deplorandis terrae sanctae periculis sed pro illis praecipue quae constitutis in ipsa fidelibus noscuntur noviter accidisse ad liberandum ipsam deo propitio de impiorum manibus totis affectibus aspiramus diffinientes sacro approbante concilio ut ita crucesignati se praeparent quod opportuno tempore universis insinuando fidelibus per praedicatores et nostros nuntios speciales omnes qui disposuerint transfretare in locis idoneis ad hoc conveniant.
Afflicted in heart for the deplorable perils of the Holy Land, but especially for those which are known recently to have happened to the faithful established in it, we aspire with all our affections, God propitious, to liberate it from the hands of the impious, defining, the sacred council approving, that the cross-signed so prepare themselves that at the opportune time, to be made known to all the faithful through preachers and our special nuncios, all who have decided to cross the sea convene in suitable places for this.
¶157
De quibus in eiusdem terrae subsidium cum divina et apostolica benedictione procedant.
Concerning whom, into the aid of the same land, let them proceed with divine and apostolic blessing.
¶158
Sacerdotes autem et alii clerici qui fuerint in exercitu christiano tam subditi quam praelati orationi et exhortationi diligenter insistant docentes eos verbo pariter et exemplo ut timorem et amorem domini semper habeant ante oculos ne quid dicant aut faciant quod aeterni regis maiestatem offendat.
Priests indeed and other clerics who shall have been in the Christian army, both subjects and prelates, let them diligently insist upon prayer and exhortation, teaching them equally by word and example that they always have the fear and love of the Lord before their eyes, lest they say or do anything that may offend the majesty of the eternal King.
¶159
Et si quando in peccatum lapsi fuerint per veram poenitentiam mox resurgant gerentes humilitatem cordis et corporis et tam in victu quam in vestitu mediocritatem servantes dissensiones et aemulationes omnino vitando rancore ac livore a se penitus relegatis ut sic spiritualibus et materialibus armis muniti adversus hostes fidei securius praelientur non de sua praesumentes potentia sed de divina virtute sperantes.
And if at any time they shall have lapsed into sin, let them rise again immediately through true penance, bearing humility of heart and body, and observing mediocrity both in food and in clothing, utterly avoiding dissensions and emulations, rancor and envy having been completely relegated from them, so that thus fortified with spiritual and material arms they may more securely fight against the enemies of the faith, not presuming upon their own power but hoping in divine virtue.
¶160
Nobiles quidem et potentes exercitus ac omnes divitiis abundantes piis praelatorum monitis inducantur ut intuitu crucifixi pro quo crucis signaculum assumpserunt ab expensis inutilibus et superfluis sed ab illis praecipue quae fiunt in comessationibus et conviviis abstinentes eas commutent in personarum illarum subsidium per quas dei negotium valeat prosperari et eis propter hoc iuxta praelatorum ipsorum providentiam peccatorum suorum indulgentia tribuatur.
Let the nobles indeed and the powerful of the army and all abounding in riches be induced by the pious admonitions of prelates that, for the sake of the Crucified for whom they have assumed the sign of the cross, abstaining from useless and superfluous expenses but especially from those which are made in banquets and feasts, they change them into the aid of those persons through whom the negotiation of God may be able to prosper, and on account of this, according to the providence of the same prelates, indulgence of their sins be granted to them.
¶161
Praedictis autem clericis indulgemus ut beneficia sua integre percipiant per triennium ac essent in ecclesiis residentes et si necesse fuerit ea per idem tempus valeant pignori obligare.
To the aforesaid clerics we grant that they receive their benefices integrally for three years, even if they were residing in the churches, and if necessary they may be able to pledge them for the same time.
¶162
Ne igitur hoc sanctum propositum impediri vel retardari contingat universis ecclesiarum praelatis districte praecipimus ut singuli per loca sua illos qui signum crucis deposuerunt resumere ac tam ipsos quam alios crucesignatos et quos adhuc signari contigerit ad reddendum domino vota sua diligentius moneant et inducant et si necesse fuerit per excommunicationis in personas et interdicti sententias in terras ipsorum omni tergiversatione cessante compellant.
Lest therefore this holy purpose happen to be impeded or retarded, we strictly command all prelates of churches that each in his place more diligently admonish and induce those who have laid down the sign of the cross to take it up again, and both them and other cross-signed and those who shall happen to be signed still, to render their vows to the Lord, and if necessary compel them by sentences of excommunication against persons and of interdict against their lands, all tergiversation ceasing.
¶163
CONCERNING THE PREACHING OF THE CROSS
Ad haec ne quid in negotio Iesu Christi de contingentibus omittatur volumus et mandamus ut patriarchae archiepiscopi episcopi abbates et alii qui curam obtinent animarum studiose proponant commissis sibi populis verbum crucis obsecrantes per patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum unum solum verum aeternum deum reges duces principes marchiones comites et barones alios que magnates necnon communia civitatum villarum et oppidorum ut qui personaliter non accesserint in subsidium terrae sanctae competentem conferant numerum bellatorum cum expensis ad triennium necessariis secundum proprias facultates in remissionem suorum peccaminum prout in generalibus litteris quas pridem per orbem terrae misimus est expressum et ad maiorem cautelam inferius etiam exprimetur.
To these things, lest anything concerning the negotiation of Jesus Christ be omitted, we wish and command that patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, abbots, and others who hold the care of souls studiously propose the word of the cross to the peoples entrusted to them, beseeching them by the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, the one true eternal God—kings, dukes, princes, margraves, counts, and barons, and other magnates, and also the communes of cities, villages, and towns—that those who do not go personally in aid of the Holy Land contribute a suitable number of warriors with the expenses necessary for three years according to their own resources, for the remission of their sins, as is expressed in the general letters which we long ago sent through the orb of the earth and, for greater caution, will also be expressed below.
¶164
Huius enim remissionis volumus esse participes non solum eos qui ad hoc naves proprias exhibebunt sed illos etiam qui propter hoc opus naves studuerint fabricare.
For we wish those to be participants of this remission not only who will furnish their own ships for this, but also those who shall have endeavored to build ships on account of this work.
¶165
Renuentibus autem si qui forte tam ingrati fuerint domino deo nostro ex parte apostolica firmiter protestemur ut se sciant super hoc nobis in novissimo districti examinis die coram tremendo iudice responsuros prius tamen considerantes qua scientia qua ve securitate comparere poterunt coram unigenito dei filio Iesu Christo cui omnia dedit pater in manus si ei pro peccatoribus crucifixo servire renuerint in hoc negotio quasi proprie sibi proprio cuius munere vivunt cuius beneficio sustentantur quin etiam cuius sanguine sunt redempti.
To those refusing, however—if any perchance shall be so ungrateful to our Lord God—let us firmly protest on the apostolic part that they know that they will be held to answer to us concerning this on the last day of the strict examination before the tremendous Judge, considering nevertheless first with what knowledge or with what security they will be able to appear before the only-begotten Son of God Jesus Christ, to whom the Father has given all things into his hands, if they shall have refused to serve him crucified for sinners in this negotiation, as if for him properly their own, by whose gift they live, by whose benefit they are sustained, and indeed by whose blood they are redeemed.
¶166
CONCERNING THE TWENTIETH FOR THE HOLY LAND
Ceterum ex communi concilii approbatione statuimus ut omnes omnino clerici tam subditi quam praelati vigesimam ecclesiarum proventuum usque ad triennium integre conferant in subsidium terrae sanctae per manus eorum qui ad hoc apostolica fuerint providentia ordinati quibusdam dumtaxat religiosis exceptis ab hac praestatione merito eximendis illis que similiter qui assumpto vel assumendo crucis signaculo sunt personaliter profecturi.
Moreover, from the common approval of the council we decree that absolutely all clerics, both subjects and prelates, fully confer a twentieth of the proceeds of churches for three full years into the aid of the Holy Land, through the hands of those who shall have been ordained for this by apostolic providence—certain religious only, deservedly to be exempted from this payment, being excepted, and likewise those who, having assumed or about to assume the sign of the cross, are going to set out personally.
¶167
Nos et fratres nostri sanctae Romanae ecclesiae cardinales plenarie decimam persolvemus sciant que se omnes ad hoc fideliter observandum per excommunicationis sententiam obligatos ita quod illi qui super hoc fraudem scienter commiserint sententiam excommunicationis incurrant.
We and our brothers the cardinals of the holy Roman church will fully pay a tenth; and let all know that they are bound to observe this faithfully by a sentence of excommunication, so that those who shall have knowingly committed fraud concerning this incur the sentence of excommunication.
¶168
CONCERNING THE PRIVILEGES OF THE CROSS-SIGNED
Sane quia iusto iudicio coelestis imperatoris obsequiis inhaerentes speciali decet praerogativa gaudere crucesignati a collectis vel talliis aliis que gravaminibus sint immunes quorum personas et bona post crucem assumptam sub beati Petri et nostra protectione suscipimus statuentes ut sub archiepiscoporum episcoporum et omnium praelatorum ecclesiae dei defensione consistant propriis nihilominus protectoribus ad hoc specialiter deputandis ita ut donec de ipsorum reditu vel obitu certissime cognoscatur integra maneant et quieta et si quisquam contra praesumpserit per censuras ecclesiasticas compescatur.
Now, because by the just judgment of the heavenly Emperor it is fitting that those adhering to his services enjoy a special prerogative, let the cross-signed be immune from collections or tolls and other burdens; we receive their persons and goods after the cross has been assumed under the protection of blessed Peter and of us, decreeing that they remain under the defense of archbishops, bishops, and all prelates of the church of God, with their own protectors to be specially deputed for this likewise, so that until it is most certainly known concerning their return or death they remain whole and quiet, and if anyone shall have presumed against this, let him be restrained by ecclesiastical censures.
¶169
CONCERNING USURERS AND JEWS
Si qui vero proficiscentium illuc ad praestandas usuras iuramento tenentur adstricti creditores eorum ut eis remittant praestitum iuramentum et ab usurarum exactione desistant eadem praecipimus districtione compelli.
If any of those setting out there are held bound by an oath to pay usuries, we command by the same distriction that their creditors be compelled that they remit the oath made to them and desist from the exaction of usuries.
¶170
Quod si quisquam creditorum eos ad solutionem coegerit usurarum eum ad restitutionem earum simili cogi animadversione mandamus.
But if any of the creditors shall have compelled them to pay the usuries, we command him to be compelled by a similar animadversion to restore them.
¶171
Iudaeos vero ad remittendas usuras per saecularem compelli praecipimus potestatem et donec illas remiserint ab universis christifidelibus per excommunicationis sententiam eis omnino communio denegetur.
We command Jews indeed to be compelled by secular power to remit usuries, and until they shall have remitted them, let communion be altogether denied to them from all the Christian faithful by a sentence of excommunication.
¶172
Iis qui Iudaeis nequeunt solvere debita in praesenti sic principes saeculares utili dilatione provideant quod post iter arreptum quousque de ipsorum reditu vel obitu cognoscatur usurarum incommoda non incurrant compulsis Iudaeis proventus pignorum quos ipsi interim perceperint in sortem expensis deductis necessariis computare cum huiusmodi beneficium non multum videatur habere dispendii quod solutionem sic prorogat quod debitum non absorbet.
For those who are unable to pay their debts to Jews at present, let secular princes provide with a useful delay that after the journey is undertaken, until it is known concerning their return or death, they not incur the disadvantages of usuries, the Jews being compelled to compute the proceeds of pledges which they themselves shall have received in the meantime into the principal, necessary expenses having been deducted, since this kind of benefit does not seem to have much disadvantage which so postpones payment that it does not absorb the debt.
¶173
CONCERNING THE PUNISHMENT OF NEGLIGENT PRELATES
Porro ecclesiarum praelati qui in exhibenda iustitia crucesignatis et eorum familiis negligentes exstiterint sciant se graviter puniendos.
Moreover, prelates of churches who shall have been negligent in showing justice to the cross-signed and their families, let them know that they will be gravely punished.
¶174

This completes the First Council of Lyons (Concilium Lugdunense I a. 1245) – all constitutions under Pope Innocent IV.
