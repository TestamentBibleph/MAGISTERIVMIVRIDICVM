Volume III

Magisterium Normativum Patristicum Universale Ordinarium Romanum III

Pre-Gregorian Latin Foundations (251–1080 AD)

Focus: attestation of dogmas and development of Latin ecclesiology and Church authority.

Section 1: Early African & Roman Synods

(Dogmatic Foundations: Pelagianism, Scripture Canon, Baptismal Theology)

  • Synod of Carthage 256 AD

    Baptismal controversy; Cyprianic ecclesiology as datum (not endorsed, but formative)

  • Council of Sardica 343 AD

    Appellate jurisdiction of Rome (canons 3–5); primary source for Latin appellate ecclesiology — contested reception is itself a doctrinal datum

  • Synod of Rome 382 AD

    Canon of Scripture; Damasus I; foundational for Latin canonical tradition

  • Council of Hippo 393 AD

    Scripture canon; African conciliar confirmation

  • Council of Carthage 397 AD

    Scripture canon; African-Roman convergence

    Text in this volume: the Code of Canons of the African Church (419), which carries the African canons

  • Council of Carthage 418 AD

    Formal condemnation of Pelagianism; Zosimus confirmation establishes procedural template for Roman doctrinal ratification

    Text in this volume: the Code of Canons of the African Church (419)

  • Second Council of Carthage c. 390 additional text

    Present in the volume’s text but not listed in the plan table.

Section 2: Iberian (Spanish) Councils — Strongest Doctrinal Section

(Trinity, Christology, Creed, Ecclesiastical Discipline)

  • Synod of Elvira c. 305–306 AD

    Earliest Latin canonical legislation; clerical celibacy; disciplinary foundation

  • Council of Toledo III 589 AD

    Visigothic conversion from Arianism; Nicene Creed with Filioque inserted into Spanish liturgy — first formal Western creedal insertion

  • Council of Toledo IV 633 AD

    Isidorian ecclesiology; liturgical uniformity; conciliar governance in the Latin West

  • Council of Toledo XI 675 AD

    Most architecturally complete pre-scholastic Latin Trinitarian confession; precise treatment of processio and relatio

  • Council of Toledo XII 681 AD

    Christological and disciplinary consolidation; reception of Constantinople III into Iberian framework

Section 3: Gallic Councils

(Grace, Predestination, Anti-Semi-Pelagianism)

  • Council of Arles 314 AD text forthcoming

    First major post-persecution Western synod; Donatist schism; Roman appellate involvement

  • Council of Orange II 529 AD

    Definitive Latin canonization of Augustinian grace theology against semi-Pelagianism; Tridentine reception makes this indispensable

  • Council of Quierzy 853 AD

    Moderate Augustinian settlement of the Gottschalk affair; formal Latin adjudication of double predestination and universal atonement questions

Section 4: Italian & Roman Synods — Very Important

(Christology, Papal Independence, Church-State Baseline)

  • Lateran Synod 649 AD text forthcoming

    Highest importance. Two wills and two operations against Monothelitism; convened by Martin I against imperial will — primary datum for papal dogmatic independence from imperial sanction

  • Synod of Rome 963 AD text forthcoming

    Reframed: Documents the nadir of Ottonian domination (deposition of John XII at Otto I's instigation). Not a positive witness to papal authority — use as the baseline condition from which Gregorian reform is a recovery. Contextual rather than doctrinal in function

  • Council of Pavia 1022 AD text forthcoming

    Clerical reform; simony; transitional to Gregorian agenda

Section 5: Carolingian Era

(Filioque, Adoptionism, Image Controversy — With Internal Tensions Flagged)

  • Council of Frankfurt 794 AD text forthcoming

    Condemnation of Adoptionism (Felix of Urgel, Elipandus); Filioque trajectory

    Critical Caveat: Frankfurt also rejected Nicaea II (787) on the basis of a defective Latin translation conflating proskynesis with latreia. Hadrian I had already accepted Nicaea II on Rome's behalf. This Carolingian-Roman asymmetry must be treated explicitly — it cannot be left implicit without weakening the volume's credibility on conciliar reception

  • Council of Aachen 809 AD text forthcoming

    Theological affirmation of Filioque

    Critical Caveat: Leo III refused creedal insertion while affirming the theology — the shields inscription. This establishes the crucial distinction between Filioque as dogmatic truth received by the Latin tradition and Filioque as creedal interpolation resisted even by Rome until Benedict VIII (1014). The Leo III paradox is a primary datum for the sophisticated Latin Filioque defense

Section 6: 11th-Century Reform Synods — Crucial Bridge to Gregorian Reform

(Simony, Clerical Celibacy, Papal Election, Church-State)

  • Synod of Sutri 1046 text forthcoming

    Deposition of three rival claimants; Henry III's intervention; initiates reform papacy

  • Council of Reims 1049 text forthcoming

    Leo IX presides; simony and celibacy; begins active reform papacy

  • Synod of Melfi 1059 text forthcoming

    Norman alliance; papal temporal strategy

  • Roman Synod (Election Decree) 1059 text forthcoming

    Cardinal-only papal election; removes imperial role; foundational for papal independence — the structural precondition of the Gregorian program

  • Synod of Rome 1060 text forthcoming

    Consolidation of reform agenda

Structural Notes for the Volume

On Sardica (343): Restored as a full chapter because its canons on Roman appellate jurisdiction are cited by Zosimus, Celestine I, and Leo I. The contested reception — including later Roman misattribution of Sardican canons to Nicaea — is not a reason to exclude it but is itself a primary datum in the papal authority genealogy that must be addressed honestly.

On Synod of Rome (963): Retained but reframed. Its function is diagnostic: it shows the condition Gregorian reform was responding to, not a positive ecclesiological achievement. Mislabeling it as a source for papal authority is the error corrected here.

On Frankfurt (794) and Aachen (809): Both retained, but both require explicit treatment of their internal tensions. Frankfurt's image-controversy position puts Carolingian conciliarism against Hadrianic Roman reception of Nicaea II. Aachen's Filioque affirmation with Leo III's creedal refusal establishes a theologically productive distinction that your Filioque defense in later volumes depends on.

Grace Theology Arc: Orange II (529) → Quierzy (853) now closes the arc properly. The Gottschalk affair shows the Latin tradition adjudicating Augustinian extremes before scholastic terminology provides resolution. This prevents the impression that Latin grace theology was simply Augustinian maximalism from 529 onward.
