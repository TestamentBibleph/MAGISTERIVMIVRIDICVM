Second Council of Orange

Concilium Arausicanum II

529

Cum ad dedicationem basilicae, quam inlustrissimus praefectus et patricius filius noster Liberius in Arausica ciuitate fidelissima deuotione construxit, deo propitiante et ipso inuitante conuenissimus et de rebus, quae ad ecclesiasticam regulam pertinent, inter nos spiritalis fuisset oborta conlatio, peruenit ad nos esse aliquos, qui de gratia et libero arbitrio per simplicitatem minus cautae et non secundum fidei catholicae regulam sentire uelint.
When to the dedication of the basilica, which the most illustrious prefect and patrician, our son Liberius, in the city of Orange with most faithful devotion built, by God propitious and by him himself inviting we had come together, and concerning those things which pertain to the ecclesiastical rule, among us a spiritual conference had arisen, it came to us that there are some who concerning grace and free will through simplicity less cautiously and not according to the rule of the catholic faith wish to think.
¶1
Vnde id nobis secundum admonitionem et auctoritatem sedis apostolicae iustum ac rationabile uisum est, ut pauca capitula ab apostolica nobis sede transmissa, quae ab antiquis patribus de sanctarum scripturarum uoluminibus in hac praecipua causa collecta sunt, ad docendos eos, qui aliter quam oportet sentiunt, ab omnibus obseruanda proferre et manibus nostris subscribere deberimus.
Wherefore it seemed to us just and reasonable according to the admonition and authority of the apostolic see, that a few chapters transmitted to us from the apostolic see, which from the volumes of the holy Scriptures by the ancient fathers in this principal cause were collected, for the teaching of those who otherwise than it is fitting think, ought by all to be observed to be set forth and by our hands to be subscribed.
¶2
Quibus lectis qui hucusque non sicut oportebat de gratia et libero arbitrio credidit, ad ea, quae fidei catholicae conueniunt, animum suum inclinare non differat.
Which having been read, let him who until now not as it was fitting concerning grace and free will believed, to those things which to the catholic faith are agreeable his mind to incline not delay.
¶3
CANON I
Si quis per offensam praeuaricationis Adae non totum, id est secundum corpus et animam, in deterius dicit hominem commutatum, sed animae libertate inlaesa durante corpus tantummodo corruptioni credit obnoxium, Pelagii errore deceptus aduersatur scripturae dicenti: Anima, quae peccauerit, ipsa morietur, et: Nescitis quoniam cui exhibetis uos seruos ad oboediendum, serui estis eius, cui oboeditis? et: A quo quis superatur, eius et seruus addicitur.
If anyone through the offense of the transgression of Adam not the whole, that is according to body and soul, for the worse says man to have been changed, but with the liberty of the soul remaining unharmed, the body only to corruption believes to be liable, by the error of Pelagius deceived, he opposes the Scripture saying: The soul which shall have sinned, itself shall die, and: Know you not that to whom you present yourselves servants to obey, servants you are of him whom you obey? and: By whom anyone is overcome, of him also a servant he is added.
¶4
CANON II
Si quis soli Adae praeuaricationem suam, non et eius propagini adserit nocuisse aut certe morte tantum corporis, quae poena peccati est, non autem et peccatum, quod mors est animae, per unum hominem in omne genus humanum transisse testatur, iniustitiam Deo dabit contradicens apostolo dicenti: Per unum hominem peccatum intrauit in mundum et per peccatum mors, et ita in omnes homines pertransiit, in quo omnes peccauerunt.
If anyone to Adam alone his transgression, not also to his progeny asserts to have harmed, or certainly by death only of the body, which is the penalty of sin, not also sin, which is death of the soul, through one man into every human kind passed over testifies, injustice to God will give, contradicting the apostle saying: Through one man sin entered into the world and through sin death, and so into all men it passed, in whom all have sinned.
¶5
CANON III
Si quis inuocatione humana gratiam Dei dicit posse conferri, non autem ipsam gratiam facere, ut inuocetur a nobis, contradicit Isiae prophetae uel apostolo idem dicenti: Inuentus sum non quaerentibus me; palam apparui his, qui me non interrogabant.
If anyone by human invocation the grace of God says to be able to be conferred, but not that grace itself makes that it be invoked by us, he contradicts Isaiah the prophet or the apostle saying the same: I was found by those not seeking me; openly I appeared to those who were not asking for me.
¶6
CANON IV
Si quis, ut a peccato purgemur, uoluntatem nostram Deum expectare contendit, non autem, ut etiam purgari uellimus, per sancti Spiritus infusionem et operationem in nos fieri confitetur, resistit ipsi Spiritui Sancto per Salomonem dicenti: Praeparatur uoluntas a Domino, et apostolo salubriter praedicanti: Deus est, qui operatur in uobis et uelle et perficere pro bona uoluntate.
If anyone that from sin we might be cleansed, our will God to await contends, but not that even to be cleansed we might wish, through the Holy Spirit's infusion and operation in us to be done confesses, he resists the Holy Spirit himself through Solomon saying: Prepared is the will by the Lord, and the apostle salubriously preaching: God it is who works in you both to will and to accomplish for the good will.
¶7
CANON V
Si quis sicut augmentum, ita etiam initium fidei ipsumque credulitatis affectum, quo in eum credimus, qui iustificat impium, et ad regenerationem sacri baptismatis peruenimus, non per gratiae donum, id est per inspirationem Spiritus Sancti corrigentem uoluntatem nostram ab infidelitate ad fidem, ab impietate ad pietatem, sed naturaliter nobis inesse dicit, apostolicis dogmatibus aduersarius adprobatur beato Paulo dicente: Confidimus quia, qui coepit in uobis bonum opus, perficiet usque in diem Christi Iesu, et illud: Vobis datum est pro Christo non solum, ut in eum credatis, uerum etiam ut pro illo patiamini, et: Gratia salui facti estis per fidem, et hoc non ex uobis, Dei enim donum est.
If anyone as the increase, so also the beginning of faith and the very affection of belief, by which in him we believe who justifies the impious, and to the regeneration of sacred baptism we come, not through the gift of grace, that is through the inspiration of the Holy Spirit correcting our will from unfaithfulness to faith, from impiety to piety, but naturally in us to be says, to the apostolic dogmas adverse is proven, the blessed Paul saying: I am confident that he who began in you a good work, will perfect it unto the day of Christ Jesus, and that: To you it is given for Christ not only that in him you believe, but also that for him you suffer, and: By grace you have been saved through faith, and this not of yourselves, of God indeed the gift it is.
¶8
Qui enim fidem, qua in Deum credimus, dicunt esse naturalem, omnes eos, qui ab Ecclesia Christi alieni sunt, quodammodo fideles esse definiunt.
He who indeed the faith by which in God we believe says to be natural, all those who from the church of Christ are alien, in some manner faithful defines to be.
¶9
CANON VI
Si quis sine gratia Dei credentibus, uolentibus, desiderantibus, conantibus, laborantibus, orantibus, uigilantibus, studentibus, petentibus, quaerentibus, pulsantibus nobis misericordiam dicit conferri diuinitus, non autem, ut credamus, uelimus uel haec omnia sicut oportet agere ualeamus, per infusionem et inspirationem Sancti Spiritus in nobis fieri confitetur et aut humilitati aut oboedientiae humanae subiungit gratiae adiutorium nec, ut oboedientes et humiles simus, ipsius gratiae donum esse consentit, resistit apostolo dicenti: Quid habes, quod non accepisti? et: Gratia Dei sum id, quod sum.
If anyone without the grace of God to believers, willers, desirers, strivers, laborers, pray-ers, watchers, studiers, seekers, askers, knockers, us mercy says to be conferred divinely, but not that we might believe or will or all these things as it is fitting to act we might be able, through the infusion and inspiration of the Holy Spirit in us to be done confesses, and either to humility or to human obedience he subjects the help of grace, nor that obedient and humble we might be, of grace itself the gift to be assents, he resists the apostle saying: What have you that you did not receive? and: By the grace of God I am what I am.
¶10
CANON VII
Si quis per naturae uigorem bonum aliquid, quod ad salutem pertinet uitae aeternae, cogitare, ut expedit, aut eligere siue salutari, id est euangelicae, praedicationi consentire posse confirmat absque inluminatione et inspiratione Spiritus Sancti, qui dat omnibus suauitatem in consentiendo et credendo ueritati, haeretico fallitur spiritu, non intellegens uocem Dei in euangelio dicentem: Sine me nihil potestis facere, et illud apostoli: Non quod idonei sumus cogitare aliquid a nobis quasi ex nobis, sed sufficientia nostra ex Deo est.
If anyone through the vigor of nature something good which to the salvation of eternal life pertains, to think as it is expedient, or to choose, or to salvation, that is to the evangelical preaching, to consent to be able confirms, without the illumination and inspiration of the Holy Spirit, who gives to all sweetness in consenting and believing the truth, by heretical spirit he is deceived, not understanding the voice of God in the Gospel saying: Without me you can do nothing, and that of the apostle: Not that we are sufficient to think anything from ourselves as from ourselves, but our sufficiency is from God.
¶11
CANON VIII
Si quis alios misericordia, alios uero per liberum arbitrium, quod in omnibus, qui de praeuaricationem primi hominis nati sunt, constat esse uitiarum, ad gratiam baptismi posse uenire contendit, a recta fide probatur alienus.
If anyone others by mercy, others indeed through free will, which in all who from the transgression of the first man are born stands to be vitiated, to the grace of baptism to be able to come contends, from the right faith proven is alien.
¶12
His enim non omnium liberum arbitrium per peccatum primi hominis adserit infirmatum aut certe ita laesum putat, ut tamen quidam ualeant sine reuelatione Dei mysterium salutis aeternae per semet ipsos posse conquirere.
For not of all does he assert the free will through the sin of the first man to be weakened, or certainly so injured thinks that yet certain ones might be able without the revelation of God the mystery of eternal salvation through themselves to be able to seek.
¶13
Quod quam sit contrarium, ipse Dominus probat, qui non aliquos, sed neminem ad se posse uenire testatur, nisi quem Pater attraxerit, sicut et Petro dixit: Beatus es, Simon Bariona, quia caro et sanguis non reuelauit tibi, sed Pater meus, qui in caelis est; et apostolus: Nemo potet dicere Dominum Iesum nisi in Spiritu Sancto.
How much this be contrary, the Lord himself proves, who not some, but no one to himself to be able to come testifies, unless whom the Father shall have drawn, just as also to Peter he said: Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who in the heavens is; and the apostle: No one can say Lord Jesus except in the Holy Spirit.
¶14
DE ADIUTORIO DEI.
CANON IX. Of the help of God.
Diuini est muneris, cum et recte cogitamus et pedes nostros a falsitate et iniustitia continemus; quotiens enim bona agimus, Deus in nobis adque nobiscum, ut operemur, operatur.
Of the divine gift it is, when both rightly we think and our feet from falsehood and injustice we restrain; as often as indeed good things we do, God in us and with us, that we may work, works.
¶15
DE ADIUTORIO DEI.
CANON X. Of the help of God.
Adiutorium Dei etiam renatis ac sanatis semper est implorandum, ut ad finem bonum peruenire uel in bono possint opere perdurare.
The help of God even to the regenerate and healed is always to be implored, that to the good end they might be able to come or in the good work to endure.
¶16
DE OBLIGATIONE VOTORUM.
CANON XI. Of the obligation of vows.
Nemo quicquam Domino recte uouet, nisi ab ipso acceperit, quod uouerit, sicut legitur: Et quae de manu tua accipimus, damus tibi.
No one anything to the Lord rightly vows, unless from him himself he shall have received that which he vows, just as it is read: And what from your hand we receive, we give to you.
¶17
QUALES NOS DILIGAT DEUS.
CANON XII. Of what kind us God loves.
Tales nos amat Deus, quales futuri sumus ipsius dono, non quales nostro merito.
Of such kind us God loves, of what kind future we are by his gift, not of what kind by our merit.
¶18
DE REPARATIONE LIBERI ARBITRII.
CANON XIII. Of the reparation of free will.
Arbitrium uoluntatis in primo homine infirmatum nisi per gratiam baptismi non potest reparari; quod amissum, nisi a quo potuit dari, non potest reddi; unde Veritas ipsa dicit: Si uos Filius liberauerit, tunc uere liberi eritis.
The will's free choice in the first man weakened unless through the grace of baptism cannot be repaired; which having been lost, unless by him by whom it was able to be given, cannot be restored; whence the Truth himself says: If the Son shall have freed you, then truly free you shall be.
¶19
CANON XIV
Nullus miser de quantacumque miseria liberatur, nisi qui Dei misericordia praeuenitur, sicut dicit psalmista: Cito antecepit nos misericordia tua, Domine, et illud: Deus meus, misericordia eius praeueniet me.
No wretched man from however great misery is freed, unless who by the mercy of God is preceded, just as the psalmist says: Quickly anticipated us your mercy, O Lord, and that: My God, his mercy shall anticipate me.
¶20
CANON XV
Ab eo, quod formauit Deus, mutatus est Adam, sed in peius per iniquitatem suam; ab eo, quod operata est iniquitas, mutatur fidelis, sed in melius per gratiam Dei. Illa ergo mutatio fuit praeuaricatoris primi, haec secundum psalmistam mutatio est dexterae Excelsi.
From that which he formed, Adam was changed, but for the worse through his iniquity; from that which iniquity worked, the faithful is changed, but for the better through the grace of God. That therefore was the change of the first transgressor, this according to the psalmist is the change of the right hand of the Most High.
¶21
CANON XVI
Nemo ex eo, quod uidetur habere, glorietur, tamquam non acceperit, aut ideo se putet accepisse, quia littera extrinsecus uel, ut legeretur, apparuit uel, ut audiretur, sonuit.
No one from that which he is seen to have, let him glory, as though he did not receive it, or on that account think himself to have received, because the letter externally either that it might be read appeared or that it might be heard sounded.
¶22
Nam sicut apostolus dicit: Si per legem iustitia, ergo Christus gratis mortuus est.
For just as the apostle says: If through law justice, therefore Christ freely died.
¶23
Ascendens in altum captiuauit captiuitatem, dedit dona hominibus.
Ascending on high he led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men.
¶24
Inde habet quicumque habet; quisquis autem inde se habere negat, aut uere non habet aut id, quod habet, auferetur ab eo.
From there he has whatever anyone has; whoever moreover from there himself to have denies, either truly does not have or that which he has shall be taken away from him.
¶25
DE FORTITUDINE CHRISTIANA.
CANON XVII. Of Christian fortitude.
Fortitudinem gentilium mundana cupiditas, fortitudinem autem Christianorum Dei caritas facit, quae diffusa est in cordibus nostris non per uoluntatis arbitrium, quod est a nobis, sed per Spiritum Sanctum, qui datus est nobis.
The fortitude of the pagans worldly desire makes; the fortitude of Christians indeed the charity of God makes, which is diffused in our hearts not through the will's free choice, which is from us, but through the Holy Spirit, who is given to us.
¶26
CANON XVIII
Debetur merces bonis operibus, si fiant; sed gratia, quae non debetur, praecedit, ut fiant.
Merits are owed to good works, if they be done; but grace, which is not owed, precedes, that they might be done.
¶27
CANON XIX
Natura humana, etiam si in illa integritate, in qua est condita, permanserit, nullo modo se ipsa creatore suo non adiuuante seruaret; unde cum sine Dei gratia salutem non possit custodire, quam accepit, quomodo sine Dei gratia poterit reparare, quod perdidit?
Human nature, even if in that integrity, in which it was created, it had remained, in no way itself by its own creator not helping would have saved itself; whence when without the grace of God salvation it cannot guard which it received, how without the grace of God will it be able to repair what it lost?
¶28
CANON XX
Multa Deus facit in homine bona, quae non facit homo; nulla uero facit homo bona, quae non Deus praestat, ut faciat homo.
Many good things God does in man, which man does not do; no indeed good things does man do, which God not bestows, that man may do.
¶29
DE NATURA ET GRATIA.
CANON XXI. Of nature and grace.
Sicut eis, qui uolentes in lege iustificari et a gratia exciderunt, uerissime dicit apostolus: Si ex lege iustitia est, ergo Christus gratis mortuus est, sic eis, qui gratiam, quam commendat et percipit fides Christi, putant esse naturam, uerissime dicitur: Si per naturam iustitia est, ergo Christus gratis mortuus est. Iam enim erat lex et non iustificabat; iam hic erat et natura et non iustificabat. Ideo Christus non gratis mortuus est, ut et lex per illum impleretur, qui dixit: Non ueni legem soluere, sed adimplere; et natura per Adam perdita per illum repararetur, qui dixit uenisse se quaerere et saluare, quod perierat.
Just as to those who, wishing in the law to be justified and from grace fell away, truly says the apostle: If from law justice is, therefore Christ freely died, so to those who grace, which faith of Christ commends and perceives, think to be nature, truly it is said: If by nature justice is, therefore Christ freely died. For already there was law and did not justify; already here was nature and did not justify. Therefore Christ not freely died, that both the law through him might be fulfilled, who said: I came not to destroy the law but to fulfill, and nature through Adam lost through him might be repaired, who said he came to seek and to save what had perished.
¶30
DE HIS QUAE HOMINUM PROPRIA SUNT.
CANON XXII. Of those things which are of man's own.
Nemo habet de suo nisi mendacium et peccatum; si quid autem habet homo ueritatis adque iustitiae, ab illo fonte est, quem debemus sitire in hac heremo, ut ex eo quasi guttis quibusdam inrorati non deficiamus in uia.
No one has from his own except lie and sin; if indeed anything has man of truth and justice, from that fountain it is, which we ought to thirst for in this desert, that from it as by drops certain bedewed we might not faint in the way.
¶31
DE VOLUNTATE DEI ET HOMINIS.
CANON XXIII. Of the will of God and of man.
Suam uoluntatem homines faciunt, non Dei, quando id agunt, quod Deo displicet; quando autem ita faciunt, quod uolunt, ut diuinae seruiant uoluntati, quamuis uolentes agant, quod agunt, illius tamen uoluntas est, a quo et praeparatur et iubetur, quod uolunt.
Their will men do, not of God, when those things they do which to God are displeasing; when indeed thus they do that which they will, that to the divine will they may serve, although willing they do that which they do, his will nevertheless it is, by whom both is prepared and is commanded that which they will.
¶32
DE PALMITIBUS VITIS.
CANON XXIV. Of the branches of the vine.
Ita sunt in uite palmites, ut uiti nihil conferant, sed inde accipiant unde uiuant; sic quippe uitis est in palmitibus, ut uitale alimentum subministret eis, non sumat ab eis.
Thus are in the vine the branches, that to the vine they might confer nothing, but from it receive whence they might live; thus indeed the vine is in the branches, that vital nourishment it might minister to them, not receive from them.
¶33
Ac per hoc et manentem in se habere Christum et manere in Christo discipulis prodest utrumque, non Christo. Nam praeciso palmite potest de uiua radice alius pullulare; qui autem praecisus est, sine radice non potest uiuere.
And through this, both remaining in himself to have Christ and to remain in Christ to the disciples profits each, not to Christ. For a branch having been cut off, from the living root another can sprout; he who indeed has been cut off, without the root cannot live.
¶34
DE DILECTIONE, QUA DILIGIMUS DEUM.
CANON XXV. Of the love by which we love God.
Prorsus donum Dei est diligere Deum. Ipse ut diligeretur dedit, qui non dilectus diligit. Displicentes amati sumus, ut fieret in nobis unde placeremus. Diffundit enim caritatem in cordibus nostris Spiritus Patris et Filii, quem cum Patre amamus et Filio.
Altogether the gift of God is to love God. He himself that he might be loved gave, who not loved loves. Displeasing we were loved, that made might be in us whence we might please. He diffuses indeed charity in our hearts the Spirit of the Father and of the Son, whom with the Father we love and with the Son.
¶35
Ac sic secundum supra scriptas sanctarum scripturarum sententias uel antiquorum patrum definitiones hoc Deo propitiante et praedicare debemus et credere, quod per peccatum primi hominis ita inclinatum et attenuatum fuerit liberum arbitrium, ut nullus postea aut diligere Deum sicut oportet aut credere in Deum aut operari propter Deum quod bonum est possit, nisi eum gratia misericordiae diuinae praeuenerit.
Thus according to the above-written sentences of the holy Scriptures or the definitions of the ancient fathers, this by God propitious both to preach we ought and to believe, that through the sin of the first man so inclined and weakened has been the free will, that no one afterwards either to love God as it is fitting or to believe in God or to work for God that which is good can, unless the grace of divine mercy shall have preceded him.
¶36
Vnde et in Abel iustum et Noe et Abraham et Isaac et Iacob et omnem antiquorum sanctorum multitudinem illam praeclaram fidem, quam in ipsorum laude praedicat apostolus Paulus, non per bonum naturae, quod prius in Adam datum fuerat, sed per gratiam Dei credimus fuisse conlatam. Quam gratiam etiam post aduentum Domini omnibus, qui baptizari desiderant, non in libero arbitrio habere, sed Christi nouimus simul et credimus largitate conferri, secundum illud, quod iam saepe dictum est et praedicat Paulus apostolus: Vobis donatum est pro Christo non solum, ut in eum credatis, sed etiam, ut pro eo patiamini, et illud: Deus, qui coepit in uobis bonum opus, perficiet usque in diem Domini nostri, et illud: Gratia salui facti estis per fidem et hoc non ex uobis; Dei enim donum est, et quod de se ipso ait apostolus: Misericordiam consecutus sum, ut fidelis essem (non dixit: quia eram, sed: ut essem), et illud: Quid habes, quod non accepisti? et illud: Omne datum bonum et omne donum perfectum desursum est descendens a Patre luminum, et illud: Nemo habet quicquam, nisi illi datum fuerit desuper.
Whence also in Abel the just and Noah and Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the multitude of the ancient saints that illustrious faith, which in their praise preaches the apostle Paul, not through the good of nature, which previously in Adam had been given, but through the grace of God we believe to have been conferred. Which grace even after the coming of the Lord to all who are baptized to desire, not in free will to have, but by Christ's we know both and believe largeness to be conferred, according to that which already often has been said and preaches Paul the apostle: To you it is given for Christ not only that in him you believe, but also that for him you suffer, and that: God, who began in you a good work, will perfect it unto the day of our Lord, and that: By grace you have been saved through faith, and this not of yourselves; of God indeed the gift it is, and that concerning himself the apostle said: Mercy I have obtained, that faithful I might be (he did not say: because I was, but: that I might be), and that: What have you that you did not receive? and that: Every good gift and every perfect gift from above is, descending from the Father of lights, and that: No one has anything, unless it shall have been given to him from above.
¶37
Innumerabilia sunt sanctarum scripturarum testimonia, quae possint ad probandam gratiam proferri, sed breuitatis studio praetermissa sunt, quia et reuera, cui pauca non sufficiunt, plura non proderunt.
Numberless are the testimonies of the holy Scriptures which could be brought forth for proving grace, but by the zeal for brevity they have been passed over, because and indeed, to whom few things do not suffice, many things will not profit.
¶38
Hoc etiam secundum fidem catholicam credimus, quod post acceptam per baptismum gratiam omnis baptizatus, Christo auxiliante et cooperante, quae ad salutem animae pertinent, possint et debeant, si fideliter laborare uoluerint, adimplere.
This also according to the catholic faith we believe, that after grace received through baptism, every baptized person, Christ helping and cooperating, those things which to the salvation of the soul pertain, can and ought, if faithfully to labor they shall have willed, to fulfill.
¶39
Aliquos uero ad malum diuina potestate praedestinatos esse non solum non credimus, sed etiam, si sunt, qui tantum mali credere uelint, cum omni detestatione illis anathema dicimus.
That some indeed to evil by divine power to have been predestined not only we do not believe, but also, if there are those who so much evil to believe wish, with every detestation to them anathema we say.
¶40
Hoc etiam salubriter profitemur et credimus, quod in omni opere bono non nos incipimus et postea per Dei misericordiam adiuuamur, sed ipse nobis nullis praecedentibus bonis meritis et fidem et amorem sui prius inspirat, ut et baptismi sacramenta fideliter requiramus et post baptismum cum ipsius adiutorio ea, quae sibi sunt placita, implere possimus.
This also salubriously we profess and believe, that in every good work not we begin and afterwards through the mercy of God we are helped, but he himself to us, no preceding good merits, both faith and love of himself first inspires, that both the sacraments of baptism faithfully we might seek and after baptism with his help those things which to himself are pleasing we might be able to fulfill.
¶41
Vnde manifestissime credendum est, quod et illius latronis, quem Dominus ad paradisi patriam reuocauit, et Cornelii centurionis, ad quem angelus Domini missus est, et Zacchaei, qui ipsum Dominum suscipere meruit, illa tam admirabilis fides non fuerit de natura, sed diuinae gratiae largitate donata.
Whence most manifestly it is to be believed, that also of that thief, whom the Lord to the fatherland of paradise recalled, and of Cornelius the centurion, to whom the angel of the Lord was sent, and of Zacchaeus, who the Lord himself to receive merited, that so admirable faith was not of nature, but by the divine grace's largeness was given.
¶42
Et quia definitionem nostram, quae supra scripta est, non solum religiosis, sed etiam laicis medicamentum esse et desideramus et cupimus, placuit, ut eam etiam inlustres ac magnifici uiri, qui nobiscum ad praefatam festiuitatem conuenerant, propria manu subscriberent.
And because our definition, which above is written, not only to the religious but even to the laymen a medicine to be both we desire and we wish, it pleased that it even by illustrious and magnificent men, who with us to the aforesaid festivity had come together, by their own hand be subscribed.

Caesarius in the name of Christ the bishop a copy of our constitution I have given forth and the authentic one in the archive of the church I have preserved.

¶67
In hoc loco continetur synodus Arausica, quam per auctoritatem sanctus papa Bonifatius confirmauit.
In this place is contained the synod of Orange, which by the authority the holy pope Boniface confirmed.
¶68
Et ideo quicumque aliter de gratia et libero arbitrio crediderit, quam uel ista auctoritas continet, uel in illa synodo constitutum est, contrarium se sedi apostolicae et uniuersae per totum mundum ecclesiae esse cognoscat.
And therefore whoever otherwise concerning grace and free will shall have believed, than either that authority contains, or in that synod was established, contrary himself to the apostolic see and to the universal through the whole world church to be let him know.
¶69
Continentur etiam in hoc codice sanctorum antiquorum patrum sententiae.
There are contained also in this code the sentences of the holy ancient fathers.
¶70
Et quamuis synodus Arausica prius sit facta quam auctoritas ista, tamen pro reuerentia sedis apostolicae hoc mihi iustum uisum est, ut prius domini papae auctoritas scriberetur. Et ne forte aliquis dicat quod insinuatio gratiae rudis sit ac nouella, et a nullo alio tam deliberate et tam frequenter quomodo a sancto Augustino episcopo commendata est, quid exinde Innocentius papa urbis Romae in sede apostolica constitutus praedicauerit, quid etiam sanctus Ambrosius Mediolanensis episcopus senserit, quid sanctus Hieronymus presbyter insinuauerit, aliqua ex ipsorum libris capitula adicienda credidimus; ut qui forte hucusque plus quam oportuit incaute et simpliciter credidit, ad tantorum patrum regulam animum suum inclinare et adcommodare non dubitet.
And although the synod of Orange was done earlier than that authority, nevertheless for the reverence of the apostolic see this just to me seemed, that first the lord pope's authority should be written. And lest perhaps someone might say that the insinuation of grace is rude and new, and by no other so deliberately and so frequently as by the holy Augustine the bishop it has been commended, what on this account Innocent the pope of the city of Rome in the apostolic see constituted preached, what also the holy Ambrose the bishop of Milan thought, what the holy Jerome the presbyter insinuated, some chapters from their books to be added we believed; so that whoever perhaps until now more than it was fitting incautiously and simply believed, to the rule of so great fathers his mind to incline and to accommodate not doubt.
¶71
Sanctus Augustinus inquit: Venerabilis [inquit] Innocentii episcopi de hac re ista est sententia ad Carthaginiense concilium data: "Liberum enim, inquit, arbitrium olim ille perpessus est, qui, dum suis inconsultius utitur bonis, cadens in praeuaricationis profunda dimersus nihil quemadmodum exinde surgere possit inuenit; suaque in aeternum libertate deceptus, huius ruinae latuisset oppressu, nisi eum post Christi pro sua gratia releuasset aduentus, qui per nouam regenerationis purificationem omne praeteritum uitium sui baptismatis lauacro purgasset."
The holy Augustine says: Of the venerable Innocent the bishop on this matter this is the sentence to the Carthaginian council given: "Free will indeed he long since suffered, who, while his own goods too incautiously he used, falling into the depths of transgression sunk down, nothing how from there he might be able to rise he found; and by his own freedom unto eternity deceived, by the weight of this ruin he would have lain hidden, unless him after Christ by his grace had raised the coming, who through the new regeneration's purification all former vice by the laver of his baptism had purged."
¶72
Quid ista sedis apostolicae sententia clarius atque manifestius? Huic se Caelestius Pelagii discipulus consentire professus est, dicens: "Damno omnia secundum sententiam antecessoris tui Innocentii."
What clearer and more manifest than this sentence of the apostolic see? To this Celestius the disciple of Pelagius asserted himself to consent, saying: "I condemn all things according to the sentence of your predecessor Innocent."
¶73
Nam quod fidem non de libero arbitrio sed de Dei gratia habeamus, probat sanctus Ambrosius, dicens in hymno paschali: Fidem refundens perditis, / caecosque uisu inluminans. / Fidem, utique, quam Adam peccando fuderat, Christus miserando refundit.
For that faith not from free will but from the grace of God we have, proves the holy Ambrose, saying in the Easter hymn: Faith pouring back to the lost, / the blind with sight enlightening. Faith, indeed, which Adam sinning poured out, Christ pitying poured back.
¶74
Item ait sanctus Augustinus: Pelagiani dicunt ab homine incipere meritum, cui Deus subsequens gratiae retribuit augmentum. Etiam hic eos repellit uenerandus Ambrosius, dicens "quod humana cura sine diuina ope inbecilla est ad medendum, Deum adiutorem requirit."
Likewise says the holy Augustine: The Pelagians say that by man begins the merit, to which God subsequent the grace's increase repays. Also here them repels the venerable Ambrose, saying "that human care without divine help is weak for healing, the helper God requires."
¶75
Item in libro qui inscribitur de Fuga Saeculi: "Frequens nobis, inquit, de fugiendo saeculo est sermo; atque utinam, quam facilis sermo, tam cautus et sollicitus effectus. Sed, quod peius est, frequenter inrepit terrenarum inlecebra cupiditatum, et uanitatum offusio mentem occupat, ut quod studeas uitare, hoc cogites animoque uoluas. Quod cauere difficile est homini, exuere autem inpossibile est. Denique uoti magis eam esse rem quam effectus, testatur propheta dicendo: 'Inclina cor meum in testimonia tua, et non in auaritiam.' Non enim est in potestate nostra cor nostrum et nostrae cogitationes, quae inproviso mentem animumque confundunt atque alio trahunt quam tu proposueris, ad saecularia reuocant, mundana inserunt, uoluptaria ingerunt, inlecebrosa intexunt, ipsoque in tempore, quo eleuare mentem paramus, inserti inanibus cogitationibus ad terrena plerumque deicimur. Quis autem tam beatus, qui in corde suo semper ascendat? sed hoc sine auxilio diuino quemadmodum fieri potest? nullo profecto modo. Denique supra eadem scriptura dicit: 'Beatus uir cui est auxilium abs te, Domine; ascensus in corde eius disposuit.'"
Likewise in the book which is inscribed "On the Fleeing the World": "Frequent to us, he says, of fleeing the world is the discourse; and would that, as easy the discourse, so cautious and solicitous the effect. But, which is worse, frequently creeps in of earthly desires the allurement, and of vanities the suffusion occupies the mind, so that what you study to avoid, this you think and in mind revolve. Which to avoid is difficult to man, to put off however impossible. Finally, of a vow rather that thing is than of an effect, the prophet testifies saying: 'Incline my heart into your testimonies, and not to covetousness.' For not it is in our power our heart and our thoughts, which unexpectedly the mind and spirit confound and elsewhere draw than you have proposed, to worldly things call back, worldly things insert, voluptuous things bring in, alluring things weave, and in that same time, in which the mind to lift up we prepare, inserted into empty thoughts to earthly things for the most part we are cast down. Who moreover so blessed, who in his heart always ascends? But this without divine help how can it be done? By no means. Finally, above the same Scripture says: 'Blessed the man to whom is help from you, O Lord; ascents in his heart he has disposed.'"
¶76
Quid apertius et sufficientius dici potest? Sed ne Pelagiani forte respondeant, eo ipso quod diuinum auxilium poscitur, praecedere hominis meritum, quo orando sit dignus cui gratia diuina subueniat, attendant quid idem sanctus uir dicat in expositione Isaiae: "Et orare Deum, inquit, gratia spiritalis est; 'nemo enim dicit Dominum Iesum, nisi in Spiritu Sancto.'" Vnde exponens Euangelium secundum Lucam: "Vides utique quod ubique Domini uirtus studio cooperatur humano, ut nemo possit aedificare sine Domino, nemo quisquam incipere sine Domino."
What more open and sufficient can be said? But lest the Pelagians perhaps might respond, that by that very thing that divine help is sought, to precede the merit of man, by which praying he might be worthy to whom divine grace might succor, let them attend what the same holy man says in the exposition of Isaiah: "And to pray God, he says, it is grace spiritual; 'no one indeed says Lord Jesus, except in the Holy Spirit.'" Whence expounding the Gospel according to Luke: "You see indeed that everywhere the Lord's power with human study cooperates, that no one can build without the Lord, no one can begin without the Lord."
¶77
Numquid quoniam ista dicit sanctus Ambrosius, et gratiam Dei sicut uir promissionis grata pietate commendat, ideo destruit liberum arbitrium? aut eam uult intellegi gratiam, quam diuersis locutionibus Pelagiani nolunt nisi legem uideri, ut uidelicet non ad faciendum quod cognouerimus, sed ad cognoscendum quid faciamus, nos adiuuare Deus credatur? Si hoc istum hominem Dei sapere existimant, quid de ipsa lege dixerit audiant. In libro de Fuga Saeculi: "Lex, inquit, omnium os potuit obruere, non potuit mentem conuertere." Item alio loco in eodem libro: "Lex, inquit, factum damnat, non aufert malitiam."
Does it follow because these things says the holy Ambrose, both the grace of God as a man of promise with gracious piety commends, that therefore he destroys free will? Or does he wish to be understood grace, which by various locutions the Pelagians are unwilling except law to be seen, that clearly not for doing what we have known, but for knowing what we might do, God to help us is believed? If this the man of God to think they suppose, what of the law itself he said let them hear. In the book "On the Fleeing the World": "The law, he says, of all the mouth could shut, could not the mind convert." Likewise elsewhere in the same book: "The law, he says, the deed condemns, does not take away malice."
¶78
Videant fidelem et catholicum uirum apostolo consentire dicenti: "Scimus autem quoniam quaecumque lex loquitur, his qui in lege sunt loquitur, ut omne os obstruatur, et subditus fiat omnis mundus Deo, quoniam non iustificabitur ex lege omnis caro coram illo." Ex hoc enim apostolico sensu illa sumpsit sanctus Ambrosius.
Let them see the faithful and catholic man to the apostle consenting saying: "We know indeed that whatever the law speaks, to those who are in the law it speaks, that every mouth might be shut, and made subject might be all the world to God, because not shall be justified from the law all flesh before him." From this apostolic sense took that the holy Ambrose.
¶79
Ait sanctus Hieronymus in expositione psalmi LXXXVIII: Qui sustinet tempestatem, uel petrae uel tecti petit refugium. Quem hostis persequitur, ad muros urbium confugit. Fessus uiator tam sole quam puluere, umbrae quaerit solacium. Si saeuissima bestia hominis sanguinem sitiat, cupit, utcumque potuerit, praesens uitare discrimen. Ita et homo a principio conditionis suae Deo utitur adiutore; et cum illius sit gratiae quod creatus sit, illiusque misericordiae quod subsistit et uiuit, nihil boni operis agere potest absque eo, qui ita liberum concessit arbitrium, ut suam per singula opera gratiam non negaret, et ne libertas arbitrii redundet ad iniuriam conditoris, et ad eius contumaciam, qui ideo liber conditus est, ut absque Deo nihil esse se nouerit.
The holy Jerome says in the exposition of Psalm 88: He who endures the storm, either of a rock or of a roof seeks refuge. Whom the enemy pursues, to the walls of the cities he flees. A traveler wearied by both sun and dust, of shade seeks solace. If a most savage beast the blood of man thirsts for, he wishes, as much as he could in any way, the present danger to avoid. Thus also man from the beginning of his condition God uses as helper; and since it is of his grace that he was created, and of his mercy that he subsists and lives, nothing of good work he can do without him, who so free will granted, that his grace through each work he would not deny, and that the freedom of will might not redound to the injury of the creator, and to his contumacy, who therefore free was created, that without God nothing he might know himself to be.
¶80
Quod autem dicit "in generatione et generatione" omnia significat tempora: et ante legem et in lege et in euangelio. Vnde et apostolus dicit: "Gratia salui facti estis per fidem, et hoc non ex uobis, Dei donum est." Omnes epistulae eius in salutationis principio non prius pacem habent, et sic gratiam; sed ante gratiam, et sic pacem, ut donatis nobis peccatis nostris pacem Domini consequamur.
What indeed he says "in generation and generation" all times signifies: both before the law and in the law and in the Gospel. Whence also the apostle says: "By grace you have been saved through faith, and this not of yourselves, of God the gift it is." All his epistles in the salutation's beginning not first peace have, and thus grace; but before grace, and thus peace, that having been forgiven our sins the peace of the Lord we might obtain.
¶81
Vbi sunt qui liberi arbitrii sibi potestate plaudentes, in eo se putant Dei gratiam consecutos, si habeant potestatem uel faciendi uel non faciendi bona siue mala? Ecce hic beatus Moyses postulat, ut splendor Domini Dei sui sit super eos qui surrexerint, et ipse splendeat in animabus corporibusque sanctorum, et opera manuum eorum ipse dirigat, faciatque esse perpetua, ipse confirmet quidquid in sanctis uidetur bonum.
Where are they who of free will's power to themselves applauding, in this they think themselves the grace of God to have obtained, if they have the power either of doing or of not doing good or evil things? Behold here blessed Moses asks that the splendor of the Lord his God might be upon those who have risen up, and himself might shine in the souls and bodies of the saints, and the work of their hands himself might direct, and might cause to be perpetual, himself might confirm whatever in the saints is seen good.
¶82
Item dicit idem Hieronymus in libro quem de nomine Attici et Critoboli titulauit: Quod autem rursum iactitant liberum a nobis arbitrium destrui, audiant e contrario eos arbitrii destruere libertatem, qui male eo abutuntur aduersum beneficium largitoris. Et quis destruit arbitrium? Ille qui semper agit Deo gratias, et quodcumque in suo fluit riuulo ad fontem refert; an qui dicit Deo: "Recede a me, quia mundus sum", non habeo te necessarium, dedisti enim mihi semel arbitrii libertatem, ut faciam quod uoluero; quid rursum te ingeris, ut nihil possim facere nisi tu in me tua dona compleueris? Fraudulenter praetendis Dei gratiam, ut ad condicionem hominis referas, et non in singulis operibus auxilium Dei requiras, ne scilicet liberum arbitrium uidearis amittere; et cum Dei contemnas adminiculum, hominum quaeris auxilium.
Likewise says the same Jerome in the book which by the name of Atticus and Critobolus he titled: That which again they vaunt, that free will by us is destroyed, let them hear on the contrary that those destroy the freedom of the will, who badly use it against the benefit of the giver. And who destroys free will? He who always gives thanks to God, and whatever in his own stream flows to the fountain refers; or he who says to God: "Depart from me, because I am clean," I have you not necessary, you have given indeed to me once the freedom of the will, that I might do what I will; why again do you press yourself in, that nothing I might be able to do unless you in me your gifts might have completed? Fraudulently you pretend the grace of God, that to the condition of man you might refer, and not in each work the help of God you might seek, lest clearly free will you might seem to lose; and when the aid of God you despise, of men you seek help.
¶83
Audite, quaeso, audite sacrilegium: Si, inquit, uoluero curuare digitum, mouere manum, sedere, stare, ambulare, discurrere, sputa iactare, duobus digitulis narium purgamenta decutere, semper mihi auxilium Dei erit necessarium? Audi, ingrate, immo sacrilege, apostolum praedicantem: "Siue manducatis, siue bibitis, siue aliud quid agitis, omnia in nomine Domini agite." Et illud Iacobi: "Age nunc, quid dicitis: hodie et cras proficiscimur in illam ciuitatem, et faciemus illic annum unum, et negotiemur et lucremur, qui nescitis de crastino? quae est enim uita uestra? aura est enim siue uapor paululum apparens, deinde dissipatur. pro eo quod debeatis dicere: si Dominus uoluerit, et si uixerimus, faciemus hoc aut illud. Nunc autem exultatis in superbiis uestris; omnis gloriatio istiusmodi pessima est."
Hear, I pray, hear the sacrilege: If, he says, I shall have willed to bend a finger, to move a hand, to sit, to stand, to walk, to run about, to cast spit, with two little fingers the filth of the nostrils to knock away, always to me the help of God will be necessary? Hear, ungrateful, yes sacrilegious, the apostle preaching: "Whether you eat, whether you drink, whether anything else you do, all things in the name of the Lord do." And that of James: "Go to now, what say you: today and tomorrow we journey into that city, and we will make there one year, and will trade and will profit, you who know not of tomorrow? What indeed is your life? A mist indeed or vapor a little appearing, then dissipating. Instead of that you ought to say: If the Lord shall have willed, and if we shall have lived, we will do this or that. Now indeed you exult in your proud acts; all boasting of this sort evil is."
¶84
Iniuriam tibi fieri putas, et destrui arbitrii libertatem, si ad Deum semper auctorem recurras, si ex illius pendeas uoluntate, et dicas: "Oculi mei semper ad Dominum, quoniam ipse euellit de laqueo pedes meos." Vnde et audes lingua proferre temeraria, unumquemque suo arbitrio regi? Si suo regitur arbitrio, ubi est auxilium Dei? si Christo rectore non indiget, quomodo Hieremias: "Non erit in homine uia eius" et "A Domino gressus hominis diriguntur"?
Do you think an injury to you to be done, and the freedom of the will to be destroyed, if to God always as author you recur, if from his will you depend, and you say: "My eyes are always to the Lord, because he himself draws out from the snare my feet"? Whence also you dare with tongue to speak rash things, that each one by his own will is ruled? If by his own will he is ruled, where is the help of God? If he does not need Christ as ruler, how does Jeremiah: "There shall not be in man his way" and "From the Lord the steps of a man are directed"?
¶85
Item ex libro de nomine Attici et Critoboli: "Dic tu peccata tua, ut iustificeris. Conclusit Deus omnia sub peccato, ut omnibus misereatur." Et haec est hominis summa iustitia, quidquid habere potuerit uirtutis, non suum putare esse, sed Domini qui largitus est.
Likewise out of the book by the name of Atticus and Critobolus: "Say you your sins, that you might be justified. God has concluded all things under sin, that to all he might have mercy." And this is the highest justice of man, whatever he might have been able of virtue, not his own to think it to be, but of the Lord who granted it.
¶86
Item: "Ecce ex hoc me beatam dicent omnes generationes: quia fecit mihi magna qui potens est, et sanctum nomen eius. Et misericordia eius a progenie in progeniem timentibus eum. Fecit potentiam in brachio suo." In quo animaduerte, quod beatam esse se dicat, non proprio merito atque uirtute, sed Dei in se habitantis clementia.
Likewise: "Behold from this blessed me shall call all generations: because he did for me great things who is powerful, and holy his name. And his mercy from generation to generation to those fearing him. He did power in his arm." In which observe, that blessed herself she says to be, not by her own merit and virtue, but by the mercy of God in her dwelling.
¶87
Item post pauca: Illud uero quid in alio loco ponis: omnes uoluntate propria regi, quis Christianorum possit audire? Si enim non unus, nec pauci, nec multi, sed omnes reguntur propria uoluntate, ubi erit auxilium Dei? et quomodo illud exponis: "A Domino gressus hominis diriguntur"? et in alio loco: "Quid enim habes, quod non accepisti? si autem accepisti, quid gloriaris quasi non acceperis?" dicente Domino Saluatore: "Non descendi de caelo ut faciam uoluntatem meam, sed uoluntatem eius qui misit me Patris." Et in alio loco: "Pater, si possibile est, transeat calix iste a me; uerumtamen non quod ego uolo, sed quod tu." Et in oratione Dominica: "Fiat uoluntas tua sicut in caelo et in terra."
Likewise after a little: That indeed what in another place you put: all by their own will to be ruled, what Christian might be able to hear? For if not one, nor few, nor many, but all are ruled by their own will, where will be the help of God? And how do you expound that: "From the Lord the steps of a man are directed"? and in another place: "What indeed have you that you did not receive? If indeed you have received, what glory you as though you did not receive?" the Lord the Savior saying: "I did not descend from heaven that I might do my will, but the will of him who sent me the Father." And in another place: "Father, if it be possible, let pass this cup from me; nevertheless not what I will, but what you will." And in the Lord's Prayer: "Let be done your will as in heaven and on earth."
¶88
Qua sententia et temeritate auferre uis Deo omne praesidium? et quod in alio loco frustra conaris adiungere: "non absque Dei gratia", quomodo sentire uelis, ex hoc loco intellegitur, dum gratiam eius non ad singula referre opera, sed ad condiciones ac leges et liberi arbitrii potestatem.
By what sentence and rashness do you wish to take away from God all protection? And that which in another place you try in vain to add: "not without the grace of God," how you wish to think, from this place is understood, while his grace not to each work you refer, but to conditions and laws and to the power of free will.
¶89
Item illic: Homo cum esset in honore, non intellexit; comparatus est iumentis insipientibus et similis factus est illis. Hic considerandum est, utrum homo qui iumentis similis factus est, aliquid boni aut cogitare aut agere sine Dei gratia possit.
Likewise there: Man when he was in honor, he did not understand; he was compared to senseless beasts and like was made to them. Here it is to be considered, whether man who like to beasts was made, anything good either to think or to do without the grace of God can.
¶90
Dicit ergo iterum sanctus Hieronymus: "Pro nihilio saluos faciet illos": haud dubium quin iustos, qui non proprio merito sed Dei saluantur clementia. Hic considerandum est, quod omnes iusti etiam ante aduentum Christi Dei gratia sint saluati. Vt et sciamus, Dei esse bonum omne quod gerimus: "Ego, ait, plantabo eos, ut nequaquam eradicentur; et ego dabo eis cogitationem et sensum, ut intellegant me." Si cogitatio et sensus datur a Deo, et intellectus Domini ex illius qui noscendus est radice pullulat, ubi est liberi tantum arbitrii superba iactatio?
Therefore says again the holy Jerome: "For nothing he will save them": without doubt the just, who not by their own merit but by God's mercy are saved. Here it is to be considered, that all the just even before the coming of Christ by the grace of God were saved. That we might know, of God to be every good which we do: "I, he says, will plant them, that never they may be rooted up; and I will give to them thought and sense, that they may understand me." If thought and sense are given by God, and the understanding of the Lord from his who is to be known root sprouts, where is of free will alone the proud boasting?
¶91
Item illic: Danihel propheta loquitur ad Nabuchodonosor, quod dominetur Excelsus regni hominum, et det illud cuicumque uoluerit, et extremum atque proiectum constituat super illud. Interroga eum qua causa ultimum atque despectum constituat in regem, et faciat quod uoluerit. Et discute utrum ipsius uoluntas iusta sit, de quo scriptum est: "Qui suscitat de terra humilem, et de stercore exaltat pauperem, ut collocet eum cum principibus, cum principibus populi sui."
Likewise there: Daniel the prophet speaks to Nebuchadnezzar, that the Most High rules over the kingdom of men, and gives it to whom he will, and the last and rejected one he establishes over it. Ask him for what cause the last and despised he establishes as king, and does what he will. And consider whether his will just is, of whom it is written: "Who raises up from the earth the humble, and from the dung heap exalts the poor, that he may place him with princes, with the princes of his people."
¶92
Item post pauca: Dominus ad discipulos suos: "Ego sum uitis, et uos palmites; qui manet in me, et ego in eo, iste affert fructum multum, quia sine me nihil potestis facere." Sicut rami et flagella uitium ilico contabescunt, cum fuerint a matrice praecisi, ita omnis hominum fortitudo marcescit et deperit, si a Dei auxilio deseratur.
Likewise after a little: The Lord to his disciples: "I am the vine, and you the branches; he who remains in me, and I in him, this one bears fruit much, because without me you can do nothing." Just as branches and shoots of vines immediately wither, when they shall have been cut off from the matrix, so all the strength of man withers and perishes, if from the help of God it be deserted.
¶93
"Nemo, inquit, potest uenire ad me, nisi Pater meus attraxerit eum." Quando dicit: nemo potest uenire ad me, frangit superbientem arbitrii libertatem, quo etiam qui uelit ad Christum pergere, nisi fiat illud quod sequitur: nisi Pater meus caelestis attraxerit eum, nequicquam cupiat, frustra nitatur. Simul et hoc animo aduertendum est, quod, qui attrahitur, non sponte currit, sed ut retractans et tardus aut inuitus adducitur.
"No one, he says, can come to me, unless my Father shall have drawn him." When he says: no one can come to me, he breaks the proud freedom of the will, so that even he who might wish to go to Christ, unless that be done which follows: unless my heavenly Father shall have drawn him, in vain he might wish, in vain he might strive. Together also this in mind to be attended to, that he who is drawn, not willingly runs, but as drawing back and slow or unwilling is brought.
¶94
Item illic: O homo, nunc mundus factus es in lauacro, et de te dicitur: "Quae est ista quae ascendit dealbata, innitens super fratruelem suum?" et lota quidem est, sed custodire non ualet puritatem, nisi a suo Domino sustentetur. Et ne aliquis putet, sicut iam supra dictum est, solum sanctum Augustinum Dei gratiam atentius commendasse, et in hoc sensu credat a sancto Hieronymo discordasse, legat titulum infra scriptum, et uideat quomodo in hoc ipso opere sanctum Augustinum beatus Hieronymus conlaudauerit; et cum legerit, discat uno illos spiritu fuisse locutos, et contra Dei gratiam desinat murmurare.
Likewise there: O man, now you have been made clean in the laver, and of you it is said: "Who is she who ascends whitened, leaning upon her cousin?" and washed indeed she is, but she is not able to guard purity, unless by her Lord she be sustained. Lest anyone might think, just as already above has been said, that only holy Augustine the grace of God more attentively commended, and in this sense might believe from holy Jerome to have dissented, let him read the title written below, and let him see how in this same work holy Augustine blessed Jerome praised; and when he shall have read, let him learn by one spirit them to have spoken, and against the grace of God let him cease to murmur.
¶95
TITULUS DE PRAEFATO LIBRO SANCTI HIERONYMI IN LAUDE SANCTI AUGUSTINI EPISCOPI.
The title from the aforesaid book of holy Jerome in praise of holy Augustine the bishop.
Sic enim ait: Scripsit dudum quidem uir sanctus et eloquens episcopus Augustinus ad Marcellinum, qui postea sub inuidia tyrannidis Heracliani ab hereticis innocens caesus est, duos libros de infantibus baptizandis, contra heresim uestram, hoc est Pelagianorum, per quam uultis adscribere, baptizari infantes, non in remissionem peccatorum, sed in regnum caelorum, iuxta quod scriptum est in euangelio: "Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu Sancto, non potest intrare in regnum caelorum." Tertium quoque ad eundem Marcellinum, contra eos qui dicunt, id est uos, posse hominem sine peccato esse, si uelit, absque Dei gratia. Et quartum nuper ad Hilarium, contra doctrinam tuam multa peruersa fingentem. Et alios quoque specialiter tuo nomini cudere dicitur, qui necdum in nostras uenere manus. Vnde supersidendum huic labori censeo, ne dicatur mihi illud Horatii: "In siluam ne ligna feras." Aut enim eadem dicimus ex superfluo, aut si noua uoluerimus dicere, a clarissimo sunt ingenio occupata meliora.
For thus he says: He wrote a while ago indeed a holy and eloquent man, the bishop Augustine, to Marcellinus, who afterwards under the envy of the tyranny of Heraclian by heretics innocent was slain, two books concerning infants to be baptized, against your heresy, that is of the Pelagians, through which you wish to inscribe, infants to be baptized, not into remission of sins, but into the kingdom of heaven, according to that which is written in the Gospel: "Unless anyone shall have been born again of water and the Holy Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of heaven." A third also to the same Marcellinus, against those who say, that is you, that man can be without sin if he wishes, without the grace of God. And a fourth recently to Hilary, against your teaching many perverse things feigning. And others also specially to your name he is said to be striking out, which until now into our hands have not come. Whence to cease from this labor I think, lest be said to me that of Horace: "Into the forest you would not carry wood." For either the same things we say superfluously, or if new things we shall have wished to say, by a most clear genius they have been occupied better.
¶96
Ecce qua laude sanctus Hieronymus beatum Augustinum praedicauit et extulit. Et ad commendandam Dei gratiam talium ac tantorum patrum quae suprascriptae sunt sententiae credimus posse cunctorum animis satis esse.
Behold with what praise holy Jerome blessed Augustine preached and extolled. And for commending the grace of God, of such and so great fathers which above-written are the sentences, we believe to all minds to be able to suffice.
