Twelfth Council of Toledo

Concilium Toletanum XII

681

IN NOMINE DOMINI INCIPIUNT ACTA SYNODALIA CONCILII TOLETANI XII TOLETANA IN URBE REGIA CELEBRATA ANNO I ORTHODOXI ATQUE SERENISSIMI DOMINI NOSTRI ERVIGII REGIS SUB DIE V ID. IANUAR. ERA DCCXVIIII.
IN THE NAME OF THE LORD BEGIN THE SYNODAL ACTS OF THE TWELFTH COUNCIL OF TOLEDO, CELEBRATED IN THE ROYAL CITY OF TOLEDO, IN THE FIRST YEAR OF THE ORTHODOX AND MOST SERENE LORD OUR KING ERVIGIUS, ON THE FIFTH DAY BEFORE THE IDES OF JANUARY, IN THE ERA 719.
¶1
Cum ex glorioso praedicti principis iussu in unum fuissemus aggregati conventum et in basilica sanctorum apostolorum debitis in sedibus locaremur, adfuit coram nobis idem clementissimus princeps humilitatis gratia plenus et claro pietatis cultu perspicuus, qui nostro se coetui reclinem exhibens ac devotum imprimis omnium sacerdotum se committit precibus adiuvandum, deinde grates multiplices omnipotenti domino ait de conventu totius concilii, quia gloriosae iussionis suae, ut in unum adessent, impleverint votum et alternae visionis innovatione se refecerint gaudiorum.
When by the glorious command of the aforesaid prince we had been gathered into one assembly and in the basilica of the holy apostles in due seats we were placed, there came before us the same most clement prince, full of the grace of humility and conspicuous with the clear cultivation of piety, who, showing himself devoted and reclining toward our assembly, first commends himself to be helped by the prayers of all the priests, then he said many thanks to almighty God for the gathering of the whole council, because they had fulfilled the vow of his glorious command that they come together into one, and by the renewal of mutual vision they had refreshed themselves with joys.
¶2
Deinde adiciens sic omne est concilium allocutus: Non dubium est, sanctissimi patres, quod optima conciliorum adiutoria ruenti mundo subveniant, si officiosis, quae corrigenda sunt, studiis peragantur, et ideo quibus plagis pro eventu dierum succedentium pereamus, paternitati vestrae non reor esse incognitum. Obinde quia certum apud nos gerimus, quod pro contemptu divinorum praeceptorum terra perniciem sustineat pressurarum, dicente domino per prophetam: Propter hoc lugebit terra et infirmabitur omnis, qui habitat in ea. Ideo oportet, ut, quia ore salvatoris nostri et domini oracula esse probamini, per vos salvationis obtineat lucrum, per quos regenerationis percepit sacramentum, ut diligentia dignationis vestrae ab omni mundata contagione et ab infirmitatis peste sit libera et bonorum omnium sit proventibus gratiosa.
Then adding, thus he addressed the whole council: There is no doubt, most holy fathers, that the best helps of councils succor the falling world, if they are carried out with the studies proper to those things which are to be corrected. And therefore by what blows on account of the outcome of succeeding days we may perish, I do not think it to be unknown to your paternity. On which account because we hold certain among ourselves that on account of contempt of divine precepts the land sustains destruction of pressures, the Lord saying through the prophet: Therefore the land shall mourn and everyone who dwells in it shall languish. Therefore it is fitting that, because you are proved to be the oracles of our Savior and Lord, through you salvation may obtain gain, through whom it received the sacrament of regeneration, that by the diligence of your dignity, cleansed from all contagion and from the pest of infirmity, it may be free and gracious with the proceeds of all good things.
¶3
Iam nunc, quicquid me adhuc dicere opportuna ratio sinit, quicquid etiam narrandum vestris auribus convenit aut memoriae curarum intercapedo subducit aut fastidium prolixae orationis intercipit, ecce in brevi complexa vel exarata devotionis meae negotia in huius tomo complicationis agnoscenda perlegite, perlecta discutite, discussa elimatis ac decretis titulorum sententiis definite, ut pura et placens deo vestrarum devotionum valitura discretio et regni nostri primordia decoret exhortatione iustitiae et terrores plebium digna cohibeat severitate censurae. Scriptum est enim: Iustitia elevat gentem, miseros facit populos peccatum.
Now already, whatever as yet the opportune reason permits me to say, whatever also is fitting to be narrated to your ears, either the interval of cares withdraws from memory or the distaste of prolix speech intercepts, behold, in brief, the matters of my devotion, both completed and written down in the tome of this compilation, read to be known, having been read through discuss, having been discussed, polished, and by the sentences of the decrees of titles define, that the pure and pleasing to God discretion of your devotions, about to be valid, may both adorn the beginnings of our kingdom with exhortation of justice and with worthy severity of censure may restrain the terrors of the peoples. For it is written: Justice exalts a nation, sin makes peoples miserable.
¶4
Tunc suscepto a glorioso principe hoc tomo pro tam salubri invitamento et copiosas grates retulimus domino Iesu Christo et idem benediximus principi glorioso. Post egressum igitur eiusdem serenissimi principis haec in iamdicto tomo scripta repperimus.
Then, the tome having been received from the glorious prince for so salutary an invitation, we both gave abundant thanks to the Lord Jesus Christ and we blessed the same glorious prince. After the departure therefore of the same most serene prince, we found these things written in the already-mentioned tome.
¶5
IN THE NAME OF THE LORD, FLAVIUS ERVIGIUS THE KING TO THE MOST HOLY FATHERS SITTING IN THIS HOLY SYNOD.
Reverentissimi patres, si patres et honorabiles ministeriorum caelestium sacerdotes, soliditatem sanctae fidei veraciter teneant sincera cordis devotione amplectentur, testimonium paternitatis vestrae fortissimum in salutis nostrae advoco adiumentum, ut, qui regnum fautore deo ad salvationem terrae et sublevationem plebium suscepisse nos credimus, sanctitudinis vestrae consiliis adiuvemur.
Most reverend fathers (if fathers and honorable priests of the heavenly ministries), let them truly hold the solidity of holy faith, embracing with sincere devotion of heart, the most strong testimony of your paternity I call to the aid of our salvation, that we who believe that we have received the kingdom by the favor of God for the salvation of the land and for the raising up of the peoples, may be helped by the counsels of your sanctity.
¶6
IN NOMINE DOMINI FLAVIUS ERVIGIUS REX SANCTISSIMIS PATRIBUS IN HAC SANCTA SYNODO RESIDENTIBUS.
Unde licet sublimationis nostrae primordia paternitati vestrae opinabili relatione non lateant, quibus clara divinorum iudiciorum dispositione praeventus et regnandi conscenderim sedem et sacrosancti regni perceperim unctionem, nunc tamen melius id poteritis et scripti relatione cognoscere et promulgationis vestrae sententiis publicare, ut, sicut eadem regni nostri primordia conventus vestrae sanctitudinis comperit divinitus ordinata, ita his et orationibus solamen impendat et salubrium conciliorum nutrimenta impertiat, quo susceptum regnum sicut iam vestris assensionibus teneo gratum, ita vestrorum benedictionum perfruar definitionibus consecrandum, ut innovatio quodammodo nostri videatur imperii haec numerositas vestri ordinis aggregati.
Whence, although the beginnings of our sublimity are not hidden from your paternity by reputed relation, by what clear disposition of divine judgments, having been prevented, I ascended the seat of ruling and received the unction of the sacrosanct kingdom, yet now the better you will be able both to know by the relation of writing and to publish by the sentences of your promulgation, that, just as the assembly of your sanctity found the same beginnings of our kingdom to have been divinely ordered, so to these it may both expend solace with prayers and impart the nourishments of salutary councils, that the received kingdom, just as already by your assents I hold it welcome, so may I enjoy it to be consecrated by the definitions of your blessings, that this number of your order assembled may seem to be in a certain way a renewal of our empire.
¶7
Et ideo quia dominus in evangelio praecepit dicens: Amen, dico vobis: Si duo ex vobis consenserint super terram de omni re, quacumque petierint, fiet illis a patre meo, qui in caelis est. Ob hoc venerabilem paternitatis vestrae coetum cum lacrimarum effusione conveni, ut zelo vestri regiminis purgetur terra contagione pravitatis. Exsurgite, quaeso, exsurgite, culpatorum eisdem legibus tenorem inconvulsum adicite, quam pro eorundem perfidorum excessibus complexas in unum sententias promulgate. Etenim valde nobis cavendum est, ne tot antiquorum canonum regulae, quae pro eorum erroribus sunt etiam comate promulgatae, nos illorum culpis obnoxios reddant, si nostri regni temporibus eorundem canonum constructio dissoluta pertranseat.
And therefore because the Lord in the Gospel commanded saying: Amen, I say to you, if two of you shall have agreed on earth concerning any thing whatsoever they shall ask, it shall be done for them by my Father who is in heaven. On this account the venerable assembly of your paternity with effusion of tears I have addressed, that by the zeal of your governance the land may be cleansed from the contagion of wickedness. Arise, I beseech, arise, to the laws of transgressors add an unshaken tenor, which for the excesses of the same perfidious ones you have bound together in one, promulgate sentences. For indeed very much it must be avoided by us, lest so many rules of ancient canons, which have been promulgated even for their errors, should render us liable for their faults, if in the times of our kingdom the construction of the same canons should pass away dissolved.
¶8
Praesertim si legis illius, quod absit, serenitatis nostrae tempore illa clarae fidei institutio cesset, ubi divae memoriae dominus atque praecessor noster, Sisebutus rex, omnes successores suos sub perpetua maledictionis censura obstrinxit, quicumque regum mancipium christianum Iudaeo servire vel famulari permiserit etcetera, post haec illud quoque vestris deo placitis insero sensibus corrigendum, quod decessoris nostri praeceptio promulgata lege sancivit, ut omnis aut in expeditionem exercitus non progrediens aut de exercitu fugiens testimonio dignitatis suae sit irrevocabiliter carens. Cuius severitatis institutio, dum per totos Spaniae fines ordinata decurrit, dimidiam fere partem populi ignobilitati perpetuae subiugavit, ita ut, quia in quibusdam villulis vel territoriis sive vicis peste huius infamationis habitatores ipsorum locorum sint degeneres redditi, quia et testificandi nullam habent licentiam, veritas ex toto videatur in terris secessura, sicque gemino malo terra atteritur, dum et lege et repperiendae veritatis destituitur adiumento. Unde solvite nodos, transgressorum mores corrigite inhonestos, excitate zeli disciplinam in perfidos, superborum mordacitates exstinguite, oppressorum ponderibus subvenite, et quod plus omnibus est, Iudaeorum pestem, quae in novam semper recrudescit insaniam, radicitus exstirpate.
Especially if in the time of our serenity, which may it be absent, that institution of clear faith should cease, where the lord and predecessor of divine memory, King Sisebutus, bound all his successors under the perpetual censure of a curse, whoever of kings should have permitted a Christian slave to serve or be enslaved to a Jew, etc. After these things, that also I insert to your senses pleasing to God to be corrected, which the precept of our predecessor sanctioned by promulgated law, that everyone either not advancing into the expedition of the army or fleeing from the army should be irrevocably lacking the testimony of his dignity. The institution of which severity, while it runs through the whole ordered faith of Spain, subjected almost half the part of the people to perpetual ignobility, so that, because in some small estates or territories or villages the inhabitants of the same places are made degenerate by the pest of this infamy, because they have no license of testifying, truth seems from the whole about to withdraw on earth, and thus the land is worn down by a double evil, while both by the law and by the aid of discovering truth it is destitute. Whence loose the knots, correct the dishonest customs of transgressors, arouse the discipline of zeal against the perfidious, extinguish the biting of the proud, come to the aid of the weights of the oppressed, and, which is more than all, extirpate by the root the pest of the Jews, which always grows fresh into new madness.
¶9
Leges quoque, quae in eorundem Iudaeorum perfidiam propter vanam gloriam noviter promulgatae sunt, omni examinationis probitate percurrite, et licet eandem legem nostrae gloriae mansuetudo temperare disponat, vestrae tamen paternitatis sententia hos, qui per illam infamiae notam titulum dignitatis amiserant, revestiri sententiarum titulis exaranda conscribite, ut, quia praesto sunt religiosi provinciarum rectores et clarissimorum ordinum totius Spaniae duces, promulgationis vestrae sententias coram positi praenoscentes, eas illos per commissas sibi terrarum latitudines inoffensibili exerant iudiciorum instantia, quo praesentaliter assistentes perspicua oris vestri perceperint instituta.
The laws also which against the perfidy of the same Jews on account of vain glory have been newly promulgated, with all probity of examination run through, and although the mildness of our glory may intend to temper the same law, yet by the sentence of your paternity, those who by the mark of that infamy had lost the title of dignity, are to be reinvested, you shall write to be drawn up with the titles of sentences, so that, because the religious rectors of provinces and the most clear leaders of the orders of all Spain are at hand, having known beforehand the sentences of your promulgation placed before them, they may exercise them with unoffensive insistence of judgments through the entrusted to them breadths of lands, by which present they shall have perceived the conspicuous institutions of your mouth.
¶10
Omnes tamen in commune convenio et vos patres sanctissimos et vos illustres aulae regiae viros, quos interesse huic sancto concilio delegit nostra sublimitas per divini nominis attestationem et terribilem cunctis futuri iudicii diem, ut sine personarum acceptione aliqua vel favore, sine aliquo quoque aut malignae contentionis scrupulo aut subvertendae veritatis studio, quae vestris sensibus audiendo ingesserint sana verborum examinatione discutite, saniorique iudicio comprobate, ut collatarum habita prius deliberatione causarum discreta vestri ordinis condatur probitas titulorum, qualiter cum vos a mora aequitatis in negotiorum accelerationem reddiderit fervidos, efficientia quoque iustorum operum connectat deo perenniter sociandos, ut bonorum vestrorum actibus laetabundus et praesentis vitae capiam lucrum et aeternarum perfruar vobiscum gaudii mansionum.
All however in common I address, both you most holy fathers and you illustrious men of the royal court, whom our sublimity has chosen to be present at this holy council, by the attestation of the divine name and by the terrible day of the future judgment to all, that without any acceptance of persons or favor, without any also scruple of malignant contention or study of subverting truth, those things which shall have been brought into your senses by hearing, with sound examination of words discuss, and with sounder judgment approve, that, the deliberation of the causes conferred having been first held, the probity of your order, discrete in titles, may be established, so that when it shall have rendered you fervent from the delay of equity into the acceleration of affairs, the efficacy also of just works may join you to God to be associated eternally, that by your good actions glad, I may both obtain the gain of present life and enjoy with you the joys of eternal mansions.
¶11
Iterum claro pristinae generositatis testimonio devotissime obstat, nunc vero misericordia praeeunte taliter haec disponite, qualiter nec nostra gloria ministerium crudelitati adhibeat, nec tamen saeva praeceptio terram sub diutino infamationis iugulo premat. Nam et hoc generaliter obsecro, ut, quicquid in nostrae gloriae legibus absurdum, quicquid iustitiae videtur esse contrarium, humanitatis vestrae iudicio corrigatur. De ceteris autem causis atque negotiis, quae novella competunt institutione formari, evidenter execranda et crudelia, et quae mansuetudini nostrae conveniunt, et quae regni nostri oportet temporibus emendari, cum fletuum cultu et ieiunii disciplina tractabitis, scituri, quod omne bonum, quod religioni et animae competit, dominationis nostrae disposuimus temporibus informari.
Again with clear testimony of pristine generosity most devoutly it stands, but now mercy preceding, thus dispose these things, that neither may our glory supply ministry to cruelty, nor yet may a savage precept press the land under the continual throat of infamy. For also this I beseech generally, that whatever in the laws of our glory is absurd, whatever is seen to be contrary to justice, by the judgment of your humanity let it be corrected. Of the remaining causes and affairs, which need to be formed by new institution, of shunning and restraining the crimes of transgressors, with the cult of tears and the discipline of fasting you will treat.
¶12
Datum sub die V Id. Ianuar. anno feliciter primo regni serenitatis et tranquillitatis nostrae in dei nomine Toleto. Item subscriptio praedicti principis manu sua exarata.
Given on the fifth day before the Ides of January, in the first year happily of the serenity and tranquillity of our reign, in the name of God, at Toledo. Likewise the subscription of the aforesaid prince, written by his own hand.
¶13
IN THE NAME OF THE LORD, FLAVIUS ERVIGIUS THE KING.
Magna salus populi gentisque nostrae ac regni conscribitur, si haec synodalium decreta gestorum, sicut pio devotionis nostrae studio acta sunt, ita inconvulsibilis nostrae legis valitudo oraculo confirmetur, ut, quod perenissimo nostrae celsitudinis iussu venerandis patribus et clarissimis palatii nostri senioribus decreta titulorum exaratione est editum, praesentis huius legis nostrae edicto ab aemulis defendatur. Est enim haec ipsa definitio canonum sub isto notata ordine titulorum.
Great salvation of the people and of our nation and of the kingdom is procured, if these synodal decrees of acts, just as by the pious study of our devotion they have been done, so by the unshaken validity of our law by an oracle they may be confirmed, that which by the most perpetual command of our highness to the venerable fathers and to the most clear elders of our palace has been published by the drawing up of decrees of titles, by the edict of this present law of ours may be defended from rivals. For this same definition of canons is this, noted under that order of titles.

CANON I
DE AGNITA ET CONFIRMATA PRAELATIONE FASTIGII PRINCIPALIS
IN NOMINE GLORIOSI DOMINI NOSTRI ERVIGII REGIS primo die synodalis exordio consedentibus episcopis atque senioribus palatii universis habita primum de sancta trinitate collatio, non quae novello exactionis stilo definita patesceret, sed quae versibus simplicioribus sese pigris sensibus patefacta monstraret, ubi praemissa semper lectio praecederet, quod sequens expositio aperiret.
In the name of the glorious lord our king Ervigius, on the first day of the synodal beginning, with the bishops and all the elders of the palace sitting, a collation was first held concerning the holy Trinity, not which might become known having been defined by a novel style of exaction, but which, having been made clear, might show itself opened to sluggish senses in simpler verses, where the reading always premised preceded that which the following exposition might open.
¶20
Credentes pariter et docentes de eadem sanctae fidei puritate, quicquid evangelica et apostolica traditio sanxit, quicquid sancta synodus Nicena constituit, quicquid Constantinopolitana patrum aggregata collectio promulgavit, quicquid Ephesini coetus definitio docuit, quicquid etiam Chalcedonensis concilii promulgatio definivit, sicut multorum aliorum catholicorum patrum documentis id sacramentum expositum traditumque nobis accepimus, sicut etiam in missarum sollemniis patulis confessionum vocibus proclamamus:
Believing equally and teaching concerning the same purity of holy faith whatever the evangelical and apostolic tradition sanctioned, whatever the holy Nicene synod established, whatever the gathered collection of the Constantinopolitan fathers promulgated, whatever the definition of the Ephesine assembly taught, whatever also the promulgation of the Chalcedonian council defined, just as by the documents of many other catholic fathers we have received that same sacrament expounded and handed down to us, just as also in the solemnities of masses with open voices of confessions we proclaim:
¶21
Credimus in unum deum patrem omnipotentem, factorem caeli et terrae, visibilium omnium et invisibilium conditorem, et in unum dominum Iesum Christum filium dei unigenitum, ex patre natum ante omnia saecula, deum ex deo, lumen ex lumine, deum verum ex deo vero, natum, non factum, homousion patri, hoc est eiusdem cum patre substantiae, per quem omnia facta sunt, quae in caelo et quae in terra, qui propter nos et propter nostram salutem descendit et incarnatus est de spiritu sancto et Maria virgine, homo factus, sub Pontio Pilato et sepultus, tertia die resurrexit, ascendit in caelos, sedet ad dexteram patris, iterum venturus in gloria iudicare vivos et mortuos, cuius regni non erit finis. Credimus in spiritum sanctum dominum et vivificatorem, ex patre et filio procedentem, cum patre et filio adorandum et glorificandum, qui locutus est per prophetas, unam catholicam atque apostolicam ecclesiam. Confitemur unum baptisma in remissionem peccatorum. Exspectamus resurrectionem mortuorum et vitam futuri saeculi. Amen.
We believe in one God the Father almighty, maker of heaven and earth, of all things visible and invisible the creator, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God only-begotten, from the Father born before all ages, God from God, light from light, true God from true God, born not made, homoousion to the Father, that is of the same with the Father substance, through whom all things were made, which in heaven and which in earth, who for us and for our salvation descended and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of Mary the virgin, man was made, suffered under Pontius Pilate and was buried, on the third day rose again, ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father, again to come in glory to judge the living and the dead, of whose kingdom there shall be no end. We believe in the Holy Spirit the Lord and life-giver, from the Father and the Son proceeding, with the Father and the Son to be adored and to be glorified, who spoke through the prophets, one catholic and apostolic church. We confess one baptism for the remission of sins. We look for the resurrection of the dead and the life of the future age. Amen.
¶22
Post pacifica igitur huius sanctae fidei collationum studia, illa se primum nostris sensibus salvatoris cognitio cognoscendam invexit, quae tanto corda omnium ardore caritatis adstrinxit, quanto se per eandem ipsa cognitio in conventu generalis concilii praenoscenda exilivit. Etenim sub qua pace vel ordine serenissimus Ervigius princeps regni conscenderit culmen regnandique per sacrosanctam unctionem susceperit potestatem, ostensa nos scripturarum evidentia docet, in quibus praeceptis et Wambae principis poenitentiae susceptio noscitur et translatus regni honor in huius nostri principis nomine derivatur.
After the pacific studies therefore of the collations of this holy faith, then first into our senses the knowledge of the Savior brought itself to be known, which bound the hearts of all with such ardor of charity, by how much through the same knowledge itself in the assembly of the general council it leaped forth to be known beforehand. For indeed under what peace or order the most serene prince Ervigius ascended the summit of the kingdom and received the power of ruling through sacrosanct anointing, the evidentness of the scriptures teaches us shown, in which both the reception of penance of prince Wamba is known and the transferred honor of the kingdom is derived in the name of this our prince.
¶23
Idem enim Wamba princeps, dum inevitabili habitabilis necessitudinis teneretur eventu, suscepto religionis debito cultu et venerabili tonsurae sacrae signaculo, mox per scripturam definitionis suae hunc inclitum dominum nostrum Ervigium post se praelegit regnaturum et sacerdotali benedictione ungendum. Vidimus enim et pariter patulo alternae visionis intuitu praelucente perspeximus huius praemissi ordinis scripturas, id est, notitiam manu seniorum palatii roboratam, coram quibus antecedens princeps et religionis cultum et tonsurae sacrae adeptus est venerabile signum, scripturam quoque definitionis ab eodem edicto, ubi gloriosum dominum nostrum Ervigium post se fieri regem exoptat, aliam quoque informationem iam dicti viri in nomine honorabilis et sanctissimi fratris nostri Iuliani Toletanae sedis episcopi, ubi eum separavit pariter et instruxit, ut cum omni diligentiae ordine iam dictum dominum nostrum Ervigium in regno ungere deberet et sub omni diligentia unctionis ipsius celebritas fieret. In qua scriptura et subscriptio nobis eiusdem Wambae principis claruit et omnis evidentia confirmationis earundem scripturarum sese manifeste monstravit.
For the same prince Wamba, while he was held by the inevitable event of habitability necessity, having received the due cultivation of religion and the venerable sign of sacred tonsure, soon by the writing of his definition chose this renowned lord our Ervigius to reign after him and to be anointed with priestly blessing. For we saw and equally with the open gaze of mutual vision shining forth we perceived the scriptures of this premised order, that is, the notice strengthened by the hand of the elders of the palace, before whom the preceding prince both the cultivation of religion and the venerable sign of tonsure obtained, also the writing of definition from the same edict where he desires that the glorious lord our Ervigius be made king after him, also another information of the already-mentioned man in the name of the honorable and most holy brother our Julian, bishop of the Toledan see, where he both separated and instructed him, that with all order of diligence he should anoint the already-mentioned lord our Ervigius for the kingdom and with all diligence the celebration of the anointing itself should be done. In which writing both the subscription of the same prince Wamba became clear to us and all the evidentness of confirmation of the same writings manifestly showed itself.
¶24
Quibus omnibus approbatis atque perlectis dignum satis nostro coetui visum est, ut praedictis definitionibus scripturarum nostrorum omnium confirmatione apponatur, ut, qui ante tempora in occultis iudiciis praescitus est regnaturus, nunc manifesto in tempore generaliter omnium sacerdotum habeatur definitioni consecratus. Et ideo soluta manus populi ab omni vinculo iuramenti, quae praedicto viro Wambae, dum regnum adhuc teneret, allegata permansit, hunc solum serenissimum Ervigium principem obsequenda gratae servitutis famulatu sequatur et libera, quem et divinum iudicium in regno praelegit et decessor princeps successorem sibi instituit et, quod superest, quem totius populi amabilitas exquisivit. Unde his praecognitis atque praescitis serviendum est deo caeli praedicto principi nostro Ervigio regi eique pia devotione obsequendum, etiam promptissima voluntate agendum et enitendum quicquid eius saluti proficiat, quicquid gentis vel utilitatibus et patriae consuluerit.
All which things having been approved and read through, it seemed worthy enough to our assembly, that to the aforesaid definitions of the scriptures the confirmation of all of us be appended, that he who before times in hidden judgments was foreknown to reign, now in manifest time by the definition of all the priests generally may be held consecrated. And therefore, the hand of the people having been loosed from every bond of oath, which to the aforesaid man Wamba, while he still held the kingdom, remained pledged, let it follow this only most serene prince Ervigius with the service of grateful service to be obeyed and free, whom both divine judgment forechose for the kingdom and the predecessor prince established as his successor and, which remains, whom the amiability of the whole people sought. Whence, these things having been foreknown and foretold, he is to be served, the king of heaven, to the aforesaid prince our Ervigius, and to him with pious devotion to be obeyed, also with most prompt will to be done and striven for whatever may profit his salvation, whatever for the utilities of the nation or the fatherland he shall have consulted.
¶25
Unde non erit iam deinceps aut anathematis sententia alienus aut a divinae animadversionis ultione securus, quisquis contra salutem eius deinceps aut erexerit vocem aut commoverit caedem aut quamcumque exquisierit laedendi occasionem.
Whence henceforth neither shall he be alien from the sentence of anathema nor secure from the vengeance of divine animadversion, whoever from now on against his salvation either shall have raised up a voice or shall have moved a slaughter or shall have sought any occasion of harming.
¶26
CANON II
DE HIS, QUI POENITENTIAM NON CONSENTIENTES ACCIPIUNT
Plerumque quibus miseratio domini etiam nolentibus subvenit, beneficiis dei videntur esse ingrati et abuti gratia largitoris, quam bene usi poterant consequi in abolitione facinoris. Impugnant saepe, quod honorare debuerunt, et profanis quaestibus indultae gratiae munus a se reiciunt, quod summis votis amplecti debuerunt. Etenim multos saepe adspeximus in salute positos ultimum desiderantes poenitentiae fructum et rursus nimietate aegritudinis ita loquendi et sentiendi perdidisse naturale officium, ut nulla illis cura salutis suae videretur inesse, nullo etiam pristinae devotionis noscerentur desiderio anhelare. Quorum tamen casibus fraternitas condolens, ita talium necessitates in fide sua susceperit, ut ultimum illis tribuatur viaticum, scilicet ne sine fructu poenitentiae videantur transire e saeculo, quod, si forsitan respiciente deo saluti pristinae reformentur, agunt cautionibus vanis et oppositionibus exsecrandis, qualiter a se tonsurae venerabile signum expellant atque habitum religionis abiciant impudentissime asserentes ideo se nullis regulis ecclesiasticae disciplinae sub hoc voto teneri, quia poenitentiam nec ipsi poenituerunt nec sentientes acceperunt.
Often those to whom the mercy of the Lord succors even unwilling are seen to be ungrateful for the benefits of God and to abuse the grace of the giver, which by using well they were able to obtain for the abolition of crime. They impugn often that which they ought to have honored, and by profane gains they reject from themselves the gift of granted grace, which they ought to have embraced with highest vows. For we have often seen many placed in health desiring the last fruit of penance and again by the excess of illness to have lost the natural office of speaking and sensing so that no care of their salvation seemed to be in them, nor by any were they known to pant with the desire of pristine devotion. Whose cases however the fraternity condoling thus has undertaken the necessities of such in their faith, that the last viaticum be granted to them, namely lest without the fruit of the sentence of penance they might be seen to pass from the world. Which, if perhaps by God regarding they are restored to pristine health, they act with vain cautions and execrable oppositions, how from themselves they may expel the venerable sign of tonsure and most impudently cast off the habit of religion, asserting that on this account they are held by no rules of ecclesiastical discipline under this vow, because they neither repented of penance nor received it consenting.
¶27
Quorum impudentia et obstinata procacitas non aliquando talia diceret, si qualiter ad vitam per sacrosancti lavacri gratiam veniret, meminisset. Etenim parvulorum infantium vita originali peccato obnoxia, quae nulli propter aetatem discernendi vel expetendi sensui apta iudicetur, nisi ex sponsione fidelium baptismi accipiant sacramentum, nullo sensu, nulla etiam discretionis industria id appetere possunt. Unde sicut baptismum, quod nescientibus parvulis sine ulla contentione in fide tantum proximorum accipitur, ita poenitentiae donum, quod nescientibus illabitur, absque impugnantia violationis hi, qui illud exceperint, observabunt. Si quis autem quolibet modo poenitentiam accipiens hoc violaverit synodale institutum, ut vere transgressor paternis regulis ferietur, nec enim ista statuentes sacerdotes, ut passim et licenter donum poenitentiae non poenitentibus audeant prorogare, absolvimus, sed hos, qui qualibet sorte poenitentiam susceperint, ne ulterius ad militare cingulum redeant, religamus.
Whose impudence and obstinate pertinacity would never say such things, if it remembered how one comes to life through the grace of the sacrosanct laver. For the life of little infants liable to original sin, which on account of age is judged fit for no sense of discerning or seeking, unless by the sponsorship of the faithful they receive the sacrament of baptism, they can desire this by no sense, by no industry of discretion. Therefore just as baptism, which is received for unknowing little infants without any contention only in the faith of the neighbors, so the gift of penance, which flows upon the unknowing, let those who shall have received it observe without the impugning of violation. But if anyone receiving penance in any way shall have violated this synodal institute, as a true transgressor by the paternal rules let him be struck. For neither do we absolve those priests who establish these things, that they should dare to grant the gift of penance promiscuously and licentiously to the not penitent, but we bind those who shall have received penance by any lot, that they no longer return to the military belt.
¶28
Sacerdotes tamen, qui non sentienti neque poenitenti ausu temerario poenitentiam dederint, neque se exhortatu eius, qui poenitentiam accipit, vel manuum indiciis vel quibuslibet aliis evidentibus significationibus invitatum fuisse probaverit, unius anni excommunicationis sententiae subiacebit.
Priests however who to one not sensing nor repenting by temerarious daring shall have given penance, nor shall have proved that they were invited either by the exhortation of him who receives penance or by the indications of hands or by any other evident signs, shall be subject to the sentence of excommunication for one year.
¶29
CANON III
DE CULPATORUM RECEPTIONE VEL COMMUNIONE APUD ECCLESIAM
Vidimus quosdam et flevimus ex numero culpatorum receptos in gratiam principum extorres exstitisse a collegio sacerdotum. Quod denotabile malum illa res agit, qua licentia principalis, in quo se solvi licentius curat, ibi alios illigat, et quos in suam communionem videtur suscipere, a communione et pace ecclesiae elegit separare, ut, qui cum illo convescuntur, sola sacerdotum communione priventur. Et ideo quia remissio talium, qui contra regem, gentem vel patriam agunt, per definitiones canonum antiquorum potestate solum regia punitur, cui et peccasse noscuntur, ab eis nulla se deinceps abstinebit sacerdotum communio, sed quos regia potestas aut in gratiam benignitatis receperit aut participes mensae suae effecerit, hos etiam sacerdotum et populorum conventus suscipere in ecclesiastica communione debebit, ut quod principalis pietas habet acceptum, nec a sacerdotibus dei habeatur extraneum.
We have seen some from the number of the guilty and we have wept, received into the favor of princes, to have been strangers from the college of priests. Which denotable evil that thing works, by which the princely license, in which it cares to be loosed more licentiously, there it binds others, and those whom it seems to receive into its communion, it chooses to separate from the communion and peace of the church, so that those who eat with him are deprived of the communion of priests alone. And therefore because the remission of such, who act against the king, the nation, or the fatherland, by the definitions of ancient canons is punished by the royal power alone, to whom they are known to have sinned, from them from now on no communion of priests shall abstain, but whom the royal power either shall have received into the grace of benignity or shall have made participants of its table, them also the assembly of priests and peoples ought to receive in ecclesiastical communion, that which the princely piety holds as accepted, let it not be held as alien by the priests of God.
¶30
CANON IIII
IN LOCIS, UBI EPISCOPUS NON FUIT, NUMQUAM EPISCOPUS ORDINETUR
Maioribus institutionibus contraire et sanctorum patrum decreta convellere, quid aliud est, quam vinculum societatis Christi abrumpere et usurpatae praeceptionis licentia statum ecclesiae dissipare? Prosequente igitur venerabili et sanctissimo viro fratre nostro Stephano, Emeritensis sedis episcopo, res nobis novellae praesumptionis usurpatione sese intulit pertractanda, tanto communionis nostro iudicio convellenda, quanto et pravitatis noscitur ausu perpetrata. Dixit enim violentia principali se impulsum fuisse, ut in monasterio villulae, in qua venerabile corpus sanctissimi Penenii confessoris debito quiescit honore, novam episcopalis honoris ordinationem efficeret.
To oppose the greater institutions and to overturn the decrees of the holy fathers, what else is it than to break the bond of the society of Christ and by the license of usurped precept to dissipate the state of the church? The venerable and most holy man our brother Stephen, bishop of the see of Merida, pursuing, a matter by the usurpation of novel presumption introduced itself to be treated by us, to be so much overturned by the judgment of our communion, by how much it is known to have been perpetrated by the daring of wickedness. For he said that he had been impelled by princely violence, that in the monastery of the small estate in which the venerable body of the most holy confessor Penenius rests with due honor, he should effect a new ordination of episcopal honor.
¶31
Et ideo quia indiscreto et facillimo assensu iniusti sui principis iussionibus parens novam et iniustam illic pontificalis sedis praelectionem induxit, ubi praedictus idem vir prostratus humo medicamine nostri praecepti et sibi dari veniam petit et, quid potissimum fieri oporteat de persona eius, qui illic ordinatus fuerat, nostri oris sententia decerni poposcit. Sed quia veraciter immo communiter noveramus praedictum principem consilio levitatis agentem non solum praecepisse, ut in praedicto loco aliquis episcopus fieret, sed etiam ita eum consuetis obstinationibus definisse, ut in suburbio Toletano in ecclesia praetoriensi sanctorum Petri et Pauli episcopum ordinaret, necnon et in aliis vicis vel villulis similiter faceret. Ideo pro tam insolenti huiusmodi exstirpationis licentia, quid de hac re haberent canonum instituta, in medium proferri praecipimus.
And therefore because by indiscreet and too easy assent, obeying the commands of his unjust prince, he introduced a new and unjust prelection of a pontifical see there, where the aforesaid same man, prostrate on the ground, by the remedy of our precept both sought pardon to be given to him and asked that what ought especially to be done concerning the person of him who had been ordained there might be decreed by the sentence of our mouth. But because truly, rather commonly, we knew the aforesaid prince, acting with the counsel of levity, to have commanded not only that some bishop should be made in the aforesaid place, but also so defined with his customary obstinacies, that in the suburb of Toledo in the pretorian church of Saints Peter and Paul he would ordain a bishop, and likewise in other villages and small estates he would do similarly. Therefore on account of so insolent a license of extirpation of this kind, what the institutes of the canons had concerning this matter, we commanded to be brought forth into the midst.
¶32
Tunc haec in ordinem constituta praelecta sunt: in primis ex epistola Pauli, ubi Tito discipulo, ut episcopos per civitates constituere debeat, praecepit; item ex concilio Niceno titulo VIII, ubi inter cetera praecipitur, ut in civitate non videantur duo episcopi esse; item ex concilio Laudiceno, titulo septimo, ubi dicit: Non oportet in vicis et in villulis episcopos ordinari, et cetera; item ex concilio Africae secundo, titulo V, ubi Felix episcopus Solemsitanus dixit: Etiam si hoc placet sanctitati vestrae, insinuo, ut dioceses, quae numquam episcopos habuerunt, non habeant, et illa diocesis, quae aliquando habuit, habeat proprium. Secundum autem hanc prosecutionem sanctitatis vestrae est aestimare, quid fieri debeat. Genecleus episcopus dixit: Si placet insinuatio patris et coepiscopi nostri Felicis, ab omnibus confirmetur. Ab universis episcopis dictum est: Placet.
Then these things were read, constituted in order: first from the epistle of Paul, where he commanded Titus the disciple that he ought to constitute bishops through the cities; likewise from the Nicene council title VIII, where among other things it is commanded that in one city two bishops are not seen to be; likewise from the Council of Laodicea, title VII, where it says: It is not fitting that bishops be ordained in villages and in small estates, etc.; likewise from the Second Council of Africa, title V, where Bishop Felix of Solemselita said: Even if this pleases your sanctity, I make known, that dioceses which have never received bishops, let them not have them, and that diocese which once had one, let it have its own. Moreover according to this prosecution, let your sanctity judge what must be done. Bishop Geneclius said: If it pleases, the insinuation of our father and fellow bishop Felix by all be confirmed. By all the bishops it was said: It pleases.
¶33
Item ex concilio Africae III titulo XLII, ubi dicit: Ut non accipiat alterum episcopum plebs, quae in diocesi semper subiacuit. Epigenius enim episcopus inter cetera sic dixit: Hoc dico non debere rectorem accipere eam plebem, quae in diocesi semper subiacuit nec umquam proprium episcopum habuit. Quapropter si universo sanctissimo coetui placet hoc, quod profectus sum, confirmetur. Aurelius episcopus dixit: Fratris et consacerdotis nostri prosecutioni non obsisto, sed hoc me et fecisse et facturum esse profiteor. Item ex concilio Sardicensi, ubi inter cetera praecipitur: Licentia danda passim non est. Si enim subito aut vicus aliquis aut modica civitas, cui satis est unus presbyter, voluerit sibi episcopum ordinari ad hoc, ut vilescat nomen episcopi et auctoritas, non debent illi ex alia provincia invitati facere episcopum in quibus antea episcopi non fuerunt.
Likewise from the Third Council of Africa, title XLII, where it says: Let the people which always lay under a diocese not receive another bishop. For Bishop Epigenius among other things thus said: This I say, that the people which always lay under a diocese and never had its own bishop ought not to receive a rector. Therefore if it pleases the whole most holy assembly, let that which I have set forth be confirmed. Bishop Aurelius said: To the prosecution of our brother and fellow priest I do not oppose, but this I profess both to have done and to be about to do. Likewise from the Council of Sardica, where among other things it is commanded: The license is not to be given promiscuously. For if suddenly either some village or a modest city, for which one presbyter suffices, shall have wished for itself a bishop to be ordained for this purpose, that the name of bishop and authority might become vile, let those invited from another province not make a bishop in places where previously there were no bishops.
¶34
Ita de sententia eorum, qui huiusmodi ordinationes faciunt, vel de his, qui contra haec instituta canonum ordinantur, ex concilio Tauritano titulo II, ubi dicit: Gestorum quoque serie conscribi placuit ad perpetem disciplinam quod circa Octavium, Ursionem, Remigium, Adtrefae episcopum synodus sancta decrevit, qui in usurpationem quandam de ordinatione sacerdotum ad invidiam vocabatur, quod ita his videtur indultum, ut de cetero hac auctoritate commoniti nihil usurpare conentur, siquidem se ab hac causa tali excusatione defenderint, qua dicerent prius se non esse conventos. Proinde iudicavit synodus, ut, si quis ex hoc fecerit contra instituta maiorum, sciat is, qui ordinatus fuerat, sacerdotii se honore privandum, et ille, qui ordinaverit, auctoritatem se in ordinationibus vel in conciliis minime retenturum.
Thus from the sentence of those who make ordinations of this kind, or concerning those who against these institutes of the canons are ordained, from the Council of Tauritanus, title II, where it says: It pleased also to be written in the series of acts for perpetual discipline that which the holy synod decreed concerning Octavius, Ursio, Remigius, and Bishop Adtrefa, who was called to a certain usurpation concerning the ordination of priests out of envy, that thus it seemed to be granted to them, that for the future, admonished by this authority, they might attempt to usurp nothing, if indeed they have defended themselves from this matter by such an excuse, that they would say that they had not been convened beforehand. Therefore the synod judged that if anyone should do this contrary to the institutes of the greater, let him who had been ordained know that he is to be deprived of the honor of the priesthood, and that he who had ordained will by no means retain authority in ordinations or in councils.
¶35
Non solum autem circa memoratum episcopum haec sententia praevalebit, sed et circa omnes simili errore deceptos, qui ordinationes huiusmodi perpetrarunt. His igitur fortissimis regulis effectum pii operis apponentes in communi definitione elegimus, ut in loco villulae supradictae Aquis deinceps sedes episcopalis non maneat, neque episcopus illic ultra constituendus exsistat. Hic tamen Coniuldus, qui contra maiorum decreta illic videtur institutus fuisse episcopus, nullis canonum erit ad condemnationem sui sententiis ulciscendus, quia non ambitione, sed principis impulsione constitit ordinatus. Et ideo hoc illi remedium humanitatis concedimus, ut in sede alia cuiuslibet decidentis episcopi transducatur et praedictus locus sub monastica deinceps institutione mansurus non episcopali ultra privilegio fretus, sed sub abbatis regimine, sicut hucusque fuit, erit modis omnibus mancipandus.
Not only however concerning the remembered bishop will this sentence prevail, but also concerning all deceived by a similar error, who have perpetrated ordinations of this kind. These most strong rules therefore applying the effect of pious work, we have chosen by common definition that from now on an episcopal see not remain in the place of the above-said small estate of Aquis, nor let a bishop be further constituted there. This Coniuldus however, who contrary to the decrees of the greater is seen to have been instituted bishop there, by no sentences of the canons for his condemnation is to be punished, because he was ordained not by ambition but by the impulse of the prince. And therefore we grant him this remedy of humanity, that he be transferred to another see of any bishop dying, and the aforesaid place, about to remain henceforth under a monastic institution, not by episcopal privilege further strengthened, but under the rule of an abbot, just as it has been until now, shall by all means be subjected.
¶36
Iam vero de cetero generale ponentes edictum, si quis contra haec canonum interdicta venire conatus fuerit, ut in locis illis episcopum eligat fieri, ubi episcopus numquam fuit, anathema in conspectu omnipotentis dei, et insuper tam ordinator quam ordinatus gradum sui ordinis perdat, quia non solum antiquorum patrum decreta, sed apostolica ausus est convellere instituta.
But now for the rest a general edict placing, if anyone against these interdicts of the canons shall have tried to come, that he choose a bishop to be made in those places where a bishop never was, let him be anathema in the sight of almighty God, and moreover both the ordainer and the ordained let them lose the grade of their order, because he dared to overturn not only the decrees of the ancient fathers but also the apostolic institutes.
¶37
CANON V
DE QUORUNDAM CONSUETUDINE SACERDOTUM FOEDISSIMA, QUI OBLATIS DEO SACRIFICIIS NON COMMUNICANT
Relatum nobis est quosdam de sacerdotibus non tot vicibus communionis sanctae gratiam sumere, quot sacrificia in una die videntur offerre, sed in uno die, si plurima per se deo offerant sacrificia, in omnibus se oblationibus a communione suspendunt et in sola tantum extremi sacrificii oblatione communionis sanctae gratiam sumant, quasi non sit toties illis vero et singulari sacrificio participandum, quoties corporis et sanguinis domini nostri Iesu Christi immolatio facta constiterit. Nam ecce apostolus dicit: Nonne qui edunt hostias, participes sunt altaris? Certum est, quod hi, qui sacrificantes non edunt, rei sunt dominicis sacramentis. Quicumque ergo sacerdotum deinceps divino altario sacrificium oblaturus accesserit et se a communione suspenderit, ab ipsa, qua se indecenter privavit gratia communionis anno uno repulsum se noverit. Nam quale erit illud sacrificium, cui nec ipse sacrificans particeps esse cognoscitur? Ergo modis omnibus est tenendum, ut, quotiescumque sacrificans corpus et sanguinem Iesu Christi domini nostri in altario immolat, toties perceptionis corporis et sanguinis Christi participem se praebeat.
It has been reported to us that some of the priests do not receive the grace of holy communion as many times as they are seen to offer sacrifices in one day, but in one day, if they offer many sacrifices to God by themselves, in all oblations they abstain from communion, and only in the oblation of the last sacrifice they take the grace of holy communion, as if they are not to be partakers in that true and singular sacrifice as many times as the immolation of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ shall have been established to have been done. For behold the apostle says: Are not those who eat the sacrifices partakers of the altar? It is certain that those who sacrificing do not eat are guilty of the Lord's sacraments. Whoever therefore of the priests from now on shall have approached to offer a sacrifice at the divine altar and shall have abstained himself from communion, from that grace by which he has indecently deprived himself of communion for one year let him know that he is repelled. For what manner of sacrifice will that be, of which not even the sacrificer himself is known to be partaker? Therefore by all means it is to be held, that as many times as the sacrificer immolates on the altar the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, so many times let him present himself a partaker of the perception of the body and blood of Christ.
¶38
CANON VI
DE CONCESSA TOLETANO PONTIFICI GENERALIS SYNODI POTESTATE, UT EPISCOPI ALTERIUS PROVINCIAE CUM CONHIBENTIA PRINCIPUM IN URBE REGIA ORDINENTUR
Illud quoque collatione mutua decernendum nobis occurrit, quod in quibusdam civitatibus decidentibus episcopis propriis, dum differtur diu ordinatio successoris, non minima creatur et officiorum divinorum offensio et ecclesiasticarum nocitura perditio. Nam dum longe lateque diffuso tractu terrarum commeantum impenditur celeritas nuntiorum, quo aut non queat regiis auribus decedentis praesulis transitus innotesci aut de successore morientis episcopi libera principis electio praestolari, nascitur saepe et nostro ordini de relatione talium difficultas et regiae potestati, dum consultum nostrum pro subrogandis pontificibus sustinet iniuriosa necessitas.
That also, occurring to us to be decreed by mutual collation, that in some cities with their own bishops dying, while the ordination of a successor is long delayed, not a little offense of divine offices and a destructive loss of ecclesiastical things is created. For while by the widely and broadly diffused stretch of lands the swiftness of traveling messengers is expended, by which either the passing away of the dying prelate cannot become known to the royal ears or the free election of the prince for the successor of the dying bishop is awaited, difficulty often arises for our order concerning the relation of such, and for the royal power, while it sustains the injurious necessity for the counsel of ours for substituting pontiffs.
¶39
Unde placuit omnibus pontificibus Spaniae atque Galliae, ut salvo privilegio uniuscuiusque provinciae licitum maneat deinceps Toletano pontifici, quoscumque regalis potestas elegerit, et iam dicti Toletani episcopi iudicium dignos esse probaverit, in quibuslibet provinciis in praecedentium sedibus praeficere praesules et decidentibus episcopis eligere successores; hic tamen et quisquis ille fuerit ordinatus, post ordinationis suae tempus infra trium mensium spatium proprii metropolitani praesentiam visurus accedat, qualiter eius auctoritate vel disciplina instructus condigne susceptae sedis gubernacula teneat. Quod si per desidiam aut neglectum quilibet constituti temporis metas excesserit, quibus metropolitani sui nequeat obtutibus praesentari, excommunicatum se per omnia noverit, excepto si regia iussione impeditum se esse probaverit. Hanc quoque definitionis formulam sicut de episcopis ita et de ceteris ecclesiarum rectoribus placuit observandam.
Whence it pleased all the pontiffs of Spain and Gaul, that, saving the privilege of each province, it remain lawful henceforth for the Toledan pontiff, whomever the royal power shall have chosen and the judgment of the already-mentioned Toledan bishop shall have proved worthy, to place over them as prelates in the preceding sees in any provinces, and for dying bishops to choose successors; yet he, and whoever he shall be that is ordained, within the space of three months after the time of his ordination, let him come to see the presence of his own metropolitan, so that by his authority or discipline instructed he may hold worthily the governance of the received see. But if through sloth or neglect anyone shall have exceeded the limits of the constituted time, by which he cannot be presented to the eyes of his metropolitan, let him know himself to be wholly excommunicated, except if he shall have proved himself to be impeded by royal command. This formula of definition also concerning bishops thus and concerning the other rectors of churches it has pleased to be observed.

CANON VII
DE RECEPTO TESTIMONIO PERSONARUM, QUI PER LEGEM, QUAE DE PRODITIONE EXERCITUS FACTA EST, TESTIFICANDI LICENTIAM PERDIDERUNT
Omnis disciplina sic subiectos debet arguere, ut spem veniae non videatur auferre nec funditus curvationis inducere iugum, sed temperantiae semper adhibere consultum. Et ideo quia legem illam a domino Wambane principe editam, quae de progressione est annotata, huius principis nostri et domini Ervigii mansuetudo temperare disposuit, ideo annuente nobis glorioso et religiosissimo Ervigio principe nostro necessario hoc sanctum concilium definivit, ut hi, qui supradictam legem testificandi dignitatem perdiderunt, recepto testimonio pristinae dignitatis causas exsequi possint debitae actionis, qualiter nobilitatis solitae titulum reportantes, et quaecumque de praeteritis legitime testificari voluerint, licentiae obtineant votum, et a iudicibus nullis prohibitionibus arceantur, hoc videlicet adicientes, ut, si quid in praeteritis testificari voluerint, si pro sola huius rei infamatione reiecti sunt, testimonio suo nuper conquirant, quod in praeteritis conquirere potuerant, tantum si illo tempore, quo in praeteritis ad testimonium dicendum vocati sunt et supradictae legis institutionibus reprobati, et aliam criminis notam eos tunc non habuisse patuerit aut tricennium tunc effluxisse, cum ad testimonium fuerant prolati, hi, qui eos reprobaverant, manifeste convicerint.
Every discipline ought so to argue its subjects, that it may not seem to take away the hope of pardon nor to introduce the yoke of utter downfall, but always to apply the counsel of temperance. And therefore because the mildness of this our prince and lord Ervigius has intended to temper that law which was published by the lord prince Wamba, which was set down concerning the advancement of the army, therefore, our glorious and most religious prince Ervigius assenting to us, this holy council has necessarily defined, that those who have lost the dignity of testifying according to the above-said law, having received the testimony of pristine dignity, may prosecute the causes of due action, so that, recovering the title of accustomed nobility, and whatever they may wish to testify legitimately concerning past things, they may obtain the vow of license, and by no prohibitions let them be kept back by the judges, this clearly adding, that if they shall have wished to testify concerning past things, if on account of the infamy of this matter alone they were rejected, by their testimony let them now obtain that which they were able to obtain concerning past things, only if at that time, when they were called to give testimony concerning past things and were rejected by the institutions of the above-said law, it shall have been evident that they had no other mark of crime then, or that a thirty-year period had then elapsed when they were brought forward for testimony, they who had rejected them have manifestly proven.

CANON VIII
DE HIS, QUI UXORES SUAS DIVORTIO INTERCEDENTE RELINQUUNT
Praeceptum domini est, ut excepta causa fornicationis uxor a viro dimitti non debeat. Et ideo quicumque citra culpam criminis supradicti uxorem suam quacumque occasione reliquerit, quia, quod deus iunxit, ille separari disposuit, tamdiu ab ecclesiastica communione privatus et a coetu omnium christianorum maneat alienus, quamdiu et societatem relictae coniugis sinceriter amplectatur et foveat, ita tamen, qui iam admoniti a sacerdote semel et bis terque, ut corrigantur, ad torum suae coniugis noluerint redire consortium, ipsi se suis meritis et a palatinae dignitatis officio separabunt et insuper generosae dignitatis testimonium, quamdiu in culpa fuerint, amissuri sunt, quia carnem suam discidii iugulo tradiderunt.
It is a precept of the Lord, that except for the cause of fornication a wife ought not to be dismissed by a husband. And therefore whoever, apart from the fault of the above-said crime, shall have left his wife on any occasion, because he has disposed to separate that which God joined, for so long let him be deprived of ecclesiastical communion and remain alien from the assembly of all Christians, as long as he both sincerely embraces and cherishes the society of the forsaken wife, yet in such a way that those who, having been admonished by the priest once and twice and a third time that they be corrected, shall have been unwilling to return to the consortium of their own wife, they themselves by their own merits both from the office of palatine dignity shall separate themselves, and moreover the testimony of generous dignity, as long as they shall be in fault, they are about to lose, because they have handed over their own flesh to the throat of separation.

CANON VIIII
DE CONFIRMATIONE LEGUM, QUAE IN IUDAEORUM NEQUITIAM PROMULGATAE SUNT
De Iudaeorum autem exsecranda perfidia discretis titulorum sententiis editas noviter a glorioso principe leges vigilanti sensuum intentione perlegimus, districto etiam gravitatis pondere earum instituta probavimus. Proinde quae debitae rationis iudicio editae synodali dignatione probatae sunt, irrevocabili deinceps iudiciorum ordine pro eorum excessibus tenebuntur, id est: Leges, quae de commemoratione priscarum cerimoniarum Iudaeorum et transgressionibus promulgatae sunt atque de novella confirmatione earum. Item de blasphematoribus sanctae trinitatis. Item ne Iudaei aut se aut filios suos vel famulos ad baptismi gratiam subtrahant. Item ne Iudaei more suo celebrent pascha vel carnis circumcisiones exerceant, ac ne christianum quemquam a fide Christi dimoveant. Item ne Iudaei sabbata ceterasque festivitates ritus sui celebrare praesumant. Item ut omnis Iudaeus diebus dominicis et in praenotatis diebus ab opere cessent. Item ne Iudaei more suo diiudicent escas. Item ne Iudaei ex propinquitate sui sanguinis connubia ducant et ut sine benedictione sacerdotis nubere non audeant. Item ne Iudaei religionis nostrae insultantes sectam suam defendere audeant nec a fide refugientes alibi se transducant, et ne quislibet refugientes eos suscipiat. Item ne christianus a Iudaeo quodcumque muneris contra fidem Christi accipiat. Item ne Iudaei libros legere audeant, quos christiana fides repudiat. Item ne Iudaeis mancipia deserviant vel adhaereant christiana. Item si se Iudaeus christianum esse testetur et ob hoc non velit a se reicere mancipium christianum. Item professio Iudaeorum, quomodo unusquisque ad fidem veniens indiculum professionis suae conscribere debeat. Item condiciones Iudaeorum, quas iurare debeant hi, qui ex eis ad fidem venientes professiones suas dederint. Item de christianis mancipiis Iudaeorum, quae se non prodiderint christiana, sive de publicatoribus eorum. Item ne Iudaei a quolibet potestate accepta extra regiam ordinationem christianum quemque imperare, plectere vel distringere audeant. Item, ut, si Iudaeorum servi necdum adhuc conversi ad Christi gratiam convolaverint, libertati donentur. Item, ne Iudaei administratorio usu in ordinem viliorum atque actorum christianam familiam redigere audeant et de damnis eorum, quis talia ordinanda iniunxerint. Item ut Iudaeus ex aliis provinciis vel territoriis ad regni nostri ditionem pertinentibus veniens episcopo loci vel sacerdoti se praesentare non differat, vel quid huic in toto observare conveniat. Item qualiter concursus in Iudaeorum diebus instituti ad episcopum fieri debeant. Item ut quicumque Iudaeum secum obsequentem habuerit, expetente sacerdote, eum apud se retinere non audeat. Item ut cura omnis distringendi Iudaeos solis sacerdotibus debeatur. Item de damnis sacerdotum vel iudicum, qui Iudaeis instituta legum adimplere distulerunt. Item ne iudices quicquam de perfidorum excessibus extra sacerdotum conhibentiam iudicare praesumant. Item ut episcopi tunc immunes habeantur a damnis, cum eorum presbyteros ad ea, quae ipsi non correxerint, remiserint corrigenda. Item de servata principibus miserendi potestate in his, qui conversi ad fidem Christi veraciter fuerint. Item ut episcopi omnes Iudaeis ad se pertinentibus libellum de suis editum erroribus tradant et ut professiones eorum vel condiciones in scriniis ecclesiae condant.
Concerning the execrable perfidy of the Jews moreover, the laws newly published by the glorious prince, set forth with discrete sentences of titles, we have read through with vigilant intention of senses, and with a distinct weight of gravity we have approved their institutes. Therefore those which by the judgment of due reason have been published have been approved by synodal dignation; henceforth by the irrevocable order of judgments for their excesses they shall be held, that is: The laws which concerning the commemoration of the former ceremonies of the Jews and concerning transgressions have been promulgated, and concerning the new confirmation of them. Likewise concerning blasphemers of the holy Trinity. Likewise that Jews not subtract either themselves or their sons or servants from the grace of baptism. Likewise that Jews not celebrate Easter after their custom nor practice circumcisions of the flesh, nor turn anyone Christian away from the faith of Christ. Likewise that Jews not presume to celebrate the Sabbaths and other festivals of their rite. Likewise that every Jew on the Lord's days and on the fore-mentioned days cease from work. Likewise that Jews not judge foods after their custom. Likewise that Jews not contract marriages from proximity of their blood and that they not dare to marry without the blessing of a priest. Likewise that Jews, insulting our religion, not dare to defend their sect nor transfer themselves elsewhere fleeing from the faith, and that no one receive them fleeing. Likewise that a Christian receive no gift from a Jew against the faith of Christ. Likewise that Jews not dare to read books which the Christian faith rejects. Likewise that Christian servants not serve or adhere to Jews. Likewise if a Jew testifies himself to be a Christian and on this account does not wish to cast off from himself a Christian servant. Likewise the profession of Jews, how each one coming to the faith ought to write a little note of his profession. Likewise the conditions of Jews, which those ought to swear who from them coming to the faith shall have given their professions. Likewise concerning the Christian servants of Jews, which shall not have betrayed themselves as Christians, or concerning their betrayers. Likewise that Jews, having received any power from someone outside the royal ordination, not dare to command, beat, or constrain any Christian. Likewise that, if the servants of Jews, not yet converted, shall have fled to the grace of Christ, they be granted liberty. Likewise that Jews not dare by the use of administration to bring the Christian household into the order of bailiffs and stewards, and concerning their damages, who shall have ordered such things to be arranged. Likewise that a Jew coming from other provinces or territories belonging to the sway of our kingdom not delay to present himself to the bishop of the place or the priest, or what it is fitting to observe in this wholly. Likewise how meetings appointed on the days of Jews ought to be made to the bishop. Likewise that whoever shall have a Jew obeying him, the priest requesting, not dare to keep him with himself. Likewise that all care of constraining Jews is owed to the priests alone. Likewise concerning the damages of priests or judges who have delayed to fulfill the institutes of the laws for the Jews. Likewise that judges not presume to judge anything concerning the excesses of the perfidious ones without the concurrence of the priests. Likewise that bishops then be held immune from damages, when they shall have sent their presbyters to correct those things which they themselves have not corrected. Likewise concerning the power of the princes to be merciful being preserved in those who shall have truly been converted to the faith of Christ. Likewise that all bishops hand over to the Jews pertaining to themselves a little book composed concerning their errors, and that they deposit their professions or conditions in the archives of the church.

Quorum omnium legum promulgatio gravida sicut synodali iudicio comprobata, ita generali omnium nostrorum definitione in eorum erit deinceps excessibus exercenda.
Of all which laws, the promulgation, pregnant as it has been approved by synodal judgment, so by the general definition of all of us shall henceforth be exercised against their excesses.

CANON X
DE HIS, QUI AD ECCLESIAM CONFUGIUM FACIUNT
Pro his, qui quolibet metu vel terrore ecclesiam appetunt, consentiente pariter et iubente gloriosissimo domino nostro Ervigio rege hoc sanctum concilium definivit, ut nullus audeat confugientes ad ecclesiam vel residentes inde abstrahere aut quodcumque nocibilitatis vel damni seu spoliis residentibus in loco sancto inferre, sed esse potius his ipsis, qui ecclesiam petunt, per omnia licitum in triginta passus ab ecclesiae ianuis progredi, in quibus triginta passibus uniuscuiusque ecclesiae in toto circuitu reverentia defendantur; sic tamen, ut hi, qui ad eam confugiunt, in extraneis vel longe separatis ab ecclesia domibus nullo modo abscedant, sed in hoc triginta passuum numero absque domorum extranearum receptaculo progredientes aditum obtinebunt, qualiter ad requisitae naturae usum debitis exeant locis et nullo teneantur eventu necessitudinis, qui dominicis se defendendos commiserint claustris. Si quis autem hoc decretum violare temptaverit, et ecclesiasticae excommunicationi subiaceat et severitatis regiae ferietur sententia. Ipsos tamen, qui ad ecclesiam confugium fecerunt, si iuxta priscorum canonum instituta hi, qui eos repetunt, sacramenta reddiderint et sacerdos ecclesiae ipsius ab ecclesiae foribus non abstraxerit aut fuga talium, si evenerit, sacerdoti quaerenda est, aut damnorum sententia secundum electionem principis huiusmodi sacerdotibus irroganda.
Concerning those who by any fear or terror seek the church, the most glorious lord our king Ervigius consenting equally and commanding, this holy council has defined, that no one dare to drag away those fleeing to the church or residing there, or to inflict any harm or damage or spoils upon those residing in the holy place, but rather it be altogether lawful for these very ones who seek the church to go out thirty paces from the doors of the church, in which thirty paces the reverence of each church in the whole circuit shall be defended; yet in such a way, that those who flee to it do not depart at all into extraneous or far-separated houses, but going out within this number of thirty paces without the reception of extraneous houses they shall obtain entrance, so that for the use of required nature they may go out to due places and be held by no event of necessity, who have committed themselves to be defended by the Lord's enclosures. If anyone however shall have tried to violate this decree, let him both be subject to ecclesiastical excommunication and be struck by the sentence of royal severity. Those however who have fled to the church, if according to the institutes of the ancient canons those who seek them have given sacraments, and the priest of the church itself has not dragged them from the doors of the church, or if a flight of such shall have occurred, it is to be sought from the priest, or the sentence of damages according to the election of the prince is to be imposed upon such priests.

CANON XI
DE CULTORIBUS IDOLORUM
Praecepta domini sunt dicentis: Non facies tibi sculptile neque omnem similitudinem, quae est in caelo desuper et quae in terra deorsum, nec horum, quae sunt in aquis sub terra, non adorabis ea neque coles. Item: Qui immolat diis, occidetur, praeter domino soli. Item: Vir aut mulier, qui faciunt malum in conspectu domini dei sui et transgrediuntur pactum illius, ut vadant et serviant diis alienis et adorent eos vel solem et lunam et omnem militiam caeli, quae non praecepit, et hoc tibi fuerit nuntiatum audiensque inquisieris diligenter et verum esse repperieris, et abominatio facta est in Israel, educes virum ac mulierem, qui rem sceleratissimam perpetrarunt ad portas civitatis tuae et lapidibus obruetur. Praecepta haec domini non in ultione, sed in terrore delinquentium apponentes non mortis sententiam promulgamus, sed cultores idolorum, veneratores lapidum, accensores facularum et excolentes fontium admonemus, ut agnoscant, quod ipsi se spontaneae mortis subiciunt, qui diabolo sacrificare videntur. Mortis enim nomen diabolus appellatur, sicut de eo scriptum est: Et erat illi nomen mors. Ac proinde omne sacrilegium idolatriae vel quicquid illud est contra sanctam fidem, in quo insipientes homines captivatis diabolicis culturis inserviunt, sacerdotis vel iudicis instantia inventum, ut sacrilega eradantur et exterminata truncentur.
The precepts of the Lord are saying: You shall not make for yourself a graven image nor any likeness which is in heaven above and which is on earth beneath, nor of those which are in the waters under the earth, you shall not adore them nor worship them. Likewise: He who sacrifices to gods shall be killed, except to the Lord alone. Likewise: A man or woman who does evil in the sight of the Lord his God and transgresses his covenant, that they go and serve alien gods and adore them or the sun and moon and all the host of heaven, which he did not command, and this shall have been reported to you, hearing you shall have sought diligently and shall have found it to be true, and an abomination has been done in Israel, you shall bring forth the man and the woman who have perpetrated the most wicked thing to the gates of your city and he shall be overwhelmed with stones. These precepts of the Lord, applying not for vengeance but for the terror of those who are about to offend, we do not promulgate the sentence of death, but we admonish the worshippers of idols, the venerators of stones, the kindlers of torches, and the cultivators of fountains, that they recognize that they subject themselves to spontaneous death, who are seen to sacrifice to the devil. For death is the name of the devil, just as of him it is written: And his name was death. And therefore every sacrilege of idolatry or whatever it is against the holy faith, in which foolish men serve, having been captivated by diabolic worships, by the insistence of the priest or judge let it be found, that sacrilegious things be rooted out and, having been exterminated, be cut off.
¶46
Eos vero, qui ad talem errorem occurrunt, et verberibus coerceant et onustos ferro suis dominis tradant, si tamen domini eorum per iusiurandi attestationem promittant se eos tam sollicite custodire, ut ultra illis non liceat tale nefas committere. Quod si domini eorum nolunt huiusmodi reos in fide sua suscipere, tunc ab eis, a quibus coerciti sunt, regiis conspectibus praesententur, ut princeps aliis auctoritate ac liberam de talibus donandi potestatem obtineat. Domini tamen eorum, qui nuntiatos sibi talium servorum errores ulcisci noluerunt, et excommunicationis sententiam perferant et iura servi illius, quem coercere noluit, se amisisse cognoscant. Quod si ingenuorum personae his erroribus fuerint implicatae, et perpetuae excommunicationis sententia feriantur et altiori exilio ulciscantur.
Those moreover who run to such error, let them both be coerced with blows and, laden with iron, be handed over to their own masters, if yet their masters by the attestation of an oath shall promise that they will guard them so solicitously, that it may not be lawful for them thereafter to commit such nefariousness. But if their masters are unwilling to receive the guilty of this kind under their faith, then by those by whom they have been coerced let them be presented to the royal sights, that the prince may obtain by his authority and free power of donating concerning such. The masters however of those who have been unwilling to avenge the errors of such servants reported to them, let them both undergo the sentence of excommunication and let them know that they have lost the rights of that servant whom they were unwilling to coerce. But if persons of free birth have been implicated in these errors, let them both be struck with the sentence of perpetual excommunication and be punished by stricter exile.

CANON XII
DE INTERDICTO TEMPORIS CONSTITUTI, QUO DEBEAT CONCILIUM CELEBRARI
Placuit huic sancto concilio, ut iuxta priorum episcoporum singularum provinciarum canonum instituta annis singulis in unaquaque provincia Kal. Novembr. concilium celebraturi conveniant. Quisquis autem in praedictis Kal. Novembr. pro celebratione synodi venire distulerit, excommunicationi debitae subiacebit.
It has pleased this holy council, that according to the institutes of the canons of the prior bishops of the individual provinces, they convene in each province on the Kalends of November to celebrate a council every year. Whoever moreover shall have delayed to come on the aforesaid Kalends of November for the celebration of the synod, let him be subject to due excommunication.

CANON XIII
CONCLUSIO DEFINITIONUM, IN QUA ET DEO GRATIAE REFERUNTUR ET PRO PRINCIPE EXORATUR
Praemissis his omnibus synodalibus gestis, quae honesto decretorum fine complevimus, perpetuum his robur per manuum nostrarum subscriptionem annectimus dantes his gloriam et honorem immortali deo et domino nostro, quo inspirante et determinationis nostrae sententia viguit et probitas causarum honesto se fine complevit. Unde sanctae trinitatis poscimus inenarrabile nomen et gloriosam ineffabilis potentiae maiestatem, ut det amatori Christi serenissimo domino nostro atque amantissimo Ervigio principi, cuius iussu ad hunc meruimus advocari conventum, imperare clementer, regnare feliciter, habere de clementia fructum, obtinere de iustitia praemium, de pietate trophaeum, qui et hic invictus victor hostium semper appareat et post diuturna huius saeculi curricula ad regnum aeternum cum suis omnibus coronandus perveniat, praestante deo et salvatore Iesu Christo domino nostro, qui cum deo patre et sancto spiritu unus in trinitate vivit et regnat deus in saecula saeculorum. Amen.
All these synodal acts having been premised, which with an honest end of decrees we have completed, we attach to them perpetual strength by the subscription of our hands, giving for them glory and honor to the immortal God and our Lord, by whom inspiring both the sentence of our determination was strong and the probity of causes completed itself with an honest end. Whence we beseech the ineffable name of the holy Trinity and the glorious majesty of ineffable power, that it grant to the lover of Christ, the most serene lord and most amiable our prince Ervigius, by whose command we have merited to be called to this assembly, to command clemently, to reign happily, to have fruit from clemency, to obtain a reward from justice, a trophy from piety, who both here may appear the invincible conqueror of enemies, and after the long courses of this world may come crowned to the eternal kingdom with all his own, our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ granting, who with God the Father and the Holy Spirit, one in Trinity, lives and reigns as God unto ages of ages. Amen.

This holy council was completed on the eighth day before the Kalends of February, in the first year happily of the most serene lord our king Ervigius, in the era 719.

¶51
Quibus omnibus synodalibus gestis, decretis atque peractis et debitam reverentiam honoris impendimus et patulum auctoritatis nostrae vigorem his innectere procuramus. Ideoque praemissas has constitutiones synodicas a praesenti die vel tempore, id est ab octavo Kal. Februar., anno primo regni nostri nullus audeat contemnere, nullus etiam praeterire, nemo earundem constitutionum audeat iura convellere, nullus temerator haec decreta subvertere, nemo illicitator vel contemptor vigorem his institutionibus subtrahat, sed generaliter per cunctas regni nostri provincias haec canonum instituta nostrae gloriae temporibus acta et auctoritatis debitae fastigio praepolleant et irrevocabili iudiciorum exercitio, prout constituta sunt, in omnibus regni nostri provinciis celebres habebuntur. Si quis autem haec instituta contemnat, contemptorum se noverit damnari sententia, id est, ut iuxta voluntatem nostrae gloriae et excommunicatus a coetu nostro resiliat et insuper decimam partem facultatis suae fisci partibus sociandam amittat. Quod si nihil habuerit facultatis, unde praedictam compositionem exsolvere possit, absque aliquo infamio sui quinquaginta eum oportebit ictibus verberari aut semper infamis permaneat.
To which all synodal acts, decrees, and completed things we both have paid the due reverence of honor and we have procured to attach to them the manifest vigor of our authority. Therefore the above-premised synodal constitutions from this present day or time, that is from the eighth day before the Kalends of February, in the first year of our reign, let no one dare to despise, let no one also pass over, let no one of the same constitutions dare to overturn the rights, let no violator subvert these decrees, let no illegal or contemptuous person withdraw the vigor from these institutions, but generally through all the provinces of our kingdom these institutes of canons, done in the times of our glory, both shall prevail with the summit of due authority and, by the irrevocable exercise of judgments, just as they have been established, shall be held celebrated in all the provinces of our kingdom. If anyone however shall have despised these institutes, let him know himself to be damned by the sentence of those who despise, that is, that according to the will of our glory, both excommunicated, he may depart from our assembly, and moreover let him lose the tenth part of his own goods to be joined to the fiscal parts. But if he shall have nothing of goods from which he can pay the aforesaid composition, without any infamy of himself it will behoove him to be beaten with fifty blows, or let him remain always infamous.
¶52
Edita lex in confirmatione concilii Toletani sub die octavo Kal. Februar. anno feliciter primo regni gloriae nostrae. In nomine domini, Flavius Ervigius rex hoc legis nostrae edictum in confirmationem huius concilii promulgatum subscripsit.
The law published for the confirmation of the Toledan council, on the eighth day before the Kalends of February, in the first year happily of the reign of our glory. In the name of the Lord, Flavius Ervigius the king has subscribed this edict of our law promulgated for the confirmation of this council.
