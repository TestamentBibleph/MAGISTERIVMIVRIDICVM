Council of Hippo

Concilium Hipponense

393

Aurelius episcopus dixit: sancti fratris elesii nobis suggestio plenam sollicitudinem ac diligentiam oportet incutiat, ut omnis omnino cauillatio amputetur cunctis que excusationibus aditus omnino claudatur.
Bishop Aurelius said: The suggestion of our holy brother Elesius ought to incite full solicitude and diligence in us, so that every altogether cavillation may be cut off and to all excuses the entrance may be altogether closed.
¶1
Quare censendum est, si placet uestrae caritati, ut semper filii sint in potestate parentum ad que disciplinae regulam ab ipsis uel maxime episcopis seu clericis redigantur, nullum in minoribus annis filium debere ab episcopo uel clerico emancipatione a potestate patria liberari, nisi tantummodo illum cuius uitam mores que probauerit, ut iam legibus cum fuerit et uoluntatis suae arbiter, peccato ipse proprio possit astringi, ne eius malae conuersationis macula quisquam episcopus uel clericus pergatur.
Wherefore it must be decreed, if it pleases your charity, that sons always be in the power of parents, by whom to the rule of discipline they themselves especially by bishops or clerics may be reduced, that no son in his younger years ought by a bishop or cleric by emancipation from paternal power to be freed, unless only that one whose life and habits he shall have approved, so that already by the laws when he shall have been and of his own will the arbiter, by his own sin he may be able to be bound, lest by the stain of his evil conversion any bishop or cleric may be defiled.
¶2
Si quidem praeceptum, ut manentibus in errore cibum cum his minime capiatur, nec filiis debent de facultatibus suis aliquid derelinquere, quia melius est unus timens deum quam mille filii.
For indeed it is a precept that for those remaining in error, food with them not at all be taken, nor ought sons from their own goods anything to leave behind, because better it is one fearing God than a thousand sons.
¶3
Epigonius episcopus dixit: omnis incontinentia quae in abscondito exercetur, ne palam publicata damnetur, uolumus itaque aliqua disciplina sauciari: lectores dicimus pubescentes coartari debere, ut matrimonia suscipiant aut certe sanctimonia profiteantur.
Bishop Epigonius said: All incontinence which in secret is exercised, lest openly having been published it be condemned, we wish therefore by some discipline to be wounded. We say that lectors who are reaching puberty ought to be constrained, that they either undertake marriages or certainly profess sanctimonia.
¶4
Sin uero uoluntate praua perseuerauerint, suspendi eos oportere a lectione usque eundem diem ut, aut uxores ducant aut, si noluerint uxores ducere, professionem continentiae suae deuouerint.
But if by a perverse will they shall have persisted, it is fitting that they be suspended from the reading until that same day, so that they either may take wives or, if they shall have been unwilling to take wives, the profession of their continence they may have vowed.
¶5
Epigonius episcopus dixit: additur aliquid quod non seiungatur de hoc titulo: saepe, patientibus propositis, uidimus lectores in ecclesiis (---). Si hoc placet mentibus uestris, qui secundam acceperit, a lectione ex hodierno die arceatur.
Bishop Epigonius said: Something is added which is not separated from this title. Often, with propositi suffering, we have seen lectors in the churches (---). If this pleases your minds, let him who has taken a second [wife] be kept away from the reading from this day onward.
¶6
Ab uniuersis episcopis dictum est: omnibus placet ut deinceps, si quis lector duas uxores habuerit, ab lectionis officio sit remotus.
By all the bishops it was said: It pleases all that hereafter, if any lector shall have had two wives, from the office of reading he be removed.
¶7
Aurelius episcopus dixit: sicut frater et collega noster saturninus salubri consideratione deprompsit, debent episcopi, non postquam pranderint, sed ieiuni populis ieiunis, quacumque hora, diuina celebrare mysteria.
Bishop Aurelius said: Just as our brother and colleague Saturninus with salutary consideration has set forth, bishops ought not after they shall have dined, but fasting to fasting peoples, at whatever hour, the divine mysteries to celebrate.
¶8
Si uero sumpserint cibos, pm cuiuscumque laici siue episcopi conmendantes, oratione eum tantummodo prosequantur.
But if they shall have taken foods, --- of whatever layman or bishop commending, with prayer him only they may accompany.
¶9
Illud autem quoniam praesentibus corporibus nonnulli audeant sacrificia celebrare et partem corporis sancti cum exanimi cadauere communicare arbitror prohibendum.
But this, because in the presence of bodies certain ones dare to celebrate sacrifices and a part of the sacred body with a lifeless corpse to communicate, I consider to be prohibited.
¶10
Superest ut, si placet, uestra sanctitas censeat.
It remains that, if it pleases, your sanctity may decree.
¶11
Ab uniuersis episcopis dictum est: sanctitatis uestrae prosecutio omnibus placet, quam nostro confirmamus consensu.
By all the bishops it was said: The prosecution of your sanctity pleases all, which we confirm by our consensus.
¶12
Ab uniuersis episcopis dictum est: omnibus placet ut scripturae canonicae quae lectae sunt, sed et passiones martyrum, sui cuiusque locis, in ecclesiis praedicentur.
By all the bishops it was said: It pleases all that the canonical scriptures which have been read, but also the passions of the martyrs, each in its own places, in the churches be proclaimed.
¶13

This completes the Documenta of the Council of Hippo (393) .
