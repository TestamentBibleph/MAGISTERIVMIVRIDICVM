Eleventh Council of Toledo

Concilium Toletanum XI

675

INCIPIT CONCILIUM TOLETANUM XI
INCIPIUNT GESTA SYNODALIA CONCILII TOLETANI XI ACTA IN URBE REGIA IN TOLETANA SEDE A DECIM ET SEPTEM EPISCOPIS ANNO IIII REGNI GLORIOSI PRINCIPIS WAMBANI SUB DIE VII ID. NOVEMBR. ERA DCCXIII.
In the name of the Lord begin the synodal acts of the Eleventh Council of Toledo, held in the royal city in the Toledan see by seventeen bishops, in the fourth year of the reign of the glorious prince Wamba, on the seventh day before the Ides of November, in the era 713.
¶1
In nomine sanctae trinitatis collectis in unum Carthaginis provinciae sacerdotibus in Toletana urbe in beatae matris domini Mariae virginis sede anno quarto excellentissimi ac religiosi Wambani principis sub die VII Id. Nov. res votivi gaudii et dies nimium optatae gratulationis occurrit, in quo nobis datum est conspicere pariter et deflere, quid lacrimarum de praeteritis domino debeamus. Eramus enim hucusque prolabentis saeculi colluvione instabiles, quia annosa series temporum subtracta luce conciliorum non tam vitia aderat quam matrem omnium errorum ignorantiam otiosis mentibus ingerebat.
In the name of the holy Trinity, the priests of the province of Carthage having been gathered together in the city of Toledo in the see of the blessed mother of the Lord, the virgin Mary, in the fourth year of the most excellent and religious prince Wamba, on the seventh day before the Ides of November, the day of votive joy and of exceedingly desired gladness occurred, in which it was given to us together both to see and to weep, what tears for past things we ought to the Lord. For we had been hitherto unstable by the defilement of a slipping world, because the aged series of times, the light of councils having been withdrawn, did not so much present vices as it poured into idle minds ignorance, the mother of all errors.
¶2
Cernentibus enim quomodo Babylonicae confusionis olla succensa nunc tempora conciliorum averteret, nunc sacerdotes domini de resolutis moribus irretiret, purpuratae enim meretricis sequebantur invitamenta, quia ecclesiastici conventus non aderat disciplina, nec erat, qui errantium exercitationem magis nos ad intellegendum redderet verborum simplex collatio quam relata condensae lectionis instructio, quia et revera tantae rei mysterium ita sacerdotes dei convenit nosse, ut non superficie verborum efferantur incogniti, sed sensus sanae intellegentiae repperiantur instructi, ut in disserendo praecipue huius sanctae trinitatis arcana plus evidentia quam eloquentia eos efficiat saporatos. Sic enim et divini muneris dono adacti, ut iuxta votum definitionis aeternae promissio monstraretur in opere. Unde quod primo die praesidentis metropolitani lingua praefudit, die tertia omnium nostrum vox singillatim collective repetiit.
For while seeing how the pot of Babylonian confusion, now turned away the times of councils, now entangled the priests of the Lord with dissolved morals, they followed the allurements of the purple-clad harlot, because the discipline of the ecclesiastical assembly was not present, nor was there anyone who the exercise of those erring would correct. The simple collation of words rendered us more to understanding than the instruction of dense reading brought back, because indeed the mystery of so great a matter it behooves the priests of God so to know, that they may not be carried away unknown by the surface of words, but may be found instructed by the sense of sound understanding, so that in discussing especially the arcana of this holy Trinity, more evident than eloquent might make them wise. For thus also by the gift of the divine gift driven, that according to the vow the promise of eternal definition might be shown in the work. Whence what on the first day the tongue of the presiding metropolitan poured forth, on the third day the voice of all of us singly and collectively repeated.
¶3
Iste ergo est tenor fidei nostrae, qui et a capite copiose profluxit et a membris probatus gloriose emicuit: cum sermo divinus haberetur extorris et, quia non erat adunandorum pontificum ulla praeceptio, crescebat in maius vita deterior. Cum tandem divina nos clementia ex alto respiciens asperitatis nostrae se occursuram praebuit et saluti praeparans nostris saeculis religiosi principis mentem devotam pariter et instructam, cuius providae sollicitudinis voto et lux conciliorum renovata resplenduit et alterna caritas se mutuo in corrigendis vel instruendis moribus excitavit, dum et aggregandi nobis hortatu principis religiosi facultas est data et opportuna corrigendis praeparata est disciplina, ut, qui decursis longe ante temporibus post decem et octo scilicet labentium annorum excursum in unum meruimus aggregari conventum, mereri possimus spirituali gratia sanitatem.
This therefore is the tenor of our faith, which both from the head copiously flowed forth and by the members proved gloriously shone forth: when the divine word was held an exile, and because there was no command for gathering pontiffs, the worse life grew into greater. When at length divine clemency, looking down from on high, offered itself to meet our harshness and preparing for salvation, for our times the mind of the religious prince both devoted and instructed, by whose provident care's vow both the light of councils renewed shone forth and mutual love roused itself mutually in correcting and instructing morals, while both the faculty of assembling us by the exhortation of the religious prince was given, and opportunity for correcting was prepared, so that we who after the course of long before times, after the course of eighteen slipping years, have merited to be gathered in one assembly, might merit by spiritual grace health.
¶4
Nec enim numerus iste alienus est a salute. Sic quippe mulier illa in evangelio ter senis annorum excursibus curva, quae figuram totius generis humani gestabat, sub sacramento huius numeri saluti pristinae sanare donatur. Nos igitur per tot annos curvo nostri ordinis persistente statu in eo, quod nulla nos conciliorum definitio iungeret, nullus etiam conventus ecclesiastici ordinis adunaret, tandem divinae voluntatis imperio et religiosi principis iussu evocati in Toletanam urbem convenimus. Qui cum in ecclesia beatae virginis Mariae debitis sedibus locaremur, inter cetera, quae subterius discreto capitulorum ordine sunt digesta, non aliunde primum coepimus habere sermonem nisi de fidei puritate, ut, quia initiandis ad beatam vitam hominibus haec prima semper est via salutis praeviaque nostris fieret praeceptis.
For neither is this number alien from salvation. For thus that woman in the Gospel with the course of three times six years bent, who bore the figure of the whole human race, under the sacrament of this number is granted to be healed to pristine health. We therefore for so many years, the bent state of our order persisting in that no definition of councils joined us, nor any assembly of the ecclesiastical order gathered us, at length by the command of the divine will and by the order of the religious prince called, into the city of Toledo we came together. Who when in the church of the blessed virgin Mary in due seats we were placed, among other things which below in a discrete order of chapters are digested, not from elsewhere did we first begin to have discourse than concerning the purity of faith, that, because for those being initiated to blessed life, this is always the first way of salvation and might be made preceding to our precepts.
¶5
Unde sacrae instructionis arcana sanctorum Niceni, scilicet Constantinopolitani, Ephesini atque Chalcedonensium conciliorum monita amplectentes, per quae et radicitus hereticorum falsa conciliabula destruuntur et fidei catholicae norma declaratur, haec communi alterno animorum iudicio definimus, ut hanc ipsam nostrae fidei regulam verbis simplicibus niteremur alternatim nobis singulariterque proferre, ita ut, quicquid per triduum de huiusmodi quaestionibus unicuique nostrum lectionis memoria ministraret, prout animis memoria occurreret, omnis sobrietatis compendio simplici notaretur stilo, relatio tamen ipsius sacramenti pura et evidens a capite primum inciperet et sic ad membra reliqua perveniret, nullas obscuritatis in se lineas habens, nullas etiam inusitatae locutionis regulas continens, sed puritas sola esset clara sermonum, quae posset evidentiam exprimere suamque exercitationem.
Whence embracing the arcana of sacred instruction of the holy councils of Nicaea, clearly of Constantinople, of Ephesus, and of Chalcedon, through which both the false assemblies of heretics are destroyed root and branch and the norm of catholic faith is declared, this by common alternate judgment of minds we define, that we would strive to bring forth this same rule of our faith in simple words, alternately among ourselves and singly, so that whatever through three days concerning matters of this sort to each one of us the memory of reading might minister, as it would occur to the minds and memory, all with the brevity of sobriety would be noted in simple style, yet the account of the sacrament itself, pure and evident, would begin first from the head and thus to the remaining members would come, having no lines of obscurity in itself, nor containing any rules of unusual speech, but purity alone would be clear of discourses, which could express evidentness and its own exercise.
¶6
CONFESSION OF FAITH
Confitemur et credimus sanctam atque ineffabilem trinitatem, patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum, unum deum naturaliter esse, unius substantiae, unius naturae, unius quoque maiestatis atque virtutis; et patrem quidem non genitum, non creatum, sed ingenitum profitemur. Ipse enim a nullo originem ducit, ex quo et filius nativitatem et spiritus sanctus processionem accepit. Fons ergo ipse et origo est totius divinitatis. Ipse quoque pater essentia quidem ineffabilis substantiae suae filium ineffabiliter genuit, nec tamen aliud quam, quod ipse est, genuit, deum deum, lucem lucem. Ab ipso ergo est omnis paternitas in caelo et in terra. Filium quoque de substantia patris sine initio ante saecula natum, nec tamen factum esse fatemur, quia nec pater sine filio, nec filius aliquando exstitit sine patre, et tamen non sicut filius de patre, ita pater de filio, quia non pater a filio, sed filius a patre generationem accepit, filius ergo deus de patre est, pater autem deus, sed non de filio, ille autem filius patris et deus de patre, aequalis tamen per omnia filius deo patri, quia nec nasci coepit aliquando nec desiit. Hic etiam unius cum patre substantiae creditur, propter quod et homousion patri dicitur, hoc est eiusdem cum patre substantiae. Omo enim graece unum, usia vero substantia dicitur, quod utrumque coniunctum sonat una substantia. Neque enim de nihilo neque de aliqua alia substantia, sed de patris utero, id est de substantia eius idem filius genitus vel natus esse credendus est, sempiternus et filius.
We confess and believe the holy and ineffable Trinity, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God naturally to be, of one substance, of one nature, of one also majesty and power; and the Father indeed not begotten, not created, but unbegotten we profess. For he from no one derives origin, from whom both the Son received nativity and the Holy Spirit procession. He therefore is the fountain and origin of all divinity. He also the Father of his own ineffable substance indeed begot the Son ineffably, nor yet anything else than that which he himself is did he beget: God from God, light from light. From him therefore is all paternity in heaven and on earth. The Son also from the substance of the Father without beginning before ages born, nor yet made, we confess, because neither the Father without the Son, nor the Son ever existed without the Father, and yet not as the Son from the Father, so the Father from the Son, because not the Father from the Son, but the Son from the Father received generation. Therefore the Son is God from the Father, the Father indeed is God, but not from the Son. Moreover the Son of the Father and God from the Father, equal yet through all things the Son to God the Father, because he neither began to be born at any time nor ceased. He also of one substance with the Father is believed, on account of which he is called homoousion to the Father, that is, of the same with the Father substance. For "homo" in Greek means one, "usia" indeed is called substance, which when joined sounds "one substance." For neither from nothing nor from any other substance, but from the womb of the Father, that is from his substance, is the same Son to be believed to have been begotten or born, eternal and Son.
¶7
Quod si semper pater fuit, semper habuit filium, cuius pater esset; et ob hoc filium de patre natum sine initio confitemur, nec enim eundem filium dei pro eo, quod de patre sit genitus, defectae naturae portiunculam nominamus, sed perfectum patrem, perfectum filium sine minutione, sine defectione genuisse asserimus, quia solius divinitatis est inaequalem filium non habere. Hic etiam filius dei natura est filius, non optione, quem deus pater nec voluntate nec necessitate genuisse credendus est, quia nec ulla in deo necessitas cadit nec voluntas sapientiam praevenit.
For if the Father always was, always he had the Son, of whom he was Father; and on this account the Son born of the Father without beginning we confess. For neither do we call the same Son of God, because he was begotten of the Father, a portion of a defective nature, but we assert that the perfect Father begot the perfect Son without diminution, without defect, because of divinity alone it is to have an unequal Son not to have. This Son of God is Son by nature, not by choice, whom God the Father neither by will nor by necessity is to be believed to have begotten, because neither does any necessity fall upon God nor does will precede wisdom.
¶8
Spiritum quoque sanctum, qui est tertia in trinitate persona, unum atque aequalem cum deo patre et filio credimus esse deum unius substantiae, unius quoque esse naturae, non tamen genitum vel creatum, sed ab utrisque procedentem amborum esse spiritum. Hic etiam spiritus sanctus nec ingenitus nec genitus creditur, ne aut, si ingenitum dixerimus, duos patres dicamus, aut si genitum, duos filios praedicare monstremur, qui tamen nec patris tantum nec filii tantum, sed simul patris et filii dicitur. Nec enim de patre procedit in filium vel de filio procedit ad sanctificandam creaturam, sed simul ab utrisque processisse monstratur, quia caritas sive sanctitas amborum esse agnoscitur. Hic igitur spiritus sanctus missus ab utrisque, sicut filius creditur, sed minor a patre et filio non habetur, sicut filius propter assumptam carnem minorem se patre et spiritu sancto esse testatur.
The Holy Spirit also, who is the third person in the Trinity, we believe to be one and equal with God the Father and the Son, to be God of one substance, of one also nature, yet not begotten nor created, but proceeding from both, to be the Spirit of both. This Holy Spirit also is believed neither unbegotten nor begotten, lest either, if we should call him unbegotten, we should speak of two Fathers, or if begotten, we should be shown to proclaim two Sons, who yet is called not of the Father only nor of the Son only, but at once of the Father and of the Son. For he proceeds not from the Father into the Son nor from the Son to sanctify creation, but is shown to have proceeded from both at once, because charity or sanctity is acknowledged to be of both. This Holy Spirit therefore is sent by both, as the Son is believed, but is not held to be less than the Father and the Son, as the Son on account of assumed flesh testifies himself to be less than the Father and the Holy Spirit.
¶9
Haec est sanctae trinitatis relata narratio, quae non triplex, sed trinitas et dici et credi debet. Nec recte dici potest, ut in uno deo sit trinitas, sed unus deus trinitas. In relativis vero personarum nominibus pater ad filium, filius ad patrem, spiritus sanctus ad utrosque refertur, quae cum relativae tres personae dicantur, una tamen natura vel substantia creditur; nec sicut tres personas, ita tres substantias praedicamus, sed unam substantiam, tres autem personas. Quod enim pater est, non ad se, sed ad filium, et quod filius est, non ad se, sed ad patrem est; similiter et spiritus sanctus non ad se, sed ad patrem et filium relative refertur in eo, quod spiritus patris et filii praedicatur. Item cum dicimus deus, non ad aliquid dicitur, sicut pater ad filium vel filius ad patrem vel spiritus sanctus ad patrem et filium, sed ad se specialiter dicitur deus. Nam si de singulis personis interrogemur, deum necesse est fateamur. Deus ergo pater, deus filius, deus spiritus sanctus singulariter dicitur, nec tamen tres dii, sed unus est deus. Item et pater omnipotens et filius omnipotens et spiritus sanctus omnipotens singulariter dicitur, nec tamen tres omnipotentes, sed unus omnipotens, sicut et unum lumen unumque principium praedicatur.
This is the recounted account of the holy Trinity, which is to be called and to be believed not triplex but Trinity. Nor can it be rightly said that in one God there is Trinity, but one God is Trinity. In the relative names of persons indeed the Father is referred to the Son, the Son to the Father, the Holy Spirit to both, which, though they are called three relative persons, yet one nature or substance is believed; nor as three persons, so do we preach three substances, but one substance, three persons. For what the Father is, is not to himself, but to the Son; and what the Son is, is not to himself, but to the Father; similarly also the Holy Spirit is referred not to himself but to the Father and the Son relatively in that he is preached as the Spirit of the Father and of the Son. Likewise when we say "God," it is not said relatively, as the Father to the Son or the Son to the Father or the Holy Spirit to the Father and the Son, but it is said of God to himself specially. For if concerning each person we are asked, we are compelled to confess God. Therefore the Father is God, the Son is God, the Holy Spirit is God, each said singly, nor yet three Gods, but one is God. Likewise also the Father is almighty and the Son is almighty and the Holy Spirit is almighty, each said singly, nor yet three almighties, but one almighty, just as one light and one principle is preached.
¶10
Singulariter ergo et unaquaeque persona unus deus confitetur et creditur, una illi et indivisa atque aequalis deitas, maiestatis sive potestas, nec minoratur in singulis, nec augetur in tribus, quia nec minus aliquid habet, cum unaquaeque persona deus singulariter dicitur, nec amplius, cum totae tres personae unus deus enuntiatur. Haec ergo sancta trinitas, quae unus et verus est deus, nec recedit a numero nec capitur numero. In relatione enim personarum numerus cernitur, in divinitatis vero substantia, quid enumeratum sit, non compraehenditur. Ergo hoc solum numerum insinuant, quod ad invicem sunt, et in hoc numero carent, quod in se sunt. Nam ita huic sanctae trinitati unum naturale convenit nomen, ut tribus personis non possit esse plurale. Ob hoc ergo credimus illud in sacris litteris dictum: Magnus dominus noster et magna virtus eius et sapientiae eius non est numerus. Nec quia tres has personas esse diximus unum deum, eundem esse patrem quem filium, vel eum esse filium, qui est pater, aut eum, qui est spiritus sanctus, vel patrem et filium dicere poterimus.
Therefore each person singly is confessed and believed as one God, and to him there is undivided and equal deity, majesty, and power, nor is it lessened in the single persons, nor increased in the three, because neither does it have anything less when each person is called God singly, nor anything more when all three persons are pronounced one God. This holy Trinity therefore, which is one and true God, neither recedes from number nor is comprehended by number. For in the relation of persons number is discerned, but in the substance of divinity, what is enumerated is not comprehended. Therefore this alone intimates number, that which they are to each other, and in this they lack number, that which they are in themselves. For thus to this holy Trinity one natural name applies, that for three persons it cannot be plural. On this account therefore we believe that which is said in the sacred letters: Great is our Lord and great is his power, and of his wisdom there is no number. Nor because we have said these three persons to be one God, do we say that the same is the Father whom the Son is, or that he is the Son who is the Father, or that he who is the Holy Spirit is the Father or the Son, for we cannot call him the Father and the Son.
¶11
Non enim ipse pater est filius nec spiritus sanctus ipse, qui est vel pater vel filius, cum tamen ipsum sit pater quod filius, ipsum filius quod pater, ipsum pater et filius quod spiritus sanctus, id est natura unus deus. Cum enim dicimus non ipsum esse patrem quem filium, ad personalem distinctionem refertur, cum autem dicamus ipsum esse patrem quod filium et ipsum filium quod patrem, ipsum spiritum sanctum quod patrem et filium, ad naturam, qua deus est vel substantiam pertinere monstratur, quia substantia unum sunt, personas enim distinguimus, non deitatem separamus. Trinitatem igitur in personarum distinctione agnoscimus, potestatem propter naturam vel substantiam profitemur. Tria ergo ista unum sunt, natura scilicet non persona. Nec tamen tres istae personae separabiles aestimandae sunt, cum nulla ante aliam, nulla sine alia vel exstitisse vel quippiam operasse aliquando creditur. Inseparabiles enim inveniuntur et in eo, quod sunt, et in eo, quod faciunt, quia inter generantem patrem et generatum filium vel procedentem spiritum sanctum nullum fuisse credimus temporis intervallum, quo aut genitor genitum aliquando praecederet aut genitus genitori deesset, aut procedens spiritus patri vel filio posterior appareret.
For not the Father is the Son nor the Holy Spirit is he who is the Father or the Son, yet the Father is the same as the Son, and the Son is the same as the Father, and the Father and the Son are the same as the Holy Spirit, that is, by nature one God. For when we say not the same is the Father whom the Son is, it refers to the personal distinction; when moreover we say the same is the Father as the Son and the same Son as the Father, and the same Holy Spirit as the Father and the Son, it is shown to pertain to the nature by which he is God or to the substance, because in substance they are one. For we distinguish persons, we do not separate deity. Trinity therefore we acknowledge in the distinction of persons, power we profess on account of nature or substance. Therefore these three are one, by nature clearly not by person. Nor yet are these three persons to be thought separable, since none before another, none without another either ever existed or is believed at any time to have done anything. For they are found inseparable both in that which they are and in that which they do, because between the Father generating and the Son generated or the Holy Spirit proceeding we believe there was no interval of time, by which either the generator might ever precede the generated, or the generated be lacking to the generator, or the proceeding Spirit appear later to the Father or the Son.
¶12
Ob hoc ergo inseparabilis et inconfusa haec trinitas a nobis et creditur et praedicatur. Tres igitur personae istae dicuntur, iuxta quod maiores definiunt, ut agnoscantur. Nam si attendamus illud, quod scriptura sancta dicit de sapientia: Splendor est lucis aeternae, sicut splendorem lucis videmus inseparabiliter inhaerere, sic confitemur filium a patre separari non posse. Tres ergo illas unius atque inseparabilis naturae personas, sicut non confundimus, ita separabiles nulla tenus praedicamus. Quando quidem ita nobis hoc dignitatem ipsa trinitas evidenter ostendit, ut etiam in omnibus, quibus voluit, singillatim personas agnosci. Unam sine altera non permittat intellegi, nec enim pater absque filio cognoscitur, nec sine patre filius invenitur. Relatio quippe ipsa vocaboli personalis personas separari vetat, quae etiam non simul nominat, simul insinuat. Nemo autem audire potest unum quodque istorum nominum, in quo non intellegere cogatur et alterum. Cum igitur haec tria sint unum et in unum tria, est tamen unicuique personae manens sua proprietas. Pater enim aeternitatem habet sine nativitate, filius aeternitatem cum nativitate, spiritus vero sanctus processionem sine nativitate.
On account of this therefore inseparable and inconfused this Trinity both is believed and preached by us. Three therefore these persons are called, according to which the greater define, that they may be known. For if we attend to that which holy Scripture says of wisdom: She is the brightness of eternal light, just as we see brightness inseparably inhere, so we confess that the Son cannot be separated from the Father. Therefore those three persons of one and inseparable nature, as we do not confound, so by no means do we call separable. Since indeed thus the Trinity itself shows its dignity to us, that also in all whom it willed, it might be known person by person. It does not permit one without the other to be understood, for neither is the Father known without the Son, nor is the Son found without the Father. For the very relation of the personal word forbids the persons to be separated, which even when it does not name them together, intimates them together. No one moreover can hear any one of these names in which he is not compelled to understand also the other. Since therefore these three are one and in one three, yet to each person abides its own property. For the Father has eternity without nativity, the Son has eternity with nativity, the Holy Spirit indeed has procession without nativity.
¶13
De his tribus personis solam filii personam pro liberatione humani generis hominem verum sine peccato de sancta et immaculata Maria virgine credimus assumpsisse, de qua novo ordine novaque nativitate est genitus, novo ordine, qua invisibilis divinitate visibilis monstraretur in carne, nova autem nativitate est genitus, quia intacta virginitas et virilem coitum nescivit et fecundatam per spiritum sanctum carnis materiam ministravit. Qui partus virginis nec ratione colligitur nec exemplo, erit singulare. Nec tamen spiritus sanctus pater esse credendus est filii pro eo, quod Maria eodem sancto spiritu obumbrante concepit, ne duo patres filii videamur asserere, quod utique nefas est dici.
Of these three persons we believe that the person of the Son alone for the liberation of the human race assumed true man without sin from the holy and immaculate virgin Mary, of whom by a new order and by a new nativity he was begotten: by a new order, because invisible in divinity he might be shown visible in flesh; by a new nativity moreover he was begotten, because intact virginity both knew not manly coition and ministered the material of flesh, having been made fruitful by the Holy Spirit. Which birth of a virgin is neither grasped by reason nor by example, for it will be singular. Nor yet is the Holy Spirit to be believed to be the Father of the Son on account of that, because Mary conceiving by the same Holy Spirit overshadowing, lest we be seen to assert two Fathers of the Son, which indeed it is nefarious to say.
¶14
In quo mirabili conceptu aedificans sibi sapientia domum, verbum caro factum est et habitavit in nobis. Nec tamen verbum ipsum ita in carnem conversum atque mutatum est, ut desisteret deus esse, qui homo esse voluisset, ut non tantum ibi sit verbum dei et hominis anima, atque hoc totum et deus dicatur propter deum et homo propter hominem. In quo dei filio duas credimus esse naturas, unam divinitatis, alteram humanitatis, quae sibi in una Christi persona univit, ut nec divinitas ab humanitate nec humanitas a divinitate possit aliquando seiungi. Unde perfectus deus, perfectus et homo in unitate personae. Nec tamen, quia duas diximus in filio esse naturas, duas causabimur in eo esse personas, ne trinitati, quod absit, accidere videatur quaternitas.
In which wonderful conception, Wisdom building for itself a house, the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us. Nor yet was the Word itself so converted and changed into flesh that it ceased to be God, who willed to be man, so that not only the Word of God and the soul of man are there, and this whole is called both God on account of God and man on account of man. In which Son of God we believe there are two natures, one of divinity, the other of humanity, which in one person of Christ he united, that neither divinity from humanity nor humanity from divinity can ever be separated. Whence perfect God, perfect also man in the unity of person. Nor yet, because we have said that there are two natures in the Son, shall we be accused of there being two persons in him, lest quaternity, which may it be absent, might seem to happen to the Trinity. For God the Word did not assume the person of man, but the nature, and in eternity the person of divinity received the temporal substance of flesh.
¶15
Item unius substantiae credimus esse deum patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum, non tamen dicimus, ut huius trinitatis unitatem Maria virgo genuerit, sed tantum modo filium, qui solus naturam nostram in unitate personae suae assumpsit. Incarnationem quoque huius filii dei tota trinitas operata esse credenda est, quia inseparabilia sunt opera trinitatis. Solus tamen filius formam servi accepit in singularitate personae, non in unitate divinae naturae, in id, quod est proprium filii, non quod commune trinitati. Quae forma illi ad unitatem personae coaptata est, ut filius dei et filius hominis unus sit Christus.
Likewise we believe that God the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are of one substance, nor yet do we say that the virgin Mary bore the unity of this Trinity, but only the Son, who alone assumed our nature in the unity of his person. The incarnation also of this Son of God is to be believed to have been wrought by the whole Trinity, because the works of the Trinity are inseparable. Yet the Son alone received the form of a servant in the singularity of his person, not in the unity of the divine nature, in that which is proper to the Son, not which is common to the Trinity. Which form was fitted to him into the unity of person, that the Son of God and the Son of man might be one Christ.
¶16
Item idem Christus in his duabus naturis tribus exstat substantiis, verbi, quod ad solius dei essentiam referendum est, corporis et animae, quod ad verum hominem pertinet. Habet igitur in se geminam substantiam divinitatis suae et humanitatis nostrae. Hic tamen per hoc, quod de deo patre sine initio prodiit natus tantum, nam neque factus neque praedestinatus accipitur; per hoc tamen, quod de Maria virgine natus est, et natus et factus et praedestinatus esse credendus est. Ambae tamen illae generationes mirabiles, quia et de patre ante saecula sine matre est genitus et in fine saeculorum de matre sine patre est generatus. Qui tamen, secundum quod homo creatus est, Mariae matris est filius. Item, per hoc quod deus est, aequalis patri, per hoc quod homo, minor est patre. Item et maior minor se ipso esse credendus est.
Likewise the same Christ in these two natures exists in three substances: of the Word, which is to be referred to the essence of God alone; of the body and of the soul, which pertains to true man. He has therefore in himself the twofold substance of his divinity and of our humanity. Yet he, through that which from God the Father without beginning he came forth, born only, for he is received neither made nor predestined; through that which from the virgin Mary he was born, he is to be believed both born and made and predestined. Both those generations are wonderful, because both from the Father before ages without mother he was begotten, and in the end of ages from a mother without a father he was generated. Who yet, according as he was created man, is the son of Mary the mother. Likewise, through that which he is God, he is equal to the Father; through that which he is man, he is less than the Father. Likewise also he is to be believed greater and less than himself.
¶17
Forma enim dei etiam ipse filius se ipso maior est propter humanitatem assumptam, qua divinitas maior est. In forma autem servi se ipso minor est, id est humanitate, quae minor divinitate accipitur. Nam sicut per assumptam carnem non tantum a patre, sed seipso minor accipitur, ita secundum divinitatem, qua aequalis est patri, et ipse et pater maior est homine, quem sola filii persona assumpsit. Item in eo, quod quaeritur, utrum possit filius sic aequalis et minor esse spiritui sancto, sicut nunc aequalis, nunc minor patre creditur esse, respondemus: Secundum formam dei aequalis est et patri et spiritui sancto, secundum formam servi minor est et a patre et ab spiritu sancto, quia nec spiritus sanctus nec deus pater, sed sola filii persona suscepit carnem, per quam minor esse creditur illis personis duabus.
For the form of God, even the Son himself, is greater than himself on account of the assumed humanity, by which divinity is greater. In the form of a servant indeed he is less than himself, that is in humanity, which is received as less than divinity. For just as through assumed flesh he is received as less not only than the Father but also than himself, so according to divinity, by which he is equal to the Father, both he and the Father are greater than man, whom the person of the Son alone assumed. Likewise in that which it is asked whether the Son can be thus equal and less than the Holy Spirit, as now equal, now less than the Father he is believed to be, we respond: According to the form of God he is equal both to the Father and to the Holy Spirit; according to the form of a servant he is less both than the Father and than the Holy Spirit, because neither the Holy Spirit nor God the Father, but the person of the Son alone received flesh, by which he is believed to be less than those two persons.
¶18
Item hic filius a deo patre et sancto spiritu inseparabiliter discretus creditur esse persona, ab homine autem assumpto natura, item, cum homine unus exstat persona, cum patre vero et spiritu sancto natura divinitatis suae. Filius tamen non solum a patre, sed et spiritu sancto missus esse credendus est in eo, quod per prophetam dicitur: Et nunc dominus misit me et spiritus eius. A se ipso quoque missus accipitur pro eo, quod inseparabilis non solum voluntatis, sed operatio totius trinitatis agnoscitur. Hinc enim, qui ante saecula unigenitus est vocatus, temporaliter primogenitus factus est, unigenitus propter deitatis substantiam, primogenitus propter assumptae carnis formam, in qua suscepit hominis formam iuxta evangelicam veritatem. Sine peccato mortuus creditur, qui solus pro nobis passionem ipsam, salva tamen divinitate sua, pro delictis nostris sustinuit mortique adiudicatus et cruci veram carnis mortem excepit, tertio quoque die virtute propria suscitatus a sepulchro surrexit.
Likewise this Son is believed to be separated in person from God the Father and the Holy Spirit inseparably, from assumed man by nature, likewise with man he exists as one person, but with the Father and the Holy Spirit by the nature of his divinity. Yet the Son is to be believed to have been sent not only from the Father but also from the Holy Spirit in that which through the prophet it is said: And now the Lord has sent me and his Spirit. From himself also he is received as sent on account of that which the operation, not only of will but also of the whole Trinity, is acknowledged inseparable. For hence he who before ages was called only-begotten, in time he was called firstborn: only-begotten on account of the substance of deity, firstborn on account of the assumed flesh, in which he received the form of man according to the evangelical truth. Without sin he is believed to have died, who alone for us endured passion itself, yet with his divinity preserved, for our sins, and being handed over to death and to the cross he underwent true death of the flesh, and on the third day by his own power raised up he rose from the sepulcher.
¶19
Hoc ergo exemplo capitis nostri confitemur vera fide resurrectionem carnis omnium mortuorum nec in aere vel qualibet alia carne, ut quidam delirantes surrecturos nos credunt, sed in ista, qua vivimus, consistimus et movemur. Peracto huius sanctae resurrectionis exemplo idem dominus noster atque salvator paternam ascendendo sedem repetiit, de qua numquam per divinitatem discessit, illic ad dexteram patris sedens, exspectatur in fine saeculorum, iudex omnium vivorum et mortuorum. Inde cum sanctis omnibus veniet ad ferendum iudicium, reddere unicuique mercedis propriae debitum, prout quisque gesserit in corpore positus sive bonum sive malum. Ecclesiam sane catholicam pretioso sanguine comparatam cum eo credimus in perpetuum regnaturam, intra cuius gremium constituti unum baptisma credimus et confitemur in remissionem omnium peccatorum, sub qua fide et resurrectionem mortuorum veraciter credimus et futuri saeculi gaudia exspectamus.
By this example therefore of our head we confess with true faith the resurrection of the flesh of all the dead, nor in the air or in any other flesh, as some raving believe that we shall rise, but in that in which we live, stand, and are moved. The example of this holy resurrection having been completed, the same our Lord and Savior, by ascending, sought again the paternal seat, from which he never departed by divinity; there sitting at the right hand of the Father, he is awaited at the end of ages, the judge of all the living and the dead. From there he will come with all the saints to bring forth judgment, to render to each one the debt of his own reward, as each one has done placed in the body whether good or evil. Moreover the catholic church, purchased by precious blood, we believe with him to reign forever, within whose bosom placed, we believe and confess one baptism for the remission of all sins, under which faith both of the resurrection of the dead we truly believe and the joys of the future age we await.
¶20
Hoc tantum orandum nobis est et petendum, ut cum peracto finitoque iudicio tradiderit filius regnum deo patri, participes nos efficiat regni sui, ut per hanc fidem, qua illi inhaesimus, cum illo sine fine regnemus.
This only is to be prayed by us and to be asked, that when after completed and finished judgment the Son shall have handed over the kingdom to God the Father, he may make us partakers of his kingdom, that through this faith, by which we have clung to him, with him we may reign without end.
¶21
Haec est confessionis nostrae fides exposita, per quam omnium hereticorum dogma perimitur, per quam fidelium corda mundantur, per quam etiam ad deum gloriose acceditur, cuius sacrosancto sapore sub triduano ieiunio continua relationum collatione rorantes ad ea, quae subnexa sunt, sequenti die decernenda transivimus.
This is the exposition of the faith of our confession, by which the dogma of all heretics is destroyed, by which the hearts of the faithful are cleansed, by which also to God one approaches gloriously, whose sacrosanct savor, under a three-day fast by the continual collation of recitals bedewed, to those things which are subjoined we passed to be decreed on the following day.

CANON I
NE TUMULTU CONCILIUM AGITETUR
In loco benedictionis consedentes domini sacerdotes nullis debent aut indiscretis vocibus perstrepere aut quibuslibet tumultibus perturbari, nullis etiam vanis fabulis vel risibus agi et, quod est deterius, obstinatis disceptationibus tumultuosas voces effundere. Si quis enim, ut apostolus ait, putat se religiosum esse non refrenans linguam suam, sed seducens cor suum, huius vana est religio. Cultum enim suum iustitia perdit, quando silentia iudicii obstrepentium turba confundit, dicente propheta: Erit cultus iustitiae silentium. Debet ergo, quicquid aut consedentium consultationibus agitur aut a causantium parte proponitur, sic mitissima verborum relatione proferri, ut nec contentiosis vocibus sensus audientium turbent nec iudicum vigorem de tumultu enervent. Quicumque ergo in conventu concilii haec, quae promissa sunt, violanda crediderit et contra haec interdicta aut tumultu aut contumeliis vel risibus concilium perturbaverit, iuxta divinae legis edictum, quae praecipitur: Eice derisorem et exibit cum eo iurgium cum omni dedecore de consessione abstractus a communi coetu secedat et trium dierum excommunicationis sententiam perferat.
In the place of blessing sitting, the lords the priests ought neither with indiscreet voices to clamor nor with any tumults to be disturbed, nor with any vain tales or laughs to be agitated, and, which is worse, with obstinate disputations to pour forth tumultuous voices. For if anyone, as the apostle says, thinks himself to be religious, not refraining his tongue, but deceiving his heart, his religion is vain. For justice loses its cult when the silences of judgment the crowd of those clamoring confuses, the prophet saying: The cult of justice shall be silence. Therefore, whatever either by the consultations of those sitting is done or by the party of the litigants is proposed, it ought to be brought forth with so mild a relation of words, that neither with contentious voices the sense of the hearers be disturbed nor the vigor of the judges from tumult be enervated. Whoever therefore in the assembly of the council these things which are promised shall have thought to be violated, and against these prohibitions either by tumult or by insults or by laughs the council shall have disturbed, according to the edict of the divine law, which commands: Cast out the scorner and with him contention will go out, with all disgrace from the assembly abstracted, from the common gathering let him depart and the sentence of three days' excommunication let him bear.

CANON II
QUOD NON DEBEAT METROPOLITANUS A CONFINITIMORUM INSTRUCTIONE CESSARE
Quantum quis praecelsi culminis obtinet locum, tantum necesse est praecedat ceteros gratia meritorum, ut in eo, quod praesidet singulis, singulariter ornet eminentiam sanctitatis habens semper et in ore gladium veritatis et in opere efficientiam luminis et iuxta Paulum potens sit exhortari in doctrina sana et contradicentes revincere. Nos proinde nostri ordinis gradum vel suscepti regiminis modum magnopere cogitare debemus, ut, qui officium praedicationis suscepimus, nullis curis a divina lectione privemur. Nam quorundam mentes pontificum ita corporis otio a lectionis gratia secluduntur, ut qui doctrinae subditis exhibeat gregibus, non inveniat praeco mutus. Insistendum ergo semper erit maioribus, ut, quos sub regiminis sui cura tuentur, fame verbi dei perire non sinant. Sicut metropolitanis in confinitimos ceterosque ecclesiasticis ordinibus deditos, sic confinitimis in commisso sibi religiosorum numero vigilandum est, qualiter nescientia talium divinae legis eruditionibus imbuatur, ita indesinenter sollicitudine praelatus quisque subditos quaerens aut profectum eorum laetabundus agnoscat aut nescientiam sine arrogantia instruat. Placuit ergo de talibus iuxta instituta Toletani concilii hoc specialiter definire, aut sponte sumant intentionem necessariam perdiscendi aut a maioribus ad lectionis exercitia cogantur inviti.
As much as anyone obtains the place of the most lofty summit, so much it is necessary that he precede others by grace of merits, that in that which he presides over individuals, he may singularly adorn the eminence of sanctity, having always both in his mouth the sword of truth and in his work the efficacy of light, and according to Paul may be powerful to exhort in sound doctrine and to refute those contradicting. We therefore ought greatly to consider the grade of our order or the measure of the received governance, that we who have received the office of preaching may not be deprived from divine reading by any cares. For the minds of certain pontiffs are so shut off from the grace of reading by the leisure of the body, that a mute herald does not find what he may show of doctrine to the subject flocks. Always therefore it must be insisted by the greater, that they whom under the care of their governance they guard, they not suffer to perish by hunger of the word of God. As to the metropolitans towards those bordering and others dedicated to ecclesiastical orders, so also to the bordering in the committed number of the religious it is to be watched, how the ignorance of such may be imbued with the eruditions of divine law. Thus unceasingly by solicitude, each prelate seeking his subjects, either may recognize their progress with joy or may instruct their ignorance without arrogance. Therefore it has pleased concerning such according to the institutes of the Toledan council this specially to define: that either spontaneously they take up the necessary intention of learning thoroughly, or by the greater they be compelled to the exercises of reading unwillingly.

CANON III
UT IN UNA PROVINCIA DIVERSITAS OFFICIORUM NON TENEATUR
De his, qui contra apostoli voluntatem circumferuntur omni vento doctrinae, placuit hoc sancto concilio, ut metropolitanae sedis auctoritate coacti uniuscuiusque provinciae cives rectoresque ecclesiarum in unum eundemque in psallendo teneant modum, quem in metropolitana sede cognoverit institutum, nec aliqua diversitate cuiusque ordinis vel officii a metropolitana se patiantur sede disiungi. Sic enim iustum est, ut inde unusquisque sumat regulas magisterii, unde honoris consecrationem accipit, ut iuxta maiorum decreta sedes, quae unicuique sacerdotalis mater est dignitatis, sit ecclesiasticae magistra rationis. Abbatibus sane indultis officiis, quae iuxta voluntatem sui episcopi regulariter illis implenda sunt, cetera officia publica, id est vesperam, matutinum sive missam aliter quam in principali ecclesia celebrare non liceat. Quisquis autem horum decretorum violator exstiterit, sex mensibus communione privatus apud metropolitanum sub poenitentiae censura permaneat corrigendus, qualiter apud illum et praeteritae transgressionis culpam lacrimis diluat et necessariam officiorum doctrinam studiose addiscat. Sub ista ergo regula disciplinae non solum metropolitanus totius suae provinciae pontifices vel sacerdotes adstringat, sed etiam ceteri episcopi subiectos sibi ecclesiarum rectores obtemperare institutionibus cogant.
Concerning those who contrary to the will of the apostle are carried about by every wind of doctrine, it has pleased this holy council that, compelled by the authority of the metropolitan see, the citizens and rectors of the churches of each province hold in psalm-singing one and the same manner which they shall have known to be instituted in the metropolitan see, nor by any diversity of any order or office do they allow themselves to be disjoined from the metropolitan see. For thus it is just, that from there each one takes the rules of magisterium, whence he receives the consecration of honor, that according to the decrees of the greater, the see which is the mother of priestly dignity to each may be the mistress of ecclesiastical reason. To abbots indeed, with the offices granted which according to the will of their bishop are to be fulfilled by them regularly, it is not lawful to celebrate the other public offices, that is vespers, matins, or mass, otherwise than in the principal church. Whoever moreover shall have been a violator of these decrees, deprived of communion for six months, let him remain with the metropolitan under the censure of penance to be corrected, so that by him both the fault of past transgression he may wash away with tears and the necessary doctrine of offices he may study to learn. Under this rule of discipline therefore, not only the metropolitan binds the pontiffs or priests of his whole province, but also the other bishops compel the rectors of churches subject to them to obey the institutions.

CANON IIII
DE DISCORDIA SACERDOTUM
Sicut omnis, qui diligit fratrem suum ex deo, ita omnis, qui odit proximum, ex diabolo est. Dilectione enim sola discernitur quis, ex quo genitus approbetur dicente Ioanne: In hoc manifesti sunt filii dei et filii diaboli: Omnis, qui non facit iustitiam, non est de deo, et qui non diligit fratrem suum, quoniam haec est annuntiatio, quam ab initio audistis, ut diligamus alterutrum. Et post paululum: Omnis, qui odit fratrem, homicida est, et scitis, quoniam omnis homicida non habet vitam aeternam in se manentem. Ecce homicida esse probabiliter declaratur, qui a fraterna societate dividitur. Nam etsi manus non moveat ad occidendum, pro eo tamen, quia immitis est ad nocendum, iam a deo homicida tenetur. Vivit ille, et iste iam interfector esse convincitur. Cum igitur his praeceptis beatus apostolus Paulus consona praedicatione concordet dicens: Non occidat sol super iracundiam vestram et nolite locum dare diabolo. Relatae sunt nobis quorundam sacerdotum personae in tantam obstinationis efferbuisse discordiam, ut non solum illos ab ira occasus solis non revocet, sed nec annosa quidem transactio temporum ad bonum caritatis reclinet, quippe in quorum cordibus sol iustitiae Christus occubuit, ad lumen caritatis redire vix possint. Horum igitur et similium discordantium fratrum oblationes iuxta antiqui canonis definitionem nullo modo recipiendas esse censemus. Personis tamen discordantium id speciali definitione praecipimus, ut, antequam eos reconciliatio vera innectat, nullus eorum accedere ad altare domini audeat vel gratiam communionis sanctae percipiat, sed geminato tempore per poenitentiam compensabunt, quo discordiae servierunt. Quod si unus eorum alio contemnente ad satisfactionem caritatis cucurrerit, ex eo tempore ut pacificus intra ecclesiam receptetur, ex quo ad concordiam festinasse convincitur, sententia tamen superiori servata, ut tempus, quod in iram expendit, geminatum in poenitentiae satisfactione persolvat.
Just as everyone who loves his brother is of God, so everyone who hates his neighbor is of the devil. For by love alone is discerned from what one is proven to be born, John saying: By this the sons of God and the sons of the devil are manifest: everyone who does not do justice is not of God, and who does not love his brother, because this is the announcement which you have heard from the beginning, that we love one another. And after a little: Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that every murderer does not have eternal life abiding in him. Behold, a murderer is probably declared, who from fraternal society is divided. For even if his hand does not move to kill, yet because he is ungentle to harming, he is already held by God as a murderer. He lives, and this one is already proven to be a killer. Since therefore with these precepts the blessed apostle Paul with consonant preaching agrees, saying: Let not the sun set upon your anger, and give no place to the devil. There have been reported to us the persons of certain priests so to have grown hot with the obstinacy of discord, that not only does the setting of the sun not recall them from anger, but nor does the aged passage of time bend them to the good of charity, indeed in whose hearts the sun of justice, Christ, has set, that they can scarcely return to the light of charity. Of these therefore and of similar discordant brothers, the offerings according to the definition of the ancient canon we decree by no means to be received. To the persons however of those discordant we command by a special definition, that before true reconciliation joins them, let none of them dare to approach the altar of the Lord or receive the grace of holy communion, but with double time by penance they shall compensate, by which they served discord. But if one of them, the other despising, shall have run to the satisfaction of charity, from that time as a peaceable man within the church let him be received, from which he is convinced to have hastened to concord, the previous sentence however being preserved, that the time which he spent in anger, doubled in the satisfaction of penance he pay.

CANON V
DE COMPESCENDIS EXCESSIBUS SACERDOTUM
Nullus vita praesulum perturbari debet excessibus, quia valde indignum est, ut, qui throni dei vocantur, levi motione turbentur, et qui debent esse iter iustitiae, ipsi efficiantur seminarium litis atque rapinae. Quomodo ergo huiusmodi iuxta apostolum irrepraehensibiles erunt, qui non solum repraehensibilia faciunt, sed mortifera potius et exsecranda committunt? Relati enim nobis sunt quidam ex sacerdotibus, quod omni gravitate sacerdotalis ordinis praetermissa audientium iudicium furore praeveniant et excessus inhonestae motionis audire refugiunt, pro quibus eos oportuerat aequitatis iudicia sustinere. Dum enim de honoris sui culmine blandiuntur, patientiam habere refugiunt, et qui inconcussae debuerant veritatis conservare statum, subito religionis mutant propositum et praecipiti furore iudicium antecedunt, sicque in quo decreverat eos iudicii sustinere conventum, pervasione agunt, unde de praesumptionibus confundantur. Qui tamen aut damno pariter et excommunicatione plectendi sunt aut omissis compositionibus rerum sola satisfactione poenitentiae curabuntur.
Let no one be disturbed by the excesses of the life of prelates, because it is very unworthy that those who are called thrones of God should be disturbed by light motion, and who ought to be the way of justice, themselves become the seminary of strife and rapine. How therefore will such according to the apostle be irreprehensible, who not only do reprehensible things but rather commit deadly and execrable things? For there have been reported to us some from among the priests, that every gravity of the priestly order having been passed over, they anticipate the judgment of those hearing by fury, and they refuse to hear the excesses of dishonorable motion, for which it had behooved them to sustain the judgments of equity. For while they flatter themselves from the summit of their honor, they refuse to have patience, and who ought to have preserved the unshaken state of truth, suddenly change the purpose of religion and by headlong fury precede judgment, and thus in that which had been determined for them to sustain the assembly of judgment, they act by seizure, whence concerning presumptions they may be confounded. Who however either by loss together and excommunication are to be punished, or, the compositions of things having been omitted, by satisfaction of penance alone shall be healed.
¶33
Illi enim, qui rei propriae facultate suffulti sunt, aut, qui rem suam iam ante in nomine ecclesiae praesentes transfudisse noscuntur, si aut per se aut per subditos seu per quoslibet aliena diripiunt vel praesumptionis seu caedis quippiam agunt, tam in rebus fiscalibus quam etiam in quorumlibet dominio constitutis, et pervasa vel praesumpta de rebus iuxta leges excellentissimi principis sarciant, et pro excessu religioni contrario, quod inhoneste ante iudicium patraverunt, duarum hebdomadarum excommunicatione plectendi sunt. Et qui nihil proprietatis habere videntur, magna discretionis arte medendi sunt, quo nec ausus illicitos ecclesiarum facultatibus redimant, nec ipsi penitus extorres a poena persistant. Nec enim iustum est, ut pro pravis actibus sacerdotum ecclesiae, quibus praeminent, sustineant damnum aut pro excessibus talibus satisfactio ab ecclesiis exigatur, cum ecclesia cultores suos non ad litem, sed ad honestatem informet.
For those who are supported by the faculty of their own property, or who are known to have already previously transferred their own property in the name of the church, if either by themselves or by their subjects or by any persons they seize others' goods or commit anything of presumption or slaughter, as well in fiscal goods as also in the property of anyone constituted, let them both restore by satisfaction the seized or presumed things according to the laws of the most excellent prince, and for the excess contrary to religion, which they have wickedly perpetrated before judgment, let them be punished by excommunication of two weeks. And those who are seen to have nothing of property, by a great art of discretion are to be healed, whereby neither may they redeem illicit daring with the goods of the churches, nor may they themselves wholly remain strangers from punishment. For neither is it just that on account of the wicked acts of priests the churches, over which they preside, sustain damage, or that for such excesses satisfaction be exacted from the churches, since the church forms its cultivators not to strife but to honesty.
¶34
De talibus ergo placuit definire, qui nullis habitis rebus propriis aut in quocumque pervasores exstiterint aut quibuslibet personis caedes vel quodcumque praesumptionis intulerint, nulla eos incurvatione status sui servituti hominum debere addici, sed iuxta quod praesumptiosus quisque ille exstiterit, ita et poenitentiae legibus subiacebit: si in decem solidorum summam praesumptor esse convincitur, viginti dierum poenitentiae satisfactione purgetur, ita ut sive minoris sive maioris summae excessum peregerit, similiter geminata hoc semper satisfactione poenitentiae recompenset. Servos tamen ecclesiarum, qui huiusmodi excessus operasse noscuntur, ad leges saeculares audiendos remittimus. Et haec quidem de generali excessu dicta sufficiant.
Concerning such therefore it has pleased to define, who, having no property of their own, either have been invaders of any sort or have inflicted slaughters or any presumption upon any persons, that they ought not to be added to the servitude of men by any bending of their state, but according as each one shall have been presumptuous, thus also let him be subject to the laws of penance: if he is convicted to be a presumer to the amount of ten solidi, let him be cleansed by the satisfaction of penance for twenty days, so that whether he has perpetrated an excess of smaller or larger sum, with double satisfaction of penance always let him compensate. The servants however of the churches who are known to have wrought such excesses, we remit to be heard by secular laws. And let these things indeed concerning general excess have been said sufficiently.
¶35
Ceterum specialitatis ordinem prosequentes, si quis episcoporum magnati cuiusquam uxorem, filiam, neptem seu quolibet illi gradu adhibito pertinentem quacumque fraude vel subtilitate adulterina pollutione fedaverit, et honoris proprii gradum amittat et sub exilii religatione perpetuaque poenitentia teneatur, cui tamen communio exeunti ex hac vita non neganda est propter domini misericordiam, qui non vult peccatoris mortem, sed ut convertatur et vivat.
Moreover following the order of speciality, if any bishop the wife, daughter, granddaughter, or any woman pertaining to him by whatever degree of a magnate he shall have defiled by adulterous pollution with any fraud or subtlety, let him both lose the grade of his own honor and under the relegation of exile and perpetual penance let him be bound, to whom however communion is not to be denied at the end of life on account of the mercy of the Lord, who does not wish the death of a sinner, but that he be converted and live.
¶36
CANON VI
NON DEBERE SACERDOTIBUS QUALIBET IN ECCLESIA FAMULIS TRUNCATIONES MEMBRORUM FACERE NEC ALIQUID, QUOD MORTE PLECTENDUM SIT, IUDICARE
His, a quibus domini sacramenta tractanda sunt, iudicium sanguinis agitare non licet. Et ideo magnopere talibus excessibus prohibendum est, ne indiscretae praesumptionis motibus agitati aut, quod morte plectendum est, sententia propria iudicare praesumant, aut truncationes quibuslibet personis aut per se inferant aut inferendas praecipiant. Quod si quisquam horum immemor praeceptorum aut in ecclesiae suae familiis aut in quibuslibet personis tale aliquid fecerit, et concessi ordinis honore privatus et loco sub perpetuo damnationis teneatur religatus, cui tamen communio exeunti ex hac vita non neganda est propter domini misericordiam, qui non vult peccatoris mortem, sed ut convertatur et vivat.
To those by whom the sacraments of the Lord are to be handled, it is not permitted to agitate the judgment of blood. And therefore by such excesses it is greatly to be prohibited, lest by the motions of indiscreet presumption moved, either they presume by their own sentence to judge that which is to be punished with death, or they either inflict mutilations upon any persons by themselves or order them to be inflicted. But if anyone of these, unmindful of the precepts, either in his church's servants or in any persons shall have done such a thing, let him both be deprived of the honor of the conceded order and be held confined in the place under perpetual damnation, to whom however communion is not to be denied at the end of life on account of the mercy of the Lord, who does not wish the death of a sinner, but that he be converted and live.
¶37
CANON VII
QUAE DEBEAT DISCRETIO ECCLESIARUM RECTORIBUS ESSE, NE PER INCONDITAM DISCIPLINAM SUBEANT HOMICIDII NOTAM
Cum iuxta antiquam institutionis edictum plus erga corrigendos agere debeat benevolentia quam severitas, plus cohortatio quam commotio, plus caritas quam potestas, relatum nobis est, quod quidam ex fratribus plus livore odii quam correctionis studio subditos insequentes, dum se simulant spiritalem eis adhibere correctionem, indiscretam subito afferunt mortem, cum inauditos a se proiciunt et illicitis eos iudiciis sub poenitentia puniunt. Non ergo de cetero perversis voluntatibus sit liberum simulare se, quod fingunt, sed quotiescumque quilibet ex subditis corrigendus est, aut publica a sacerdote debet disciplina curari, aut, si aliter rectoribus placet, duorum vel trium fratrum spiritalium testimonio peculiariter adhibito et modus criminis agnoscatur et modus poenitentiae irrogetur, ita tamen, ut, si exilio vel retrusione dignum eum esse cognoverit, aut quid aliud iudicium peculiare decreverit, modus poenitentiae, quem coram tribus fratribus sacerdos transgressori indixerit, speciali debeat eius, qui sententiam protulit, manus propriae subscriptione notari. Sicque fiet, ut nec transgressores sine testimonio excidia vitae suae incurrant, nec rectores accusatos se de quorumlibet interemptionibus erubescant.
Since according to the ancient edict of institution more ought benevolence to act toward those to be corrected than severity, more exhortation than commotion, more charity than power, it has been reported to us that some of the brothers, following their subjects more by the spite of hatred than by the study of correction, while they pretend to apply to them spiritual correction, suddenly bring indiscreet death, when they cast them out unheard and punish them under penance with illicit judgments. Therefore for the future let it not be free for perverse wills to pretend that which they feign, but as often as anyone of the subjects is to be corrected, either by the priest ought the public discipline to be cared for, or, if otherwise it pleases the rectors, with the testimony of two or three spiritual brothers specially added, both the measure of the crime may be known and the measure of penance may be imposed, yet in such a way that, if he shall have known him to be worthy of exile or seclusion, or whatever else a special judgment shall have decreed, the measure of penance which the priest before three brothers shall have imposed upon the transgressor, ought to be noted by the subscription of the proper hand of him who brought forth the sentence. And thus it will be done, that neither transgressors without testimony may incur the destruction of their life, nor may the rectors be ashamed of being accused of the killings of any.
¶38
CANON VIII
NE QUISQUAM PRAEMII PRO DIVINIS SACRAMENTIS ACCIPIATUR
Quicquid invisibilis gratiae collatione tribuitur, numquam quaestu vel quibuslibet praemiis venundari penitus debet dicente domino: Quod gratis accepistis, gratis date. Et ideo quicumque deinceps in ecclesiastico ordine constitutus aut pro baptizandis consignandisque fidelibus chrismatis vel promotionibus graduum pretia quaelibet vel praemia nisi voluntarie oblata pro huiusmodi ambitione susceperit, equidem si scienti loci episcopo tale quicquam a subditis perpetratur, idem episcopus duobus mensibus excommunicationi subiaceat pro eo, quod in scientia mala contexit et correptionem necessariam non adhibuit, sin autem suorum quispiam eodem nesciente quodcumque pro supradictis capitulis accipiendum sibi esse crediderit, si presbyter est, trium mensium excommunicatione plectatur, si diaconus, quattuor, subdiaconus vero vel clericus his cupiditatibus serviens et competenti poena et debita excommunicatione plectendus est.
Whatever is granted by the conferral of invisible grace, ought never to be sought or by any rewards to be sold, the Lord saying: Freely you have received, freely give. And therefore whoever from now on constituted in the ecclesiastical order either for baptizing and consigning the faithful or for the chrism or for promotions of grades shall have received any prices or rewards, unless voluntarily offered, if indeed to the bishop of the place knowing such a thing is perpetrated by his subjects, let the same bishop be subject to excommunication for two months for that which in evil knowledge he concealed and necessary correction did not apply. If moreover any of his own, he being ignorant, whatever for the above-written chapters believed to be received by him, if a presbyter, let him be punished with excommunication of three months; if a deacon, four months; a subdeacon indeed or cleric serving these cupidities, both with competent penalty and with due excommunication is to be punished.
¶39
CANON VIIII
QUID CUSTODIRI DEBEAT, SI PER PRAEMIUM QUIS EPISCOPUS FIAT VEL QUA SENTENTIA FERIATUR, QUI POST HONOREM ACCEPTUM PER PRAEMIUM ORDINATUS FUISSE DETEGITUR
Multae super hoc capitulo patrum sententiae manaverunt, scilicet ne inappretiabilem sancti spiritus gratiam donis vel muneribus quis existimet comparandam. Sed quod non sine gravi dolore dicendum est, quanto hoc frequenti decretorum est praeceptione prohibitum, tanto a nobis fraudibus cognoscitur iteratum, dum hi, qui tali pretio mercari nituntur gratiam ordinationis suae, tempora praeveniunt munere aut post acceptum honorem promissis suis conferunt a patribus turpis lucri mercedem. Et ideo, ut horum et similium argumentorum deinceps amputetur occasio, hoc sancta synodus definivit, ut, cum quisque pontificale culmen ante domini altare percepturus accesserit, sacramentis et exactione adstringatur, quod pro conferenda sibi consecratione honoris nulli personae cuiuslibet praemii collationem vel aliquando dedisset vel aliquando in futurum dare procuret, sicque aut mundus ab hoc contagio praelationis consecrationem accipiat aut publicato hoc scelere manifeste denudatus coram ecclesia ad honorem, quem mercari voluit, non accedat.
Many sentences of the fathers have flowed forth concerning this chapter, namely that no one think the inappreciable grace of the Holy Spirit to be purchased by gifts or rewards. But that which not without great sorrow is to be said, by how much this has been prohibited by the frequent precept of decrees, by so much it is known to be repeated by us in frauds, while those who strive to purchase by such a price the grace of their ordination either anticipate the time by a gift or, after the received honor, give by their promises the reward of foul gain to the fathers. And therefore, that from now on the occasion of these and of similar arguments may be cut off, this holy synod has defined, that when anyone is about to approach to receive the pontifical summit before the altar of the Lord, let him be bound by sacraments and exaction, that for the conferral upon himself of the consecration of honor he would never have given a conferral of any reward to any person, nor in the future would he procure to give, and thus either let him receive consecration clean from this contagion of prelation, or this crime having been made public and manifestly laid bare, let him not approach to the honor which he wished to purchase.
¶40
Illos tamen, quos deinceps post praelationem per praemium ordinatos fuisse patuerit, sub definitis poenitentiae legibus, ut vere simoniacos ab ecclesia separandos esse censemus, id est, ut duorum annorum spatio exilio religati et digna satisfactione vel excommunicationis sententia coerciti honoris gradum, quem praemiis emerant, lacrimis conquirere et reparare intendant. Unde si digna satisfactio eos poenitentiae tempore invenerit, non tantum communioni, sed et loco et totius ordinis officiis, a quibus separati fuerant, restaurandi sunt.
Those moreover who from now on after prelation shall have been evident to have been ordained through reward, under the defined laws of penance, as truly Simonians to be separated from the church we decree, that is, that for the space of two years, relegated into exile and constrained by worthy satisfaction or the sentence of excommunication, they may strive by tears to seek and recover the grade of honor which they purchased by rewards. Whence if worthy satisfaction shall have found them during the time of penance, they are to be restored not only to communion, but also to the place and to all offices of order from which they were separated.
¶41
CANON X
UT OMNES PONTIFICES RECTORESQUE ECCLESIARUM TEMPORE, QUO ORDINANDI SUNT, SUB CAUTIONE PROMITTANT, QUAM IUSTISSIME VIVERE DEBEANT
Quamquam omnes, qui sacris mancipantur ordinibus, canonicis regulis teneantur adstricti, expeditibile tamen est, ut promissionis suae vota sub cautione spondeant, quos ad promotionis gradus ecclesiastica probat disciplina. Solet enim plus timeri, quod singulariter pollicetur, quam quod generali sponsione concluditur. Et ideo placuit huic sancto concilio, ut unusquisque, qui ad ecclesiasticos gradus est accessurus, non ante honoris consecrationem accipiat, quam placiti sui innotatione promittat, ut fidem catholicam sinceram cordis devotione custodiens iuste et pie vivere debeat et in nullis operibus suis canonicis regulis contradicat atque ut debitum per omnia honorem atque obsequii reverentiam praeminenti sibi unusquisque dependat iuxta illud beati papae Leonis edictum: Qui se scit aliquibus esse praepositum, non moleste ferat aliquem sibi esse praelatum, sed oboedientiam, quam exigit, etiam ipse dependat. Poena tamen iuxta ecclesiasticae consuetudinis morem et placitis talium inserenda et ab his, qui transgressores fuerint, persolvenda est.
Although all who are given to sacred orders are held bound by canonical rules, yet it is expedient that those whom the ecclesiastical discipline approves for the grades of promotion should vow the vows of their promise under a caution. For that which is promised specially is accustomed to be feared more than that which is concluded by a general pledge. And therefore it has pleased this holy council, that each one who is about to approach to the ecclesiastical grades, should not receive the consecration of honor before he by the notation of a pledge promise that he will guard the catholic faith with sincere devotion of heart, that he ought to live justly and piously, and that in no works of his does he contradict the canonical rules, and that the due honor and reverence of service each one pay to him who presides over him according to the edict of blessed Pope Leo: He who knows himself to be set over some, let him not bear with annoyance that someone is set over him, but the obedience which he demands, let him also himself give. The penalty however according to the manner of ecclesiastical custom is both to be inserted into the pledges of such and to be paid by those who shall have been transgressors.
¶42
CANON XI
DE ELUCIDATIONE ANTIQUI CANONIS, QUO PRAECIPITUR, UT, SI QUIS ACCEPTAM A SACERDOTE EUCHARISTIAM NON SUMPSERIT, VELUT SACRILEGUS PROPELLATUR
Cum nihil in divino canone debeat esse confusum, nihil dubium, nihil etiam indiscretum, in collationem nostri coetus relatus est canon Toletani concilii primi, in quo praeceptum est, ut, si quis acceptam a sacerdote eucharistiam non sumpserit, ut sacrilegus repellatur. Nec adiecta est ibi discretio voluntatum, cum et infidelis fidenter accipiat, quod naturaliter illum necessitas deglutire non sinat. Solet enim humanae naturae infirmitas in ipso mortis exitu praegravata tanto siccitatis pondere deprimi, ut nullis ciborum illationibus refici, sed vix tantundem illati delectetur poculi gratia sustentari. Quod etiam in multorum exitu vidimus, qui optatum suis votis sacrae communionis expetentes viaticum collatam sibi a sacerdote eucharistiam reiecerunt, non quod infidelitate hoc agerent, sed quod praeter dominici calicis haustum traditam sibi non possent eucharistiam deglutire. Non ergo huiusmodi a corpore ecclesiae separandi sunt, qui talia non infidelitate, sed necessitate fecerunt, praesertim hi, de quibus nihil fidei sinistrae sentitur. Placuit ergo definire, quod nec fideli officiat nec infideli multum exsistat.
Since in the divine canon there ought to be nothing confused, nothing doubtful, nothing even indiscrete, in the collation of our assembly, a canon of the first Toledan council has been brought forward, in which it is commanded that, if anyone having received the Eucharist from a priest does not take it, he be cast out as sacrilegious. Nor was there added there a distinction of wills, since both an unfaithful man confidently receives that which naturally necessity does not allow him to swallow. For it is customary that the infirmity of human nature, weighed down at the very exit of death, is so oppressed by so great a weight of dryness, that it cannot be refreshed by any bringing in of foods, but hardly with as much of the brought-in cup is it delighted to be sustained by grace. Which also we have seen in the death of many, who, seeking the longed-for viaticum of holy communion, have rejected the Eucharist handed to them by a priest, not that they did this by unfaithfulness, but that they could not swallow the Eucharist handed to them except by the draught of the Lord's cup. Therefore let not such be separated from the body of the church, who have done such things not by unfaithfulness but by necessity, especially those concerning whom nothing of sinister faith is perceived. Therefore it has pleased to define, that which may harm neither the faithful nor much exist for the unfaithful.
¶43
Quicumque ergo fidelis inevitabili qualibet infirmitate coactus eucharistiam perceptam reiecerit, in nullo ecclesiasticae damnationi subiaceat, similiter nec illos cuiusquam punitionis censura redarguet, qui talia aut tempore infantiae faciunt aut in quolibet mentis alienatione positi, quid fecerint, ignorare videntur. Iam vero quicumque aut de fidelium aut de infidelium numero corpus domini absque inevitabili, ut dictum est, infirmitate proiecerit, si fidelis est, perpetua communione privetur, si infidelis est, et verberibus subdatur et perpetuo exilio relegetur. Quod si horum quislibet huiusmodi excessus digna poenitentiae satisfactione defleverit, post quinquennium licitum erit illum communioni pristinae reformari.
Therefore whoever a faithful person, compelled by any unavoidable infirmity, shall have rejected the perceived Eucharist, let him be subject to no ecclesiastical condemnation. Similarly neither shall the censure of any punishment rebuke those who do such things either at the time of infancy or placed in any alienation of mind seem to be ignorant of what they did. But as for whoever, either from the number of the faithful or from the number of the unfaithful, shall have cast out the body of the Lord without unavoidable infirmity, as it has been said, if he is faithful, let him be deprived of perpetual communion; if he is unfaithful, let him both be subjected to blows and be bound by perpetual exile. But if any of these shall have washed away an excess of this sort by worthy satisfaction of penance, after five years it will be lawful for him to be restored to pristine communion.
¶44
CANON XII
NE IN CONFINIO MORTIS POENITENS A RECONCILIATIONE DIU SUSPENDATUR, ET UT OBLATIO EIUS, QUI POENITENS NEC TAMEN RECONCILIATUS DE HAC VITA EXIERIT, AB ECCLESIA RECEPTETUR
Qui poenitentiam in mortis agit periculo, non diutine a reconciliationis gratia differendus est, sed si praecincto mortis urguetur periculo, poenitentia per manus impositionem accepta statim et reconciliatio adhibenda est, ne prius ab humanis rebus aeger abscedat, quam donum reconciliationis accipiat, sicque superstitibus quodammodo doloris videatur esse perpetui, si praecisum ab ecclesiae membris eum, qui utique reconciliationem non meruit, raptim a praesenti vita mortis natura subduxerit. Unde iuxta papae Leonis edictum hi, qui in tempore necessitatis et in periculi urgentis instantia praesidium poenitentiae ex more reconciliationis implorant, nec satisfactio interdicenda est nec reconciliatio deneganda, quia in misericordia dei nec mensuras possumus ponere nec tempora definire. De his autem, qui acceptam poenitentiam, antequam reconcilientur, ab hac vita recesserint, quamquam diversitas praeceptorum de hoc capitulo habeatur, illorum tamen nobis sententiam placuit, qui multiplici numero de huiusmodi humanius decreverunt, ut et memoria talium in ecclesiis commendetur et oblatio pro eorum dedicata spiritalibus accipiatur.
He who does penance in the peril of death is not to be long deferred from the grace of reconciliation, but if he is urged by the impending peril of death, penance having been received through imposition of hands, immediately also reconciliation is to be applied, lest the sick man depart from human affairs before he receives the gift of reconciliation, and thus to survivors it might seem of perpetual sorrow, if nature of death has snatched him swiftly from present life, him who had not merited reconciliation, cut off from the members of the church. Whence according to the edict of Pope Leo, those who in time of necessity and in the instant of pressing peril implore the aid of penance according to the custom of reconciliation, neither is satisfaction to be forbidden nor reconciliation to be denied, because in the mercy of God we can neither set measures nor define times. Concerning those moreover who, having received penance, before they are reconciled have departed from this life, although a diversity of precepts is held concerning this chapter, yet that sentence of those has pleased us who with a more numerous number have decreed more humanely concerning such, that both the memory of such be commended in the churches and the offering dedicated for them be received by the spiritual.
¶45
CANON XIII
DE SACERDOTIBUS, QUI VEXATI CADERE VIDENTUR
Bene siquidem maiorum regulis definitum est, ut daemoniis aliisque similibus passionibus irretitis mysteria sacra tractare non liceat. Cui praecepto consensu rationis adhibito, id communiter definivimus, ut nullus de his, qui aut in terra arrepti a daemonibus eliduntur aut quolibet modo vexationis incursibus efferuntur, vel sacris audeant ministrare altaribus vel indiscussi se divinis ingerant sacramentis, exceptis illis, qui variis corporum commoditatibus dediti sine huiusmodi passionibus in terram approbantur elisi, qui tamen et ipsi tamdiu erunt ab officii sui ordine et loco suspensi, quousque unius anni spatio per discretionem episcopi inveniuntur ab incursu daemonum alieni.
Well indeed it has been defined by the rules of the greater, that it is not lawful for those entangled by demons and other similar passions to handle the sacred mysteries. To which precept, with the consent of reason having been applied, we have commonly defined this, that none of those who either on the ground seized by demons are thrown down or in any way are borne by the incursions of vexation should dare to minister at the sacred altars or thrust themselves into the divine sacraments without examination, except those who given to various commodities of bodies are approved to be thrown down on the ground without such passions, who yet themselves also will be suspended from the order and place of their office for so long as by the discretion of the bishop they may be found for the space of one year alien from the incursion of demons.
¶46
CANON XIIII
UT HI, QUI DOMINO CANUNT ATQUE SACRIFICANT, POST SE SEMPER HABEANT ADIUTORIA CONSTITUTA
Ut illud divini oraculi monentis singuli praecaventes, quo scribitur: Vae soli, quia, cum ceciderit, non habet sublevantem, summopere verendum nobis est et cavendum, ne horis illis atque temporibus, quibus domino psallitur vel sacrificatur, unicuique divinis singulariter officiis insistenti perniciosa passio vel corporis quaelibet valitudo occurrat, quae aut corpus subito subrui faciat aut mentem alienatione vel terrore confundat. Pro huiusmodi ergo casibus praecaventes necessarium duximus instituere, ut ubi temporis vel loci sive cleri copia suffragatur, habeat quisquis ille canens deo atque sacrificans post se vicini solaminis adiutorem, ut, si aliquo casu ille, qui officia impleturus accedit, turbatus fuerit vel ad terram elisus, a tergo semper habeat, qui eius vicem exsequatur intrepidus.
That the divine oracle warning, by which it is written: Woe to the alone, because when he falls, he has not one lifting him up, we ought especially to fear and beware, lest at those hours and times at which psalm is sung to the Lord or is sacrificed, to each one singly attending to divine offices a pernicious passion or any ailment of body occur, which either cause the body suddenly to collapse or confound the mind by alienation or terror. Therefore, guarding against such chances, we have deemed it necessary to establish, that where the abundance of time or place or clergy suffices, whoever that is who sings to God and sacrifices may have after him a helper of neighboring solace, that if by any chance he who approaches to fulfill the offices shall have been disturbed or thrown to the ground, he may always have from behind one who may perform his part intrepidly.
¶47
CANON XV
DE INSTITUTIONE CERTI TEMPORIS, QUO CONCILIUM AGATUR
Peractis omnibus, quae ad correctionem nostri ordinis in hoc concilio promulgata sunt, placuit definire, ut paternis institutionibus obsequentes omni anno ad peragendam celebritatem concilii in metropolitana sede tempore, quo principis vel metropolitani electio definierit, devotis semper animorum studiis conferamus, nec quibuslibet requisitis occasionibus absentemur, sed in praeparato die, quo indictum fuerit, adunatis in metropolitana sede omnibus provinciae pontificibus concilium deo praesule celebretur. Quisquis autem episcoporum excepto inevitabili causa vel necessitate de peragendo se concilio absentaverit, unius anni metas erit excommunicatione plectendus. Quod si deinceps absque celebratione concilii anni unius metas transierit, omnium in commune pontificum Carthaginis provinciae censurae sententiam obnoxius retinebit, id est, si nulla sibi impediente principis potestate vel infirmitate aut inevitabili causa, sed solius propriae voluntatis libitu sese ad celebrandum concilium non collegerit.
All things having been completed which have been promulgated in this council for the correction of our order, it has pleased to define, that obeying the paternal institutions, every year for performing the celebration of the council in the metropolitan see at the time which the election of the prince or of the metropolitan shall have defined, we should assemble with devoted studies of minds, nor by any sought occasions should we be absent, but on the prepared day, on which it shall have been appointed, the pontiffs of the whole province having been assembled in the metropolitan see, let the council be celebrated with God as president. If moreover any bishop, an unavoidable cause or necessity excepted, shall have absented himself from performing the council, let him be punished with excommunication for the space of one year. But if thereafter without the celebration of the council he shall have passed the space of one year, let him be held liable to the sentence of the censure of all the pontiffs of the province of Carthage in common, that is, if no power of the prince or infirmity or unavoidable cause impedes him, but by the will of his own will alone he shall not have gathered himself to celebrate the council.
¶48
CANON XVI
DE RELATIONE GRATIARUM PRO CONSUMMATIONE CONCILII
His igitur constitutionibus nostris, quas necessario decernendas credidimus, finalem manus nostrae subscriptionem adiecimus, immortali deo nostro et domino gloriam et honorem reddentes, qui nos de conventu alternae visionis laetos effecit, qui os nostrum in confessione laudis suae aperuit, qui etiam decreta huius nostri concilii honeste complevit. Post haec religioso domino et amabili principi nostro Wambani regi gratiarum actiones persolvimus, cuius etiam studio aggregati sumus, qui ecclesiasticae disciplinae nostris saeculis novus reparator occurrens omissos conciliorum ordines non solum restaurare intendit, sed etiam annuis recursibus celebrandos instituit, ut ad alternam morum correctionem annuo tempore alacriter concurrentes iuxta prophetae vaticinium, quod in nobis defractum est, alligetur, et quod abiectum est, reducatur. Det ergo eidem principi dominus pro huius sacrae sollicitudinis voto et cursum praesentis vitae in pace transire et post diuturna tempora ad se in pace remissis iniquitatibus pervenire, qualiter et ille felicia tempora ducat, et felix cum omnibus, quibus principatur, ad Christum sine confusione perveniat, ut, quia per eum corona nostri ordinis in melius restauretur, coronam futuri regni capiat ex hoc in regione vivorum regnans cum Christo in saecula saeculorum. Amen.
To these therefore our constitutions, which we believed necessarily to be decreed, we have added the final subscription of our hand, to the immortal God and our Lord rendering glory and honor, who made us joyful from the assembly of mutual vision, who opened our mouth in the confession of his praise, who also completed the decrees of this our council honorably. After these things to the religious lord and amiable our prince Wamba the king we have rendered thanks, by whose study also we were assembled, who the ecclesiastical discipline for our times happening as a new repairer, the omitted orders of councils not only intended to restore but also established to be celebrated by annual returns, that to mutual correction of morals at the annual time eagerly running together, according to the prophecy of the prophet, that which in us is broken may be bound up, and that which is cast away may be brought back. May the Lord therefore grant to the same prince for the vow of this sacred solicitude both to pass the course of the present life in peace and after long times to arrive to himself in peace, iniquities having been remitted, that both he may lead happy times, and happy with all over whom he rules, may come to Christ without confusion, that because through him the crown of our order is restored for the better, he may receive the crown of the future kingdom from this in the land of the living, reigning with Christ unto ages of ages. Amen.

Interfuerunt huic concilio pontifices XVII. Ego Quiricus, urbis regiae metropolitanus episcopus, haec gesta synodica a nobis definita subscripsi. Similiter et alii episcopi subscripserunt numero XVI.
There were present at this council seventeen pontiffs. I, Quiricus, metropolitan bishop of the royal city, these synodal acts defined by us have subscribed. Similarly also the other bishops subscribed, in number sixteen.
