Council of Quierzy

Concilium Carisiacum

853

DECLARATIO CONCILII CARISIACI
CELEBRATA ANNO DOMINI DCCCLIII PRAESIDENTE HINCMARO ARCHIEPISCOPO RHEDENSI
DE LIBERO HOMINIS ARBITRIO ET DE PRAEDESTINATIONE
THE DECLARATION OF THE COUNCIL OF QUIERZY
CELEBRATED IN THE YEAR OF THE LORD 853 UNDER THE PRESIDENCY OF HINCMAR, ARCHBISHOP OF REIMS
CONCERNING THE FREE WILL OF MAN AND CONCERNING PREDESTINATION
CAPUT I
CHAPTER I
Deus omnipotens hominem sine peccato rectum cum libero arbitrio condidit, et in paradiso posuit, quem in sanctitate iustitiae permanere voluit. Homo libero arbitrio male utens peccavit et cecidit, et factus est massa perditionis totius humani generis. Deus autem bonus et iustus elegit ex eadem massa perditionis secundum praescientiam suam quos per gratiam praedestinavit ad vitam, et vitam illis praedestinavit aeternam: ceteros autem, quos iustitiae iudicio in massa perditionis reliquit, perituros praescivit, sed non ut perirent praedestinavit; poenam autem illis, quia iustus est, praedestinavit aeternam. Ac per hoc unam Dei praedestinationem tantummodo dicimus, quae aut ad donum pertinet gratiae aut ad retributionem iustitiae.
Almighty God created man without sin, righteous, with free will, and placed him in paradise, whom he willed to remain in the holiness of justice. Man, using free will badly, sinned and fell, and was made the "mass of perdition" of the whole human race. But the good and just God chose from that same mass of perdition according to his foreknowledge those whom by grace he predestined to life, and for them he predestined eternal life. The others indeed, whom by the judgment of justice he left in the mass of perdition, he foreknew would perish, but he did not predestine that they would perish; yet punishment for them, because he is just, he predestined eternal. And through this we speak of only one predestination of God, which pertains either to the gift of grace or to the retribution of justice.
¶1
CAPUT II
CHAPTER II
Libertatem arbitrii in primo homine perdidimus, quam per Christum Dominum nostrum recepimus: et habemus liberum arbitrium ad bonum, praeventum et adiutum gratia, et habemus liberum arbitrium ad malum, desertum gratia. Liberum autem habemus arbitrium, quia gratia liberatum et gratia de corrupto sanatum.
We lost the freedom of the will in the first man, which through Christ our Lord we have received; and we have free will toward good, prevented and helped by grace, and we have free will toward evil, deserted by grace. Moreover we have free will, because freed by grace and healed by grace from corrupted.
¶2
CAPUT III
CHAPTER III
Deus omnipotens omnes homines sine exceptione vult salvos fieri, licet non omnes salventur. Quod autem quidam salvantur, salvantis est donum: quod autem quidam pereunt, pereuntium est meritum.
Almighty God wills "all men" without exception "to be saved," although not all are saved. But that some are saved is the gift of the one saving; that some indeed perish is the merit of those perishing.
¶3
CAPUT IV
CHAPTER IV
Christus Iesus Dominus noster, sicut nullus homo est, fuit vel erit, cuius natura in illo assumpta non fuerit, ita nullus est, fuit vel erit homo, pro quo passus non fuerit; licet non omnes passionis eius mysterio redimantur. Quod vero omnes passionis eius mysterio non redimuntur, non respicit ad magnitudinem et pretii copiositatem, sed ad infidelium et ad non credentium ea fide, quae per dilectionem operatur, respicit partem; quia poculum humanae salutis, quod confectum est infirmitate nostra et virtute divina, habet quidem in se, ut omnibus prosit: sed si non bibitur, non medetur.
Christ Jesus our Lord, just as there is no man, was, or will be, whose nature in him was not assumed, so there is no man, was, or will be, for whom he did not suffer; although not all are redeemed by the mystery of his passion. But that not all are redeemed by the mystery of his passion does not look to the magnitude and abundance of the price, but to the part of the unfaithful and of those not believing with that faith "which works through love"; because the cup of human salvation, which was prepared by our infirmity and by divine power, has indeed in itself that it might profit all, but if it is not drunk, it does not heal.
¶4

This completes the full text of Concilium Carisiacum - Declaratio de libero hominis arbitrio et de praedestinatione (Council of Quierzy, A.D. 853).
