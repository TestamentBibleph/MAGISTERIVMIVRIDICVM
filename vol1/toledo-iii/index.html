Third Council of Toledo

Concilium Toletanum III

589

*INCIPIT TOLETANA SYNODUS TERTIA DUORUM EPISCOPORUM IN QUA ARRIATIA HERESIS IN SPANIA CONDEMNATUR.*
BEGINNING OF THE THIRD TOLEDAN SYNOD OF TWO BISHOPS IN WHICH THE ARIAN HERESY IN SPAIN IS CONDEMNED.
IN NOMINE DOMINI NOSTRI IESU CHRISTI anno regnante quarto gloriosissimo atque piissimo et deo fidelissimo domno Recaredo rege, die VIII Id. Maiar. era DCXXVII haec sancta synodus habita est in civitate regia Toletana ab episcopis totius Spaniae vel Galliae, qui infra scripti sunt.
In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, in the fourth year of the reign of the most glorious and most pious and most faithful to God, the Lord King Recared, on the 8th day of the Ides of May, in the Era 627, this holy synod was held in the royal city of Toledo by the bishops of all Spain and Gaul, who are written below.
¶1
Cum pro fidei suae sinceritate idem gloriosissimus princeps omnes regiminis suae pontifices in unum convenire mandasset, ut tam de eius conversione quam de gentis Gothorum innovatione in domino exultarent et divinae dignationi pro tanto munere gratias agerent, memoratus sanctissimus princeps sic venerandum concilium alloquitur dicens:
Since for the sake of the sincerity of his faith the same most glorious prince had commanded all the pontiffs of his governance to convene into one, so that both concerning his conversion and concerning the renewal of the Gothic people they might exult in the Lord and to the divine condescension for so great a gift give thanks, the aforementioned most holy prince thus addresses the venerable council, saying:
¶2
Non incognitum reor esse vobis, reverentissimi sacerdotes, quod propter instaurandam disciplinae ecclesiasticae formam ad nostrae vos serenitatis praesentiam evocaverim, et quia recursis retro temporibus heresis imminens in tota ecclesia catholica agere synodica negotia denegabat, deus, cui placuit per nos eiusdem heresis obicem depellere, admonuit instituta de more ecclesiastico reparare. Ergo sit vobis iocunditatis, sit gaudii, quod mos canonicus prospectu, dum per nostram gloriam ad paternos reducitur terminos. Prius tamen admoneo pariter et exhortor ieiuniis vos et vigiliis atque orationibus operam dare, ut ordo canonicus, quem a sacerdotalibus sensibus detraxerat longa ac diurna oblivio, quam aetas nostra se nescire fatetur, divino vobis rursus dono patefiat.
It is not unknown, I think, to you, most reverent priests, that for the sake of restoring the form of ecclesiastical discipline I have summoned you to the presence of our serenity, and because, with the times past having been traversed, the impending heresy was denying synodal business to be transacted in the whole catholic church, God, to whom it has pleased through us the barrier of the same heresy to drive away, has admonished the institutes according to ecclesiastical custom to be restored. Therefore let it be to you a matter of joy, let it be a matter of gladness, that the canonical custom, while by our glory it is led back to the paternal boundaries, is in view. First however I admonish equally and exhort that you give your labor to fastings and vigils and prayers, that the canonical order, which long and daily oblivion has withdrawn from priestly senses, which our age confesses itself not to know, by divine gift again to you may be laid open.
¶3
Ad haec autem gratias deo agentes et religiosissimo principi universo concilio in laudibus acclamante, triduanum est exinde praedicatum ieiunium.
To these things moreover, giving thanks to God and to the most religious prince with the whole council acclaiming in praises, a three-day fast was thereupon proclaimed.
¶4
Sed cum die octavo Id. Maiar. in unum coetum dei sacerdotes adessent et oratione praemissa unusquisque sacerdotum competenti loco resedisset, ecce in medio eorum adfuit serenissimus princeps seque cum dei sacerdotibus orationi communicans, divino deinceps flumine plenus sic alloquendum exorsus est dicens:
But when on the eighth day of the Ides of May the priests of God had come together into one assembly and, prayer having been offered, each of the priests had sat down in his proper place, behold in their midst the most serene prince stood forth and, having communicated with the priests of God in prayer, thereafter full of the divine stream, thus began speaking, saying:
¶5
Non credimus vestram latere sanctitatem, quanto tempore in errore Arrianorum laborasset Spania et non multos post discessum genitoris nostri dies, quibus nos vestra beatitudo fidei sanctae catholicae cognovit esse sociatos, credimus generaliter magnum et aeternum gaudium habuisse, et ideo, venerandi patres, ad hanc vos peragendam congregavimus synodum, ut de hominibus nuper advenientibus ad Christum ipsi aeternas domino gratias deferatis. Quicquid vero verbis apud sacerdotium vestrum nobis agendum erat de fide atque spe nostra, quam gerimus, in hoc tomo conscripta atque allegata innotescimus. Relegatur enim in medio vestri et in iudicio synodali examinata per omne succiduum tempus gloria nostra eiusdem fidei testimonio decorata clarescat.
We do not believe that your holiness is ignorant how long a time in the error of the Arians Spain had labored, and that not many days after the departure of our parent, in which your blessedness knew us to have been joined to the faith of the holy catholic church, we believe you had great and eternal joy; and therefore, venerable fathers, we have assembled you to this synod to be held, so that concerning men recently coming to Christ you yourselves may give eternal thanks to the Lord. Whatever indeed was to be done among the priesthood of you by words concerning our faith and hope which we bear, in this tome, written and presented, we make known. Let it be read therefore in your midst, and in synodal judgment having been examined, through all future time may our glory, adorned by the testimony of the same faith, become bright.
¶6
Susceptus est autem ab omnibus dei sacerdotibus offerente rege sacrosanctae fidei tomus et pronuntiante notario clara voce recensitus est:
The tome of sacrosanct faith was moreover received by all the priests of God, with the king offering it, and with a notary pronouncing, it was read out in a clear voice:
¶7
THE TOME OF FAITH
Quamvis deus omnipotens pro utilitatibus populorum regni nobis culmen subire tribuerit et moderamen gentium non paucarum regiae nostrae curae commiserit, meminimus tamen nos mortalium condicione perstringi nec posse famulabatur errori, ad agnitionem fidei et ecclesiae catholicae consortium revocarem. Adest enim omnis gens Gothorum inclita et fere omnium gentium genuina virilitate opinata, quae licet suorum pravitate doctorum a fide hactenus vel unitate ecclesiae fuerit catholicae segregata, toto nunc tamen infelicitatem futurae beatitudinis aliter promereri, nisi nos cultui verae fidei reputemus et conditori nostro salutis confessione, quo dignus ipse est, placeamus. Pro qua re quanto subditorum gloria regali extollimus, tanto providi esse debemus in his, quae a deo sunt vel nostram spem agere vel gentibus nobis a deo creditis consulere.
Although omnipotent God for the utilities of peoples has granted to us to undergo the summit of the kingdom, and the governance of not a few nations He has committed to our royal care, we remember nevertheless that we are bound by the condition of mortals, and that it cannot be imputed to error, unless to the acknowledgment of faith and to the fellowship of the catholic church we should recall. For every renowned Gothic nation stands present, and the genuine manhood of nearly all nations has esteemed it, which, although by the depravity of its own teachers it has thus far been segregated from the faith or from the unity of the catholic church, now nevertheless cannot otherwise merit the unhappiness of future blessedness, unless we repute ourselves to the cult of true faith and please our Creator with the confession of salvation, by which, as He Himself is worthy, we may please. For which reason, by as much as we are exalted by the glory of royal subjects, by so much we ought to be provident in those things which are of God, either to conduct our hope or to consult for the nations entrusted to us by God.
¶8
Ceterum quid pro tantis beneficiorum collationibus omnipotentiae divinae valemus tribuere, quando omnia ipsius sint et bonorum nostrorum nihil egeat, nisi ut in eum sic tota devotione credamus, quemadmodum per scripturas sacras se ipse intellegi voluit et credi praecepit, id est, ut confiteamur esse patrem, qui genuerit ex sua substantia filium sibi coaequalem et coaeternum, non tamen, ut ipse idem natus ingenitus, sed persona alius sit pater, qui genuit, alius sit filius, qui fuerit generatus, unius tamen uterque substantiae in divinitate subsistat.
Moreover, what for so great a collection of benefits are we able to render to omnipotent God, when all things are His and He has need of nothing of our goods, unless that we believe in Him with such total devotion as He Himself wished through the sacred scriptures to be understood and commanded to be believed, that is, that we confess the Father to exist, who begot from His own substance a Son co-equal and co-eternal to Himself, not however that He Himself the same is begotten, ingenerated, but that the person is other, the Father who begot, other is the Son who was begotten, yet both subsist of one substance in divinity.
¶9
Spiritusque sanctus confitendus a nobis et praedicandus est a patre et filio procedere, et cum patre et filio unius esse substantiae. Tertiam vero in trinitate spiritus sancti esse personam, qui tamen communionem habet cum patre et filio in divinitatis essentia. Haec enim sancta trinitas unus est deus, pater et filius et spiritus sanctus, cuius bonitate hominis, licet bona sit condita creatura, per assumptam tamen a filio humani habitus formam a damnata progenie reformamur ad beatitudinem pristinam.
And the Holy Spirit is to be confessed and preached by us to proceed from the Father and the Son, and with the Father and the Son to be of one substance. The third indeed in the Trinity is the person of the Holy Spirit, who nevertheless has communion with the Father and the Son in the essence of divinity. For this holy Trinity is one God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, by whose goodness of man, although a good creature was founded, through the assumed however by the Son of the form of human habit, we are reformed from a damned progeny to pristine blessedness.
¶10
Sed sicut verae salutis indicium est trinitatem in unitate et unitatem in trinitate sentiri, ita erit consummata iustitia, si eandem intra universalem ecclesiam teneamus et apostolicam unitatem in apostolico positi fundamento servemus. Vos tamen dei sacerdotes meminisse oportet, quanta hucusque ecclesia dei catholica per Spanias aversae partis molestiis laboraverit, dum et catholici constanter fidei suae tenerent ac defenderent veritatem, et heretici pertinaciori animositati propriae niterentur perfidiae.
But just as it is an indication of true salvation that the Trinity in unity and the unity in Trinity be understood, so will there be consummated justice, if we hold the same within the universal church and preserve apostolic unity placed upon the apostolic foundation. Yet you, priests of God, ought to remember how greatly the catholic church of God through Spain has thus far labored under the troubles of the adverse faction, while catholics consistently held and defended the truth of their faith, and heretics strived with more pertinacious animosity for their own perfidy.
¶11
Me quoque, ut res ipsa conspicit, calore fidei accensum in hoc dominus excitavit, ut depulsa obstinatione infidelitatis et discordiae submoto furore populum, qui sub nomine religionis diu laboraverat, non alium scilicet nisi ut nostrae credulitatis assensus concordans eius ecclesiae communioni participatur, quae diversarum gentium multitudinem materno in sinu suscipit et caritatis uberibus nutrit, de qua propheta canente dicitur: Domus mea domus orationis vocabitur omnibus gentibus.
Me also, as the matter itself sees, inflamed by the heat of faith, the Lord has aroused for this purpose, that with the obstinacy of infidelity having been driven away and the fury of discord having been removed, the people who had labored under the name of religion, by such assent concordantly, may participate in the communion of His church, which the multitude of diverse nations receives in maternal bosom and nourishes with the breasts of charity, concerning which the prophet singing says: "My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations."
¶12
Nec enim sola Gothorum conversio ad cumulum nostrae mercedis accessit, quin immo et Suevorum gentis infinita multitudo, quam praesidio caelesti nostro regno subiecimus. Alieno licet vitio in heresim deductam, nostro tamen ad veritatis originem studio revocavimus.
For not only the conversion of the Goths to the accumulation of our reward has come, but indeed also the infinite multitude of the Suevi people, whom by heavenly aid we have subjected to our kingdom. Although brought into heresy by a foreign fault, yet by our zeal to the origin of truth we have recalled.
¶13
Proinde, sanctissimi patres, has nobilissimas gentes, quae lucris per nos dominicis applicatae sunt, quasi sanctum et placabile sacrificium, per vestras manus aeterno deo offero. Erit enim mihi immarcescibilis corona vel gaudium in retributione iustorum, si hi populi, qui nostra ad unitatem ecclesiae sollertia transcurrerunt, fundati in eadem et stabiliti permaneant.
Therefore, most holy fathers, these most noble peoples, which have been applied to the Lord's gains by us, as a holy and placable sacrifice, by your hands I offer to the eternal God. For there will be for me an unfading crown or joy in the retribution of the just, if these peoples, who have passed over to the unity of the church by our diligence, founded in the same and stabilized, remain.
¶14
Sicut enim divino nutu nostrae curae fuit hos populos ad unitatem Christi ecclesiae pertrahere, ita sit vestrae docibilitatis catholicis eos dogmatibus instituere, quo in tota cognizione veritatis instructi noverint ex toto ex solido errores perniciosos respuere et verae fidei tramitem ex caritate retinere vel catholicae ecclesiae communionem desiderio avidiori amplectere.
For just as by divine will it was for our care to draw these peoples to the unity of the Church of Christ, so let it be for your teachableness to instruct them in catholic dogmas, that instructed in the whole cognition of truth they may know altogether and entirely to reject pernicious errors and to retain the path of true faith out of charity and to embrace the communion of the catholic church with more avid desire.
¶15
Ceterum sicut facile ad veniam pervenisse confido, quod nescia huc usque tam carissima erraverit gens, ita gravius esse non dubito, si agnitam veritatem dubio corde teneant atque a potenti lumine, quod absit, oculos suos avertant. Unde valde pernecessarium esse prospexit vestram in unum convenire beatitudinem, habens sententiae dominicae fidem, qua dicit: Ubi fuerint duo aut tres collecti in nomine meo, ibi ero in medio eorum.
Moreover, just as I trust that it has easily come to forgiveness that a so cherished people has erred until now unknowingly, so I do not doubt that it is graver if, having acknowledged the truth, they should hold it with doubtful heart and turn away their eyes from the powerful light (which may God forbid). Whence I have seen it to be very necessary that your blessedness convene into one, having faith in the lordly sentence, by which He says: "Where two or three have been gathered in my name, there I will be in their midst."
¶16
Credo enim beatam sanctae trinitatis divinitatem huic sancto interesse concilio, et ideo tam quam ante conspectum dei ita in medio vestri fidem meam, quam protuli, conscius admodum sententiae divinae dicentis: Non celavi misericordiam tuam et veritatem tuam a congregatione multa, vel apostolum Paulum Timotheo discipulo praecipientem audivi: Certa bonum certamen fidei, appraehende vitam aeternam, in qua vocatus es, confessus bonam confessionem coram multis testibus.
For I believe the blessed divinity of the holy Trinity to be present at this holy council, and therefore as if before the sight of God, thus in your midst my faith, which I have brought forth, conscious as it were of the divine sentence saying: "I have not hidden your mercy and your truth from a great congregation," and I have heard the apostle Paul enjoining Timothy his disciple: "Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life, to which you have been called and have confessed the good confession before many witnesses."
¶17
Vera est enim redemptoris nostri ex evangelio sententia, qua confitentem se coram hominibus confiteri dicit coram patre et negantem se esse negaturum. Expedit enim nobis id ore profiteri, quod corde credimus, secundum caeleste mandatum, quo dicitur: Corde enim creditur ad iustitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem.
True indeed is the sentence of our Redeemer from the gospel, by which He says that He who confesses Him before men He will confess before the Father, and that He who denies Him He will deny. For it is expedient for us to profess with the mouth that which we believe with the heart, according to the heavenly command, by which it is said: "For with the heart it is believed unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."
¶18
Proinde sicut anathematizo Arrium cum omnibus dogmatibus et complicibus suis, qui unigenitum dei filium a paterna degenerem nascebatur esse substantiam nec ex nihilo dicebat esse creatum, vel omnia concilia malignantium, quae adversus sanctam synodum Nicenam exstiterunt. Ita in honorem et in laudem dei fidem sanctam Niceni concilii observo et honoro, et ea per vestras manus aeterno deo offero.
Therefore just as I anathematize Arius with all the dogmas and accomplices of his, who asserted that the only-begotten Son of God was from a degenerate mass of the Father's substance nor from nothing said was created, or all the councils of those acting wickedly, which have stood against the holy synod of Nicaea. Thus in honor and in praise of God, I observe and honor the holy faith of the Nicene council, and through your hands I offer it to the eternal God.
¶19
Et anathematizo omnia quae contra eundem rectae fidei pestem Arrium trecentorum et decem et octo sancta episcopalis scripsit synodus. Amplector itaque et teneo fidem centum quinquaginta episcoporum Constantinopolim congregatorum, quae Macedonium spiritus sancti substantiam minorantem et a patris et filii unitate et essentia segregantem iugulo veritatis interemit. Prima quoque Ephesina synodus fidem, quae adversus Nestorium eiusque doctrinam lata est, credo pariter et honoro. Similiter et Chalcedonensis concilii fidem, quam plenam sanctitate et eruditione adversus Eutycen et Dioscorum protulit, cum omni ecclesia catholica reverenter suscipio. Omnium quoque orthodoxorum venerabilium sacerdotum concilia, quae ab ipsis suprascriptis quattuor synodis fidei puritate non dissonant, pari veneratione observo.
And I anathematize all those things which the same pest of the right faith, Arius, and the holy episcopal synod of 318 wrote against him. I embrace therefore and hold the faith of the 150 bishops gathered at Constantinople, which Macedonius, diminishing the substance of the Holy Spirit and separating Him from the unity and essence of the Father and the Son, with the sword of truth slew. The first also Ephesian synod, the faith which against Nestorius and his doctrine was issued, I believe equally and honor. Similarly also the faith of the Chalcedonian council, which full of sanctity and erudition against Eutyches and Dioscorus it put forth, together with the whole catholic church I reverently receive. The councils also of all orthodox venerable priests, which from those same four synods written above in purity of faith do not dissent, with equal veneration I observe.
¶20
Properet ergo reverentia vestra fidem hanc nostram canonicis applicare monumentis, ut ab episcopis vel religiosis et gentis nostrae primoribus sollerter fides, quam in ecclesia catholica crediderunt, legatur. Quam renovata apicibus vel eorum subscriptionibus roboratam futuris olim temporibus in testimonium dei atque omnium fidelium reservate, ut gentes, quas in dei nomine regia potestate praecellimus, et quae relicto antiquo errore per unctionem sacrosancti chrismatis et manus impositionem paraclitum intra dei ecclesiam perceperunt spiritum, quem unum et aequalem cum patre et filio confitentur, eiusque dono in sinu ecclesiae sanctae catholicae collocatae sunt.
Let your reverence therefore hasten to apply this our faith to canonical monuments, so that by bishops and religious persons and the chief men of our nation the faith which they have believed in the catholic church may be read skillfully. Which, renewed by copies and with their subscriptions strengthened, for future times as a testimony of God and of all the faithful, preserve, that the nations over which in the name of God we excel by royal power, and which, the ancient error having been abandoned, through the unction of sacrosanct chrism and the imposition of hands have received the Paraclete Spirit within the church of God, whom one and equal with the Father and the Son they confess, and by whose gift within the bosom of the holy catholic church they have been placed.
¶21
Quodsi eorum aliqui hanc credulitatem et sanctam confessionem nostram minime credere voluerint, iram dei cum anathemate aeterno percipiant, ut de interitu suo fidelibus gaudium, infidelibus sint in exemplum. Huic vero confessioni meae sanctarum scripturarum et conciliorum constitutionibus consentienti et testimonio divino tota cordis simplicitate subscripsi.
But if any of them have been unwilling to believe this credulity and our holy confession, let them receive the wrath of God with eternal anathema, so that from their destruction there may be joy for the faithful, and for the unbelieving they may be an example. To this my confession, moreover, consenting to the constitutions of the holy scriptures and councils, and with divine testimony, with all simplicity of heart I have subscribed.
¶22
THE FAITH ISSUED BY THE HOLY NICENE COUNCIL
*INCIPIT FIDES A SANCTO NICENO CONCILIO*
Credimus in unum deum patrem omnipotentem omnium visibilium et invisibilium conditorem et in unum dominum Iesum filium dei de patre natum unigenitum, hoc est, de substantia patris, deum ex deo, lumen ex lumine, deum verum ex deo vero, natum non factum, homousion patri, hoc est eiusdem cum patre substantiae, per quem omnia facta sunt, quae in caelo et quae in terra, qui propter nos et propter nostram salutem descendit et incarnatus est, homo factus passus est et resurrexit tertia die et ascendit in caelos, inde venturus iudicare vivos et mortuos, et in spiritum sanctum. Eos autem, qui dicunt, erat, quando non erat, et antequam nasceretur, non erat, et quia ex nullis exsistentibus factus est aut ex alia substantia vel subsistentia dicunt esse aut convertibilem filium dei, anathematizat catholica et apostolica ecclesia.
We believe in one God the Father omnipotent, of all visible and invisible the Creator, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God, of the Father born, only-begotten, that is, of the substance of the Father, God from God, light from light, true God from true God, born not made, homousion to the Father, that is, of the same substance with the Father, through whom all things were made, which in heaven and which in earth, who for us and for our salvation descended and was incarnate, was made man, suffered and rose again on the third day and ascended into heaven, from there about to come to judge the living and the dead, and in the Holy Spirit. Those moreover who say: "There was when He was not," and "Before He was born, He was not," and that from non-existent things He was made, or from another substance or subsistence they say He is, or changeable the Son of God, the catholic and apostolic church anathematizes.
¶23
*ITEM SANCTA FIDES, QUAM EXPOSUERUNT PATRES CL CONSONA VOCE, QUI CONSTANTINOPOLIM CONGREGATI SUNT.*
LIKEWISE THE HOLY FAITH, WHICH THE 150 FATHERS WITH CONSONANT VOICE SET FORTH, WHO WERE GATHERED AT CONSTANTINOPLE
Credimus in unum deum patrem omnipotentem factorem caeli et terrae, visibilium omnium et invisibilium conditorem et in unum dominum Iesum Christum filium dei unigenitum, ex patre natum ante omnia saecula, deum ex deo, lumen ex lumine, deum verum ex deo vero, natum non factum, homousion patri, hoc est, eiusdem cum patre substantiae, per quem omnia facta sunt, et quae in caelo et quae in terra, qui propter nos et propter nostram salutem descendit et incarnatus est de spiritu sancto et Maria virgine, homo factus passus est sub Pontio Pilato et sepultus est et tertia die resurrexit, ascendit in caelos, sedit ad dexteram patris, iterum venturus cum gloria iudicare vivos et mortuos, cuius regni non erit finis, et in spiritum sanctum dominum et vivificatorem ex patre procedentem cum patre et filio adorandum et glorificandum, qui locutus est per prophetas, unam catholicam atque apostolicam ecclesiam. Confitemur unum baptisma in remissionem peccatorum, exspectamus resurrectionem mortuorum, vitam futuri saeculi. Amen.
We believe in one God the Father omnipotent, maker of heaven and earth, of all visible and invisible the Creator, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God the only-begotten, of the Father born before all ages, God from God, light from light, true God from true God, born not made, homousion to the Father, that is, of the same substance with the Father, through whom all things were made, both which in heaven and which in earth, who for us and for our salvation descended and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and Mary the Virgin, was made man, suffered under Pontius Pilate and was buried, and rose again on the third day, ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father, again about to come with glory to judge the living and the dead, of whose kingdom there shall be no end, and in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and Life-giver, from the Father proceeding, with the Father and the Son to be adored and glorified, who spoke through the prophets, one catholic and apostolic church. We confess one baptism for the remission of sins, we await the resurrection of the dead, the life of the future age. Amen.
¶24
LIKEWISE THE TREATISE OF THE CHALCEDONIAN COUNCIL
*ITEM TRACTATUS CHALCEDONENSIS CONCILII*
Suffecerat quidem ad plenissimam pietatis agnitionem et confirmationem cautissimum hoc et salutare divinae gratiae symbolum. De patre enim et filio et spiritu sancto doctrinam perfectam edocet et incarnationem dominicam fideliter suscipientibus manifestat. Sed quoniam, qui praedicationem veritatis destruere nituntur, quasdam propriae hereseos novitates pariunt, quidam enim mysterium pro nobis actum divinae dispensationis audent corrumpere et vocem illam virginei partus factam ad virginem denegant, alii temperamentum confusionum inducentes et unam esse naturam carnis et deitatis insensate componentes, passibilem unigeniti divinam naturam tali confusione prodigiose divulgant, idcirco omnem adversus veritatem opponendam ab ipsis machinationem volens excludere sancta et magna universalis synodus antiquam praedicationem immobilem docens, statuit praecipue trecentorum X et VIII sanctorum patrum fidem incontaminatam manere.
This most cautious and salutary symbol of divine grace would have sufficed indeed for the most full acknowledgment and confirmation of piety. For concerning the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit it teaches perfect doctrine and to those faithfully receiving it manifests the Lord's incarnation. But since those who strive to destroy the preaching of truth bring forth certain novelties of their own heresy — for some dare to corrupt the mystery of divine dispensation done for us and deny that saying of the virgin birth was made to the Virgin, others introducing a mixture of confessions and insanely composing one nature of flesh and deity, by such confusion prodigiously divulge the divine nature of the only-begotten as passible — therefore the holy and great universal synod, wishing to exclude every machination from these themselves to be opposed against the truth, teaching the ancient preaching immovable, established that the faith of the 318 holy fathers remain incontaminated.
¶25
Et propter eos, qui spiritui sancto adversantur, centum quinquaginta patrum paulo posteriori tempore in urbe Constantinopolitana convenientium de substantia spiritus sancti traditam doctrinam corroborat, quam etiam illi omnibus insinuaverunt, non quod in praecedentibus aliquid deesset adicientes, sed de spiritu sancto eorundem intellectum, adversus eos, qui deitatis eius damnationem nituntur adimere scripturarum testimoniis plenius manifestantes.
And on account of those who oppose the Holy Spirit, it corroborates the doctrine concerning the substance of the Holy Spirit delivered by the 150 fathers convening in the city of Constantinople at a somewhat later time, which also they made known to all, not that they added anything lacking in the preceding, but concerning the Holy Spirit their understanding against those who strive to take away the damnation of His deity, manifesting more fully by the testimonies of the scriptures.
¶26
Propter eos sane, qui dispensationis mysterium temptant corrumpere et purum hominem esse, qui ex sancta virgine natus est, impudenter divulgant, beatissimi quondam viri Cyrilli Alexandrinae ecclesiae sacerdotis synodicas epistolas tam ad Nestorium quam ad ceteros per orientem congruas et sibi consentientes suscipit ad confutationem quidem Nestorianae amentiae, interpretationem vero eorum, qui religioso zelo salutaris symboli cupiunt intellectum, quibus et epistolam sancti ac beatissimi primae sedis archiepiscopi Leonis ad Flavianum scriptam sanctae recordationis archiepiscopo ad perimendam Euticetis malignitatem, quae magni Petri confessioni concordat et communem quendam paginam exsistentem contra eos, qui recte non glorificant ad confirmationem catholicae religionis evidenter subiunxit; et eos, qui in duos filios dispensationis dominicae mysterium scindere moliuntur, exsecratur et eos, qui in duas naturas Christi temperamentum vel confusionem argumentantur, adversatur, et qui caelestem aut alterius cuiusque substantiam exsistere formam servi, quam pro nobis assumpsit, insaniendo asserunt, procul abicit, et eos, qui duas quidem ante unitionem naturas, unam vero post unitionem configunt, anathema facit.
On account of those truly who tempt to corrupt the mystery of dispensation and impudently divulge that the man who was born of the holy Virgin is a pure man, it receives the synodical epistles of the once blessed man Cyril, priest of the Alexandrian church, both to Nestorius and to the others through the east, suitable and consentient to himself, for the confutation indeed of Nestorian madness, and for the interpretation however of those who with religious zeal wish to grasp the understanding of the salvific symbol; and also the epistle of the holy and most blessed Leo, archbishop of the first see, written to Flavian, archbishop of holy memory, for destroying the malignity of Eutyches, which epistle agrees with the confession of great Peter and as it were a certain common page existing against those who do not rightly glorify, to the confirmation of the catholic religion he evidently added; and those who strive to split the mystery of the Lord's dispensation into two sons he execrates; and those who argue for a mixture or confusion of the two natures of Christ he opposes; and those who in madness assert that the form of a servant, which He assumed for us, is of a heavenly or of any other substance he casts far away; and those who contrive two natures indeed before the union but one after the union he anathematizes.
¶27
Consentientes igitur sanctis patribus unum eundemque filium confiteri dominum nostrum Iesum Christum consona voce pariter edocemur, perfectum eundem in divinitate, perfectum eundem in humanitate, deum verum et hominem verum eundem ex anima rationali et corpore, secundum divinitatem unius cum patre naturae, secundum humanitatem eundem unius naturae nobiscum, per omnia similem nobis absque peccato, ante saecula quidem ex patre natum secundum divinitatem, novissimis vero diebus eundem propter nos et propter salutem nostram ex Maria virgine dei genetrice secundum humanitatem, unum eundemque Christum filium dominum unigenitum in duabus naturis inconfuse, immutabiliter, indivise, inseparabiliter cognoscendum, in nullo naturarum differentias propter unitatem perimendas, magis autem salva utriusque naturae proprietate et in una coeuntem persona unoque statu concurrentem, non in duabus personis partiendum vel dividendum, sed unum eundemque filium unigenitum deum verum dominum Iesum Christum, sicut ab exordio prophetae de eo, et ipse nos erudivit, et patrum nobis tradidit symbolum.
Consenting therefore to the holy fathers, we are taught with consonant voice equally to confess one and the same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, the same perfect in divinity, the same perfect in humanity, true God and true man, the same from a rational soul and body, according to divinity of one nature with the Father, according to humanity of one nature with us, similar to us in all things without sin, before ages indeed of the Father born according to divinity, in the last days however the same for us and for our salvation from Mary the Virgin, the God-bearer, according to humanity, one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, only-begotten, in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation, to be acknowledged, in nothing the differences of the natures on account of the union to be destroyed, but rather the property of each nature being saved and concurring into one person and one state, not to be divided or split into two persons, but one and the same Son, only-begotten, true God, Lord Jesus Christ, just as from the beginning the prophets concerning Him, and He Himself taught us, and the symbol of the fathers delivered to us.
¶28
His itaque cum omni subtilitate et diligentia a nobis ordinatis, statuit sancta et universalis synodus aliam fidem nulli licere proferre aut scribere aut edere aut separare aut docere aliter. Qui autem audent aut disponere aliam fidem aut proferre aut docere aut tradere alium symbolum volentibus converti ad scientiam veritatis ex gentibus vel Iudaeis vel hereticis quibuscumque, si quidem aut episcopi aut clerici fuerint, alienos se esse episcopos ab episcopatu et clericos a clero, si vero monachi aut laici fuerint, anathemate feriri.
These things therefore with all subtlety and diligence having been ordained by us, the holy and universal synod established that it is not lawful for anyone to put forth or write or compose or separate or teach otherwise. Whoever moreover dare to prepare another faith or to put forth or to teach or to deliver another symbol to those wishing to be converted to the knowledge of the truth, whether from gentiles or from Jews or from any heretics, if indeed they are bishops or clerics, they are to be alien bishops from the episcopate and clerics from the clergy; if however they are monks or laypersons, they are to be struck with anathema.
¶29
Ego Recaredus rex fidem hanc sanctam et veram confessionem, quam una per totum orbem catholica confitetur ecclesia corde retinens, ore affirmans, mea dextera deo protegente subscripsi.
I, King Recared, holding in heart, affirming with mouth, this holy faith and true confession, which the one catholic church throughout the whole world confesses, with my right hand, God protecting, have subscribed.
¶30
Ego Balda gloriosa regina hanc fidem, quam credidi et suscepi, manu mea de toto corde subscripsi.
I, Badda, glorious queen, this faith which I have believed and received, with my hand from my whole heart have subscribed.

THE ACCLAMATIONS
Tunc acclamatum est in laudibus dei et in favore principis ab universo concilio:
Then it was acclaimed in the praises of God and in favor of the prince by the whole council:
¶32
Gloria deo patri et filio et spiritui sancto, cui cura est pacem et unitatem ecclesiae suae sanctae catholicae providere.
I. Glory to God the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, whose care it is to provide peace and unity to His holy catholic church.
¶33
Gloria domino nostro Iesu Christo, qui pretio sanguinis sui ecclesiam catholicam ex omnibus gentibus congregavit.
II. Glory to our Lord Jesus Christ, who by the price of His blood gathered the catholic church from all nations.
¶34
Gloria domino nostro Iesu Christo, qui tam illustrem gentem unitati verae fidei copulavit et unum gregem et unum pastorem instituit.
III. Glory to our Lord Jesus Christ, who has joined such an illustrious nation to the unity of true faith and has established one flock and one shepherd.
¶35

IV. To whom from God is eternal merit, if not to the truly catholic King Recared?
Cui a deo aeterna corona nisi vero orthodoxo Recaredo regi?
V. To whom from God is an eternal crown, if not to the truly orthodox King Recared?
Cui praesens gloria et aeterna, nisi vero amatori deo Recaredo regi?

Ipse novarum plebium in ecclesia catholica conquisitor.

Ipse mereatur veraciter apostolicum meritum, qui apostolicum implevit officium.

Ipse sit deo et omnibus amabilis, qui tam mirabiliter deum glorificavit in terris praestante domino nostro Iesu Christo, qui cum deo patre vivit et regnat in unitate spiritus sancti in saecula saeculorum.
IX. May he himself be lovable to God and to all, who so wonderfully glorified God on earth, our Lord Jesus Christ granting, who with God the Father lives and reigns in the unity of the Holy Spirit forever and ever.
¶36
IN NOMINE DOMINI NOSTRI IESU CHRISTI ITEM FIDEI CONFESSIO episcoporum, presbyterorum vel primorum Gothicae gentis, qui infra subscripserunt.
IN THE NAME OF OUR LORD JESUS CHRIST, LIKEWISE THE CONFESSION OF FAITH OF THE BISHOPS, PRESBYTERS, AND CHIEF MEN OF THE GOTHIC NATION, WHO HAVE SUBSCRIBED BELOW.
¶37
Praecipiente autem universo venerabili concilio atque iubente, unus episcoporum catholicorum ad episcopos et religiosos clericos vel maiores natu ex heresi Arriana conversos eiusmodi allocutione exorsus est dicens:
With the whole venerable council commanding and ordering, one of the catholic bishops, to the bishops and religious clerics and the elder men converted from the Arian heresy, began with an address of this kind, saying:
¶38
Officii nostri cura et fidelissimi ac gloriosissimi principis admonitione compellimur, diligenter a vestra caritate perquirere, vel quid damnetis in heresi aut quid intra sanctam catholicam credatis ecclesiam. Nam dicente psalmista dicimus: Incipite domino in confessione, optimum est et vestrae saluti conveniens palam confiteri, quod creditis, et sub auditu universorum anathematizare, quod respuitis. Tunc prorsus optime poteritis evangelicae atque apostolicae fidei participes fieri, si eandem fidem catholicam ex confessione catholica incipiatis vel propria subscriptione firmetis, et sicuti deo iam de bona consensionis cogniti estis conscientia, ita et proximus vos fidei sanctae adstipulatione monstretis. Eo itaque fiet, ut et vos Christi esse corporis membra significetis et nostra exiguitas nihil dubium, nihil infidum umquam de vestra suspicetur fraternitate, dum patuerit vos pestem perfidiae Arrianae cum omnibus dogmatibus, regulis, officiis, communione, codicibus praedamnare ac detestandae hereseos exspoliati contagione, innovati quodammodo intra ecclesiam dei splendido habitu verae fidei clareatis.
The care of our office and the admonition of the most faithful and most glorious prince compel us diligently to inquire from your charity both what you condemn in the heresy and what you believe within the holy catholic church. For, as the psalmist says, we say: "Begin to the Lord in confession"; it is best and suitable for your salvation openly to confess what you believe, and in the hearing of all to anathematize what you reject. Then indeed you will be able most excellently to become partakers of the evangelical and apostolic faith if you begin that same catholic faith by catholic confession and strengthen it by your own subscription, and just as you are already known to God concerning the good conscience of agreement, thus also you may show yourselves to the neighbor by the affirmation of holy faith. By this it will come about that both you may signify that you are members of the body of Christ, and our insignificance may never suspect anything doubtful or anything faithless concerning your fraternity, when it shall have been made clear that you condemn the pest of Arian perfidy with all its dogmas, rules, offices, communion, books, and, despoiled of the contagion of the detestable heresy, being in a manner renewed, you may become bright within the church of God in the splendid habit of true faith.
¶39
Tunc episcopi omnes una cum clericis suis primoresque gentis Gothicae pari consensione dixerunt: Licet hoc, quod paternitas atque fraternitas vestra a nobis cupit audire vel fieri, tam olim conversionis nostrae tempore egerimus, quando secuti gloriosissimum dominum nostrum Recaredum regem hac die ad ecclesiam transivimus et perfidiam Arrianam cum omnibus superstitionibus suis anathematizavimus pariterque abiecimus, nunc vero propter caritatem et devotionem, quam vel deo vel ecclesiae sanctae catholicae meminimus nos debere, non tantum haec eadem, quae petitis, promptissime agere properamus, sed et, si qua adhuc congrua fidei esse prospicitis ac nobis caritate persuadetis, faciemus. Nos etenim semel rectae fidei amor in eam devotionem advexit, ut omne, quod nobis verius fraternitate vestra patefacitis, teneamus et libera fateamur confessione.
Then all the bishops, together with their clerics and the chief men of the Gothic nation, with equal consent said: Although this which your paternity and fraternity desires to hear or to be done from us, we have already done at the time of our conversion long ago, when we followed our most glorious Lord King Recared and on this day passed over to the church and anathematized Arian perfidy with all its superstitions and likewise cast it away, yet now on account of the charity and devotion which we remember that we owe both to God and to the holy catholic church, we hasten not only to do these same things which you ask most promptly, but also, if you see anything yet suitable to the faith and persuade us in charity, we will do it. For the love of the right faith once for all has brought us into that devotion, that we may hold whatever you more truly make known to us by your fraternity and confess with free confession.
¶40
THE ANATHEMAS
Omnis ergo, qui fidem et communionem ab Arrio venientem et usque ad nos retentam adhuc tenere desiderat et de tota cordis intentione non damnat, anathema sit.
Whosoever wishes still to hold the faith and communion coming from Arius and retained even to us, and from the whole intention of the heart does not condemn it, let him be anathema.
¶41
Quicumque filium dei dominum Iesum Christum negaverit a paterna substantia sine initio genitum et aequalem patri esse vel consubstantialem, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall have denied that the Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, was begotten from the paternal substance without beginning and is equal to the Father or consubstantial, let him be anathema.
¶42
Quicumque spiritum sanctum non credit aut non crediderit a patre et filio procedere, eumque non dixerit coaeternum esse patri et filio et coessentialem, anathema sit.
Whosoever does not believe that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son, or shall not have believed, and does not say that He is co-eternal with the Father and the Son and co-essential, let him be anathema.
¶43
Quicumque in patre et filio et spiritu sancto et personas non distinguit et unius divinitatis substantiam non agnoscit, anathema sit.
Whosoever in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit both does not distinguish the persons and does not acknowledge the substance of one divinity, let him be anathema.
¶44
Quicumque filium dei dominum nostrum Iesum Christum et spiritum sanctum esse iuxta deitatem patrem minorem asseruerit et gradibus separaverit creaturamque esse dixerit, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall have asserted that the Son of God our Lord Jesus Christ and the Holy Spirit, according to divinity, are lesser than the Father, and shall have separated them by degrees, and shall have said that they are creatures, let him be anathema.
¶45
Quicumque patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum unius substantiae, omnipotentiae, aeternitatis esse non credit, anathema sit.
Whosoever does not believe the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit to be of one substance, one omnipotence, one eternity, let him be anathema.
¶46
Quicumque nescire filium dei deus, quae pater sciat, dixerit, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall have said that the Son of God does not know those things which the Father knows, let him be anathema.
¶47
Quicumque initium filio dei et spiritui sancto deputaverit, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall have assigned a beginning to the Son of God and to the Holy Spirit, let him be anathema.
Quicumque filium dei secundum divinitatem suam visibilem aut passibilem ausus fuerit profiteri, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall have dared to profess the Son of God according to His divinity as visible or passible, let him be anathema.
¶48
Quicumque spiritum sanctum sicut patrem et filium verum deum et omnipotentem esse non credit, anathema sit.
Whosoever does not believe the Holy Spirit, like the Father and the Son, to be true God and omnipotent, let him be anathema.
¶49
Quicumque alibi fidem et communionem catholicam praeter ecclesiam, quam Niceni et Constantinopolitani et primi Ephesini et Chalcedonensis concilii decreta tenent pariter et honorant, anathema sit.
Whosoever holds the catholic faith and communion elsewhere than in the church which the decrees of the Nicene, Constantinopolitan, first Ephesian, and Chalcedonian councils hold and likewise honor, let him be anathema.
¶50
Quicumque patrem et filium honore et gloria et divinitate separat et disiungit, anathema sit.
Whosoever separates and disjoins the Father and the Son in honor and glory and divinity, let him be anathema.
Quicumque filium dei et spiritum sanctum cum patre non crediderit esse glorificandos et honorandos, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall not have believed that the Son of God and the Holy Spirit are to be glorified and honored with the Father, let him be anathema.
¶51
Quicumque non dixerit: Gloria patri et filio et spiritui sancto, anathema sit.
Whosoever shall not say: "Glory to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit," let him be anathema.
Quicumque rebaptizandi sacrilegium opus bonum esse credit aut crediderit, agere aut egerit, anathema sit.
Whosoever believes or shall have believed that the sacrilegious work of rebaptizing is good, or does or has done it, let him be anathema.
¶52
Quicumque libellum detestabilem XII anno Leobigildi regis a nobis editum, in quo continetur Romanorum ad heresem Arrianam transductio et in quo gloria patri per filium in spiritu sancto, quo male a nobis instituta continetur, hunc libellum, si quis pro vero habuerit, anathema sit in aeternum.
Whosoever shall have held as true that detestable little book published in the 12th year of King Leovigild by us, in which is contained the leading over of Romans to the Arian heresy and in which "Glory to the Father through the Son in the Holy Spirit" is contained, which was wickedly instituted by us, let that little book, if anyone has held it as true, be anathema forever.
¶53
THE CONFESSION
Confitemur enim nos ex heresi Arriana toto corde, tota anima et de tota mente nostra ad ecclesiam catholicam fuisse conversos. Nulli dubium sit nos nostrosque successores errasse in heresi Arriana et fidem evangelicam atque apostolicam nunc intra ecclesiam catholicam didicisse. Proinde fidem sanctam, quam religiosissimus dominus noster patefecit in medio concilii et manu sua subscripsit, hanc et nos tenemus, hanc confitemur pariter et suscipimus, hanc in populis praedicare atque docere promittimus.
For we confess that we have been converted from the Arian heresy with our whole heart, with our whole soul, and with our whole mind to the catholic church. Let there be doubt to no one that we and our predecessors have erred in the Arian heresy and have now learned the evangelical and apostolic faith within the catholic church. Therefore the holy faith which our most religious Lord laid open in the midst of the council and with his own hand subscribed, this we also hold, this we confess equally and receive, this we promise to preach and to teach among the peoples.
¶54
Haec est vera fides, quam omnis ecclesia, dum per totum mundum tenet ecclesia catholica esse, creditur et probatur. Cui haec fides non placet aut non placuerit, sit anathema maranatha in adventu domini nostri Iesu Christi.
This is the true faith, which the whole church, while it holds throughout the whole world, is believed and proven to be the catholic church. To whom this faith does not please or shall not have pleased, let him be anathema maranatha at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.
¶55
Qui fidem spernit Niceni concilii et Constantinopolitani centum quinquaginta episcoporum veramque esse non dixerit, anathema sit.
Whosoever despises the faith of the Nicene council and of the 150 bishops of Constantinople and does not say it is true, let him be anathema.
¶56
Qui fidem Ephesini synodi primi et Chalcedonensis non tenet et ea non delectatur, anathema sit.
Whosoever does not hold the faith of the first Ephesian synod and of Chalcedon and is not delighted by them, let him be anathema.
¶57
Qui concilia omnium orthodoxorum episcoporum consona conciliorum Niceni, Constantinopolitani, primi Ephesini et Chalcedonensis non recepit, anathema sit.
Whosoever does not receive the councils of all orthodox bishops consonant with the councils of Nicaea, Constantinople, the first Ephesus, and Chalcedon, let him be anathema.
¶58
Proinde damnationem hanc perfidiae communionis Arrianae et omnium conciliorum heresem Arrianam foventium cum anathemate eorum propria manu subscripsimus.
Therefore this condemnation of the perfidy of Arian communion and of all the councils favoring the Arian heresy, with anathema, we have subscribed with our own hand.
¶59
Constitutiones vero sanctorum conciliorum Niceni, Ephesini vel Chalcedonensis, quas gratissima aure audivimus et consensione nostra veram esse probavimus, de toto corde et de tota anima et de tota mente nostra subscripsimus, nihil ad cognitionem veritatis lucidius arbitrantes quam supradictorum conciliorum continent auctoritates. De trinitate autem et unitate patris et filii et spiritus sancti nihil his verius, nihil lucidius umquam potest vel poterit demonstrari, de mysterio incarnationis unigeniti filii dei pro salute humani generis, quo et vera probatur humanae naturae sine peccati constitutione susceptio et permanet incorruptae in eo divinitatis plenitudo, dum haec natura utraque non deperit et una fit ex utraque domini nostri Iesu Christi persona, satis plane in his conciliis probatur patefieri veritas, quae a nobis creditur omni remota dubitatione.
The constitutions indeed of the holy councils of Nicaea, Ephesus, and Chalcedon, which we have heard with most grateful ear and by our consent have proven to be true, with our whole heart and with our whole soul and with our whole mind we have subscribed, judging nothing more brilliant to the cognition of truth than that which the authorities of the aforesaid councils contain. Concerning the Trinity moreover and the unity of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, nothing truer than these, nothing more brilliant can ever or will ever be demonstrated. Concerning the mystery of the incarnation of the only-begotten Son of God for the salvation of the human race, by which both the true assumption of human nature without the constitution of sin is proven and the fullness of the incorrupt divinity in Him remains, while each nature does not perish and one person of our Lord Jesus Christ is made from both, it is sufficiently clearly proven that the truth is laid open in these councils, which is believed by us with all removed doubt.
¶60
Qui umquam hanc fidem sanctam depravare, corrumpere, mutare temptaverit aut ab eadem fide vel communione catholica, quam nuper sumus deo miserante adepti, se separare vel dissociare voluerit, sit deo et universo mundo crimine infidelitatis in aeternum obnoxius. Floreat autem sancta ecclesia catholica per omnem mundum pacatissime et emineat doctrina sanctitate et potestate. Si qui intra eam fuerint communicaverintque, hi audiant ad dexteram patris positi: Venite, benedicti patris mei, percipite regnum, quod vobis paratum est ab origine mundi. Si qui autem ab ea recesserint eiusque detraxerint fidei et communionem respuerint, hi audiant ore divino in die iudicii: Discedite a me, maledicti, nescio vos, ite in ignem aeternum, qui paratus est diabolo et angelis eius. Sint ergo damnata in caelo et in terra, quaecumque per hanc catholicam fidem damnantur, et sint accepta in caelo et in terra, quaecumque per hanc fidem accipiuntur, regnante domino nostro Iesu Christo, cui cum patre et spiritu sancto est gloria in saecula saeculorum.
Whoever ever shall have attempted to deprave, corrupt, or change this holy faith, or shall have wished to separate or dissociate himself from the same faith or catholic communion, which we have lately, God having mercy, attained, let him be liable to God and to the whole world for the crime of infidelity forever. Let the holy catholic church flourish however through the whole world most peacefully, and let it excel in doctrine, holiness, and power. If any shall be within it and shall have communicated, let them hear, placed at the right hand of the Father: "Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world." If any however shall have withdrawn from it and detracted from its faith and rejected communion, let them hear by the divine mouth on the day of judgment: "Depart from me, accursed ones, I know you not, go into eternal fire which has been prepared for the devil and his angels." Let those things therefore be condemned in heaven and on earth, which by this catholic faith are condemned, and let those things be accepted in heaven and on earth, which by this faith are accepted, our Lord Jesus Christ reigning, to whom with the Father and the Holy Spirit is glory forever and ever.

THE FAITH ISSUED BY THE HOLY NICENE COUNCIL (REPEATED)
*INCIPIT FIDES A SANCTO NICENO CONCILIO EDITA.*
Credimus in unum deum patrem omnipotentem omnium visibilium et invisibilium conditorem et in unum dominum Iesum Christum filium dei de patre natum unigenitum, hoc est de substantia patris, deum ex deo, lumen ex lumine, deum verum ex deo vero, natum non factum, homousion patri, hoc est eiusdem cum patre substantiae, per quem omnia facta sunt, quae in caelis et quae in terris, qui propter nos et propter nostram salutem descendit et incarnatus est, passus est et resurrexit tertia die, ascendit in caelos, inde venturus est iudicare vivos et mortuos, et in spiritum sanctum. Eos autem, qui dicunt, erat, quando non erat, et antequam nasceretur, non erat, et quia ex nullis exstantibus factus est aut ex alia substantia vel subsistentia dicunt esse aut convertibilem vel mutabilem filium dei, anathematizat catholica et apostolica ecclesia.
We believe in one God the Father omnipotent, of all visible and invisible the Creator, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God, of the Father born only-begotten, that is, of the substance of the Father, God from God, light from light, true God from true God, born not made, homousion to the Father, that is, of the same substance with the Father, through whom all things were made, which are in the heavens and which are in the earth, who for us and for our salvation descended and was incarnate, suffered and rose again on the third day, ascended into heaven, from there He is about to come to judge the living and the dead, and in the Holy Spirit. Those moreover who say: "There was when He was not," and "Before He was born, He was not," and that from non-existent things He was made, or from another substance or subsistence they say He is, or changeable or mutable the Son of God, the catholic and apostolic church anathematizes.

*ITEM SANCTA FIDES, QUAM EXPOSUERUNT CL PATRES IN CONCILIO CONSTANTINOPOLITANO.*
LIKEWISE THE HOLY FAITH, WHICH THE 150 FATHERS SET FORTH IN THE COUNCIL OF CONSTANTINOPLE
Credimus in unum deum patrem omnipotentem factorem caeli et terrae, visibilium omnium et invisibilium conditorem et in unum Iesum Christum filium dei unigenitum, ex patre natum ante omnia saecula, deum ex deo, lumen ex lumine, deum verum ex deo vero, natum non factum, homousion patri, hoc est, eiusdem cum patre substantiae, per quem omnia facta sunt, quae in caelis et quae in terris sunt, qui propter nostram salutem descendit et incarnatus est de spiritu sancto et Maria virgine, homo factus passus est sub Pontio Pilato, sepultus tertia die resurrexit, ascendit in caelos, sedet ad dexteram patris, iterum venturus in gloria iudicare vivos et mortuos, cuius regni non erit finis, et in spiritum sanctum dominum et vivificatorem ex patre procedentem cum patre et filio adorandum et glorificandum, qui locutus est per prophetas, unam catholicam atque apostolicam ecclesiam. We believe in one God the Father omnipotent, maker of heaven and earth, of all visible and invisible the Creator, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God the only-begotten, of the Father born before all ages, God from God, light from light, true God from true God, born not made, homousion to the Father, that is, of the same substance with the Father, through whom all things were made, which are in the heavens and which are in the earth, who for our salvation descended and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and Mary the Virgin, was made man, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was buried, on the third day rose again, ascended into heaven, sits at the right hand of the Father, again about to come in glory to judge the living and the dead, of whose kingdom there shall be no end, and in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and Life-giver, from the Father proceeding, with the Father and the Son to be adored and glorified, who spoke through the prophets, one catholic and apostolic church. We confess one baptism for the remission of sins, we await the resurrection of the dead, the life of the future age. Amen.
¶63
LIKEWISE THE TREATISE OF THE CHALCEDONIAN COUNCIL
*ITEM TRACTATUS CHALCEDONENSIS CONCILII.*
Suffecerat quidem ad plenissimam pietatis agnitionem et confirmationem cautissimum hoc salutare divinae gratiae symbolum. De patre enim et filio et spiritu sancto doctrinam perfectam edocet et incarnationem dominicam fideliter suscipientibus manifestat. Sed quoniam hi, qui praedicationem veritatis destruere nituntur, quasdam propriae hereseos novitates partiuntur, quidam enim mysterium pro nobis actum divinae dispensationis audent corrumpere et vocem illam virginei partus factam ad virginem denegant, alii temperamentum confusionemque inducentes et unam esse naturam carnis et deitatis insensate componentes, passibilem unigeniti divinam naturam tali confessione prodigiose divulgant, idcirco omnem adversus veritatem opponendam ab ipsis machinationem volens excludere sancta et magna universalis synodus antiquam praedicationem immobilem docens, statuit praecipue trecentorum decem et octo sanctorum patrum fidem incontaminatam manere.
This most cautious and salutary symbol of divine grace would have sufficed indeed for the most full acknowledgment and confirmation of piety. For concerning the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit it teaches perfect doctrine and to those faithfully receiving it manifests the Lord's incarnation. But since those who strive to destroy the preaching of truth bring forth certain novelties of their own heresy — for some dare to corrupt the mystery of divine dispensation done for us and deny that saying of the virgin birth was made to the Virgin, others introducing a mixture and confusion and insanely composing one nature of flesh and deity, prodigiously divulge the divine nature of the only-begotten as passible by such confession — therefore the holy and great universal synod, wishing to exclude every machination from these themselves to be opposed against the truth, teaching the ancient preaching immovable, established that the faith of the 318 holy fathers remain incontaminated.
¶64
Et propter eos, qui spiritui sancto adversabantur, CL patrum paulo posteriori tempore in urbe Constantinopolitana convenientium de substantia spiritus sancti traditam doctrinam corroborat, quam etiam omnibus insinuaverunt, non quod in praecedentibus aliquid deesset adicientes, sed de spiritu sancto eorundem intellectus adversus eos, qui deitatis dominationem nituntur adimere scripturarum testimoniis plenius manifestantes.
And on account of those who opposed the Holy Spirit, it corroborates the doctrine concerning the substance of the Holy Spirit delivered by the 150 fathers convening in the city of Constantinople at a somewhat later time, which also they made known to all, not that they added anything lacking in the preceding, but concerning the Holy Spirit their understanding against those who strive to take away the domination of His deity, manifesting more fully by the testimonies of the scriptures.
¶65
Propter eos sane, qui dispensationis mysterium temptant corrumpere et purum hominem esse, qui ex sancta virgine natus est, impudenter divulgant, beatissimi quondam Cyrilli Alexandrinae ecclesiae sacerdotis synodicas epistolas tam ad Nestorium quam ad ceteros per orientem congruas et sibi consentientes suscipit ad confutationem quidem Nestorianae amentiae et ad interpretationem eorum, qui religioso zelo salutaris symboli capiunt intellectum, quibus et epistolam sancti ac beatissimi primae sedis archiepiscopi Leonis ad Flavianum scriptam sanctae recordationis archiepiscopum ad perimendam Euticetis malignitatem, quaeque magni Petri confessioni concordat et communem quandam paginam exsistentem contra eos, qui recte non glorificant ad confirmationem catholicae religionis evidenter subiunxit. Nam eos, qui in duos filios dispensationis dominicae mysterium scindere moliuntur, exsecratur et eos, qui passibilem divinitatem unigeniti filii dei audent asserere, de concilio sacerdotum repellit, et eos, qui in duabus naturis Christi temperamentum vel confusionem argumentantur, adversatur, et qui caelestem aut alterius cuiusque substantiae exsistere formam servi, quam pro nobis assumpsit, insaniendo asserunt, procul abicit, et eos, qui duas quidem ante adunationem naturas domini delirant, unam vero post adunationem confingunt, anathema facit.
On account of those truly who tempt to corrupt the mystery of dispensation and impudently divulge that the man who was born of the holy Virgin is a pure man, it receives the synodical epistles of the once blessed man Cyril, priest of the Alexandrian church, both to Nestorius and to the others through the east, suitable and consentient to himself, for the confutation indeed of Nestorian madness, and for the interpretation however of those who with religious zeal wish to grasp the understanding of the salvific symbol; and also the epistle of the holy and most blessed Leo, archbishop of the first see, written to Flavian, archbishop of holy memory, for destroying the malignity of Eutyches, which epistle agrees with the confession of great Peter and as it were a certain common page existing against those who do not rightly glorify, to the confirmation of the catholic religion he evidently added. For those who strive to split the mystery of the Lord's dispensation into two sons he execrates, and those who dare to assert the passible divinity of the only-begotten Son of God he repels from the council of priests, and those who argue for a mixture or confusion of the two natures of Christ he opposes, and those who in madness assert that the form of a servant, which He assumed for us, is of a heavenly or of any other substance he casts far away, and those who contrive two natures indeed before the union of the Lord but one after the union he anathematizes.
¶66
Consentientes igitur sanctis patribus unum eundemque filium confiteri dominum nostrum Iesum Christum consona voce pariter edocemur, perfectum eundem in humanitate, deum verum et hominem verum eundem ex anima rationali et corpore, secundum divinitatem unius cum patre naturae, secundum humanitatem eundem unius naturae nobiscum, per omnia similem nobis absque peccato, ante saecula quidem ex patre natum secundum divinitatem, in novissimis vero diebus eundem propter nos et propter salutem nostram ex Maria virgine dei genetrice secundum humanitatem, unum eundemque Christum filium dominum unigenitum in duabus naturis inconfuse, immutabiliter, indivise, inseparabiliter cognoscendo, in nullo naturarum differentias propter unitatem perimendas, magis autem salva utriusque naturae proprietate et in una coeunte persona unoque statu concurrente, non in duabus personis partiendum vel dividendum, sed unum eundemque filium unigenitum deum verum dominum Iesum Christum, sicut ab exordio prophetae de eo, et ipse nos erudivit, et patrum nobis tradidit symbolum.
Consenting therefore to the holy fathers, we are taught with consonant voice equally to confess one and the same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, the same perfect in humanity, true God and true man, the same from a rational soul and body, according to divinity of one nature with the Father, according to humanity of the same nature with us, similar to us in all things without sin, before ages indeed of the Father born according to divinity, in the last days however the same for us and for our salvation from Mary the Virgin, the God-bearer, according to humanity, one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, only-begotten, in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation, to be acknowledged, in nothing the differences of the natures on account of the union to be destroyed, but rather the property of each nature being saved and concurring into one person and one state, not to be divided or split into two persons, but one and the same Son, only-begotten, true God, Lord Jesus Christ, just as from the beginning the prophets concerning Him, and He Himself taught us, and the symbol of the fathers delivered to us.
¶67
His itaque cum omni subtilitate et diligentia a nobis ordinatis, statuit sancta et universalis synodus aliam fidem nulli licere proferre et scribere aut edere aut separare aut docere aliter. Qui autem audent exponere aliam fidem aut proferre aut docere aut tradere alium symbolum volentibus converti ad scientiam veritatis ex gentibus vel Iudaeis vel hereticis quibuscumque, si quidem episcopi aut clerici fuerint, alienos esse episcopos ab episcopatu et clericos a clero praecipimus, si vero monachi aut laici fuerint, anathemate feriantur.
These things therefore with all subtlety and diligence having been ordained by us, the holy and universal synod established that it is not lawful for anyone to put forth and to write or to compose or to separate or to teach otherwise. Whoever moreover dare to set forth another faith or to put forth or to teach or to deliver another symbol to those wishing to be converted to the knowledge of the truth, whether from gentiles or from Jews or from any heretics, if indeed they are bishops or clerics, we command that they are to be alien bishops from the episcopate and clerics from the clergy; if however they are monks or laypersons, let them be struck with anathema.

THE KING'S ADDRESS ON DISCIPLINE AND THE CANONS
Post confessionem igitur et subscriptionem omnium episcoporum et totius gentis Gothicae seniorum gloriosissimus domnus noster Recaredus rex pro reparandis simul et confirmandis disciplinae ecclesiasticae moribus dei sacerdotes taliter affectus est dicens:
After the confession therefore and subscription of all the bishops and of all the elders of the Gothic nation, the most glorious lord our King Recared, for restoring and at the same time for confirming the morals of ecclesiastical discipline, addressed the priests of God in such a manner, saying:
¶79
Regia cura usque in eum modum protendi debet et dirigi, quoadusque plenam constiterit aetatis et scientiae capere rationem. Nam sicut in rebus humanis gloriosius eminet potestas regia, ita et prospiciendae commoditati comprovincialium maior debet esse et providentia. Ac nunc, beatissimi sacerdotes, non in eis tantummodo rebus diffundimus sollertiam nostram, quibus populi sub nostro regimine, quae adhuc per vestram sanctitatem regulis ecclesiasticis adicienda sunt, hoc pro fidei sanctae reverentia et firmitate proponite, quod de proferendo symbolo nostra deo docente decrevit serenitas.
Royal care ought to be extended and directed up to that point at which it shall have been established that it captures the reason of age and knowledge. For just as in human things the royal power shines forth more gloriously, so for looking after the advantage of fellow-provincials greater providence ought to be present. And now, most blessed priests, we extend our skill not only in those things concerning which the peoples under our governance, which are still to be added by your sanctity to the ecclesiastical rules, propose this concerning the symbol that our serenity has decreed to be set forth for teaching us concerning God.
¶80
De cetero autem prohibendis insolentium moribus mea vobis consentiente clementia sententiis terminate districtioribus et firma disciplina, quae facienda non sunt, prohibete, et positi pacatissime gubernentur et vivant, sed etiam in adiutorio Christi extendimus nos ad ea, quae sunt caelestia cogitare et quae populos fideles efficiant, satagimus non nescire.
Moreover, concerning the remaining matters, for prohibiting the insolent morals, with my clemency consenting, let the sentences be terminated by stricter measures and firm discipline; prohibit those things which ought not to be done, so that the people being placed may be governed most peacefully and may live; but also in the aid of Christ we extend ourselves to think upon those things which are heavenly, and we are not ignorant concerning those things which make the faithful peoples, we attend.
¶81
Ceterum si totis nitendum est viribus humanis moribus modum ponere et insolentium rabiem regia potestate refrenare, si quieti et paci propagandae opem debemus impendere, multo magis est adhibenda sollicitudo desiderare et cogitare divina. Inhiare sublimia et ab errore retractis populis veritatem eis serenae lucis ostendere, sic enim agit, qui multiplici bono se a deo remunerari confidit, sic enim audit, qui super id, quod ei committitur, auget, dum illi dicitur: Quicquid supererogaveris, ego, cum rediero, reddam tibi.
Moreover, if it must be striven with all our strength to set a limit to human morals and to restrain the rage of the insolent by royal power, if we ought to expend aid to propagate quiet and peace, much more is solicitude to be applied to desire and to think upon divine things. To gape at sublime things, and, the peoples having been drawn back from error, to show to them the truth of serene light. For thus he acts who trusts that he will be rewarded by God with manifold good; thus he hears who increases upon that which is committed to him, while it is said to him: "Whatever you shall have expended beyond, I, when I return, will render to you."
¶82
Ergo quia iam fidei nostrae et confessionis formam plena serie vestra beatitudo recensuit simul et sacerdotum nostrorumque procerum fides atque confessio sanctitati vestrae perpatuit, hoc adhuc necessarium pro firmitate catholicae fidei nostra deo supplex instituere decrevit auctoritas, ut propter roborandam gentis nostrae novellam conversionem omnes Spaniarum et Galliae ecclesiae hanc regulam servent, omni sacrificii tempore ante communicationem corporis Christi vel sanguinis iuxta orientalium partium morem unanimiter clara voce sacratissimum fidei recenseant symbolum, ut primum populi, quam credulitatem teneant, fateantur et sic corda fide purificata ad Christi corpus et sanguinem percipiendum exhibeant.
Therefore, because now the form of our faith and confession your blessedness has run through in full series, and simultaneously the faith and confession of our priests and nobles has become plain to your sanctity, this yet necessary for the firmness of the catholic faith our suppliant authority has decreed to establish for God, that for strengthening the new conversion of our nation all the churches of Spain and Gaul should preserve this rule: that at every time of sacrifice, before the communion of the body and blood of Christ, according to the custom of the eastern regions, with unanimous and clear voice they recite the most sacred symbol of the faith, so that first the peoples may confess what credulity they hold, and thus with hearts purified by faith they may present themselves to receive the body and blood of Christ.
¶83
Dum enim constitutio haec fuerit perenniter conservata in dei ecclesia et fidelium ex solido corroboratur credulitas et perfidia infidelium confutata ad id, quod repetitum saepius recognoscit, facillime inclinatur nec se quisquam iam de ignorantia fidei excusabit a culpa, quando universorum ore cognoscit, quid catholica teneat et credat ecclesia. Omnibus ergo capitulis, quae fieri debent, immobili constitutione firmate.
For when this constitution shall have been perpetually preserved in the church of God, and the credulity of the faithful is strengthened from the solid, and the perfidy of the unbelievers is confuted, it easily inclines to that which, having been repeated often, it recognizes, nor will anyone any longer excuse himself from guilt concerning ignorance of the faith, when by the mouth of all he recognizes what the catholic church holds and believes. Therefore, with all the chapters which ought to be done, establish with immovable constitution.

CANON XXII

That the bodies of religious persons be led to the grave with singing only, and that dances be prohibited on the feast days of saints.
¶106
CANON I (Detailed)
Post damnationem heresis Arrianae et fidei sanctae catholicae expositionem hoc sanctum praecepit concilium, ut, quia a nonnullis vel heresis vel gentilitatis necessitate per Spaniarum ecclesias canonicus praetermissus est ordo, dum et licentia abundaret transgrediendi et disciplinae optio negaretur, dum omnis excessus heresis foveretur patrocinio et abundantia mali temporis procul esset et districtio disciplinae, at nunc pace ecclesiae Christi misericordia reparata omne, quod priscorum canonum auctoritas prohibet, sit resurgente disciplina inhibitum, et agatur omne, quod praecepit. Permaneant in suo vigore conciliorum omnium constituta simul et synodicae sanctorum praesulum Romanorum epistolae. Nullus deinceps ad promerendos honores ecclesiasticos contra canonum aspiret indignus. Nihil ex hoc fiat, quod sancti patres spiritu dei pleni sanxerunt debere non fieri, et qui praesumpserit, severitate priorum canonum distringatur.
After the condemnation of the Arian heresy and the exposition of the holy catholic faith, this holy council commanded that, because by some either of heresy or of heathenism by necessity through the Spanish churches the canonical order has been omitted, while there abounded license of transgressing and the option of discipline was denied, while every excess was fostered by the patronage of heresy and the abundance of evil time, and the strictness of discipline was far away, yet now with the peace of the Church of Christ repaired by mercy, let everything which the authority of the ancient canons prohibits be forbidden, discipline rising again, and let everything be done which it commands. Let the constitutions of all councils remain in their force, and likewise the synodical epistles of the holy Roman prelates. Let no one henceforth unworthy aspire to ecclesiastical honors against the canons. Let nothing be done concerning which the holy fathers, full of the Spirit of God, have sanctioned that it ought not to be done, and whoever shall have presumed, let him be constrained by the severity of the prior canons.
¶107
CANON II (Detailed)
Pro reverentia sanctissimae fidei et propter corroborandas hominum invalidas mentes consulto piissimi et gloriosissimi Recaredi regis constituit synodus, ut per omnes ecclesias Spaniae et Galliae vel Galliciae secundum formam orientalium ecclesiarum concilii Constantinopolitani, hoc est centum quinquaginta episcoporum symbolum fidei recitetur et, priusquam dominica dicatur oratio, voce clara praedicetur populo, quo et fides vera manifesta sit et testimonium habeat et ad Christi corpus et sanguinem praelibandum pectora populorum fide purificata accedant.
For the reverence of the most holy faith and for strengthening the weak minds of men, by the wise decision of the most pious and most glorious King Recared, the synod established that through all the churches of Spain and Gaul or Gallaecia, according to the form of the eastern churches of the Constantinopolitan council, that is, of the 150 bishops, the symbol of the faith be recited, and before the Lord's prayer is said, it be preached to the people in a clear voice, so that both the true faith may be manifest and have testimony, and with hearts purified by faith they may approach to partake of the body and blood of Christ.
¶108
CANON III (Detailed)
Haec sancta synodus nulli episcoporum licentiam tribuit rem alienare ecclesiae, quoniam et antiquioribus canonibus hoc prohibetur. Si quid vero, quod utilitatem non gravet ecclesiae, pro suffragio monachorum vel ecclesiis ad suam parrochiam pertinentium dederint, firmum maneat. Peregrinorum vel clericorum et egenorum necessitati salvo iure ecclesiae praestare permittuntur pro tempore, quae potuerint.
This holy synod grants license to no bishop to alienate the property of the church, since this is also prohibited by the more ancient canons. If however they shall have given anything for the support of monks or of churches pertaining to their own parish, which may not burden the church, let it remain firm. For the necessity of strangers or of clerics and of the poor, saving the right of the church, they are permitted for the time to provide what they are able.
¶109
CANON IV (Detailed)
Si episcopus unam de parrochitanis ecclesiis suis monasterium dicare voluerit, ut in ea monachorum regulariter congregatio vivat, hoc de consensu concilii sui habeat licentiam faciendi. Qui etiam, si de rebus ecclesiae pro eorum substantia aliquid, quo detrimentum ecclesiae non exhibeat, eidem loco donaverit, sit stabile. Rei enim bonae statuendum sanctum concilium dat consensum.
If a bishop wishes to dedicate as a monastery one of the parish churches of his own, so that in it a congregation of monks may live regularly, let him have license to do this with the consent of his council. And if also concerning the property of the church he shall have donated something to the same place for their sustenance, which may not cause detriment to the church, let it be stable. For to a good work the holy council gives its consent as it should be established.
¶110
CANON V (Detailed)
Compertum est a sancto concilio episcopos, presbyteros et diaconos venientes ex heresi carnali adhuc desiderio uxoribus copulari. Ne ergo de cetero fiat, hoc praecipitur, quod et prioribus canonibus continetur, ut non liceat eis vivere libidinosa societate, sed manente inter eos fide coniugali communem utilitatem habeant et non sub uno conclavi maneant, vel certe si suffragatur virtus in aliam domum suam uxorem faciat habitare, ut castitas apud deum et apud homines habeat testimonium bonum. Si qui vero post hanc conventionem obscene cum uxore elegerit vivere, ut neglecto ita habeatur. Qui vero semper sub canone ecclesiastico iacuerunt, si contra veterum imperata in suis cellulis mulierum, quae infamiae suspicionem possunt ignorare, consortium habuerint, illi canonice quidem distringantur, mulieres vero ab episcopis venundatae, pretium ipsum pauperibus erogetur.
It has been discovered by the holy council that bishops, presbyters, and deacons coming from the heresy are still joined with their wives by carnal desire. Lest this be done henceforth, this is commanded which is also contained in the prior canons: that it is not lawful for them to live in libidinous society, but with the conjugal faith remaining between them, let them have common utility and not dwell under one roof, or certainly, if virtue assists, let him cause his own wife to dwell in another house, so that chastity may have good testimony before God and before men. If any however after this agreement shall have chosen to live obscenely with his wife, let him be regarded as an outcast. Those who have always lain under the canonical rule, if against the command of the ancients in their own cells they shall have had the company of women, which can ignore the suspicion of infamy, let them indeed be canonically constrained; but let the women, having been sold by the bishops, the price itself be distributed to the poor.
¶111
CANON VI (Detailed)
De libertis autem hoc praecepit sancta synodus, ut, si qui presbyteri vel diaconi ab episcopis facti sunt, secundum modum, quo canones antiqui dant licentiam, sint liberi, et tamen a patrocinio ecclesiae tam ipsi quam ab eis progeniti non recedant. Ab aliis quoque libertati traditi et ecclesiis commendati patrocinio episcopi colligantur, et ne cuiquam donentur a principe, hoc episcopus postulet.
Concerning freedmen moreover, this the holy council commanded: that if any have been made presbyters or deacons by bishops, according to the measure by which the ancient canons give license, they are to be free, and yet both they themselves and those procreated from them shall not depart from the patronage of the church. Freedmen also handed over to liberty by others and commended to churches are to be gathered under the patronage of the bishop; and lest they be donated to anyone by the prince, let the bishop request this.
¶112
CANON VII (Detailed)
Pro reverentia dei et sacerdotum id universa constituit synodus, ut, quia solent crebro mensis otiosae fabulae interponi, in omni sacerdotali convivio lectio scripturarum divinarum misceatur. Per hoc enim et animae aedificantur ad bonum et fabulae non necessariae prohibentur.
For the reverence of God and of the priests, the universal synod established this, that because idle fables are accustomed often to be interposed at meals, in every priestly banquet let the reading of the divine scriptures be mixed. For by this both souls are edified unto good and unnecessary fables are prohibited.
¶113
CANON VIII (Detailed)
Iubente autem atque consentiente domino Recaredo rege id praecepit sacerdotale concilium, ut clericos ex familia fisci nullus audeat a principe donatos expetere, sed traditos capitis sui tributum ecclesiae dei, cui sunt alligati, usque dum vivunt, regulariter administrent.
With the Lord King Recared ordering and consenting, the priestly council commanded this: that no one dare to seek from the prince clerics from the family of the fisc given as a gift, but let those delivered administer regularly the tribute of their head to the church of God to which they are attached, as long as they live.
¶114
CANON IX (Detailed)
Decreto huius concilii hoc statuitur, ut ecclesiae, quae fuerunt in heresi Arriana, nunc autem sunt catholicae, ad eos episcopos cum suis rebus pertineant, ad quos parrochiae ipsae, in quibus ecclesiae ipsae fundatae sunt, pertinere videntur.
By the decree of this council it is established that churches which were in the Arian heresy but now are catholic pertain with their goods to those bishops to whose parish the same churches themselves, in which they are founded, are seen to pertain.
¶115
CANON X (Detailed)
Pro consulto castitatis, quod maxime hortamento concilii proficere debeat, annuente glorioso domino nostro Recaredo rege, hoc sanctum affirmat concilium, ut viduae, quibus placuerit, teneant castitatem et nulla vidua ad nuptias intrandas venire cogatur. Quod si, priusquam profiteantur continentiam, nubere elegerint, illis nubant, quos propria voluntate voluerint habere maritos. Similis condicio et de virginibus habeatur, nec extra voluntatem parentum vel suam cogantur maritos accipere. Si quis vero propositum castitatis viduae vel virginis impedierit, a sancta communione et a liminibus ecclesiae habeatur extraneus.
Concerning the counsel of chastity, which especially ought to profit by the exhortation of the council, with the glorious lord our King Recared assenting, this holy synod affirms: that widows who wish, keep chastity, and let no widow be compelled to enter into marriage. But if, before they profess continence, they have chosen to marry, let them marry those whom they have wished to have as husbands by their own will. Let the same condition be held concerning virgins, nor let them be compelled to accept husbands outside the will of their parents or their own. If anyone however shall have impeded a widow or a virgin in her purpose of chastity, let him be held an alien from holy communion and from the thresholds of the church.
¶116
CANON XI (Detailed)
Quoniam comperimus per quasdam Spaniarum ecclesias non secundum canonem, sed fedissime pro suis peccatis homines agere poenitentiam, et, quoties peccare libuerit, toties a presbyteris se reconciliari expostulent. Et ideo pro coercenda tam exsecrabili praesumptione, id a sancto concilio iubetur, ut secundum formam canonum antiquorum detur poenitentia, hoc est, ut prius eum, quem sui poenitet facti, a communione suspensum faciat et inter reliquos poenitentes ad manus impositionem crebro recurrere. Expleto autem satisfactionis tempore, sicuti sacerdotalis contemplatio probaverit, eum communioni restituat. Hi vero, qui ad propria vitia vel infra poenitentiae tempus vel post reconciliationem relabuntur, secundum priorum canonum severitate damnentur.
Since we have discovered that through certain Spanish churches men do penance not according to the canon but most shamefully for their sins, and as often as it has pleased them to sin, so often they demand to be reconciled by the presbyters. And therefore for coercing so execrable a presumption, it is commanded by the holy council that penance be given according to the form of the ancient canons, that is, that first let him whom his deed repents be suspended from communion and among the remaining penitents let him have recourse frequently to the imposition of hands. But when the time of satisfaction has been completed, just as priestly contemplation shall have approved, let him restore him to communion. Those who relapse into their own vices, either within the time of penance or after reconciliation, according to the severity of the prior canons are to be condemned.
¶117
CANON XII (Detailed)
Quicumque ab episcopo vel presbytero sanus vel infirmus poenitentiam postulat, id ante omnia episcopus observet et presbyter, ut, si vir est sive sanus sive infirmus, prius eum tondat aut in cinere et cilicio habitum mutare faciat et sic poenitentiam ei tradat. Si vero mulier fuerit, non accipiat poenitentiam, nisi prius aut velata fuerit aut mutaverit habitum. Saepius enim laicis vel feminis tribuendo desidiose poenitentiam ad lamentanda rursum facinora post acceptam poenitentiam relabuntur.
Whoever seeks penance from a bishop or presbyter, whether healthy or infirm, let the bishop or presbyter observe this before all things: if it is a man, whether healthy or infirm, let him first tonsure him or cause him to change his habit into ashes and sackcloth, and thus deliver penance to him. If it is a woman, let her not receive penance unless first she shall have been veiled or changed her habit. For too often by giving penance lazily to laymen or women, they relapse after receiving penance again into lamentable crimes.
¶118
CANON XIII (Detailed)
Inolita praesumptio usque adeo illicitis ausibus aditum patefecit, ut clerici conclericos suos relicto pontifice suo ad iudicia publica pertrahant. Proinde statuimus, ut hoc de cetero non praesumatur. Si quis hoc praesumpserit facere, conventus et causam perdat et a communione efficiatur extraneus.
An inveterate presumption has opened an approach to illicit acts to such an extent that clerics drag their fellow clerics, with their pontiff abandoned, to public judgments. Therefore we establish that this not be presumed henceforth. If anyone shall have presumed to do this, let him both lose the case and be made an alien from communion.
¶119
CANON XIV (Detailed)
Noster hoc canonibus inserendum praecepit, ut Iudaeis non liceat christianas habere uxores vel concubinas neque mancipium christianum in usus proprios comparare, sed et filii, qui ex tali coniugio nati sunt, assumendos esse ad baptismum. Nulla officia publica eis iniungantur, per quae eis occasio tribuatur poenam christianis inferre. Si qui vero christiani ab eis iudaismo ritu sunt maculati vel etiam circumcisi, non reddito pretio ad libertatem et religionem redeant christianam.
Our synod commanded this to be inserted into the canons: that it is not lawful for Jews to have Christian wives or concubines, nor to purchase a Christian slave for their own uses; but also sons who are born from such a marriage are to be brought to baptism. Let no public offices be imposed upon them, through which occasion may be given them to inflict punishment upon Christians. If any Christians have been defiled by them with the Jewish rite or also circumcised, without payment of a price let them return to freedom and the Christian religion.
¶120
CANON XV (Detailed)
Si qui ex servis fiscalibus fortasse ecclesias construxerint easque de sua paupertate dotaverint, hoc procuret episcopus prece sua auctoritate confirmari.
If any from the fiscal slaves perhaps have built churches and have endowed them from their own poverty, let the bishop take care to have it confirmed by his request and authority.
¶121
CANON XVI (Detailed)
Quoniam pene per omnem Spaniam sive Galliam idolatriae sacrilegium inolevit, hoc cum consensu gloriosissimi principis sancta synodus ordinavit, ut omnis sacerdos in loco suo una cum iudice territorii sacrilegium memoratum studiose perquirat et exterminari inventum non differat. Omnes vero, qui ad talem errorem concurrunt, sine discrimine, qua potuerint animadversione, coerceant. Quod si neglexerint, sciant se utrique excommunicationis periculum esse subituros. Si qui vero domini exstirpare hoc malum de possessione sua neglexerint vel familiae prohibere noluerint, ab episcopo et ipsi a communione pellantur.
Since the sacrilege of idolatry has grown strong throughout nearly all Spain and Gaul, this holy synod, with the consent of the most glorious prince, has ordained that every priest in his own place, together with the judge of the territory, studiously seek out the aforesaid sacrilege and not delay to have it destroyed when found. But let all who run together to such error be coerced without discrimination with whatever animadversion they shall have been able. But if they shall have neglected, let them know that each of them will be about to undergo the peril of excommunication. If any lords shall have neglected to root out this evil from their possession or been unwilling to prohibit their household, let them themselves be driven from communion by the bishop.
¶122
CANON XVII (Detailed)
Dum multae querelae ad aures sancti concilii deferrentur, inter cetera tantae crudelitatis est opus nuntiatum, quantum consedentium aures sacerdotum non possunt sustinere, ut in quibusdam Spaniae partibus filios suos parentes interimant fornicatione avidi, pietatis alieni. Quibus si taedium est filios numerosius alere, prius se ipsos debent castigare a fornicatione. Nam dum causa propagandae prolis sortiantur coniugia, parricidio et fornicatione tenentur obnoxii, qui filios necando proprios docent se non pro filiis, sed pro libidine uxores duxisse. Proinde tantum nefas ad cognitionem gloriosissimi domini nostri Recaredi regis perlatum est, cuius gloria dignata est iudicibus earundem partium imperare, ut amovendum tantum facinus diligenter cum sacerdote procurent et habita severitate prohibeant. Ergo et sacerdotes locorum eorundem, in quibus sceleris huius immanitas peragitur, sancta synodus praecipiendo convenit, ut idem scelus cum iudice curiosius quaerant et sine capitali vindicta acriori disciplina prohibeant.
While many complaints were being brought to the ears of the holy council, among other things a work of such great cruelty was reported as the ears of the seated priests are not able to sustain: that in certain parts of Spain parents kill their own sons, greedy for fornication, alien to piety. If they are weary of raising numerous children, they ought first to chastise themselves from fornication. For while they obtain marriage for the sake of propagating offspring, they are held liable to parricide and fornication, who by killing their own sons teach that they have taken wives not for children but for lust. Therefore so great a crime has been brought to the knowledge of our most glorious lord King Recared, whose glory deigned to command the judges of those same parts that they take care diligently to remove so great a crime with the priest and to prohibit it with severity being observed. Therefore also the priests of those same places, in which the enormity of this crime is perpetrated, the holy synod, commanding, convenes that they seek out the same crime more carefully with the judge and prohibit it without capital vengeance but with sharper discipline.
¶123
CANON XVIII (Detailed)
Praecipit haec sancta et universalis synodus, ut stante priorum auctoritate canonum, quae bis in anno praecepit congregari concilia, consulta itineris longitudine et paupertate ecclesiarum Spaniae, semel in anno in loco, quem metropolitanus elegerit, episcopi congregentur. Iudices vero locorum vel actores fiscalium patrimoniorum ex decreto domini nostri simul cum sacerdotum concilio autumnali tempore die Kal. Novembr. in unum conveniant, ut discant, quam pie et iuste cum populis agere debeant, ne in angariis aut in operationibus superfluis sive privatum onerent sive fiscalem gravent. Sint etenim perspectores episcopi secundum regiam monitionem, qualiter iudices cum populis agant, ut ipsos praemonitos corrigant aut insolentias eorum auribus principum innotescant. Quod si correptos emendare nequiverint, et ab ecclesia et a communione suspendant. A sacerdote vero et senioribus deliberetur, quid per provincias in suo detrimento praestare debeant iudices, si ad concilium non venerint. Concilium autem non solvatur, nisi locum prius elegerint, quo succedenti tempore iterum ad concilium veniatur, ut iam non necesse habeat metropolitanus episcopus pro congregando concilio litteras destinare, si in priori concilio tempus omnibus denuntiatur et locus.
This holy and universal synod commands that, with the authority of the prior canons standing, which commanded councils to be held twice a year, on account of the consulted length of journey and the poverty of the Spanish churches, once a year in a place which the metropolitan shall have chosen, the bishops assemble. Let the judges of the places or the managers of the fiscal patrimonies, by the decree of our lord, together with the council of priests, in the autumn time, on the day of the Kalends of November, convene into one, so that they may learn how piously and justly they ought to act with the peoples, lest they burden the private person or burden the fisc in transport services or in superfluous works. Let there be indeed observers, according to the royal admonition, of how the judges act with the peoples, so that they may correct them having been forewarned or make known their insolence to the ears of the princes. But if having been corrected they shall not have been able to amend them, let them suspend them both from church and from communion. But let it be deliberated by the priest and the elders what the judges ought to provide for the provinces to their own detriment, if they have not come to the council. Moreover, let the council not be dissolved unless first they shall have chosen a place where at the succeeding time they may come again to the council, so that the metropolitan bishop may no longer have need to send letters for gathering the council, if in the prior council the time is announced to all and the place.
¶124
CANON XIX (Detailed)
Si quidem contra omnem auctoritatem ecclesias, quas aedificaverint, postulant consecrari, ut dotem, quam eidem ecclesiae contulerint, censeant ad episcopi ordinationem non pertinere, quod factum est taliter in praeterito, corrigatur et in futuro, ne fiat, prohibetur. Sed omnia secundum constitutionem antiquam ad episcopi ordinationem et potestatem pertineant.
If any, against all authority, seek to have consecrated churches which they have built, so that they may judge that the dowry which they have conferred upon the same church does not pertain to the disposition of the bishop, let what has been done thus in the past be corrected, and in the future let it be prohibited, lest it be done. But let all things according to the ancient constitution pertain to the disposition and power of the bishop.
¶125
CANON XX (Detailed)
Quia cognovimus episcopos per parrochias suas non sacerdotaliter, sed crudeliter desaevire, et dum scriptum sit: Forma estote gregi non ut dominantes in clero, exactionesque diocesis suae vel damna infligant. Ideoque censemus excepto, quod veterum constitutiones a parrochiis habere iubent episcopos, ut alia illis, quae hucusque praesumpta sunt, denegentur, hoc est neque in angariis presbyteris aut diaconibus neque in aliquibus fatigent indictionibus, ne videamur in ecclesia dei exactores potius quam dei pontifices nominari. Hi vero clerici tam locales, quam diocesani, qui se ab episcopo gravari cognoverint, querelas suas ad metropolitanum deferre non differant, ut metropolitanus non moretur eiusmodi praesumptiones coercere.
Because we have known that bishops rave through their parishes not in a priestly but in a cruel manner, and while it is written: "Be a form to the flock, not as dominating over the clergy," they inflict exactions and losses upon their own diocese. Therefore we decree, except that which the constitutions of the ancients command bishops to have from parishes, that other things which thus far have been presumed are denied to them, that is, neither in transport services nor in any indictions let them fatigue presbyters or deacons, lest we seem to be named in the church of God exactors rather than bishops of God. But those clerics, both local and diocesan, who shall have known that they are burdened by the bishop, let them not delay to carry their complaints to the metropolitan, so that the metropolitan not delay to coerce such presumptions.
¶126
CANON XXI (Detailed)
Ecclesiarum servos et episcoporum vel omnium clericorum a iudicibus vel actoribus publicis in diversis angariis fatigari dolemus. Propter quod omne concilium a pietate gloriosissimi domini nostri poposcit, ut tales deinceps ausus inhibeat, sed servi suprascriptorum officiorum in eorum usibus vel ecclesiae laborent. Si qui vero iudicum aut actorum clericum aut servum clerici vel ecclesiae in publicis ac privatis negotiis occupare voluerit, a communione ecclesiastica, cui impedimentum facit, efficiatur extraneus.
We grieve that the servants of churches and of bishops and of all clerics are fatigued by judges or public managers in various transport services. For which reason the whole council asked from the piety of our most glorious lord that he henceforth forbid such attempts; but let the servants of the aforesaid offices labor in their own uses or of the church. If any of the judges or managers shall have wished to occupy a cleric or servant of a cleric or of the church in public or private business, let him be made alien from ecclesiastical communion, to which he is an impediment.
¶127
CANON XXII (Detailed)
Qui divina vocatione ab hac vita recedunt, cum psalmis tantummodo et psallentium vocibus debere ad sepulchra deferri. Nam funeris precamen, quod vulgo defunctis cantari solet vel pectoribus se aut proximos aut familias cedere omnino prohibemus. Sufficiat autem, quod in spe resurrectionis christianorum corporibus famulatus divinorum impenditur canticorum. Prohibet enim nos apostolus sanctus lugere defunctos dicens: De dormientibus autem nolo vos contristari, sicut et ceteri, qui spem non habent. Et dominus non flevit Lazarum mortuum, sed ad huius vitae aerumnas ploravit resuscitandum. Si enim potest hoc episcopus, omnes christianos prohibere agere non moretur. Religiosis tamen omnino aliter fieri non debere censemus. Sic enim christianorum per omnem mundum humari oportet corpora defunctorum.
Those who depart from this life by divine vocation ought to be carried to the graves with psalms only and with the voices of psalm-singers. For the funeral lamentation which is commonly chanted for the dead or the beating of breasts or the rending of themselves or of families we altogether forbid. Let it suffice that to the bodies of Christians the service of divine canticles is bestowed in the hope of the resurrection. For the holy apostle forbids us to mourn the dead, saying: "But concerning those who sleep, I am unwilling for you to grieve, just as the rest who have no hope." And the Lord did not weep over Lazarus dead, but over the hardships of this life He wept for him about to be raised. If therefore the bishop is able, let him not delay to prohibit all Christians from doing this. We decree however that for religious persons it ought altogether not to be done otherwise. For thus it is fitting that the bodies of deceased Christians be buried throughout the whole world.
¶128
CANON XXIII (Detailed)
Irreligiosa consuetudo est, quam vulgus per sanctorum solemnitates agere consuevit. Populi, qui debent officia divina attendere, saltationibus turpibus invigilant cantica non solum mala canentes, sed et religiosorum officiis perstrepentes. Hoc etenim, ut ab omni Spania depellatur, sacerdotum et iudicum a concilio sancto curae committitur.
It is an irreligious custom which the common people are accustomed to practice on the solemnities of the saints. The peoples, who ought to attend to divine offices, keep vigils for shameful dances, singing not only evil songs but also making a din against the offices of the religious. For this, that it be driven out from all Spain, is committed to the care of the priests and judges by the holy council.

THE ROYAL EDICT IN CONFIRMATION OF THE COUNCIL
BEGINNING OF THE EDICT OF THE KING IN CONFIRMATION OF THE COUNCIL
*INCIPIT EDICTUM REGIS IN CONFIRMATIONE CONCILII*
Gloriosissimus dominus Recaredus rex universorum sub regimine nostrae potestatis consistentium: Amatores vestri divina faciens veritas nostris principaliter sensibus inspiravit, ut causa instaurandae fidei ac disciplinae ecclesiasticae episcopos omnis Hispaniae nostro praesentari culmini iuberemus.
The most glorious lord King Recared, to all persons established under the governance of our power: The divine truth, lover of you, has inspired in our princely senses above all that for the sake of restoring the faith and ecclesiastical discipline we should order the bishops of all Spain to be presented to our summit.
¶130
Praecedente autem diligenti et cauta deliberatione, sive quae ad fidem conveniant seu quae ad morum correctionem respiciunt, sensus maturitate et intelligentiae gravitate constant esse digesta. Nostra proinde auctoritas hac omnibus hominibus ad regnum nostrum pertinentibus iubet, ut, si qua definita sunt in hoc sancto concilio acto in urbe Toletana anno regni nostri feliciter quarto, nulli contemnere liceat, nullus praeterire praesumat.
Diligent and cautious deliberation having preceded, both those things which pertain to the faith and those which look to the correction of morals, with maturity of sense and gravity of understanding, are established to have been digested. Our authority therefore commands by this to all men pertaining to our kingdom that, if any things have been defined in this holy council held in the city of Toledo in the fourth year of our reign happily, no one be allowed to contemn, no one presume to pass over.
¶131
Capitula enim, quae sensibus nostris placita et disciplinae congrua a praesenti conscripta sunt synodo, in omni auctoritate sive clericorum sive quorumcumque omnium observentur et maneant, id est:
The chapters indeed which have been written down by the present synod, pleasing to our senses and suitable to discipline, in all authority both of clerics and of all whatsoever they may be, are to be observed and remain, that is:
¶132
SUMMARY OF THE CANONS IN THE ROYAL EDICT
de observatione priorum canonum;
Concerning the observance of the prior canons.
de symbolo proferendo a populis in ecclesia;
Concerning the symbol to be set forth to the peoples in church.
de episcopis, ut eis non liceat rem alienare ecclesiae;
Concerning bishops, that it is not lawful for them to alienate the property of the church.
ut episcopo liceat unam de parrochitanis ecclesiis monasterium facere;
That it be lawful for a bishop to make a monastery from one of the parish churches.
ut episcopis et presbyteris et diaconibus ex heresi conversis iam non liceat misceri uxoribus, vel quod hi, qui semper catholici fuerint, in cellulis suis cum mulieribus extraneis non morentur;
That it is no longer lawful for bishops and presbyters and deacons converted from the heresy to be mixed with their wives, and that those who have always been catholic not dwell with extraneous women in their own cells.
¶133
quod liberti ab episcopis vel aliis facti et ecclesiis commendati permanere debeant liberi;
That freedmen made by bishops or by others and commended to churches ought to remain free.
quod lectio in omnibus sacerdotalibus mensis legi debeat;
That reading ought to be read at all priestly tables.
quod clericos ex familiis fisci nostri nullus umquam a rege postulet, et qui acceperit, irrita talis donatio maneat;
That no one ever request from the king clerics from the families of our fisc, and whoever shall have received, let such donation remain void.
¶134
de ecclesiis ab heresi translatis ad episcopos, in quorum sunt parrochiis, pertineant;
Concerning churches transferred from the heresy, that they pertain to the bishops in whose parishes they are.
de viduis, quod, quae voluerint, continentiam teneant, et quae nubere elegerint, quibus voluerint, nubant, aeque de virginibus;
Concerning widows: that those who wish keep continence, and those who have chosen to marry, let them marry whom they wish; and likewise concerning virgins.
¶135
quod poenitentes secundum modum canonum antiquorum debeant agere poenitentiam;
That penitents ought to do penance according to the measure of the ancient canons.
quod qui voluerint poenitentiam agere prius tondantur aut habitum mutent;
That those who wish to do penance first be tonsured or change their habit.
quod non liceat Iudaeis ducere de christianis uxores vel concubinas sive comparare mancipia christiana et iudaizare non liceat vel publica officia peragere;
That it not be lawful for Jews to take wives or concubines from Christians, nor to buy Christian slaves, and that it not be lawful to judaize or to perform public offices.
¶136
quod manere debeat firmum, si servi fisci nostri ecclesias fecerint easque de peculio suo dotaverint;
That it remain firm if slaves of our fisc have built churches and endowed them from their own means.
quod idolatriae cultum a sacerdotibus vel iudicibus exquirenda sit atque exterminanda;
That the cult of idolatry is to be sought out and exterminated by priests and judges.
quod quae filium suum necaverint, a sacerdotibus vel a iudicibus distringantur;
That those who have killed their own son are to be constrained by priests and by judges.
quod semel in anno ad concilium sacerdotes et iudices atque actores patrimonii nostri debeant convenire;
That once a year the priests and judges and managers of our patrimony ought to convene to the council.
quod ecclesiarum omnium causa ad episcopi ordinationem pertinere debeat;
That the cause of all churches ought to pertain to the disposition of the bishop.
quod sacerdotes moderanter agere debeant per parrochias;
That priests ought to act moderately through the parishes.
quod servi ecclesiae sive clericorum non debeant a iudicibus vel nostris actoribus in aliqua angaria fatigari;
That the servants of the church or of clerics ought not to be fatigued by judges or our managers in any transport service.
¶137
quod religiosorum corpora cum hymnis et canticis tantum deferenda sint ad sepulchra;
That the bodies of religious persons are to be carried to the graves with hymns and canticles only.
quod balmathiae et turpia cantica prohibenda sunt a sanctorum sollemniis.
That dances and shameful songs are to be prohibited at the solemnities of the saints.
Has omnes constitutiones ecclesiasticas, quas summatim breviterque perstrinximus, sicut plenius in canone continentur, manere perenni stabilitate sancimus. Si quis ergo clericus aut laicus harum sanctionum oboediens esse noluerit, si episcopus, presbyter, diaconus aut clericus fuerit, ab omni concilio excommunicationi subiaceat, si vero laicus fuerit et honestioris loci persona est, medietatem facultatum suarum amittat fisci viribus profuturam. Si vero minoris loci persona est, amissione rerum suarum mulctatus in exilium deputetur.
All these ecclesiastical constitutions, which we have run through summarily and briefly, just as they are more fully contained in the canon, we sanction to remain with perpetual stability. If therefore any cleric or layperson has been unwilling to be obedient to these sanctions: if it is a bishop, presbyter, deacon, or cleric, let him be subject to excommunication by the whole council; if indeed it is a layperson and a person of more honorable place, let him lose half of his goods, about to profit the resources of the fisc; if indeed it is a person of lesser place, let him be mulcted by the loss of his goods and be consigned to exile.
¶138
SIGNATURE OF THE KING
Flavius Recaredus rex hanc deliberationem, quam cum sancta definimus synodo, confirmans subscripsi.
I, Flavius Recaredus king, confirming this deliberation which we have defined with the holy synod, have subscribed.
¶139
SIGNATURE OF THE METROPOLITAN BISHOP
Masona in Christi nomine ecclesiae catholicae Emeretensis metropolitanus episcopus provinciae Lusitani eis constitutionibus quibus in urbe Toletana interfui, manu mea subscripsi.
Masona, in the name of Christ, metropolitan bishop of the catholic church of Emerita of the province of Lusitania, to those constitutions of which in the city of Toledo I was present, with my hand I have subscribed.
¶140

Similarly also the other bishops subscribed, that is, 68.
