LXXIV. Concerning the institutes and decrees of the ecclesiastical order, which pertain to the discipline of certain persons.
¶29
Anno tertio regnante domino nostro gloriosissimo principe Sisenando die Non. Dec. era DCLXXXI, dum studio amoris Christi ac diligentia religiosissimi Sisenandi regis Hispaniae atque Galliae sacerdotes apud Toletanam urbem in nomine domini convenissemus, ut eius imperiis atque iussis commoniti a nobis agitaretur de quibusdam ecclesiae disciplinis tractatus, primum gratias salvatori nostro deo omnipotenti egimus. Post haec antefato ministro excellentissimo et glorioso regi, cuius tanta erga deum devotio exstat, ut non solum in rebus humanis, sed etiam in causis divinis sollicitus maneat. Hic quippe, dum in basilica beatissimae et sanctae martyris Leocadiae omnium nostrum pariter iam coetus adesset, pro merito fidei suae, cum magnificentissimis et nobilissimis viris ingressus, primum coram sacerdotibus dei humi prostratus cum lacrimis et gemitibus pro se interveniendum domino postulavit, deinde religiosa prosecutione synodum exhortatus est, ut paternorum decretorum memores ad confirmanda in nobis iura ecclesiastica studium praeberemus, et illa corrigere, quae, dum per neglegentiam in usum venerunt contra ecclesiasticos mores, licentiam sibi de usurpatione fecerunt. Talibus igitur eius monitis congaudentes necessarium exstitit iuxta eius nostrumque votum tractare, quae competunt, sive in sacramentis divinis, quae diverso atque illicito modo in Spaniarum ecclesiis celebrantur, seu quae in moribus prave usurpata noscuntur. Et quoniam generale concilium agimus, oportet primum nostrae vocis sermonem de deo esse, ut post professionem fidei sequentia operis nostri vota, quasi super fundamentum firmissimum disponantur. Quemadmodum a sanctis patribus accepimus, patrem et filium et spiritum sanctum unius deitatis atque substantiae confitemur, in personarum diversitate trinitatem credentes, in divinitate unitatem praedicantes nec personas confundimus nec substantiam separamus. Patrem a nullo factum vel genitum dicimus, filium a patre non factum, sed genitum asserimus, spiritum vero sanctum nec creatum nec genitum, sed procedentem ex patre et filio profitemur. Ipsum autem dominum Iesum Christum dei filium et creatorem omnium ex substantia patris ante saecula genitum descendisse ultimo tempore pro redemptione mundi a patre, qui numquam desinit esse cum patre, incarnatus est enim ex spiritu sancto et sancta gloriosa dei genetrice virgine Maria et natus ex ipsa solus. Idem dominus Iesus Christus unus in sancta trinitate anima et carne perfectus sine peccato suscipiens hominem manens, quod erat, assumens, quod non erat, aequalis patri secundum divinitatem, minor patre secundum humanitatem, habens in una persona duarum naturarum proprietatem, naturae enim in illo duae, deus et homo, non autem duo filii et dii duo, sed idem una persona in utraque natura; perferens passionem et mortem pro nostra salute, non in virtute divinitatis, sed infirmitate humanitatis, descendit ad inferos, ut sanctos, qui ibi tenebantur, erueret et devicto mortis imperio resurrexit, assumptus in caelis venturus est in futurum ad iudicium vivorum et mortuorum, cuius morte et sanguine mundati remissionem peccatorum consecuti sumus, resuscitandi ab eo in die novissima in ea, qua nunc vivimus, carne et in ea, qua resurrexit, idem dominus forma, percepturi ab ipso alii pro iustitiae meritis vitam aeternam, alii pro peccatis supplicii aeterni sententiam. Haec est catholicae ecclesiae fides, hanc confessionem conservamus atque tenemus, quam quisque firmissime custodierit, perpetuam salutem habebit.
In the third year of the reign of our most glorious lord Prince Sisenand, on the Nones of December, in the Era 681, while by the study of the love of Christ and the diligence of the most religious King Sisenand of Spain and Gaul, the priests had convened in the name of the Lord at the Toledan city, so that, being reminded by his commands and orders, a treatise concerning certain ecclesiastical disciplines might be agitated by us, first we gave thanks to our Savior, omnipotent God. After these things, the aforementioned most excellent and glorious king, whose so great devotion toward God stands forth that he remains solicitous not only in human affairs but also in divine causes, while in the basilica of the most blessed and holy martyr Leocadia the assembly of all of us was already present, he entered with the most magnificent and most noble men on account of the merit of his faith, and first, having prostrated himself with the priests of God, he sought with tears and groans that the Lord intercede for him, then with a religious prosecution he exhorted the synod, that, mindful of the paternal decrees, we should give study to confirming ecclesiastical rights in us, and to correct those things which, while through negligence they had come into use against ecclesiastical customs, had made license for themselves from usurpation. Rejoicing therefore at such warnings of his, it seemed necessary according to his and our desire to treat those things which are fitting, both in the divine sacraments, which are celebrated in a diverse and illicit manner in the churches of Spain, and in those things which are known to have been perversely usurped in morals. And because we are conducting a general council, it is fitting first that the discourse of our voice be concerning God, so that after the profession of faith the subsequent vows of our work may be disposed as if upon a most firm foundation. Just as we have received from the holy fathers, we confess the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit of one deity and substance, believing in the Trinity in diversity of persons, preaching unity in divinity, neither confounding the persons nor separating the substance. We say the Father has been made or begotten by no one, we assert the Son has been not made but begotten by the Father, the Holy Spirit truly we profess neither created nor begotten, but proceeding from the Father and the Son. Moreover the Lord Jesus Christ Himself, the Son of God and the creator of all things, begotten from the substance of the Father before the ages, descended in the last time for the redemption of the world from the Father, who never ceases to be with the Father, for He was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the holy, glorious, God-bearing Virgin Mary, and was born of her alone. The same Lord Jesus Christ, one in the holy Trinity, perfect in soul and flesh without sin, receiving man, remaining what He was, assuming what He was not, equal to the Father according to divinity, less than the Father according to humanity, having in one person the property of two natures, for there are two natures in Him, God and man, but not two sons and two gods, but the same one person in each nature; undergoing passion and death for our salvation, not in the virtue of divinity but in the infirmity of humanity, He descended to hell, that He might free the saints who were held there, and, the dominion of death having been overcome, He rose again, having been taken up into heaven He is about to come in the future for the judgment of the living and the dead, by whose death and blood, having been cleansed, we have obtained remission of sins, about to be raised by Him on the last day in that flesh in which we now live, and in that form in which the same Lord rose, about to receive from Him eternal life for the merits of justice, and for sins the sentence of eternal punishment. This is the faith of the catholic church, we preserve and hold this confession, which whoever shall have most firmly guarded, will have perpetual salvation.

CANON I
Post rectae fidei confessionem, quae in sancta dei ecclesia praedicatur, placuit omnes sacerdotes, qui catholicae fidei unitate complectimur, ut nihil ultra diversum aut dissonum in ecclesiasticis sacramentis agamus, ne quaelibet nostra diversitas apud ignotos seu carnales schismatis errorem videatur ostendere et multis exstet in scandalum varietas ecclesiarum. Unus igitur ordo orandi atque psallendi a nobis per omnem Spaniam atque Galliam conservetur, unus modus in missarum sollemnitatibus, unus in vespertinis officiis, nec diversa sit ultra in nobis ecclesiastica consuetudo, quia una fide continemur et regno. Hoc enim et antiqui canones decreverunt, ut unaquaeque provincia et psallendi et ministrandi parem consuetudinem teneat.
After the confession of the right faith, which is preached in the holy church of God, it has pleased that all priests, who are embraced by the unity of the catholic faith, that we should do nothing further diverse or dissonant in the ecclesiastical sacraments, lest any diversity of ours might seem to show the error of schism among the ignorant or carnal, and the variety of churches stand as a scandal to many. Therefore let one order of praying and of psalm-singing be preserved by us throughout all Spain and Gaul, one mode in the solemnities of masses, one in vespertine offices, nor let ecclesiastical custom be further diverse among us, because we are contained by one faith and kingdom. For this also the ancient canons decreed, that each province hold a like custom both of psalm-singing and of ministering.
¶31
CANON II
Nulla pene res disciplinae mores ab ecclesia Christi magis depulit, quam sacerdotum neglegentia, qui contemptis canonibus ad corrigendos ecclesiasticos mores synodum facere neglegunt. Ob hoc a nobis universaliter definitum est, ut, quia iuxta antiqua patrum decreta bis in anno difficultas temporis fieri concilium non sinit, saltem vel semel a nobis celebretur ita tamen, ut, si fidei causa est aut quaelibet alia ecclesiae communis, generalis totius Spaniae et Galliae synodus convocetur; si vero nec de fide, nec de communi ecclesiae utilitate tractabitur, speciale erit concilium, prout uniuscuiusque provinciae metropolitanus elegerit peragendum. Omnes autem, qui causas contra episcopos aut iudices ac potentes aut contra quoslibet alios habere noscuntur, ad eundem concilium concurrant, et quaecumque examina synodalia a quibuslibet prave usurpata inveniuntur regii exsecutoris instantia iustissime his, quibus iura sunt, reformentur, ita ut pro compellendis iudicibus vel saecularibus viris ad synodum metropolitani studio quidam exsecutor a principe postuletur. Quinta autem decima Kal. Iun. congreganda est in unaquaque provincia synodus propter vernale tempus, quando herbis terra vestitur et pabula germinum inveniuntur.
Almost no thing has more driven the customs of discipline from the church of Christ than the negligence of priests, who, the canons having been despised, neglect to hold a synod for correcting ecclesiastical customs. On account of this it has been universally defined by us, that, because according to the ancient decrees of the fathers the difficulty of time does not permit a council to be held twice a year, at least even once let it be celebrated by us, yet in such a way that, if it is a cause of faith or any other common matter of the church, a general synod of all Spain and Gaul be convened; if indeed neither concerning faith nor concerning the common utility of the church shall be treated, it will be a special council, just as the metropolitan of each province shall have chosen to be performed. Moreover all who are known to have causes against bishops or judges or powerful persons or against any others, let them run together to the same council, and whatsoever synodal examinations are found to have been perversely usurped by any persons, let them be reformed most justly by the insistence of the royal executor to those to whom the rights belong, so that for compelling judges or secular men to the synod, a certain executor may be requested from the prince by the study of the metropolitan. Moreover the synod is to be gathered in each province on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of June, on account of the springtime, when the earth is clothed with grasses and the food of germs is found.
¶32
CANON III
De formula, secundum quam debeat sancta synodus in dei nomine fieri.
Concerning the formula according to which a holy synod ought to be conducted in the name of God.
¶33
Hora itaque diei prima ante solis ortum eiciantur omnes ab ecclesia. Obseratisque foribus cunctis ad unam ianuam, per quam sacerdotes ingredi oportet, ostiarii sedeant et convenientes omnes episcopi pariter introeant, et secundum ordinationis suae tempora resideant. Post ingressum omnium episcoporum atque consessum vocentur deinde presbyteri, quos causa probaverit introire. Nullus se inter eos ingerat diaconorum. Post hos ingrediantur diacones probabiles, quos ordo poposcerit interesse, et corona facta de sedibus episcoporum presbyteri a tergo eorum resideant, diacones in conspectu episcoporum stent. Deinde ingrediantur laici, qui electione concilii interesse meruerint, tum quoque et notarii, quos ad recitandum vel excipiendum ordo requirit, et obserentur ianuae. Sedentesque in diuturno silentio sacerdotes et cor tantum habentes ad deum, dicat archidiaconus: Orate. Statimque omnes in terram prostrabuntur, et orantes diutius tacite cum fletibus atque gemitibus, unus ex episcopis senioribus surgens orationem palam fundat ad dominum, cunctis adhuc in terra iacentibus. Finita autem oratione et responso ab omnibus Amen, rursus dicat diaconus: Erigite vos, et confestim omnes surgant et cum omni timore dei et disciplina, tam episcopi quam presbyteri sedeant, sicque omnibus in suis locis in silentio consedentibus diaconus alba indutus codicem canonum in medio proferens capitula de conciliis agendis pronuntiet, finitisque titulis metropolitanus episcopus concilium alloquatur dicens: Ecce, sanctissimi sacerdotes, recitatae sunt ex canonibus priscorum patrum sententiae de concilio celebrando. Si qua igitur quempiam vestrum actio commovet coram suis fratribus proponat. Tunc si aliquis quamcumque querelam, quae contra canonem agit, in audientiam sacerdotalem protulerit, non prius ad aliud transeatur capitulum, nisi primum, quae praeposita est actio, terminetur. Nam et si presbyter aliquis aut diaconus vel clericus sive laicus de his, qui foris steterint, concilium pro qualibet re crediderit appellandum, ecclesiae metropolitanae archidiacono causam suam intimet, et ille concilio denuntiet. Tunc illi et introeundi et proponendi licentia concedatur. Nullus autem episcoporum a coetu communi secedat antequam hora generalis secessionis adveniat. Concilium quoque nullus solvere audeat, nisi fuerint cuncta determinata, ita ut quaecumque deliberatione communi finiuntur, episcoporum singulorum manibus subscribantur. Tunc enim deus suorum sacerdotum interesse credendus est, si tumultu omni abiecto sollicite atque tranquille ecclesiastica negotia terminentur.
Therefore at the first hour of the day before the rising of the sun, let all be cast out from the church. And all doors having been closed, at the one gate through which the priests ought to enter, let the doorkeepers sit, and all the bishops together entering, let them sit down according to the time of their ordination. After the entrance and sitting of all the bishops, then let the presbyters be called, whom the cause shall have approved to enter. Let none of the deacons thrust himself among them. After these let the deacons, approved, whom the order shall have demanded to be present, enter, and with a circle made from the seats of the bishops, let the presbyters sit behind them, let the deacons stand in the sight of the bishops. Then let the laypersons enter, who shall have deserved by the choice of the council to be present, then also the notaries, whom the order requires for reading or receiving, and let the doors be closed. And the priests sitting in prolonged silence and having their heart only to God, let the archdeacon say: "Pray." Immediately let all prostrate themselves on the ground, and praying for a long time silently with tears and groans, let one of the senior bishops, rising, pour forth a prayer openly to the Lord, with all still lying on the ground. But the prayer having been finished and the response "Amen" by all, let the deacon say again: "Raise yourselves," and immediately let all rise and with all fear of God and discipline, both bishops and presbyters, sit down; and thus with all sitting in their places in silence, let a deacon, clothed in a white garment, bringing forth the book of canons into the midst, pronounce the chapters concerning councils to be conducted, and the titles having been finished, let the metropolitan bishop address the council, saying: "Behold, most holy priests, the sentences from the canons of the ancient fathers concerning holding a council have been recited. If therefore any action moves any of you, let him set it forth before his brothers." Then if anyone shall have brought forth any complaint, which acts against the canon, into the priestly hearing, let it not be passed over to another chapter, unless first the action which has been set forth be terminated. For if any presbyter or deacon or cleric or layperson, of those who have stood outside, shall have believed that the council ought to be appealed to for any matter, let him make known his cause to the archdeacon of the metropolitan church, and he shall announce it to the council. Then let license be conceded to them both of entering and of setting forth. But let none of the bishops depart from the common gathering before the hour of general departure arrives. Nor let anyone dare to dissolve the council unless all things shall have been determined, so that whatsoever things are concluded by common deliberation, let them be subscribed by the hands of the individual bishops. For then God is to be believed to be present with His priests, if with all tumult cast aside, ecclesiastical matters be terminated solicitously and tranquilly.
¶34
CANON IV
De sollemnitate paschali, qualiter solet in Spaniis varietas exsistere praedicationis.
Concerning the paschal solemnity, in what manner variety of preaching is accustomed to exist in Spain.
¶35
Solet in Spaniis de solemnitate paschali varietas exsistere praedicationis. Diversa enim observantia laterculorum paschalis festivitatis interdum errorem parturit. Proinde placuit, ut ante tres menses epiphaniorum metropolitani sacerdotes litteris se invicem inquirant, ut communi scientia docti diem resurrectionis Christi comprovincialibus suis insinuent et uno tempore celebrandum annuntient.
Variety of preaching concerning the paschal solemnity is accustomed to exist in Spain. For the diverse observance of the paschal festival tablets sometimes produces error. Therefore it has pleased, that three months before the Epiphany, the metropolitan priests should inquire of one another by letters, so that, taught by common knowledge, they may make known the day of Christ's resurrection to their fellow-provincials and announce that it is to be celebrated at one time.
¶36
CANON V
De baptismi sacramento.
Concerning the sacrament of baptism.
¶37
De baptismi autem sacramento, propter quod in Spaniis quidam sacerdotes trinam, simplam quidam mersionem faciunt, a nonnullis schisma esse conspicitur et unitas fidei scindi videtur. Nam dum partes diversae in baptizandis aliqua contrario modo agunt, ab aliis non baptizatos esse contendunt. Proinde quid a nobis in sacramenti diversitate fiendum sit, apostolicae sedis informemur praeceptis non nostram, sed paternam institutionem sequentes. Beatae igitur memoriae Gregorius Romanae ecclesiae pontifex, qui non solum partes Italiae illustravit, sed et longe exsistentes ecclesias sua doctrina perdocuit, efflagitante sanctissimo Leandro episcopo de hac Spaniae diversitate, quid potius esset sequendum, inter cetera rescribens ei sic ait: "De trina vero mersione baptismatis nihil responderi verius potest, quam ipsi sensistis, quia in una fide nihil efficit sanctae ecclesiae consuetudo diversa. Nos autem, quod tertio mergimus, triduanae sepulturae sacramenta signamus, ut, dum tertio ab aquis infans educitur, resurrectio triduani temporis exprimatur. Quod si quis forte etiam pro summae trinitatis veneratione existimet fieri, neque ad hoc aliquid obsistit baptizandum semel in aquam mergere, quia dum in tribus subsistentiis una substantia est, repraehensibile esse nullatenus poterit infantem in baptismate in aquam vel ter vel semel mergere, quando et in tribus mersionibus personarum trinitas et in una potest divinitatis singularitas designari. Sed si hucusque ab hereticis infans in baptismate tertio mergebatur, fiendum apud vos esse non censeo, ne dum mersiones numerant, divinitatem dividant, dumque, quod faciebant, faciunt, morem vestrum se vicisse glorientur." Quapropter, quia de utroque sacramento, quod fit in sancto baptismo a tanto viro reddita est ratio, quod utrumque rectum, utrumque irrepraehensibile in sancta dei ecclesia habeatur, propter vitandum autem schismatis scandalum vel heretici dogmatis usum simplam teneamus baptismi mersionem, ne videantur apud nos, qui tertio mergunt, hereticorum approbare assertionem, dum sequuntur et morem, et ne forte cuiquam sit dubium huius simplicis mysterium sacramenti, videat in eo mortem et resurrectionem Christi significare. Nam in aquis mersio quasi in infernum descensio est et rursus ab aquis emersio resurrectio est. Item videant in eo unitatem divinitatis et trinitatem personarum ostendi: unitatem, dum semel mergimus; trinitatem, dum in nomine patris et filii et spiritus sancti baptizamus. Panditur huius singularis baptismatis mysterium etiam scripturarum sanctarum exemplis Paulo apostolo attestante: Nolo vos ignorare, fratres, quoniam patres nostri omnes sub nube fuerunt et omnes mare transierunt et omnes in Moyse baptizati sunt in nube et in mari. Mare quippe rubrum significat baptismum Christi sanguine consecratum, per quod populus dei semel transiit, ubi tamen tota erat trinitas praecedente populum columna ignis et nubis. In igne quippe significatur pater, in columna filius, in nube spiritus sanctus. Iordanis quoque fluenta cum arca populus dei semel transiit, per quod significatur simpla mersio baptismatis, cuius sacramento ecclesia abluitur et de saeculi huius laboribus per baptismum quasi per Iordanem ad terram caelestis repromissionis ingreditur.
But concerning the sacrament of baptism, on account of which in Spain certain priests make a triple, certain a single immersion, by some it is seen to be a schism and the unity of faith is seen to be rent. For while different parties in baptizing do something in a contrary manner, they contend by some that they are not baptized. Therefore what is to be done by us in the diversity of the sacrament, let us be informed by the precepts of the apostolic see, following not our own but the paternal institution. Therefore Gregory of blessed memory, pontiff of the Roman church, who not only illuminated the parts of Italy, but also thoroughly taught with his own doctrine the churches existing far away, the most holy Bishop Leander having inquired concerning this diversity of Spain, what rather ought to be followed, among other things responding to him thus says: "Concerning the triple immersion of baptism, nothing can be answered more truly than what you yourselves have thought, because in one faith the diverse custom of holy church does nothing. We however, because we immerse three times, signify the sacraments of the three-day burial, so that while the infant is brought forth a third time from the waters, the resurrection of the three-day time may be expressed. But if perhaps anyone also for the veneration of the supreme Trinity thinks it to be done, neither does anything stand in the way of immersing the baptized once into the waters, because while in three subsistences there is one substance, it can be by no means reprehensible to immerse the infant in baptism into the water either three times or once, when both in three immersions the Trinity of persons and in one the singularity of divinity can be designated. But if hitherto by heretics the infant was immersed three times in baptism, I do not think it to be done by you, lest while they number the immersions, they divide the divinity, and while they do what they were doing, they might glory that they have overcome your custom." Wherefore, because by so great a man concerning each sacrament, which is done in holy baptism, a reason has been rendered, that each is right, each is irreprehensible in the holy church of God, for the sake of avoiding however the scandal of schism or the use of heretical dogma, let us hold the single immersion of baptism, lest among us, those who immerse three times might seem to approve the assertion of heretics, while they follow both their custom, and lest perhaps there might be doubt to anyone concerning the mystery of this single sacrament, let him see in it the death and resurrection of Christ to be signified. For immersion into the waters is as it were a descent into hell, and again a coming forth from the waters is a resurrection. Likewise let them see in it the unity of divinity and the trinity of persons to be shown: unity, while we immerse once; trinity, while we baptize in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. The mystery of this single baptism is also unfolded by examples of the holy scriptures, the apostle Paul testifying: "I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, because our fathers all were under the cloud and all passed through the sea and all were baptized in Moses in the cloud and in the sea." For the Red Sea signifies baptism consecrated by the blood of Christ, through which the people of God passed once, where yet the whole Trinity was present, the column of fire and of cloud preceding the people. For in the fire is signified the Father, in the column the Son, in the cloud the Holy Spirit. The flowing waters of the Jordan also the people of God passed through once with the ark, through which the single immersion of baptism is signified, by whose sacrament the church is washed and from the labors of this world through baptism as if through the Jordan enters into the land of heavenly promise.
¶38
CANON VI
Comperimus, quod per nonnullas ecclesias in die sextae feriae passionis domini clausis basilicarum foribus nec celebretur officium nec passio domini populis praedicetur, dum idem salvator noster apostolis suis praecipiat dicens: Passionem et mortem et resurrectionem meam omnibus praedicate. Ideoque oportet eodem die mysterium crucis, quod ipse dominus cunctis annuntiandum voluit, praedicari atque indulgentiam criminum clara voce omnem populum postulare, ut poenitentiae compunctione mundati venerabilem diem dominicae resurrectionis remissis iniquitatibus suscipere mereamur corporisque eius et sanguinis sacramentum mundi a peccato sumamus.
We have discovered that in not a few churches on the sixth feria, the day of the Lord's passion, with the doors of the basilicas closed, the office is not celebrated, nor is the passion of the Lord preached to the peoples, while the same our Savior commands to His apostles saying: "Preach my passion and death and resurrection to all." Therefore it is fitting that on the same day the mystery of the cross, which the Lord Himself wished to be announced to all, be preached, and that the whole people seek the indulgence of sins with clear voice, so that, cleansed by compunction of penance, we may deserve to receive the venerable day of the Lord's resurrection, the iniquities having been remitted, and receive the sacrament of His body and blood, cleansed from sin.
¶39
CANON VII
Quidam in die eiusdem dominicae passionis ab hora nona ieiunium solvunt, conviviis adhibentur, et dum sol ipse eadem die tenebris palluerit, lumen subduxerit, ipsaque elementa turbata mestitiam totius mundi ostenderint, illi ieiunium tanti diei polluunt epulisque inserviunt, et quia totum eundem diem universalis ecclesia propter passionem domini in merore et abstinentia peragit, quicumque in eo ieiunium praeter parvulos, senes et languidos ante peractas indulgentiae preces solverit, a paschali gaudio depellatur, nec in eo sacramentum corporis et sanguinis domini percipiat, qui diem passionis eius per abstinentiam non honorat.
Certain persons on the day of the same Lord's passion from the ninth hour break the fast, apply themselves to feasts, and while the sun itself on that day has become pale with darkness, withdrawn its light, and the very elements, disturbed, have shown the grief of the whole world, they pollute the fast of so great a day and serve feasts. And because the universal church performs the whole of that same day on account of the passion of the Lord in grief and abstinence, whoever on it shall have broken the fast, excepting little ones, the aged, and the infirm, before the prayers of indulgence have been completed, let him be driven from the paschal joy, nor in it let him receive the sacrament of the body and blood of the Lord, who does not honor the day of His passion by abstinence.
¶40
CANON VIII
Lucerna et cereus infra vigilias apud quasdam ecclesias non benedicuntur et, cur a nobis benedicantur, inquirunt. Propter gloriosum enim noctis ipsius sacramentum sollemniter haec benedicimus, ut sacrae resurrectionis Christi mysterium, quod tempore huius votivae noctis advenit, benedictione sanctificati luminis suscipiamus, et quia haec observatio per multarum loca terrarum regionesque Spaniae in ecclesiis commendatur, dignum est, ut propter unitatem pacis in Gallicanis ecclesiis conservetur. Nulli autem impune erit, qui haec contempserit, sed patrum regulis subiacebit.
The lamp and candle during the vigils in certain churches are not blessed, and they inquire why they are blessed by us. On account of the glorious sacrament of that same night we bless these things solemnly, so that we may receive the mystery of the holy resurrection of Christ, which comes at the time of this votive night, with the blessing of sanctified light, and because this observance is commended in the churches through the places of many lands and the regions of Spain, it is worthy that on account of the unity of peace it be preserved in the Gallic churches. But it will not be impunity for anyone who shall have despised these things, but let him be subject to the rules of the fathers.
¶41
CANON IX
Nonnulli sacerdotum in Spaniis reperiuntur, qui dominicam orationem, quam salvator noster docuit et praecepit, non cotidie, sed tantum die dominica dicunt, et quia sine intermissione oremus, ut apostolus docuit, qualiter autem oremus, Christus praecepit dicens: Cum autem oratis, dicite: Pater noster qui es in caelis. Quomodo ergo cotidie non dicitur, quod sine intermissione dici iubetur? Nam in tantum cotidie haec oratio dicenda est, ut etiam ipso titulo utatur, nam vocatur oratio cotidiana. Sic eam sancti patres nuncupaverunt, quod etiam apud doctores, quorum illustris doctrina est, invenitur. Sanctus quippe Cyprianus dicit: Itaque in oratione dominica panem nostrum, id est Christum, dari nobis cotidie petimus, ut, qui in Christo manemus et vivimus, a sanctificatione et corpore eius non recedamus. Sanctus Hilarius dicit: Panem nostrum cotidianum da nobis hodie. Quid enim tam vult deus quam, ut cotidie Christus habitet in nobis, qui est panis vitae et panis de caelo? Et quia cotidiana oratio est, cotidie quoque, ut detur, oratur? Sanctus Augustinus dicit: De cotidianis autem brevibusque peccatis, sine quibus vita haec non ducitur, cotidiana oratio fidelium satisfacit. Eorum est enim dicere: Pater noster, qui es in caelis, qui iam patri tali regenerati sunt ex aqua et spiritu sancto. Delet igitur haec cotidiana oratio minima cotidiana peccata, delet illa, a quibus vita fidelium etiam scelerate gesta poenitendo in melius mutata discedit. Ergo sicut Christus praecepit, sicut apostolus admonuit, et quemadmodum doctores ecclesiastici instituerunt, quia cotidie vel cogitatione vel verbo delinquimus, cotidie hanc orationem effundere in conspectu dei debemus. Quisquis ergo sacerdotum vel subiacentium clericorum hanc orationem dominicam cotidie aut in publico aut in privato officio praeterierit propter superbiam, iudicatus ordinis sui honore multetur.
Not a few priests throughout Spain are found who say the Lord's prayer, which our Savior taught and commanded, not daily but only on the Lord's day. And because we ought to pray without interruption, as the apostle taught, but in what manner we ought to pray, Christ commanded, saying: "When you pray, say: Our Father, who art in heaven." How therefore is it not said daily, which is commanded to be said without interruption? For to such an extent is this prayer to be said daily, that it even uses this title, for it is called the daily prayer. Thus the holy fathers have named it, which is also found among the doctors, whose illustrious doctrine is. For holy Cyprian says: "Therefore in the Lord's prayer we ask that our bread, that is Christ, be given to us daily, so that we who remain and live in Christ may not depart from His sanctification and body." Holy Hilary says: "Give us our daily bread today." For what so much does God will, that Christ, who is the bread of life and bread from heaven, might dwell in us daily? And because it is a daily prayer, is it also prayed daily that it be given? Holy Augustine says: "Concerning daily and brief sins, without which this life is not led, the daily prayer of the faithful satisfies. For it is theirs to say: 'Our Father, who art in heaven,' who have already been regenerated to such a Father from water and the Holy Spirit. Therefore this daily prayer blots out the smallest daily sins, it blots out those sins, from which the life of the faithful departs even by changing wickedly done deeds for the better through repentance." Therefore, just as Christ commanded, just as the apostle admonished, and just as the ecclesiastical doctors have instituted, because daily we sin either in thought or in word, daily we ought to pour forth this prayer in the sight of God. Whoever therefore of priests or of the clerics lying under them shall have passed over this Lord's prayer daily, either in public or in private office, on account of pride, let him be judged and mulcted by the honor of his order.
¶42
CANON X
Item cognovimus quosdam Spaniae sacerdotes, qui in quadragesimae diebus alleluia decantent praeter in ultima hebdomada paschae, quod deinceps fieri interdicimus statuentes, ut in omnibus praedictis quadragesimae diebus, quia tempus est non gaudii, sed meroris, alleluia non decantetur. Tunc enim opus est fletibus ac ieiuniis insistere, corpus cilicio et cinere induere, animum meroribus deicere, gaudium in tristitiam vertere, quousque veniet tempus resurrectionis Christi, quando oporteat iam in laetitia manere et merorem in gaudium commutare. Hoc enim ecclesiae universalis consensio in cunctis terrarum partibus roboravit, quod et a nobis omnibus, ut conservetur per Spanias Galliasque provincias oportebit. In temporibus quoque reliquis, id est Kalend. Ianuar., quae propter errorem gentilitatis aguntur, omnino alleluia non decantabitur, in quibus etiam praeter piscem et olus, sicut et in illis XL diebus, ceteris carnibus abstinetur et a quibusdam etiam nec vinum bibitur. Si quis igitur episcopus aut presbyter aut diaconus aut quilibet ex ordine clericorum fuerit repertus, qui arbitrium suum huic constitutioni aestimet praeferendum, ordinis sui officio carere cogatur et communione eiusdem paschae privetur.
Likewise we have known certain priests of Spain who chant alleluia during the days of Lent, except in the last week of Easter, which henceforth we forbid to be done, establishing that in all the aforesaid days of Lent, because it is a time not of joy but of grief, alleluia not be chanted. For then it is fitting to attend to tears and fastings, to clothe the body with sackcloth and ashes, to cast down the mind with grief, to turn joy into sadness, until the time of the resurrection of Christ comes, when it is fitting to remain in joy and to change grief into gladness. For this the consensus of the universal church has strengthened in all parts of the world, which it will be fitting also by all of us to be preserved throughout the provinces of Spain and Gaul. In the remaining times also, that is, on the Kalends of January, which are observed on account of the error of heathenism, alleluia will by no means be chanted, in which also besides fish and vegetables, as on those forty days, abstinence is practiced from other meats, and by some even wine is not drunk. If therefore any bishop or presbyter or deacon or any from the order of clerics shall have been found who should judge his own judgment to be preferred to this constitution, let him be compelled to lack the office of his order and be deprived of the communion of the same Easter.
¶43
CANON XI
In quibusdam quoque Spaniarum ecclesiis laudes post apostolum decantantur, priusquam evangelium praedicetur, dum canones praecipiant post apostolum non laudes, sed evangelium annuntiare, praesumptio est enim, ut anteponant ea, quae sequi debent. Nam laudes ideo evangelium secuntur propter gloriam Christi, quae per idem evangelium praedicatur. Circa omnes igitur sacerdotes hic ordo deinceps retineatur, excommunicationis poenam suscepturi, qui hunc ordinem perturbaverint.
In certain Spanish churches also, laudes are chanted after the apostle, before the gospel is preached, while the canons command that after the apostle not laudes but the gospel be announced; for it is presumption that they place before those things which ought to follow. For laudes follow the gospel on account of the glory of Christ, which is preached through the same gospel. Therefore let this order be retained henceforth among all priests, about to undergo the penalty of excommunication who shall have disturbed this order.
¶44
CANON XII
De hymnis etiam canendis et salvatoris et apostolorum habemus exemplum. Nam et ipse dominus hymnum dixisse perhibetur Matthaeo evangelista testante: Et hymno dicto exierunt in montem Oliveti. Et Paulus apostolus ad Ephesios scripsit dicens: Implemini spiritu loquentes vobiscum in psalmis et hymnis et canticis spiritalibus. Et quia a nonnullis hymni humano studio in laudem dei atque apostolorum et martyrum triumphos compositi esse noscuntur, sicut hi, quos beatissimi doctores Hilarius atque Ambrosius ediderunt, quos tamen quidam specialiter reprobant pro eo, quod de scripturis sanctorum canonum vel apostolica traditione non exsistunt. Respuant ergo et illum hymnum ab hominibus compositum, quem cotidie publico privatoque officio in fine omnium psalmorum dicimus: Gloria et honor patri et filio et spiritui sancto in saecula saeculorum. Amen. Nam et ille hymnus, quem nato in carne Christo angeli cecinerunt: Gloria in excelsis deo et in terra pax hominibus bonae voluntatis, reliqua, quae ibi sequuntur, ecclesiastici doctores composuerunt. Ergo nec ipsi in ecclesiis canendi sunt, quia in sanctarum scripturarum libris non inveniuntur? Componuntur missae sive preces vel orationes sive commendationes seu manus impositiones, ex quibus, si nulla decantetur in ecclesia, vacant officia omnia ecclesiastica. Admonet haec fieri atque hortatur Timotheum apostolus dicens: Obsecro igitur primo omnium fieri obsecrationes, orationes, postulationes, gratiarum actiones pro omnibus hominibus, pro regibus et pro omnibus, qui in sublimitatibus sunt. Sicut igitur orationes, ita et hymnos in laudem dei compositos nullus nostrum ulterius improbet, sed pari modo Gallia Spaniaque celebret. Excommunicatione plectendi, qui hymnos reicere fuerint ausi.
Concerning hymns also to be sung, we have the example of the Savior and of the apostles. For the Lord Himself is reported to have said a hymn, Matthew the evangelist testifying: "And a hymn having been said, they went out to the Mount of Olives." And Paul the apostle wrote to the Ephesians, saying: "Be filled with the Spirit, speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual canticles." And because by some hymns composed by human study for the praise of God and for the triumphs of the apostles and martyrs are known to exist, such as those which the most blessed doctors Hilary and Ambrose have put forth, which yet certain persons specially reprobate for the reason that they do not exist from the scriptures of the holy canons or from apostolic tradition. Let them therefore also reject that hymn composed by men, which daily in public and private office at the end of all the psalms we say: "Glory and honor to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit forever and ever. Amen." For also that hymn which the angels sang to Christ born in the flesh: "Glory to God in the highest and on earth peace to men of good will," the rest which follow there, ecclesiastical doctors have composed. Therefore are not even these to be sung in the churches, because they are not found in the books of the holy scriptures? Masses or prayers or addresses or commendations or impositions of hands are composed, from which, if none should be chanted in the church, all ecclesiastical offices would be empty. The apostle admonishes and exhorts Timothy that these things be done, saying: "I beseech therefore first of all that supplications, prayers, intercessions, thanksgivings be made for all men, for kings and for all who are in high positions." Therefore, just as prayers, so also let none of us further reject hymns composed for the praise of God, but let Gaul and Spain celebrate them in like manner. About to be punished with excommunication, those who shall have dared to reject hymns.
¶45
CANON XIII
Hymnum quoque trium puerorum, in quo universa caeli terraeque creatura deum collaudat et quem ecclesia catholica per totum orbem diffusa celebrat, quidam sacerdotes in missa dominicorum dierum et in sollemnitatibus martyrum canere neglegunt. Proinde sanctum concilium instituit, ut per omnes ecclesias Spaniae vel Galliae in omnium missarum solemnitate idem in publico decantetur, communionem amissuri, qui antiquam huius hymni consuetudinem nostramque definitionem excesserint.
The hymn also of the three youths, in which every creature of heaven and earth praises God, and which the catholic church diffused throughout the whole world celebrates, certain priests neglect to sing in the mass of the Lord's days and in the solemnities of the martyrs. Therefore the holy council has established that through all the churches of Spain and Gaul, in the solemnity of all masses, the same be chanted in public, about to lose communion, those who shall have exceeded the ancient custom of this hymn and our definition.
¶46
CANON XIV
In fine psalmorum non, sicut a quibusdam hucusque, gloria patri, sed gloria et honor patri dicatur David propheta docente: Afferte domino gloriam et honorem, et Ioanne evangelista exercitus dicentium: Honor et gloria deo nostro sedenti in throno. Ac per hoc haec duo sic oportet in terris dici, sicut in caelis resonant. Universis igitur ecclesiasticis hanc observantiam damus, quam quisque praeterierit, communionis iacturam habebit.
At the end of the psalms not, as by some until now, "Glory to the Father," but "Glory and honor to the Father" be said, David the prophet teaching: "Give to the Lord glory and honor," and John the evangelist of the armies saying: "Honor and glory to our God sitting upon the throne." And through this it is fitting that these two be said on earth, just as they resound in heaven. Therefore we give this observance to all ecclesiastical persons, which whoever shall have passed over, will have the loss of communion.
¶47
CANON XV
Sunt quidam, qui in fine responsoriorum gloriam non dicunt, propter quod interdum inconvenienter resonat, sed haec est discretio, ut in laetis sequatur gloria, in tristioribus repetatur principium.
There are certain persons who do not say the "Glory" at the end of responsories, on account of which it sometimes resounds inconveniently. But this is the discretion, that in joyful things the "Glory" follows, in sadder things the beginning is repeated.
¶48
CANON XVI
De apocalypsi libro, ut teneatur et quando legatur.
Concerning the book of the Apocalypse, that it be held and when it be read.
¶49
Apocalypsin librum multorum conciliorum auctoritas et synodica sanctorum praesulum Romanorum decreta Ioannis evangelistae esse rescribunt, et inter divinos libros recipiendum constituerunt. Et quia plurimi sunt, qui eius auctoritatem non recipiunt eamque in ecclesia dei praedicare contemnunt, si quis eum deinceps aut non receperit aut a pascha usque ad pentecosten missarum tempore in ecclesia non praedicaverit, excommunicationis sententiam habebit.
The authority of many councils and the synodal decrees of the holy prelates of the Romans write that the book of the Apocalypse is of John the evangelist, and have established that it is to be received among the divine books. And because there are very many who do not receive its authority and despise to preach it in the church of God, if anyone henceforth either does not receive it or shall not have preached it in the church from Easter until Pentecost at the time of mass, he will have the sentence of excommunication.
¶50
CANON XVII
Nonnulli sacerdotes post dictam orationem dominicam statim communicant et postea benedictionem in populo dant, quod deinceps interdicimus, sed post orationem dominicam benedictio in populum sequatur, et tunc demum corporis et sanguinis domini sacramentum sumatur, eo videlicet ordine, ut sacerdos et levita ante altare communicent, in choro clerus, extra chorum populus.
Not a few priests, after the Lord's prayer having been said, immediately communicate and afterward give the blessing to the people, which henceforth we forbid. But after the Lord's prayer, let the blessing follow upon the people, and then at length let the sacrament of the body and blood of the Lord be taken, by that order namely, that the priest and levite communicate before the altar, the clerics in the choir, the people outside the choir.
¶51
CANON XVIII
Perniciosa consuetudo nequaquam est recipienda, quae maiorum statuta praeteriens omnem ecclesiae ordinem perturbavit, dum alii per ambitus sacerdotia appetunt, alii oblatis muneribus pontificatum assumunt, nonnulli etiam sceleribus implicati vel saeculari militiae dediti indigni ad honorem summi ac sacri ordinis pervenerunt, de quorum scilicet causa atque remotione oportuerat quidem statuere, sed ne perturbatio quamplurima ecclesiae oriretur, praeteritis omissis deinceps, qui non promoveantur ad sacerdotium ex regulis canonum, necessario credimus inserendum, id est: qui in aliquo crimine detecti sunt, qui infamiae nota aspersi sunt, qui scelera aliqua per publicam poenitentiam admisisse confessi sunt, qui in heresim lapsi sunt, qui in heresi baptizati aut rebaptizati esse noscuntur, qui semetipsos absciderunt aut naturaliter defectu membrorum decisione aliquid minus habere noscuntur, qui secundae uxoris coniunctionem sortiti sunt aut numerosa coniugia frequentaverunt, qui viduam vel a marito relictam duxerunt aut corruptarum mariti fuerunt, qui concubinas aut fornicarias habuerunt, qui servili condicione obnoxii sunt, qui ignoti sunt, qui neophyti vel laici sunt, qui saeculari militiae dediti sunt, qui curiae nexibus obligati sunt, qui inscii litterarum sunt, qui nondum ad triginta annos pervenerunt, qui per gradus ecclesiasticos non accesserunt, qui ambitu honorem quaerunt, qui muneribus honorem obtinere moliuntur, qui a decessoribus in sacerdotium eliguntur. Sed nec ille deinceps sacerdos erit, quem nec clerus nec populus propriae civitatis elegit vel auctoritas metropolitani vel comprovincialium sacerdotum assensio exquisivit. Quicumque igitur deinceps ad ordinem sacerdotii postulatus et in his, quae praedicta sunt, exquisitus in nullo horum depraehensus fuerit et examinatus probabili vita atque doctrina exstiterit, tunc secundum synodalia vel decretalia constituta cum omnium clericorum vel civium voluntate ab universis comprovincialibus episcopis aut certe a tribus in sacerdotium die dominica consecrabitur, conhibentibus ceteris, qui absentes sunt, litteris suis, et magis auctoritate vel praesentia eius, qui est in metropoli constitutus. Episcopus autem comprovincialis ibi consecrandus est, ubi metropolitanus elegerit; metropolitanus autem non nisi in civitate metropoli comprovincialibus ibidem convenientibus. Si quis autem deinceps contra praedicta vetita canonum ad gradum sacerdotii indignus aspirare contenderit, cum ordinatoribus suis adepti honoris periculo subiacebit.
A pernicious custom is by no means to be received, which, passing over the statutes of the greater ones, has disturbed every order of the church, while some seek priesthoods by canvassing, others assume the pontificate by offered gifts, not a few also, implicated in crimes or given to secular military service, unworthy have come to the honor of the supreme and sacred order. Concerning whose cause and removal indeed it would have been fitting to establish, but lest too great a perturbation of the church might arise, the past having been omitted, we believe it necessary to be inserted henceforth who are not to be promoted to the priesthood according to the rules of the canons: that is, those who have been detected in any crime, those who have been sprinkled with the mark of infamy, those who have confessed to have committed some crimes through public penance, those who have lapsed into heresy, those who are known to have been baptized or rebaptized in heresy, those who have cut themselves or are known naturally to have something less by defect of limbs or by cutting off, those who have obtained the union of a second wife or have frequented numerous marriages, those who have married a widow or a woman left by her husband, or have been of corrupted husbands, those who have had concubines or fornicatresses, those who are subject to servile condition, those who are unknown, those who are neophytes or laypersons, those who are given to secular military service, those who are bound by the obligations of the curia, those who are unlettered, those who have not yet reached thirty years of age, those who have not approached through ecclesiastical grades, those who seek honor by ambition, those who strive to obtain honor by gifts, those who are chosen into the priesthood by predecessors. But neither shall he be a priest henceforth whom neither the clerics nor the people of his own city have chosen, nor the authority of the metropolitan nor the assent of the suffragan priests has sought out. Therefore whoever henceforth, sought for the order of priesthood and examined concerning those things which have been said, shall have been detected in none of these, and having been examined, shall be found to be of probable life and doctrine, then according to synodal or decretal constitutions, with the will of all clerics or citizens, he shall be consecrated by all the suffragan bishops or at least by three, on the Lord's day, into the priesthood, with the others who are absent consenting by their letters, and especially by the authority or presence of him who is constituted in the metropolis. Moreover a suffragan bishop is to be consecrated there where the metropolitan shall have chosen; a metropolitan however only in the city of the metropolis, with the suffragans meeting there. If indeed anyone henceforth, unworthy, should contend to aspire to the grade of priesthood contrary to the aforesaid prohibited things of the canons, let him together with his ordainers lie under the peril of the honor acquired.
¶52
CANON XIX
In veteri lege ab anno vigesimo et quinto levitae in tabernaculo servire mandantur, cuius auctoritatem in canonibus et sancti patres secuti sunt. Nos et divinae legis et conciliorum praecepti immemores infantes et pueros levitas facimus ante legitimam aetatem, ante experientiam vitae. Ideoque ne ulterius fiat, a nobis et divinae legis et canonum admonemur sententiis, sed viginti quinque annorum aetatis levitae consecrentur et triginta presbyteri ordinentur, ita ut secundum praeceptum probentur primum, et sic ministrent nullum crimen habentes.
In the old law, from the age of twenty-five years, the Levites are commanded to serve in the tabernacle, whose authority both the canons and the holy fathers have followed. We, forgetful of the precept of the divine law and of the councils, make infants and boys Levites before the legitimate age, before the experience of life. Therefore, lest it be done further, we are admonished by the sentences of the divine law and of the canons, but let Levites be consecrated at the age of twenty-five years, and presbyters be ordained at thirty, so that according to the precept they first be proved, and thus minister, having no crime.
¶53
CANON XX
Quicumque in sacerdotio dei positi sunt, irrepraehensibiles esse debent Paulo apostolo attestante: Oportet episcopum irrepraehensibilem esse. Inoffensos igitur et immaculatos decet dei exsistere sacerdotes, nec ullius eos fornicationis contagio pollui, sed caste viventes mundos semetipsos celebrandis exhibeant sacramentis. Abstineamus ergo nos ab omni opere malo, et ab omni inquinamento carnis liberi maneamus, ut mundi corpore, purgati mente possimus ad sacrificium Christi digni accedere et deum pro delictis omnium deprecari.
Whoever are placed in the priesthood of God ought to be irreprehensible, the apostle Paul testifying: "A bishop ought to be irreprehensible." Therefore it befits the priests of God to be inoffensive and immaculate, nor let any contagion of fornication pollute them, but living chastely, let them keep themselves clean to be exhibited for the sacraments to be celebrated. Let us therefore abstain from every evil work, and let us remain free from every stain of the flesh, so that, clean in body, purified in mind, we may be able to approach worthily to the sacrifice of Christ and to pray to God for the offenses of all.
¶54
CANON XXI
Quamvis conscientiam puram apud deum nos habere oporteat, tamen et apud homines famam optimam custodire convenit, ut iuxta praeceptum apostolicum non tantum coram deo, sed etiam coram hominibus vitae sanctae testimonium habeamus. Quidam enim hucusque sacerdotum non modicum scandalum creaverunt, dum in conversatione vitae non bonae famae exsistunt. Ut igitur excludatur deinceps omnis nefanda suspicionis occasio et, ne detur ultra saecularibus obtrectandi locus, oportet episcopos testimonium probabilium personarum conversationis et vitae in conclavi suo habere, ut deo placeant per conversationem bonam et ecclesiae per optimam famam.
Although we ought to have a pure conscience before God, yet it is fitting to keep the best fame among men, so that according to the apostolic precept we have testimony of holy life not only before God but also before men. For certain priests until now have created not a little scandal, while in the conversation of life they are not of good reputation. Therefore, that all nefarious occasion of suspicion may be excluded henceforth, and that no place be given to secular persons for detraction, it is fitting that bishops have testimony of probable persons concerning their conversation and life in their own conclave, that they may please God by good conversation and the church by the best fame.
¶55
CANON XXII
Non aliter placuit, ut quemadmodum antistites, ita presbyteri atque levitae, quos forte infirmitas aut aetatis gravitas in conclavi manere non sinit, ut in cellulis suis testes vitae habeant vitamque suam sicut nomine ita et meritis teneant.
It has pleased not otherwise, that just as the prelates, so the presbyters and levites, whom perhaps infirmity or the gravity of age does not permit to remain in the conclave, that they have witnesses of life in their own cells, and that they hold their life as in name so also in merits.
¶56
*DE CONVERSATIONE CLERICORUM*
CONCERNING THE CONVERSATION OF CLERICS
CANON XXIII
Prona est omnis aetas ab adolescentia in malum, nihil enim incertius quam vita adolescentium. Ob hoc constituendum oportuit, ut, si qui in clero pueri aut adolescentes exsistunt, omnes in una conclavi atrii commorentur, ut lubricae aetatis annos non in luxuria, sed in disciplinis ecclesiasticis agant deputati probatissimo seniori, quem et magistrum doctrinae et testem vitae habeant. Quod si aliqui ex his pupilli exsistunt, sacerdotali tutela foveantur, ut et vita eorum a criminibus intacta sit et res ab iniuria improborum. Qui autem his praeceptis resultaverint, monasteriis deputentur, ut vagantes animi et superbi severiori regula distringantur.
Every age is prone from adolescence to evil, for nothing is more uncertain than the life of adolescents. On account of this it was fitting to be established, that if any boys or adolescents exist in the clergy, all abide in one hall of the atrium, so that the years of a slippery age they may spend not in luxury but in ecclesiastical disciplines, deputed to a most approved senior, whom they may have both as a teacher of doctrine and a witness of life. But if any among them are orphans, let them be fostered by priestly guardianship, so that both their life may be untouched by crimes and their goods from the injury of the wicked. But those who shall have resisted these precepts, let them be assigned to monasteries, so that wandering and proud spirits may be constrained by a more severe rule.
¶57
CANON XXIV
Ignorantia mater cunctorum errorum, maxime in sacerdotibus dei vitanda est, qui docendi officium in populis susceperunt. Sacerdotes enim legere sanctas scripturas frequenter admonet Paulus apostolus dicens ad Timotheum: Intende lectioni, exhortationi. Doctores semper manere in his se sciant. Igitur sacerdotes scripturas sanctas et canones meditentur, ut omne opus eorum in praedicatione et doctrina consistat atque aedificent cunctos tam fidei scientia quam operum disciplina.
Ignorance, the mother of all errors, is especially to be avoided in the priests of God, who have received the office of teaching among the peoples. For the apostle Paul admonishes priests to read the holy scriptures frequently, saying to Timothy: "Attend to reading, to exhortation." Let them know that they are always to remain in these things. Therefore let priests meditate on the holy scriptures and canons, that all their work may consist in preaching and doctrine, and that they may edify all both by the knowledge of faith and by the discipline of works.
¶58
CANON XXV
Quando presbyteri in parrochiis ordinantur, libellum officialem a sacerdote suo accipiant, ut ad ecclesias sibi deputatas instructi succedant, ne per ignorantiam etiam in ipsis divinis sacramentis offendant, ut, quando ad laetanias vel ad concilium venerint, rationem episcopo suo reddant, qualiter susceptum officium celebrent vel baptizent.
When presbyters are ordained in the parishes, let them receive an official book from their own priest, that instructed they may succeed to the churches deputed to them, lest through ignorance they even offend in the divine sacraments themselves, so that, when they come to rogations or to the council, they may give an account to their bishop in what manner they celebrate the received office or baptize.
¶59
CANON XXVI
Quando presbyteri aut diaconi per parrochias constituuntur, oportet eos professionem episcopo suo facere, ut caste et pure vivant sub dei timore, ut dum eos tali professione obligaverit, sanctam disciplinam retineat.
When presbyters or deacons are established throughout parishes, it is fitting that they make a profession to their own bishop, that they live chastely and purely under the fear of God, so that when he has bound them by such a profession, he may retain holy discipline.
¶60
CANON XXVII
Episcopus, presbyter aut diaconus, si a gradu suo iniuste deiectus in secunda synodo innocens reperiatur, non potest esse, quod fuerat, nisi gradus amissos recipiat coram altario de manu episcoporum, si episcopus est, orarium, anulum et baculum; si presbyter, orarium et planetam; si diaconus, orarium et albam; si subdiaconus, patenam et calicem; sic et reliqui gradus ea in reparationem sui recipiant, quae, cum ordinarentur, perceperant.
If a bishop, presbyter, or deacon, having been unjustly cast down from his grade, is found innocent in a second synod, he cannot be what he was, unless he receives back the lost grades before the altar from the hand of bishops: if he is a bishop, the orarium, ring, and staff; if a presbyter, the orarium and planet; if a deacon, the orarium and alb; if a subdeacon, the paten and chalice. Thus also the remaining grades receive those things for their restoration which, when they were ordained, they had received.
¶61
CANON XXVIII
Si episcopus quis aut presbyter sive diaconus vel quilibet ex ordine clericorum magos aut aruspices aut ariolos aut certe augures vel sortilegos vel eos, qui profitentur artem aliquam, aut aliquis eorum similia exercentes consuluisse fuerit depraehensus, ab honore dignitatis suae depositus monasterii curam excipiat ibique perpetua poenitentia deditus scelus admissum sacrilegii solvat.
If any bishop or presbyter or deacon or any from the order of clerics shall have been discovered to have consulted magicians, soothsayers, diviners, augurs, or sorcerers, or those who profess any art, or any of them practicing similar things, let him, deposed from the honor of his dignity, receive the care of a monastery, and there, given to perpetual penance, let him atone for the crime of sacrilege committed.
¶62
CANON XXIX
Confinitimi hostium sacerdotes praeter eos, qui a regia potestate licentiam acceperunt, a quolibet mandatum a gente extraneum occulte accipere vel dirigere non praesumant. Qui autem depraehenditur aut convincitur denuntiatus principi apud concilium condigna animadversione multabitur.
Priests bordering upon enemies, beyond those who have received license from the royal power, let them not presume to receive or send secretly any mandate from a foreign people. Whoever moreover is detected or convicted, let him be denounced to the prince and be mulcted with condign animadversion before the council.
¶63
CANON XXX
Saepe principes contra quoslibet maiestatis obnoxios sacerdotibus negotia sua committunt. Et quia sacerdotes a Christo ad ministerium salutis electi sunt, ibi consentiant regibus fieri iudices, ubi iureiurando supplicii indulgentia promittitur, non ubi discriminis sententia praeparatur. Si quis ergo sacerdotum contra hoc commune consultum discussor in alienis periculis exstiterit, sit reus effusi sanguinis apud Christum et apud ecclesiam perdat proprium gradum.
Often princes entrust their own matters to priests concerning those liable to charges of majesty. And because priests have been chosen by Christ for the ministry of salvation, let them consent to be made judges by kings there where the indulgence of punishment is promised by oath, not where the sentence of peril is prepared. If therefore any priest against this common counsel shall be a discerner in the perils of others, let him be guilty of shed blood before Christ, and before the church let him lose his own grade.
¶64
CANON XXXI
Episcopi in protegendis populis ac defendendis impositam a deo sibi curam non ambigant, ideoque dum conspiciunt iudices ac potentes pauperum oppressores exsistere, prius eos sacerdotali admonitione redarguant, et si contempserint emendari, eorum insolentiam regiis auribus intiment, ut, quos sacerdotalis admonitio non inflectit ad iustitiam, regalis potestas ab improbitate coerceat. Si quis autem episcoporum id neglexerit, concilio reus erit.
Bishops not doubt the care imposed upon themselves by God in protecting and defending the peoples. Therefore while they see judges and powerful persons to be oppressors of the poor, let them first rebuke them by priestly admonition, and if they have despised to be amended, let them make known their insolence to the royal ears, so that those whom priestly admonition does not bend to justice, the royal power may coerce from wickedness. But if any bishop shall have neglected this, let him be guilty before the council.
¶65
CANON XXXII
Avaritia radix est cunctorum malorum, cuius sitis etiam sacerdotum mentes obtinet. Multi enim fidelium in amore Christi et martyrum in parrochiis episcoporum basilicas construunt, oblationes sanctas tribuunt, sacerdotes haec auferunt atque in usus suos convertunt. Inde est, quod cultores sacrorum deficiunt, dum stipendia sua perdunt, inde labentium basilicarum ruinae non reparantur, quia avaritia sacerdotali omnia auferuntur. Pro qua re constitutum est a praesenti concilio episcopos ita dioceses suas regere, ut nihil ex earum iure praesumant auferre, sed iuxta priorum auctoritatem conciliorum tam de oblationibus quam de tributis ac frugibus tertiam consequantur. Quod si amplius quippiam ab eis praesumptum exstiterit, per concilium restauretur appellantibus aut ipsis conditoribus aut certe propinquis eorum, si iam illi a saeculo discesserunt. Noverint autem conditores basilicarum in rebus, quas eisdem ecclesiis conferunt, nullam potestatem habere, sed iuxta canonum instituta sicut ecclesiam ita et dos eius ad ordinationem episcopi pertineat.
Avarice is the root of all evils, whose thirst also seizes the minds of priests. For many of the faithful in the love of Christ and the martyrs build basilicas in the parishes of bishops, bestow holy oblations, priests take these away and convert them into their own uses. Hence it is that the cultivators of sacred things fail, while they lose their stipends; hence the ruins of falling basilicas are not repaired, because by priestly avarice all things are taken away. For which matter it has been established by the present council that bishops so rule their dioceses that they presume to take away nothing from their right, but according to the authority of prior councils, let them obtain a third both of oblations and of tributes and of fruits. But if anything further shall have been presumed by them, let it be restored through the council, with either the founders themselves or certainly their relatives, if they have already departed from the world, appealing. Moreover let the founders of basilicas know that in the goods which they confer upon the same churches they have no power, but according to the institutes of the canons, just as the church, so also its dowry pertains to the disposition of the bishop.
¶66
CANON XXXIII
Quicumque episcopus alterius episcopi diocesim per triginta annos sine aliqua interpellatione possederit, quia secundum ius legis eius videtur esse diocesis, admittenda non est contra eum actio reposcendi, sed hoc intra unam parrochiam, extra vero nullo modo, ne dum diocesis defenditur, provinciarum termini confundantur.
Whoever bishop has possessed the diocese of another bishop for thirty years without any challenge, because according to the right of the law it seems to be his diocese, an action of reclaiming is not to be admitted against him, but this within one parish, outside truly by no means, lest while the diocese is defended, the boundaries of provinces be confused.
¶67
CANON XXXIV
Sicut diocesim alienam trigennalis possessio tollit, ita territorium conventum non admittit. Ideoque basilicae, quae novae conditae fuerint, ad eum procul dubio episcopum pertinebunt, cuius conventus esse constiterit.
Just as a thirty-year possession takes away a diocese of another, so it does not admit the conventus of territory. Therefore basilicas which have been newly built will without doubt pertain to that bishop whose conventus it shall have been established to be.
¶68
CANON XXXV
Ut episcopum per cunctas dioceses parrochiasque suas per singulos annos ire oporteat.
That a bishop ought to go through all his dioceses and parishes every single year.
¶69
Episcopum per cunctas dioceses parrochiasque suas per singulos annos ire oportet, ut exquirat, quid unaquaeque basilica in reparationem sui indiget. Quod si ipse aut languore detentus aut aliis occupationibus implicatus id explere nequiverit, presbyteros probabiles aut diacones mittat, qui et reditus basilicarum et reparationes et ministrantium vitam inquirant.
It is fitting that a bishop go through all his dioceses and parishes every single year, that he may seek out what each basilica needs for its repair. But if either hindered by illness or implicated by other occupations he cannot accomplish this, let him send approved presbyters or deacons, who may inquire into the incomes of the basilicas and their repairs and the life of those ministering.
¶70
CANON XXXVI
Quicumque episcopi suffragio cuiuslibet aliquid de ecclesiasticis utilitatibus providerint et pro eo quodcumque commodum in remunerationem promiserint, promissi solutionem eos exsolvere oportebit, ita ut id ad concilium comprovinciale deferatur, ut eorum coniventia confirmetur, quia, sicut Paulus ait: Dignus est operarius mercede sua.
Whoever bishops by the aid of anyone shall have provided something for ecclesiastical utilities, and on account of that shall have promised any advantage in remuneration, it is fitting that they pay the solution of the promise, so that it be referred to the provincial council, that it may be confirmed by their consent, because, as Paul says: "The laborer is worthy of his reward."
¶71
CANON XXXVII
Praebendum est a sacerdotibus vitae solacium indigentibus et maxime his, quibus restituenda vicissitudo est.
That the solace of life ought to be provided by priests to the needy, and especially to those to whom a return is to be rendered.
¶72
Quicumque ergo fidelium de facultatibus suis ecclesiae aliquid devotione propria contulerint, si forte ipsi aut filii eorum redacti fuerint ad inopiam, ab eadem ecclesia suffragium vitae pro temporis usu percipiant. Si enim clericis vel monachis seu peregrinis aut quamlibet necessitatem sustinentibus pro solo religionis intuitu in usum res ecclesiasticae largiuntur, quanto magis consulendum est, quibus retributio iusta debetur?
Therefore, whoever of the faithful shall have conferred anything from their goods to the church by their own devotion, if by chance they themselves or their sons have been reduced to poverty, from the same church let them receive the support of life for the use of the time. For if to clerics or monks or pilgrims or those sustaining any necessity, for the sole consideration of religion, ecclesiastical goods are bestowed for use, how much more are those to be consulted to whom a just retribution is owed?
¶73
CANON XXXVIII
Nonnulli diacones in tantam erumpunt superbiam, ut sese presbyteris anteponant atque in primo choro ipsi priores stare praesumant, presbyteris in secundo choro constitutis; ergo ut sublimiores se presbyteros agnoscant, tam hi quam illi in utroque choro consistant.
Not a few deacons break out into such great pride that they prefer themselves to presbyters and presume to stand themselves first in the first choir, with the presbyters established in the second choir, therefore that they may acknowledge themselves to be lower than presbyters, let both these and those stand in each choir.
¶74
CANON XXXIX
Si oraria duo nec episcopo quidem induere licet nec presbytero, utique multo minus diacono, qui minister eorum est. Unum igitur orarium oportet levitam gestare in sinistro humero, propter quod orat, id est praedicat, dexteram autem partem oportet habere liberam, ut expeditus ad ministerium sacerdotale discurrat. Caveant igitur amodo levitae geminum uti orarium, sed unum tantum et purum nec ullis coloribus aut auro ornatum.
If it is not lawful for bishops nor for presbyters to be vested with two oraria, how much more for deacons, who are their ministers? Therefore it is fitting that a levite wear one orarium on the left shoulder, on account of which he prays, that is, preaches, but it is fitting that he have the right part free, that he may run about unencumbered for the priestly ministry. Therefore let levites henceforth beware of using a double orarium, but only one and plain, nor adorned with any colors or gold.
¶75
CANON XL
Ut omnes clerici vel lectores sicut levitae et sacerdotes detonso superius toto capite, inferius solam circuli coronam relinquant.
That all clerics or readers, just as levites and priests, with the whole head shaved above, leave only a circular crown below.
¶76
Omnes clerici vel lectores sicut levitae et sacerdotes detonso superius toto capite, inferius solam circuli coronam relinquant, non sicut hucusque in Galliciae partibus facere lectores videntur, qui prolixis ut laici comis in solo capitis apice modicum circulum tondunt; ritus enim iste in Spaniis hucusque hereticorum fuit. Unde oportet, ut pro amputando ab ecclesiis scandalo hoc signum dedecoris auferatur et una sit tonsura vel habitus sicut totius Spaniae est usus. Qui autem hoc non custodierit, fidei catholicae reus erit.
All clerics or readers, just as levites and priests, with the whole head shaved above, let them leave only a circular crown below, not as until now readers are seen to do in the parts of Gallaecia, who, like laypersons with long hair, shave a small circle only on the top of the head. For this rite in Spain until now has been of heretics. Whence it is fitting that for cutting away scandal from the churches, this sign of disgrace be removed, and let the tonsure or habit be one, as is the use of all Spain. Whoever moreover shall not have kept this, let him be guilty of the catholic faith.
¶77
CANON XLI
Ut extraneae feminae cum clericis non habitent.
That extraneous women not dwell with clerics.
¶78
Cum clericis extraneae feminae nullatenus habitent, nisi tantum mater et soror, filia, amita, in quibus personis nihil sceleris aestimari foedus naturae permittit. Id enim et constitutio antiquorum patrum decrevit.
With clerics let extraneous women by no means dwell, except only mother and sister, daughter, aunt, in which persons the bond of nature permits nothing of crime to be suspected. For this also the constitution of the ancient fathers decreed.
¶79
CANON XLII
De clericis, qui non habentes legitimum coniugium extranearum mulierum vel ancillarum suarum interdicta sibi consortia appetunt.
Concerning clerics who, not having legitimate marriage, seek forbidden associations of extraneous women or of their own female slaves.
¶80
Ideo, quae cum clericis taliter coniunctae sunt, ab episcopo auferantur et venundentur, illis pro tempore religatis ad poenitentiam, quos sua libidine infecerunt.
Therefore those women who have been so joined to clerics, let them be taken away by the bishop and sold, with those being bound for penance for a time whom they have infected with their lust.
¶81
CANON XLIII
De clericis, qui sine consultu episcopi sui uxores duxerunt.
Concerning clerics who have taken wives without the counsel of their own bishop.
¶82
Clerici, qui sine consultu episcopi sui uxores duxerint aut viduam vel repudiatam vel meretricem in coniugium acceperint, separari eos a proprio episcopo oportebit.
Clerics who without the counsel of their own bishop have taken wives, or have received a widow or a divorced woman or a prostitute into marriage, it will be fitting that they be separated by their own bishop.
¶83
CANON XLIV
De clericis, qui in quacumque seditione arma volentes sumpserint.
Concerning clerics who willingly have taken up arms in any sedition.
¶84
Clerici in quacumque seditione arma volentes sumpserint, reperti amisso ordinis sui gradu in monasterium poenitentiae contradantur.
Clerics who willingly have taken up arms in any sedition, having been found, let them, having lost the grade of their order, be handed over to a monastery of penance.
¶85
CANON XLV
Si quis clericus in demoliendis sepulchris fuerit depraehensus.
If any cleric shall have been discovered in demolishing tombs.
¶86
Si quis clericus in demoliendis sepulchris fuerit depraehensus, quia facinus hoc pro sacrilegio legibus publicis sanguine vindicatur, oportet canonice in tali scelere proditum a clericatus ordine submoveri et poenitentiae triennio deputari.
If any cleric shall have been discovered in demolishing tombs, because this crime is punished by blood by the public laws on account of sacrilege, it is fitting that, having been betrayed in such a crime, he be removed from the order of clerics and be assigned to penance for three years.
¶87
CANON XLVI
De praecepto domini nostri Sisenandi regis.
By the command of our lord Sisenand the king.
¶88
Praecipiente domino nostro atque excellentissimo Sisenando rege id constituit sanctum concilium, ut omnes ingenui clerici pro officio religionis ab omni publica indictione atque labore habeant immunes, ut liberi deo serviant nullaque praedicti necessitate ab ecclesiasticis officiis retrahantur.
By the command of our lord and most excellent King Sisenand, the holy council established this, that all free-born clerics for the sake of the office of religion be immune from all public indiction and labor, that free they may serve God and by no such necessity be withdrawn from ecclesiastical offices.
¶89
CANON XLVII
De eligendis oeconomis.
Concerning choosing stewards.
¶90
Eos, quos oeconomos Graeci vocant, hoc est, qui vice episcoporum res ecclesiasticas tractant, sicut sancta synodus Chalcedonensis instituit, omnes episcopos de proprio clero ad regendas ecclesias habere oportet. Qui autem deinceps contempserit, obnoxius eiusdem magni concilii erit.
That all bishops ought to have those whom the Greeks call stewards, that is, who handle ecclesiastical goods in place of bishops, from their own clergy for governing churches, just as the holy synod of Chalcedon established. Whoever moreover henceforth shall have despised this, let him be liable to that great council.
¶91
CANON XLVIII
De professione monachi, ut, quicquid fuerit allegatum, tenebit.
Concerning the profession of a monk, that whatever shall have been promised, he shall hold.
¶92
Monachum aut paterna devotio aut propria professio facit. Quicquid horum fuerit, allegatum tenebit. Proinde his ad mundum reverti intercludimus aditum et omnes ad saeculum interdicimus regressus.
Either paternal devotion or his own profession makes a monk. Whichever of these shall have been promised, he shall hold. Therefore we close the entrance to these to return to the world, and we forbid all regresses to the world.
¶93
CANON XLIX
De clericis, qui monachorum praepositum appetunt.
Concerning clerics who seek the position of monks.
¶94
Clerici, qui monachorum praepositum appetunt, quia meliorem vitam sequi cupiunt, liberos eis ab episcopo in monasteriis largiri oportet ingressus, nec interdici propositum eorum, qui ad contemplationis desiderium transire nituntur.
Clerics who seek the position of monks, because they desire to follow a better life, it is fitting that free entrances into monasteries be granted to them by the bishop, nor let the purpose of those who strive to pass over to the desire of contemplation be forbidden.
¶95
CANON L
De monachis, qui egredientes a monasterio non solum ad saeculum revertuntur, sed etiam uxores accipiunt.
Concerning monks who, departing from a monastery, not only return to the world, but even take wives.
¶96
Nuntiatum est praesenti concilio, quod monachi episcopali imperio servili opere mancipentur et iura monasteriorum contra constituta canonum illicita praesumptione usurpentur, ita ut pene ex coenobio possessio fiat atque illustris portio Christi ad ignominiam servitutemque perveniat. Quapropter monemus eos, qui ecclesiis praesunt, ut ultra talia non praesumant, sed hoc tantum sibi in monasterio vindicent sacerdotes, quod praecipiunt canones, id est, monachos ad conversationem sanctam praemonere, abbates aliaque officia instituere atque extra regulam facta corrigere. Quod si aliquid in monachis canonibus interdictum praesumpserint aut usurpare quippiam de monasterii rebus temptaverint, non deerit ab illis sententia excommunicationis, qui se deinceps nequaquam sustulerint ab illicitis.
It has been announced to the present council that monks are subjected by episcopal command to servile work, and the rights of monasteries contrary to the constitutions of the canons are usurped by illicit presumption, so that almost from a coenobium a possession is made, and the illustrious portion of Christ comes to ignominy and servitude. Wherefore we warn those who preside over churches that they no longer presume such things, but let them claim for themselves in a monastery only that which the canons command, that is, to admonish monks concerning holy conversation, to appoint abbots and other offices, and to correct things done beyond the rule. But if they shall have presumed anything forbidden by the canons in monks, or shall have attempted to usurp anything from the goods of a monastery, there will not be lacking to them the sentence of excommunication, who they henceforth shall not have removed themselves from illicit things.
¶97
CANON LI
De nonnullis monachis egredientibus a monasteriis.
Concerning not a few monks departing from monasteries.
¶98
Nonnulli monachorum egredientes a monasterio non solum ad saeculum revertuntur, sed etiam uxores accipiunt. Igitur revocati in eodem monasterio, a quo exierant, poenitentiae deputentur ibique defleant crimina sua, unde discesserunt.
Not a few monks, departing from a monastery, not only return to the world, but also take wives. Therefore, recalled to the same monastery from which they had departed, let them be assigned to penance and there let them bewail their crimes, from which they departed.
¶99
CANON LII
De religiosis propriae regionis, qui nec inter clericos nec inter monachos habentur.
Concerning religious persons of their own region, who are held neither among clerics nor among monks.
¶100
Religiosi propriae regionis, qui nec inter clericos nec inter monachos habentur, sive hi, qui per diversa loca vagi feruntur, ab episcopis, in quorum conventu commorari noscuntur, licentia eorum coerceatur, ut aut in clero aut in monasterio deputentur praeter eos, qui ab episcopo suo aut propter languorem fuerint absoluti.
Religious persons of their own region, who are held neither among clerics nor among monks, whether those who are carried about wandering through diverse places, let them be coerced by the license of the bishops in whose conventus they are known to dwell, so that they be assigned either to the clergy or to a monastery, excepting those who have been absolved by their own bishop either on account of illness.
¶101
CANON LIII
De eis, qui in discrimine constituti poenitentiam accipiunt.
Concerning those who, placed in danger, receive penance.
¶102
Hi, qui in discrimine constituti poenitentiam accipiunt, nulla manifesta scelera confitentes, sed tantum se peccatores esse praedicantes, huiusmodi, si revaluerint, possunt etiam per morum probitatem ad gradus ecclesiasticos pervenire. Qui vero ita poenitentiam accipiunt, ut aliquod mortale peccatum perpetrasse publice fateantur, ad clerum vel honores ecclesiasticos pervenire nullatenus poterunt, quia se confessione propria notaverunt.
Those who, placed in danger, receive penance, confessing no manifest crimes, but only proclaiming themselves to be sinners, of this kind, if they shall have recovered, can even through the probity of morals attain to ecclesiastical grades. Those who truly receive penance in such a way that they publicly confess that they have perpetrated some mortal sin, they can by no means attain to the clergy or ecclesiastical honors, because they have denounced themselves by their own confession.
¶103
CANON LIV
De saecularibus, qui accipientes poenitentiam se totonderant et rursus praevaricantes laici effecti sunt.
Concerning secular persons who, receiving penance, shaved themselves and again, transgressing, became laypersons.
¶104
Quicumque ex saecularibus accipientes poenitentiam totonderunt se et rursus praevaricantes laici effecti sunt, compraehensi ab episcopo suo ad poenitentiam, ex qua recesserunt, revocentur. Quod si aliqui per potentiam irrevocabiles sunt nec admoniti revertuntur, vere ut apostatae coram ecclesia anathematis sententia condemnentur. Non aliter et hi, qui detonsi a parentibus fuerint aut sponte sua amissis parentibus seipsos religioni devoverunt et postea habitum saecularem sumpserunt, et idem a sacerdote compraehensi ad cultum religionis acta prius poenitentia revocentur. Quod si reverti non possunt, velut a potestate anathematis sententia subiciantur. Quae forma servabitur etiam in viduis virginibusque sacris ac poenitentibus feminis, quae sanctimonialem habitum induerunt et postea aut vestem mutaverunt aut ad nuptias transierunt.
Whoever from secular persons, receiving penance, shaved themselves and again, transgressing, became laypersons, having been apprehended by their bishop, let them be recalled to the penance from which they departed. But if any are irrevocable through power nor, having been admonished, return, let them truly as apostates be condemned before the church with the sentence of anathema. Not otherwise also those who have been shaved by parents or, with parents lost, of their own will have devoted themselves to religion and afterward have assumed the secular habit, and the same, having been apprehended by a priest, let them be recalled to the cult of religion, penance having first been performed. But if they cannot return, let them be subjected as if from the power of the sentence of anathema. Which form shall be preserved also in widows and sacred virgins and penitent women, who have put on the religious habit and afterward either changed their clothing or passed over to marriage.
¶105
CANON LV
Quod duo sint genera viduarum, saeculares et sanctimoniales.
That there are two kinds of widows: secular and religious.
¶106
Duo sunt genera viduarum, saeculares et sanctimoniales. Saeculares viduae sunt, quae adhuc disponentes nubere laicalem habitum non deposuerunt; sanctimoniales sunt, quae iam mutato habitu saeculari sub religioso cultu in conspectu sacerdotis vel ecclesiae apparuerunt. Hae, si ad nuptias transierint, iuxta apostolum non sine damnatione erunt, quia se primum deo voventes postea castitatis propositum abiecerunt.
There are two kinds of widows: secular and religious. Secular widows are those who, intending still to marry, have not laid aside the lay habit. Religious widows are those who, the secular habit having already been changed, have appeared under religious cult in the sight of a priest or of the church. These, if they shall have passed over to marriage, according to the apostle will not be without damnation, because they, having first vowed themselves to God, afterward cast away the purpose of chastity.
¶107
CANON LVI
De Iudaeis.
Concerning Jews.
¶108
De Iudaeis autem praecepit sancta synodus nemini deinceps ad credendum vim inferre. Cui enim vult deus miseretur, et quem vult, indurat. Non enim tales inviti salvandi sunt, sed volentes, ut integra sit forma iustitiae. Sicut enim homo propria arbitrii voluntate serpenti oboediens periit, sic vocante se gratia dei propriae mentis conversione homo quisque credendo salvatur. Ergo non vi, sed libera arbitrii facultate, ut convertantur, suadendi sunt, non potius impellendi. Qui autem iam pridem ad christianitatem coacti sunt, sicut factum est temporibus religiosissimi principis Sisebuti, quia iam constat eos sacramentis divinis associatos et baptismi gratiam suscepisse et chrismate unctos esse et corporis domini et sanguinis exstitisse participes, oportet, ut fidem, etiam quam vi vel necessitate susceperunt, tenere cogantur, ne nomen domini blasphemetur et fides, quam susceperunt, vilis ac contemptibilis habeatur.
Concerning Jews moreover the holy synod has commanded that henceforth no one should compel them to believe. For he has mercy on whom He wishes, and He hardens whom He wishes. For such persons are not to be saved unwillingly, but willing, that the form of justice may be entire. For just as man perished by his own will obeying the serpent, so by the grace of God calling him, each person is saved by the conversion of his own mind believing. Therefore they are to be persuaded, not rather forced, that they may be converted, not by force but by the faculty of free will. But those who have long ago been forced to Christianity, as was done in the times of the most religious prince Sisebut, because it is already established that they have been associated with the divine sacraments and have received the grace of baptism and have been anointed with chrism and have been partakers of the body and blood of the Lord, it is fitting that they be compelled to hold the faith, which they received even by force or necessity, lest the name of the Lord be blasphemed and the faith which they received be held vile and contemptible.
¶109
CANON LVII
De cupiditate episcopi, presbyteri, diaconi sive clericorum, qui contra fidem christianam a Iudaeis suffragium praestiterit.
Concerning the cupidity of a bishop, presbyter, deacon, or clerics, who against the Christian faith shall have given assistance to Jews.
¶110
Tanta est quorumdam cupiditas, ut quidam eam appetentes, iuxta quod ait apostolus, etiam a fide erraverint. Multi quippe hucusque ex sacerdotibus atque laicis accipientes a Iudaeis munera, perfidiam eorum patrocinio suo foveant, qui non immerito ex corpore antichristi esse noscuntur, quia contra Christum faciunt. Quicumque igitur deinceps episcopus sive clericus sive saecularis illis contra fidem christianam suffragium vel munere vel favore praestiterit, vere ut profanus et sacrilegus anathema effectus ab ecclesia catholica et regno dei efficiatur extraneus, quia dignus est, ut a corpore Christi separetur, qui inimicis Christi patronus efficitur.
So great is the cupidity of certain persons, that some, seeking it, according to what the apostle says, have even erred from the faith. For many until now from priests and laypersons, receiving gifts from Jews, foster their perfidy by their patronage, who are not undeservedly known to be of the body of antichrist, because they act against Christ. Whoever therefore henceforth, bishop or cleric or secular, shall have given them assistance against the Christian faith, either by gift or by favor, let him, truly as profane and sacrilegious, having been made an anathema, be made an alien from the catholic church and from the kingdom of God, because he is worthy that he be separated from the body of Christ who is made a patron of the enemies of Christ.
¶111
CANON LVIII
De his, qui ex Iudaeis dudum ad christianam fidem promoti sunt.
Concerning those who from Jews were long ago promoted to the Christian faith.
¶112
Plerique, qui ex Iudaeis dudum ad christianam fidem promoti sunt, nunc blasphemantes Christo non solum iudaicos ritus perpetrasse noscuntur, sed etiam abominandas circumcisiones exercere praesumpserunt, de quibus consultu piissimi ac religiosissimi domini nostri Sisenandi regis hoc sanctum decrevit concilium, ut huiusmodi transgressores pontificali auctoritate correcti ad cultum christiani dogmatis revocentur, ut, quos voluntas propria non emendat, animadversio sacerdotalis coerceat. Eos autem, quos circumciderunt, si filii eorum sunt, a parentum consortio separentur; si servi, pro iniuria corporis sui libertati tradantur.
Very many who from Jews were long ago promoted to the Christian faith, now blaspheming Christ, are known not only to have perpetrated Jewish rites, but also have presumed to practice abominable circumcisions. Concerning whom, by the counsel of our most pious and most religious lord King Sisenand, this holy council has decreed, that transgressors of this kind, corrected by pontifical authority, be recalled to the cult of Christian dogma, so that those whom their own will does not amend, priestly animadversion may coerce. Moreover those whom they have circumcised, if they are their sons, let them be separated from the company of their parents; if they are servants, for the injury of their body let them be handed over to freedom.
¶113
CANON LIX
De Iudaeorum filiis vel filiabus, ne parentum ultra involvantur erroribus.
Concerning the sons or daughters of Jews, lest they be further involved in the errors of their parents.
¶114
Iudaeorum filios vel filias, ne parentum ultra involvantur erroribus, ab eorum consortio separari decernimus deputatos aut monasteriis aut christianis viris ac mulieribus deum timentibus, ut sub eorum conversatione cultum fidei discant atque in melius instituti tam in moribus quam in fide proficiant.
We decree that the sons or daughters of Jews, lest they be further involved in the errors of their parents, be separated from their company, being deputed either to monasteries or to Christian men and women fearing God, that under their conversation they may learn the cult of faith and, having been better instructed, may make progress both in morals and in faith.
¶115
CANON LX
De Iudaeis baptizatis, si postea praevaricaverint in Christo.
Concerning baptized Jews, if afterward they transgress in Christ.
¶116
Iudaei baptizati, si postea praevaricantes in Christum qualibet poena damnati exstiterint, a rebus eorum fideles filios excludi non oportebit, quia scriptum est: Filius non portabit iniquitatem patris.
Baptized Jews, if afterward, transgressing in Christ, they shall have been condemned by any penalty, it will not be fitting that faithful sons be excluded from their goods, because it is written: "The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father."
¶117
CANON LXI
De malorum consortia, quae etiam bonos corrumpunt.
Concerning the associations of the wicked, which corrupt even the good.
¶118
Saepe malorum consortia etiam bonos corrumpunt, quanto magis eos, qui ad vitia proni sunt? Nulla igitur ultra communio sit Hebraeis ad fidem christianam translatis cum his, qui adhuc in veteri ritu consistunt, ne forte eorum participio subvertantur. Quicumque igitur amodo ex his, qui baptizati sunt, infidelium consortia non vitaverint, et hi christianis donentur, et illi publicis caedibus deputentur.
Often the associations of the wicked corrupt even the good, how much more those who are prone to vices? Therefore let there be no further communion of Hebrews translated to the Christian faith with those who still remain in the old rite, lest perhaps by their participation they be subverted. Whoever therefore henceforth from those who have been baptized shall not have avoided the associations of unbelievers, let both these be given to Christians, and those be assigned to public beatings.
¶119
CANON LXII
De Iudaeis, qui christianas mulieres in coniugio habent.
Concerning Jews who have Christian women in marriage.
¶120
Iudaei, qui christianas mulieres in coniugio habent, admoneantur ab episcopo civitatis ipsius, ut, si cum eis permanere cupiunt, christiani efficiantur. Quod si admoniti noluerint, separentur, quia non potest infidelis in eius permanere coniunctione, quae iam in christianam translata est fidem. Filii autem, qui ex talibus nati exsistunt, fidem atque condicionem matris sequantur. Similiter et hi, qui procreati sunt de infidelibus mulieribus et fidelibus viris, christianam sequantur religionem, non iudaicam superstitionem.
Jews who have Christian women in marriage are to be admonished by the bishop of that city, that if they wish to remain with them, they be made Christians. But if, having been admonished, they shall be unwilling, let them be separated, because an unbeliever cannot remain in that union with her who has already been translated into Christian faith. Moreover the sons who have been born from such, let them follow the faith and condition of the mother. Similarly also those who have been procreated from unbelieving women and faithful men, let them follow the Christian religion, not the Jewish superstition.
¶121
CANON LXIII
Non potest erga homines esse fidelis, qui exstiterit impius.
He cannot be faithful toward men who has been impious toward God.
¶122
Iudaei ergo, qui dudum christiani effecti sunt et nunc in Christi fidem praevaricati sunt, ad testimonium dicendum admitti non debent, quamvis esse se christianos annuntient, quia sicut in Christi fide suspecti sunt, ita in testimonio humano dubii habentur. Infirmari ergo oportet eorum testimonium, qui in fide falsi docentur, nec eis esse credendum, qui veritatis a se fidem abiecerunt.
Therefore Jews who have long ago been made Christians and now have transgressed in the faith of Christ are not to be admitted for giving testimony, although they announce themselves to be Christians, because just as they are suspect in the faith of Christ, thus they are held doubtful in human testimony. Therefore it is fitting that their testimony be invalidated, who are proven false in faith, nor is to be believed to them who have cast away the faith of truth from themselves.
¶123
CANON LXIV
Praecipiente domino atque excellentissimo Sisenando rege, ut Iudaei aut, qui ex Iudaeis sunt, officia publica nullatenus appetant.
By the command of our lord the most excellent King Sisenand, that Jews or those who are from Jews by no means seek public offices.
¶124
Praecipiente domino atque excellentissimo Sisenando rege id constituit sanctum concilium, ut Iudaei aut hi, qui ex Iudaeis sunt, officia publica nullatenus appetant, quia sub hac occasione christianis iniuriam faciunt. Ideoque iudices provinciarum cum sacerdotibus eorum subreptiones fraudulenter relictas suspendant et officia publica eos agere non permittant. Si quis autem hoc permiserit, velut in sacrilegum excommunicatio proferatur, et is, qui suppresserit, publicis caedibus deputetur.
By the command of our lord and most excellent King Sisenand, the holy council established this, that Jews or those who are from Jews by no means seek public offices, because under this occasion they do injury to Christians. Therefore let the judges of the provinces with their priests suspend their fraudulent subreptions left behind and not permit them to perform public offices. But if anyone shall have permitted this, let excommunication be brought forth as against a sacrilegious person, and let him who shall have suppressed it be assigned to public beatings.
¶125
CANON LXV
Ut Iudaeis non liceat christianos servos habere.
That it not be lawful for Jews to have Christian servants.
¶126
Ex decreto gloriosissimi principis hoc sanctum elegit concilium, ut Iudaeis non liceat christianos servos nec christiana mancipia emere nec cuiusquam consequi largitate. Nefas est enim, ut membra Christi serviant antichristi ministris. Quod si deinceps servos christianos vel ancillas Iudaei habere praesumpserint, sublati ab eorum dominatu libertatem a principe consequantur.
By the decree of the most glorious prince, this holy council has chosen, that it is not lawful for Jews to buy Christian servants or Christian slaves, nor to receive by the largess of anyone. For it is wicked that the members of Christ should serve the ministers of antichrist. But if henceforth Jews shall have presumed to have Christian servants or slaves, let them, taken away from their dominion, obtain freedom from the prince.
¶127
CANON LXVI
De eis, qui de rebus suis pauperibus Christi nulla distribuunt.
Concerning those who distribute nothing from their goods to the poor of Christ.
¶128
Et si illi, qui nulla ex rebus suis pauperibus Christi distribuunt, aeterni iudicis voce in futuro condemnabuntur, quanto magis hi, qui auferunt pauperibus, quod non dederunt. Quapropter episcopi, qui nihil ex proprio suo ecclesiae Christi compensaverunt, hanc divinam sententiam metuant et liberos ex famulis ecclesiae ad condemnationem suam facere non praesumant. Impium est enim, ut, qui res suas ecclesiae Christi non contulerit, damnum inferat et ius ecclesiae alienare contendat. Tales igitur libertos successor episcopus absque aliqua oppositione ad ius ecclesiae revocabit, quia eos non aequitas sed improbitas absolvit.
And if even those who distribute nothing from their goods to the poor of Christ will be condemned in the future by the voice of the eternal Judge, how much more those who take away from the poor what they have not given. Wherefore bishops who have compensated nothing from their own to the church of Christ, let them fear this divine sentence, and let them not presume to make freedmen from the servants of the church for their own condemnation. For it is impious that he who has not conferred his own goods to the church of Christ should bring loss and contend to alienate the right of the church. Therefore the succeeding bishop will recall such freedmen to the right of the church without any opposition, because not equity but wickedness absolved them.
¶129
CANON LXVII
De episcopis, qui mancipium iuris ecclesiae non retento ecclesiastico patrocinio manumitti desiderant.
Concerning bishops who wish a slave of the church's right to be manumitted without retaining the ecclesiastical patronage.
¶130
Episcopus, qui mancipium iuris ecclesiae non retento ecclesiastico patrocinio manumitti desiderat, duo meriti eiusdem et peculii coram concilio ecclesiae, cui praeminet, per commutationem subscribentibus sacerdotibus offerat, ut rata et iusta inveniatur definitio commutantis. Tunc enim liberam manumissionem sine patrocinio ecclesiae concedere poterit, quia eum, quem libertati tradere disponit, iam iuri proprio acquisivit. Huiusmodi autem liberto adversus ecclesiam, cuius iuris exstitit, accusandi vel testificandi denegetur licentia. Quod si praesumpserit, placet, ut stante commutatione in servitutem propriae ecclesiae revocetur, quam nocere conatur.
A bishop who wishes a slave of the church's right to be manumitted without retaining the ecclesiastical patronage, let him offer before the council of the church over which he presides two of its value and of its peculium by commutation, with the priests subscribing, that the definition of the commuting person may be found ratified and just. For then he will be able to grant free manumission without the patronage of the church, because he has already acquired to his own right him whom he intends to hand over to freedom. Moreover against the church, of whose right he existed, let the license of accusing or testifying be denied to a freedman of this kind. But if he shall have presumed, it pleases that, the commutation standing, he be recalled into the servitude of his own church, which he strives to harm.
¶131
CANON LXVIII
Ut sacerdotes, qui aut res suas ecclesiae relinquunt aut nihil habentes aliqua praedia ecclesiis suis conquirunt, si voluerint, possunt de ecclesiae famulis aliquos libertos facere.
That priests who either leave their own goods to the church, or having nothing, yet acquire some estates for their churches, if they wish, are able to make some freedmen from the servants of the church.
¶132
Consensus totius concilii definivit, ut sacerdotes, qui aut res suas ecclesiae relinquunt aut nihil habentes aliqua tamen praedia aut familias ecclesiis suis conquirunt, licebit illis aliquos de familiis eiusdem ecclesiae manumittere iuxta rei collatae modum, quem antiqui canones decreverunt, ita ut cum peculio et posteritate sua ingenui sub patrocinio ecclesiae maneant, utilitates iniunctas sibi, iuxta quod potuerint, prosequentes.
The consensus of the whole council has defined that priests who either leave their own goods to the church, or having nothing, yet acquire some estates or households for their churches, it will be lawful for them to manumit some of the households of the same church according to the measure of the thing conferred, which the ancient canons decreed, so that with their peculium and their posterity they may remain free under the patronage of the church, pursuing the services enjoined upon them, according to what they shall have been able.
¶133
CANON LXIX
De libertis ecclesiae, quia nusquam moritur eorum patrona, a patrocinio eiusdem nusquam discedant.
Concerning freedmen of the church, because their patroness never dies, let them never depart from its patronage.
¶134
Liberti ecclesiae, quia nusquam moritur eorum patrona, a patrocinio eiusdem numquam discedant nec posteritas quidem eorum, sicut priores canones decreverunt. Ac ne forte libertas eorum in futura prole non pateat ipsaque posteritas naturali ingenuitate obnitens sese ab ecclesiae patrocinio subtrahat, necesse est, ut tam idem liberti, quam ab eis progeniti professionem episcopo suo faciant, per quam ex familia ecclesiae liberos effectos se esse fateantur eiusque patrocinium non relinquant, sed iuxta virtutem suam obsequium ei vel oboedientiam praebeant.
Freedmen of the church, because their patroness never dies, let them never depart from its patronage, nor indeed their posterity, just as the prior canons decreed. And lest perhaps their freedom not be open in future offspring and the posterity itself, striving with natural freedom, may not withdraw itself from the church's patronage, it is necessary that both the freedmen themselves and those procreated from them make a profession to their own bishop, by which they may confess that they have been made free from the household of the church and that they do not leave its patronage, but according to their virtue they render service or obedience to it.
¶135
CANON LXX
De libertis ecclesiae, qui a patrocinio eius discedunt.
Concerning freedmen of the church who depart from its patronage.
¶136
Liberti ecclesiae, qui a patrocinio eius discedentes quibuslibet personis adhaeserunt, si admoniti redire contempserint, manumissio eorum irrita sit, quia per inoboedientiae contemptum ingrati actione tenentur.
Freedmen of the church who, departing from its patronage, have adhered to any persons, if, having been admonished, they have despised to return, let their manumission be void, because they are held by the act of ingratitude through the contempt of disobedience.
¶137
CANON LXXI
De libertis, qui a quibuscumque manumissi sunt atque ad ecclesiae patrocinio commendati exsistunt.
Concerning freedmen who have been manumitted by any persons whatsoever and exist commended to the church's patronage.
¶138
Liberti, qui a quibuscumque manumissi sunt atque ecclesiae patrocinio commendati exsistunt, sicut regulae antiquorum patrum constituerunt, sacerdotali defensione a cuiuslibet insolentia protegantur sive in statu libertatis eorum seu in peculio, quod habere noscuntur.
Freedmen who have been manumitted by any persons whatsoever and exist commended to the church's patronage, just as the rules of the ancient fathers have established, let them be protected by priestly defense from the insolence of anyone, both in the state of their freedom and in the peculium which they are known to have.
¶139
CANON LXXII
Ut, qui libertatem a dominis suis ita percipiunt, ut nullum in eis obsequium patronus retentet, suscipiantur ad clerum.
That those who receive freedom from their lords in such a way that the patron retains no service in them, let them be received into the clergy.
¶140
Quicumque libertatem a dominis suis ita percipiunt, ut nullum sibi in eis obsequium patronus retentet, isti, si sine crimine sunt, ad clericatus ordinem liberi suscipiantur, quia directa manumissione absoluti noscuntur. Qui vero retento obsequio manumissi sunt, pro eo, quod adhuc patroni servitute tenentur obnoxii, nullatenus sunt ad ecclesiasticum ordinem promovendi, ne, quando voluerint, eorum domini fiant ex clericis servi.
Whoever receive freedom from their lords in such a way that the patron retains no service in them, these, if they are without crime, let them be received freely into the order of clerics, because they are known to have been absolved by direct manumission. Those who truly have been manumitted with service retained, because they are still held liable to the service of the patron, are by no means to be promoted to the ecclesiastical order, lest, when they wish, their lords might be made servants from clerics.
¶141
CANON LXXIII
De familiis ecclesiae constituere presbyteros et diaconos per parrochias licet.
That it is lawful to establish presbyters and deacons from the households of the church throughout the parishes.
¶142
De familiis ecclesiae constituere presbyteros et diaconos per parrochias liceat, quos tamen vitae rectitudo et probitas morum commendat, ea tamen ratione, ut antea manumissi libertatem status sui percipiant et denuo ad ecclesiasticos honores succedant. Irreligiosum est enim obligatos exsistere servituti, qui sacri ordinis suscipiunt dignitatem. Quicquid autem talibus aut per libertatem concessum aut successione exstiterit debitum aut quolibet commodo collatum, non licebit eis quippiam inde in extraneas personas transmittere, sed omnia ad ius ecclesiae a qua manumissi sunt, oportet post eorum obitum pertinere. His quoque sicut et ceteris ecclesiae libertis accusandi vel testificandi adversus ecclesiam aditus intercluditur. Quod si aspiraverint, non solum libertatis beneficio careant, sed etiam honoris gradu, quem non dignitate naturae, sed tempore necessitatis promeruerunt.
From the households of the church it is lawful to establish presbyters and deacons throughout the parishes, those whom nevertheless the rectitude of life and probity of morals commend, yet with this reason, that before they perceive the freedom of their state, having been manumitted, and then succeed to ecclesiastical honors. For it is irreligious that those who receive the dignity of sacred order should exist bound to servitude. Moreover whatever shall have been due to such persons either by concession through freedom or by succession or conferred by any advantage, it will not be lawful for them to transmit anything from there to extraneous persons, but it is fitting that all things after their death pertain to the right of the church from which they were manumitted. To these also, just as to the other freedmen of the church, let the approach of accusing or testifying against the church be closed. But if they shall have aspired, not only let them lack the benefit of freedom, but also the grade of honor, which they have merited not by dignity of nature but by time of necessity.
¶143
CANON LXXIV
De ecclesiastici ordinis institutis vel decretis, quae ad quorundam pertinent disciplinam.
Concerning the institutes and decrees of the ecclesiastical order, which pertain to the discipline of certain persons.
¶144
Post instituta quaedam ecclesiastici ordinis vel decreta, quae ad quorundam pertinent disciplinam, postrema nobis cunctis sacerdotibus sententia est pro robore nostrorum regum et stabilitate gentis Gothorum pontificale ultimum sub deo iudice ferre decretum. Multarum quippe gentium, ut fama est, tanta exstat perfidia animorum, ut fidem sacramento promissam regibus suis observare contemnant et ore simulent iuramenti professionem, dum retineant mente perfidiae impietatem. Iurant enim regibus suis et fidem, quam pollicentur, praevaricantur nec metuunt volumen illud iudicii dei, per quod inducitur maledictio multaque poenarum comminatio super eos, qui iurant in nomine dei mendaciter. Quae igitur spes talibus populis contra hostes laborantibus erit? Quae fides ultra cum aliis gentibus in pace credenda? Quod foedus non violandum? Quae in hostibus iurata sponsio permanebit, quando nec ipsis propriis regibus iuratam fidem conservant? Quis enim adeo furiosus est, qui caput suum manu sua propria desecet? Illi, ut notum est, immemores salutis suae propria manu seipsos interimunt, in semetipsos suosque reges proprias convertendo vires, et dum dominus dicat: Nolite tangere Christos meos, et David: Quis, inquit, extendit manum suam in christum domini et innocens erit? Illis nec vitare metus est periurium nec regibus inferre exitium. Hostibus quippe fides pacti datur nec violatur. Quod si in bello fides valeat, quanto magis in suis servanda est. Sacrilegium quippe est, si violetur a gentibus regum suorum promissa fides, quia non solum in eis fit pacti transgressio, sed et in deum, in cuius nomine pollicetur ipsa promissio. Inde est, quod multa regna terrarum caelestis iracundia ita permutavit, ut pro impietate fidei et morum alterum ab altero solveretur. Unde et nos cavere oportet casum huiusmodi gentium, ne similiter plaga feriamur praecipiti et poena puniamur crudeli. Si enim deus angelis in se praevaricantibus non pepercit, qui per inoboedientiam caeleste habitaculum perdiderunt, unde et per Esaiam dicit: Inebriatus est gladius meus in caelo, quanto magis nos nostrae salutis interitum timere debemus, ne per infidelitatem eodem saeviente dei gladio pereamus. Quod si divinam iracundiam vitare volumus et severitatem eius ad clementiam provocare cupimus, servemus erga deum religionis cultum atque timorem, custodiamus erga principes nostros pollicitam fidem atque sponsionem. Non sit in nobis sicut in quibusdam gentibus infidelitatis subtilitas impia, non subdolae mentis perfidia, non periurii nefas et coniurationum nefanda molimina. Nullus apud nos praesumptione regnum arripiat, nullus excitet mutuas seditiones civium, nemo meditetur interitus regum, sed defuncto in pace principe primatus totius gentis cum sacerdotibus successorem regni consilio communi constituant, ut, dum unitatis concordia a nobis retinetur, nullum patriae gentisque discidium per vim atque ambitum moliatur. Quod si haec admonitio mentes nostras non corrigit et ad salutem communem cor nostrum nequaquam perducit, audi sententiam nostram: Quicumque igitur a nobis vel totius Spaniae populis qualibet coniuratione vel studio sacramentum fidei suae, quod pro patriae gentisque Gothorum statu vel conservatione regiae salutis pollicitus est, temeraverit aut regem nece attrectaverit aut potestate regni exuerit aut praesumptione tyrannica regni fastigium usurpaverit, anathema sit in conspectu dei patris et angelorum atque ab ecclesia catholica, quam periurio profanaverit, efficiatur extraneus et ab omni coetu christianorum alienus et cum omnibus impietatis suae sociis, quia oportet, ut una poena teneat obnoxios, quos similis error invenerit implicatos, quod iterum secundo replicamus dicentes: Quicumque amodo ex nobis vel cunctis Spaniae populis quolibet tractatu vel studio sacramentum fidei suae, quod pro patriae gentisque Gothorum statu vel conservatione regiae salutis pollicitus est, violaverit aut regem nece attrectaverit aut potestate regni exuerit aut praesumptione tyrannica regni fastigium usurpaverit, anathema in conspectu Christi et apostolorum eius sit atque ab ecclesia catholica, quam periurio profanaverit, efficiatur extraneus et ab omni consortio christianorum alienus et damnatus in futuro dei iudicio habeatur cum conparticibus suis, quia dignum est, ut, qui talibus sociantur, ipsi etiam damnationis eorum participatione obnoxii teneantur. Hoc etiam tertio acclamamus dicentes: Quicumque amodo ex nobis vel cunctis Spaniae populis qualibet meditatione vel studio sacramentum fidei suae, quod pro patriae salute gentisque Gothorum statu vel incolumitate regiae potestatis pollicitus est, violaverit aut regem nece attrectaverit aut potestatem regni exuerit aut praesumptione tyrannica regni fastigium usurpaverit, anathema sit in conspectu spiritus sancti et martyrum Christi atque ab ecclesia catholica, quam periurio profanaverit, efficiatur extraneus et ab omni communione christianorum alienus neque partem habeat iustorum, sed cum diabolo et angelis eius aeternis suppliciis condemnetur una cum eis, qui eadem coniuratione nituntur, ut par poena perditionis constringat, quos in perniciem prava societas copulat. Et ideo, si placet omnibus, qui adestis, haec tertio reiterata sententia vestrae vocis consensu firmetur. Ab universo clero vel populo dictum est: Qui contra hanc vestram definitionem praesumpserint, anathema maranatha, hoc est perditio in adventu domini sint, et cum Iuda Scariot partem habeant et ipsi et socii eorum. Amen.
After certain institutes and decrees of the ecclesiastical order, which pertain to the discipline of certain persons, the final sentence for us all priests is to bring forth under God as judge the ultimate pontifical decree for the strength of our kings and for the stability of the Gothic nation. For the perfidy of mind of many nations, as rumor has it, is so great that they despise to keep the faith promised by oath to their kings, and with mouth they pretend the profession of an oath, while with mind they retain the impiety of perfidy. For they swear to their kings, and the faith which they promise they transgress, nor do they fear that volume of the judgment of God, through which a curse and many threats of punishments are brought upon those who swear falsely in the name of God. What hope therefore will there be for such peoples laboring against enemies? What faith is thereafter to be trusted in peace with other nations? What pact is not to be violated? What sworn promise will remain with enemies, when they do not keep the sworn faith even to their own kings? For who is so furious that he would cut off his own head with his own hand? They, as is known, unmindful of their own salvation, kill themselves with their own hand, turning their own forces against themselves and their own kings, and while the Lord says: "Do not touch my anointed," and David: "Who," he says, "extends his hand against the anointed of the Lord and will be innocent?" By them neither is there fear to avoid perjury nor to bring destruction upon kings. For faith of a pact is given to enemies and is not violated. But if faith is valid in war, how much more is it to be preserved among one's own? For it is a sacrilege if the promised faith of their kings is violated by the peoples, because not only is a transgression of a pact made in them, but also against God, in whose name the promise itself is pledged. Hence it is that the heavenly wrath has so changed many kingdoms of the earth, that on account of the impiety of faith and morals, one was dissolved from another. Whence also we ought to beware of the fall of such peoples, lest similarly we be struck by a headlong blow and be punished with a cruel penalty. For if God did not spare the angels sinning against Him, who lost the heavenly dwelling through disobedience, whence through Isaiah He says: "My sword is inebriated in heaven," how much more ought we to fear the destruction of our own salvation, lest through infidelity we perish by the same raging sword of God? But if we wish to avoid divine wrath and to provoke His severity to clemency, let us keep the cult and fear of religion toward God, let us keep the promised faith and pledge toward our princes. Let there not be in us, as in certain peoples, the impious subtlety of infidelity, not the perfidy of a deceitful mind, not the crime of perjury and the nefarious conspiracies. Let no one seize the kingdom by presumption among us, let no one excite mutual seditions of citizens, let no one meditate the destruction of kings, but when the prince has died in peace, let the chief men of the whole nation with the priests establish the successor of the kingdom by common counsel, so that, while the concord of unity is retained by us, no division of the fatherland and of the nation be brought about by force and ambition. But if this admonition does not correct our minds and by no means lead our heart to common salvation, hear our sentence: Therefore whoever from us or from all the peoples of Spain by any conspiracy or study shall have violated the oath of his faith, which he has promised for the state of the fatherland and of the Gothic nation or for the preservation of the royal safety, or shall have attacked the king with death, or shall have deprived him of the power of the kingdom, or shall have usurped the summit of the kingdom by tyrannical presumption, let him be anathema in the sight of God the Father and the angels, and from the catholic church, which he will have profaned by perjury, let him be made an alien, and an alien from every gathering of Christians, and with all the associates of his impiety — because it is fitting that one penalty hold liable those whom a similar error shall have found implicated — which we repeat a second time again saying: Whoever from now on from us or from all the peoples of Spain by any arrangement or study shall have violated the oath of his faith, which he has promised for the state of the fatherland and of the Gothic nation or for the preservation of the royal safety, or shall have attacked the king with death, or shall have deprived him of the power of the kingdom, or shall have usurped the summit of the kingdom by tyrannical presumption, let him be anathema in the sight of Christ and His apostles, and from the catholic church, which he will have profaned by perjury, let him be made an alien, and let him be held alien from every fellowship of Christians and damned in the future judgment of God together with his partakers, because it is worthy that those who are joined to such persons, they also be held liable by participation in their damnation. This also we acclaim a third time, saying: Whoever from now on from us or from all the peoples of Spain by any meditation or study shall have violated the oath of his faith, which he has promised for the safety of the fatherland and for the state of the Gothic nation or for the safety of the royal power, or shall have attacked the king with death, or shall have deprived him of the power of the kingdom, or shall have usurped the summit of the kingdom by tyrannical presumption, let him be anathema in the sight of the Holy Spirit and the martyrs of Christ, and from the catholic church, which he will have profaned by perjury, let him be made an alien, and let him be alien from every communion of Christians, nor let him have a part with the just, but with the devil and his angels let him be condemned to eternal punishments together with those who strive in the same conspiracy, that a like penalty of perdition may bind those whom a wicked society joins together for destruction. And therefore, if it pleases all of you who are present, let this sentence, reiterated a third time, be confirmed by the consent of your voice. It was said by the whole clergy and people: Those who shall have presumed against this your definition, let them be anathema maranatha, that is, let them be destruction in the coming of the Lord, and let them have a part with Judas Iscariot, both they and their associates. Amen.
¶145
Quapropter nos ipsi sacerdotes omnes ecclesiam Christi ac populum admonemus, ut haec tremenda et toties reiterata sententia nullum ex nobis praesenti atque aeterno condemnet iudicio, sed fidem promissam erga gloriosissimum dominum nostrum Sisenandum regem custodientes ac sincera illi devotione famulantes, non solum divinae pietatis clementiam in nobis provocemus, sed etiam gratiam antefati principis percipere mereamur. Te quoque praesentem regem futurosque aetatum sequentium principes humilitate, qua debemus, deposcimus, ut moderati et mites erga subiectos exsistentes cum iustitia et pietate populos a deo vobis creditos regatis bonamque vicissitudinem, qui vos nobis constituit, largitori Christo respondeatis, regnantes in humilitate cordis cum studio bonae actionis nec quisquam vestrum solus in causam capitum aut rerum sententiam ferat, sed consensu publico cum rectoribus ex iudicio manifesto delinquentium culpa patescat servata a vobis in offensis mansuetudine, ut non severitate magis in illis quam indulgentia polleatis, ut, dum omnia haec auctore deo pio a vobis moderamine conservantur et reges in populis et populi in regibus et deus in utrisque laetetur. Sane de futuris regibus hanc sententiam promulgamus, ut, si quis ex eis contra reverentiam legum superba dominatione et fastu regio in flagitiis et facinore sive cupiditate crudelissimam potestatem in populis exercuerit, anathematis sententia a Christo domino condemnetur et habeat a deo separationem atque iudicium, propter quod praesumpserit prava agere et in perniciem regnum convertere.
Therefore we ourselves, all priests, admonish the church of Christ and the people, that this tremendous and so often reiterated sentence condemn none of us at the present and eternal judgment, but that we, keeping the promised faith toward our most glorious lord King Sisenand and serving him with sincere devotion, not only may provoke the clemency of divine piety in ourselves, but also may deserve to receive the grace of the aforementioned prince. We also beseech you, the present king and the future princes of the following ages, with the humility with which we ought, that, being moderate and mild toward subjects, with justice and piety you rule the peoples entrusted to you by God, and that you render a good return to Christ, the giver who established you for us, reigning in humility of heart with the study of good action, nor let any of you alone give sentence concerning the case of heads or of things, but by public consent with the rectors, let the guilt of the delinquent be made manifest by evident judgment, the gentleness being preserved by you in offenses, that you may prevail over them not more by severity than by indulgence, so that, while all these things are preserved by you with pious moderation, God being the author, both kings may rejoice in the peoples and peoples in the kings and God in both. Truly concerning future kings we promulgate this sentence, that if any of them against the reverence of the laws, with proud domination and royal pride, shall have exercised most cruel power in flagitious deeds and wickedness or cupidity among the peoples, let him be condemned by the sentence of anathema from Christ the Lord, and let him have separation and judgment from God, on account of which he presumed to do evil things and to turn the kingdom into destruction.
¶146
De Suinthilane vero, qui scelera propria metuens seipsum regno privavit et a potestatis fascibus exuit, id cum gentis consultu decrevimus, ut neque eum vel uxorem eius propter mala, quae commiserunt, neque filios eorum unitati nostrae umquam consociemus, nec eos ad honores, a quibus ob iniquitatem deiecti sunt, aliquando promoveamus, quique etiam sicut fastigio regni habentur extranei, ita et possessione rerum, quas de miserorum praesumptibus hauserant, maneant alieni praeter id, quod pietate piissimi principis nostri fuerint consecuti. Non aliter et Geilanem memorati Suinthilani et sanguine et scelere fratrem, qui nec in germanitatis fide stabilis exstitit nec fidem gloriosissimo domino nostro pollicitam conservavit, hunc igitur cum coniuge sua, sicut et antefatum, a societate gentis atque consortio nostro placuit separari, nec in amissis facultatibus, in quibus per iniquitatem creverant, reduces fieri praeter id, quod consecuti fuerint pietate clementissimi principis, cuius gratia bonos donorum praemiis ditat et malos a beneficentia sua non separat.
Concerning Suinthila truly, who, fearing his own crimes, deprived himself of the kingdom and put off from the fasces of power, we have decreed with the counsel of the nation that neither him nor his wife on account of the evils which they committed, nor their sons, should ever be associated to our unity, nor should we ever promote them to the honors from which on account of iniquity they were cast down; and just as they are held alien from the summit of the kingdom, thus also let them remain alien from the possession of goods, which they had taken from the presumptions of the wretched, except that which they shall have obtained by the piety of our most pious prince. Not otherwise also Geila, the brother of the aforementioned Suinthila both in blood and in crime, who was not stable in the faith of brotherhood nor kept the faith promised to our most glorious lord, him therefore with his wife, just as the aforementioned, it has pleased to be separated from the society of the nation and from our fellowship, nor to be made returns in the lost goods, in which they had grown through iniquity, except that which they shall have obtained by the piety of the most clement prince, whose grace enriches the good with the gifts of lords and does not separate the evil from his beneficence.
¶147
Gloria autem et honor omnipotenti deo nostro, in cuius nomine congregati sumus. Post haec pax, salus et diuturnitas piissimo et amatori Christi domino nostro Sisenando regi, cuius devotio nos ad hoc decretum salutiferum convocavit. Corroboret Christi gloria regnum illius gentisque Gothorum in fide catholica, annis et meritis protegat illum usque ad ultimam senectutem summi dei gratia et post praesentis regni gloriam ad aeternum regnum transeat, ut sine fine regnet, qui in saeculo feliciter imperat, ipso praestante, qui est rex regum et dominus dominorum cum patre et cum spiritu sancto in saecula saeculorum. Amen.
But glory and honor to our omnipotent God, in whose name we have been gathered. After these things, peace, health, and long life to the most pious and lover of Christ, our lord King Sisenand, whose devotion has convened us for this salutary decree. May the glory of Christ strengthen his kingdom and of the Gothic nation in the catholic faith, may the grace of the most high God protect him with years and merits until the utmost old age, and after the glory of the present kingdom, let him pass to the eternal kingdom, that he may reign without end, who rules happily in the age, He Himself granting, who is King of kings and Lord of lords with the Father and with the Holy Spirit forever and ever. Amen.

Definitis itaque his, quae superius compraehensa sunt, annuente religiosissimo principe, placuit deinde nulla re impediente a quolibet nostrum ea, quae constituta sunt, temerari, sed cuncta salubri consilio conservare et, quia profectibus ecclesiae et animae nostrae conveniunt, etiam propria subscriptione, ut permaneant, roboramus. Ego Isidorus in Christi nomine ecclesiae Spalensis metropolitanus episcopus haec statuens subscripsi. Similiter et alii episcopi subscripserunt numero XL.
These things therefore having been defined, which are comprehended above, with the most religious prince assenting, it pleased then that none of us should violate the things which have been established by any impediment, but that we preserve all things by salutary counsel, and, because they benefit the progress of the church and our soul, we also strengthen them by our own subscription that they may remain. I, Isidore, in the name of Christ, metropolitan bishop of the church of Seville, establishing these things, have subscribed. Similarly also the other bishops subscribed, forty in number.
