Volume I

Magisterium Universale Extraordinarium Primarium I

The Continuity of Faith: Eastern Dogmas and Their Latin Expressions

Methodological Note for the Volume: The dogmatic column in each chapter identifies newly defined dogmas at each council — reaffirmations and synthetic clarifications of prior councils are distinguished from formal new definitions. This governs the selection of primary dogma throughout.

Chapter 5 — Constantinople II (553)

De Perpetua Virginitate Mariae, Semper Virgine

Newly Defined Dogma (in the sense of new formal definition): Perpetual Virginity of Mary (aeiparthenos) as dogmatic title formally received

Note on methodology: The neo-Chalcedonian synthesis (Three Chapters condemnation, vindication of Cyril's Twelve Anathemas within a Chalcedonian framework) was the primary historical occasion and controversy of Constantinople II. However, measured by newly defined dogma — formal definition of something not previously defined at an ecumenical council — the aeiparthenos affirmation is the relevant datum for this volume's structure. The neo-Chalcedonian synthesis was a clarification and reconciliation of prior Chalcedonian definition, not a new dogmatic object. This distinction should be stated explicitly in the chapter introduction to preempt the Byzantine objection that the primary dogma has been misidentified.

Chapter 7 — Nicaea II (787)

DE LEGITIMITATE ET FUNDAMENTO DOCTRINAE VENERATIONIS SACRARUM IMAGINUM

Newly Defined Dogma: Legitimacy and doctrinal grounding of the veneration of sacred images (proskunesis distinguished from latreia)

Texts in this chapter

  • Second Council of Nicaea 787 Greek source
  • Council of Trent — Session 25 1563 Latin · post-Gregorian
Reception table (Greek source → Latin witnesses)
TierSourceNotes
Post-Gregorian LatinTrent, Session 25 (1563)Explicit affirmation of the veneration of images, relics, and invocation of saints — the primary Post-Gregorian Latin reception of Nicaea II's substance
Pre-Gregorian LatinNo strong positive equivalentFrankfurt (794) is an anti-parallel: it rejected Nicaea II on the basis of a defective Latin translation that conflated proskunesis (veneration) with latreia (worship). Hadrian I had already accepted Nicaea II on Rome's behalf. The Carolingian-Roman asymmetry must be stated explicitly — Frankfurt cannot be listed as a supporting council for Nicaea II without this caveat. The honest position is that the Latin pre-Gregorian tradition has no positive conciliar parallel to Nicaea II, and that the Frankfurt confusion was corrected not by a council but by gradual Roman practice and ultimately Trent
