Appendix — Lateran IV Canon 1 as Latin Dogmatic Synthesis

Lateran IV Canon 1 functions as the single most important Latin synthesis of the first six Greek councils. Its dense prologue covers in sequence: Trinitarian theology (Nicaea I, Constantinople I), Christology and hypostatic unity (Ephesus, Chalcedon), dyophysitism (Chalcedon), and Eucharistic theology rooted in the same Christological framework. It does not address dyothelitism (Constantinople III) explicitly, but its Christological framework presupposes it.

Addition from assessment: The appendix should note explicitly that Canon 1 functions as the Latin tradition's own internal recapitulation of the first six councils — not an external reception statement, but a synthetic doctrinal confession that demonstrates the Latin church's self-understanding as continuous with the Greek conciliar tradition. This is the argumentative capstone of the volume's thesis.
